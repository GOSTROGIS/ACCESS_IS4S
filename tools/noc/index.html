    What is a NOC code and why it matters

    The National Occupational Classification (NOC) is Canada’s list of occupations. Each occupation, called a unit group, has a 5-digit code. Statistics Canada and Employment and Social Development Canada build it together.1

    • Read the code. The first digit is the broad category, for example 7 for trades, transport and equipment operators. The second digit is the TEER category: the training, education, experience and responsibilities the job needs.2
    • Job Bank. Job Bank shows wages and job outlooks by occupation with the NOC code, for example carpenter (NOC 72310).3
    • Immigration. Express Entry asks you to find the NOC for each job you have held. Skilled work experience must be in TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3, and you choose the NOC whose main duties match what you did, not only the job title.4,5
    • Government programs. Federal programs such as Employment Insurance and the Temporary Foreign Worker Program use the NOC.2
    • It changes. A new version, NOC 2026, is planned for December 2026. Some codes may change, so open the official profile before you put a code on a form.6

    NOC 2021 Version 1.0 from Statistics Canada: 516 unit groups and 27,935 official example job titles, downloaded 2026-11-07. NOC 2021 Version 1.0 is the version in force on that date. All searching happens on this device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

    This is the complete NOC 2021 Version 1.0 classification, including trades outside the trades and transport category.
