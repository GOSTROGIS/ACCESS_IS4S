Lesson 10. Parallel line development

Lay out the pattern for a pipe cut at an angle.

Listen

The voice reads this lesson slowly. Pause any time. Play it again as often as you like.

  1. The pattern is the pipe rolled flat.

    Length = π × D

  2. Pipe D = 168 mm.

    SO = π × 168 = 527.8 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    π×168=

    Screen 527.7875658

  3. Split it into 12 equal spaces.

    527.8 ÷ 12 = 44.0 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    π×168÷12=

    Screen 43.98229715

  4. Each space gets a line. Measure each line.

    Cut 22.5°, L = 200: shortest 165 mm, longest 235 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    200−84×22.5TAN=

    Screen 165.2060608

  5. Join the tops with a smooth curve. That is the cut line.

    Lines 8 to 12 repeat lines 6 to 2.

Listen: worked examples

Two more examples, a mistake to watch for, and practice questions, read aloud.

Worked example 1. All 7 lines for a 45° cut

  1. Rise of the cut: r × tan θ.

    57 × tan 45° = 57 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    57×45TAN=

    Screen 57.

  2. Line = L − 57 × cos(angle). Angles go up by 30°.

    cos 0° = 1, cos 30° = 0.866, cos 60° = 0.5, cos 90° = 0

  3. Line 2.

    150 − 57 × 0.866 = 100.6 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    150−57×30COS=

    Screen 100.636552

  4. All 7 lines.

    93, 100.6, 121.5, 150, 178.5, 199.4, 207 mm

Answer: 93 to 207 mm

Worked example 2. A 90° tee: branch on a main

  1. Main radius 84. Branch radius 57.

    Point at 30°: out to the side 57 × sin 30° = 28.5 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    57×30SIN=

    Screen 28.5

  2. Height of the main there.

    √(84² − 28.5²) = 79.02 mm

  3. Line = H − height.

    200 − 79.02 = 120.98 mm

    On the TI-30Xa

    200−(84x²−28.5x²)√x=

    Screen 120.9825969

  4. Lines at 0°, 30°, 60°, 90°.

    116, 121, 132, 138.3 mm

Answer: 116, 121, 132, 138 mm

Watch out: Calculator in RAD, not DEG.

Look for DEG on the screen before you press sin, cos or tan.
