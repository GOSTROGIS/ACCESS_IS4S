Lesson 6. Plate mass by the shop rule

Find how much a steel plate weighs before you lift it.

Listen

The voice reads this lesson slowly. Pause any time. Play it again as often as you like.

  1. Imperial rule: 40.8 lb per square foot, for each inch thick.

    lb = L' × W' × 40.8 × T"

  2. Plate 8' × 4' × 1/2".

    8 × 4 × 40.8 × 0.5 = 652.8 lb

    On the TI-30Xa

    8×4×40.8×.5=

    Screen 652.8

  3. Metric rule: 7.85 kg per square metre, for each mm thick.

    kg = L m × W m × T mm × 7.85

  4. Plate 2,440 × 1,220 × 10 mm.

    2.44 × 1.22 × 10 × 7.85 = 233.68 kg

Listen: worked examples

Two more examples, a mistake to watch for, and practice questions, read aloud.

Worked example 1. Sizes in inches

  1. Square inches to square feet: ÷ 144.

    48 × 30 ÷ 144 = 10 sq ft

  2. × 40.8 × thickness in inches.

    10 × 40.8 × 0.75 = 306 lb

    On the TI-30Xa

    48×30×40.8×.75÷144=

    Screen 306.

Answer: 306 lb

Worked example 2. A round plate, metric

  1. Area in square metres.

    π × 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.2827 sq m

  2. × thickness in mm × 7.85.

    0.2827 × 20 × 7.85 = 44.39 kg

    On the TI-30Xa

    .3x²×π×20×7.85=

    Screen 44.3907042

Answer: 44.39 kg

Watch out: Putting the thickness in metres.

Metric rule: length and width in metres, thickness in millimetres.
