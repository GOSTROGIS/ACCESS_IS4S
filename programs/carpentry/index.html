Carpentry

The carpenter trade in BC, the materials every carpentry intake shares, and each intake’s own schedule.

About the trade

Carpenters build the forms, frames, floors, walls, stairs and finishes that turn a drawing into a building. The work takes careful measuring and layout and a lot of math: fractions on a tape, areas and volumes for materials, and right angles for work that is square, level and plumb. In BC, carpenters learn the trade through an apprenticeship, and a Foundation program is one way to start.

What carpenters do
Build, install, renovate, maintain and repair homes and other buildings and structures, working with wood, steel, concrete and other materials.[2][4]
Certification in BC
Red Seal trade. Finishing the apprenticeship and passing the interprovincial Red Seal exam gives a Certificate of Qualification with a Red Seal endorsement, which is recognized across Canada.[1][2][4]
Work-based training
6,360 hours of training on the job.[1]
Technical training
28 weeks of classroom training over 4 years: Levels 1 to 4, 210 hours each.[1][2]
Carpenter Foundation
A program for people new to the trade. It generally takes about 720 hours, and finishing it gives credit for Level 1 technical training and 450 hours of work-based training.[2]
Compulsory in BC?
No. Carpenter is not one of BC’s Skilled Trades Certification (compulsory) trades, so you can work in the trade without a certificate. Certification is voluntary everywhere in Canada except Quebec.[3][4]
NOC 2021 code
72310 Carpenters, in the Statistics Canada National Occupational Classification. Apprentices are included in this code.[4] Look up any job’s NOC code.
