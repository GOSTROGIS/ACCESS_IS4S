Who Pays

Lottery and casino profits go to the provincial government. The people who hand over the biggest share of what they have are the people with the least. That is why this page has the name it has.

Share of household spending on games of chance, Canada, 2019

The poorest fifth of households spent $111 a year, the richest fifth $256. As a share of what each household spends, the poorest pay three times as much.

Statistics Canada, Insights on Canadian Society, August 2022, from the Survey of Household Spending.

When US states brought in a lottery, the lowest-income third of households cut spending on rent, mortgage and other bills by 5.8% and on food at home by 2.8%. The ticket money came out of the household budget, not out of other gambling.

Kearney, State lotteries and consumer behavior, Journal of Public Economics (2005).

43%

Lottery spending tracks innumeracy and poor statistical reasoning, which both drop as income rises. Together they account for 43% of lottery purchases in a nationally representative US survey.

Lockwood, Allcott, Taubinsky and Sial, Review of Economic Studies (2025).

50%

GameSense, BCLC's own player information site, lists the lottery's house advantage at 50%. Every dollar spent on tickets returns about half.
