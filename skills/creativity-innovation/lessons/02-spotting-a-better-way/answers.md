# Spotting a better way on the job site

## Task 1 (level 1)

Answers will vary. A good process map:

- lists at least five steps in the real order
- is specific ("walk to the tool crib and wait for sign-out", not "get stuff")
- marks at least one W, D or R, such as walking to the crib (W), waiting for the key to the material rack (D), measuring each piece of stock separately (R)
- if the routine was timed, writes the times beside the steps

## Task 2 (level 1)

Any two of:

- keeping the gang box in the sea-can all day
- only the lead hand handing out fasteners
- each framer walking for their own fasteners instead of one person stocking for the crew

The lock-up at the end of the day is not a habit to question. It is a site security rule.

## Task 3 (level 2)

| Limit | Real or possibly artificial? | Who to ask |
|---|---|---|
| The gang box stays on the ground floor | Possibly artificial | Foreman or site superintendent |
| Hot work needs a permit from the prime contractor | Real | No change. Follow the permit process |
| Fittings are ordered one day at a time | Possibly artificial | Foreman or the person who orders material |
| Only the foreman can carry the drawings | Possibly artificial | Foreman |
| Workers wear fall protection near the open slab edge | Real | No change. It is a safety requirement |

Accept "Real" for a possibly artificial item if the learner gives a sensible reason and says they would ask. The two safety items must be marked Real.

## Task 4 (level 2)

a) 3 × 5 × 6 = 90 minutes a day.

b) 90 × 5 = 450 minutes. 450 ÷ 60 = 7.5 hours a week.

c) Brackets first: (5 − 2) × 6 × 3 = 3 × 6 × 3 = 54 minutes a day saved.

## Task 5 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good statement:

- starts with "How might we …"
- names the people affected and the problem
- does not name one fix
- includes a real limit

Example: "How might we cut the pipefitters' trips to the ground floor for fittings, while keeping tools locked up at night and following the hot work permit rules?"

## Check yourself

**1.** Walking, waiting and repeating. Level 1.

**2.** Real: any safety rule, permit, code requirement or site rule from the prime contractor, such as locking up tools at night or wearing fall protection. Possibly artificial: any unchecked habit, such as where the gang box sits during the day. Level 1.

**3.** Safety rules protect people from injury. Even when they slow work, the right move is to raise the concern with a supervisor, the joint health and safety committee or the worker health and safety representative, not to work around the rule. Level 1.

**4.** Brackets first: (8 − 3) × 5 × 2 = 5 × 5 × 2 = 50 minutes a day. Level 2.

**5.** It picks one answer (a second sea-can) before anyone has looked at other ideas. A better version: "How might we cut the time the crew spends getting tools and fasteners?" Level 2.
