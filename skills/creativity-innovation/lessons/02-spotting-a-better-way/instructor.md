# Spotting a better way on the job site

Session 2 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 60 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask the group: "Think of a job, a shop or even a kitchen where you did something the slow way because that was how everyone did it. What was it?" Take two or three answers. Then tell this fictional story: "A first-year apprentice on a framing crew counted his trips to the sea-can for one day. It was 14. He showed the number to his lead hand, who had never thought about it. The next week the crew had a rolling box on the deck." Point out that the apprentice did not invent anything. He noticed, counted and spoke up.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- map a routine task and mark walking, waiting and repeating (Use your imagination and curiosity)
- tell real limits from possibly artificial ones, and question habits where it is appropriate (Identify opportunities for you to innovate)
- put a number on wasted time using BEDMAS
- write an opportunity statement that leaves room for more than one answer (Identify opportunities for you to innovate)

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. Name one rule or habit at a job you have had that seemed to slow the work down. Do you know why it was there?
2. If 2 workers each walk 10 minutes a day for supplies, how many minutes is that for both of them in a 5-day week?

**Safety note.** Some learners will want to label every rule they dislike as artificial. Hold the line: safety rules, permits and PPE requirements are real limits. The creative move is to raise a concern with the supervisor or the committee, not to work around it.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Name the three kinds of waste and give a site example of one.
2. "The welding screens have to be up before anyone strikes an arc." Is this a real or a possibly artificial limit? Why?
3. Rewrite "How might we get a forklift?" as an opportunity statement that leaves room for other answers.

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- noticing norms and habits in routine work
- walking, waiting and repeating as waste you can see
- real limits, possibly artificial limits, and why safety rules are always real
- putting a number on wasted time with BEDMAS
- writing an opportunity statement that starts with "How might we …"
