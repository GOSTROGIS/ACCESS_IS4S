Building a quick model and explaining it

Turn an idea into a quick model with building bricks, then give spoken directions clear enough for someone else to build it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Develop your ideas; Apply your ideas

Session 5 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 60 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone ever been given directions on a job that made no sense until someone drew it on a scrap of plywood or showed you?" Take two answers. Tell learners that today they will build a quick model of an idea and then find out how hard it is to explain it well enough for someone else to build.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • build a quick model of a job-site idea from building bricks (Develop your ideas)
  • add the details another person needs to build it: what, how many, where, which way, in what order (Develop your ideas)
  • test their directions, learn from what went wrong and improve them (Apply your ideas)

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. What makes spoken directions easy to follow? Name two things.
  2. If someone facing you says "turn it to the left," which way do you turn it?

Learning tasks

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening question. Share examples. None 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Ask questions. Learner page 3 min
1.3 Pose the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. Paper 4 min
2.1 Teach the key ideas: quick models, the five kinds of detail, whose left, expecting the first try to go wrong. Read the Tess example aloud. Follow the example. Learner page 7 min
2.2 Hand out bags. Each pair must have identical pieces. Set Task 1. Build a model in secret. Bags of 10 to 12 building bricks, identical within each pair 6 min
2.3 Set up pairs back to back so neither can see the other's work. Run Round 1 (Task 2). Enforce no questions and no looking. Give or follow directions, then compare. Chairs back to back 8 min
2.4 Set Task 3. Ask two pairs to share their biggest miss. List problems and missing details. Learner page 5 min
2.5 Run Round 2 with questions allowed, then have pairs switch roles and run both rounds (Task 4). Improve directions, compare, switch. Bricks 10 min
2.6 Set Task 5. Point out that written steps are the same skill without the chance to answer questions. Write numbered build steps. Learner page 5 min
3.1 Run the post-assessment. Answer the three questions. Paper 5 min
3.2 Summarize the session. Tell learners the next session tests a model with a load, and they will expect some designs to fail. Keep the written steps. None 2 min

Materials. If you do not have building bricks, use identical sets of paper cups, wooden blocks or cut cardboard shapes. Prepare cardboard pieces before class. If learners cut any cardboard, have them use scissors on a stable surface and close them when not in use.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Give one instruction from your Round 2 directions that was better than Round 1, and say which detail you added.
  2. How did being allowed to ask questions change Round 2?
  3. On a job site, what is one idea you could explain better with a quick model than with words alone?

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • quick models as a fast, cheap way to show an idea
  • the five kinds of detail: what, how many, where, which way, in what order
  • whose left and right, and saying "from where you are sitting"
  • testing directions, finding what went wrong and fixing it
  • writing build steps someone else can follow

Access option: If back-to-back speech or handling small bricks is a barrier, use typed, signed or written directions and larger pieces, or let a partner place pieces as directed. Keep the reference model hidden for the first round and allow questions in the second. Assess the clarity and improvement of directions, not hearing, speech or hand dexterity.
