Building a quick model and explaining it

Turn an idea into a quick model with building bricks, then give spoken directions clear enough for someone else to build it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Develop your ideas; Apply your ideas

Task 1 (level 1)

Open task. A good model uses every brick, is something with a job-site purpose, and stays hidden from the partner.

Task 2 (level 1)

Open task. A good Round 1:

  • the speaker gives one step at a time and waits for the builder
  • the builder does not ask questions or look
  • both compare honestly at the end and point out where the models differ

A poor match is normal in Round 1 and is not a failure of the task.

Task 3 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good response lists at least two real problems and names the missing detail for each. Examples:

What went wrong Missing detail
Used a 2 × 2 brick instead of a 2 × 4 What
Put two bricks on top instead of three How many
Built on the right end instead of the left Where
Laid the brick on its side Which way
Built the roof before the walls In what order

Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good Round 2:

  • adds the details found missing in Task 3
  • uses "from where you are sitting" for left and right
  • the builder asks short, useful questions ("Long side toward me or away?")
  • the match is closer than Round 1
  • both partners take both roles

Task 5 (level 2)

Answers will vary. Good build steps:

  • are numbered, 5 to 8 steps
  • use order words (first, next, then, last)
  • name each piece by size, shape or colour
  • give counts, positions and which way each piece faces
  • could be followed by someone who never saw the model

Example step: "3. Next, take the blue brick with 1 row of 6 bumps. Stand it on its long edge along the back of the base, from where you are sitting."

Check yourself

1. A rough version of an idea, made fast. Cheap materials mean you can find problems early without wasting real time or material. Level 1.

2. What, how many, where, which way, in what order. Level 1.

3. Their own left, which is your right. That is why you say "from where you are sitting" or use a fixed point such as "the end near the door." Level 2.

4. The person giving directions knows the model and forgets to say details the builder cannot see. You find what was missing, add it and try again. Level 2.
