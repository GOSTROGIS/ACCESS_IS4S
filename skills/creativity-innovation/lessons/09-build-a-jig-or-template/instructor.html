Creativity and innovation · Lesson 09 · Instructor

Build a jig or template

Design a drilling jig for a repeated shop task, test it as a cardboard prototype, then work out how much time it saves.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Apply your ideas; Develop your ideas; Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others

Session 9 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a jig, template, story stick or stop block from the shop. Ask: "What does this do? Why would someone spend time making it?" Remind learners of the plate-marking ideas from lesson 3. Say: "Today you design a real jig, test it in cardboard, and prove whether it pays for itself."

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read a dimensioned jig drawing and work out hole positions on a fixed spacing (Develop your ideas)
  • generate several jig ideas for a repeated task before choosing one (Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others)
  • plan and test a cheap prototype, and use the result to improve the design (Apply your ideas)
  • work out time saved, net time saved and break-even with BEDMAS (Apply your ideas)
  • state the safety steps for drilling with a jig

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. Holes are 32 mm apart. The first is at 22 mm. Where is the third hole?
  2. A task takes 2 minutes by hand and 0.5 minutes with a jig. How much time does the jig save on 40 pieces?

Learning tasks

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening question with a real jig. Say what the jig does. A jig, template or stop block 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Ask questions. Learner page 3 min
1.3 Pose the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. Paper 4 min
2.1 Teach the key ideas: jig and template, fence, prototype loop, the 32 mm system and the three time formulas. Demonstrate the drill safety steps with a cordless drill, clamps and safety glasses. Follow the demo. Repeat the safety steps back. Complete Task 9. Cordless drill, clamps, safety glasses, learner page 12 min
2.2 Walk through the Westshore example. Have learners list the 9 hole positions and check the 256 mm span. Follow along. Complete Tasks 1 and 2. Learner page, calculator 12 min
2.3 Set Tasks 3 and 4. Work one line of Task 3 on the board with brackets first. Work out time saved and break-even. Calculator 8 min
2.4 Set Task 5 as a 5-minute idea round, no judging. Then set Task 6. Generate at least 6 ideas, then write a prototype plan. Paper, learner page 10 min
2.5 Hand out cardboard, rulers and awls. Each pair makes a cardboard version of the 9-hole jig and measures hole 1 to hole 9. Nothing is drilled in this step. Build and measure a cardboard prototype. Record the error. Cardboard, steel rulers, awls, cutting mats, scissors 10 min
2.6 Set Tasks 7 and 8. Circulate and ask: "How many gaps between 19 holes?" Solve the zone and error problems. Calculator 13 min
2.7 Set Task 10. Ask two learners to give their pitch. Write and give a pitch. Learner page 5 min
3.1 Run the post-assessment. Answer the three questions. Paper 5 min
3.2 Summarize. Tell learners the next session is about sketching ideas so others can build them. Keep the cardboard prototype. None 3 min

Safety. In this session, learners only draw, cut cardboard with scissors and poke holes with an awl. Any real drilling of a jig happens later, under your shop's rules, with the work clamped, glasses on and the chuck key removed. If utility knives are used for cardboard, follow the cutting steps from lesson 6.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A jig has 6 holes on 32 mm centres. What is the span from the first hole to the last? (160 mm)
  2. A jig takes 45 minutes to make and saves 9 minutes a piece. After how many pieces does it pay for itself? (5)
  3. Name two safety steps for drilling with a jig.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • what jigs and templates are, and why they locate from a fixed edge
  • the 32 mm system for shelf-pin holes
  • prototyping in cardboard before building the real jig
  • time saved, net time saved and break-even
  • drilling safely with a jig
