# Build a jig or template

Session 9 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a jig, template, story stick or stop block from the shop. Ask: "What does this do? Why would someone spend time making it?" Remind learners of the plate-marking ideas from lesson 3. Say: "Today you design a real jig, test it in cardboard, and prove whether it pays for itself."

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- read a dimensioned jig drawing and work out hole positions on a fixed spacing (Develop your ideas)
- generate several jig ideas for a repeated task before choosing one (Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others)
- plan and test a cheap prototype, and use the result to improve the design (Apply your ideas)
- work out time saved, net time saved and break-even with BEDMAS (Apply your ideas)
- state the safety steps for drilling with a jig

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. Holes are 32 mm apart. The first is at 22 mm. Where is the third hole?
2. A task takes 2 minutes by hand and 0.5 minutes with a jig. How much time does the jig save on 40 pieces?

**Safety.** In this session, learners only draw, cut cardboard with scissors and poke holes with an awl. Any real drilling of a jig happens later, under your shop's rules, with the work clamped, glasses on and the chuck key removed. If utility knives are used for cardboard, follow the cutting steps from lesson 6.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. A jig has 6 holes on 32 mm centres. What is the span from the first hole to the last? (160 mm)
2. A jig takes 45 minutes to make and saves 9 minutes a piece. After how many pieces does it pay for itself? (5)
3. Name two safety steps for drilling with a jig.

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- what jigs and templates are, and why they locate from a fixed edge
- the 32 mm system for shelf-pin holes
- prototyping in cardboard before building the real jig
- time saved, net time saved and break-even
- drilling safely with a jig
