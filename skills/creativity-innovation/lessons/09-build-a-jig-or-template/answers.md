# Build a jig or template

## Task 1 (level 1)

a) **32 mm** apart, centre to centre.

b) **37 mm** from the fence face.

c) **9 holes**, each **5 mm** in diameter.

## Task 2 (level 2)

a) **20, 52, 84, 116, 148, 180 and 212 mm.** Start at 20 and add 32 each time.

b) **232 mm.** 7 holes have 6 gaps. 6 × 32 = 192 mm. 20 + 192 + 20 = 232 mm.

## Task 3 (level 2)

a) **176 holes.** 8 × 2 × 11 = 176.

b) **123.2 minutes.** (1.2 − 0.5) × 176 = 0.7 × 176 = 123.2. Brackets first. Check another way: 176 × 1.2 = 211.2 and 176 × 0.5 = 88. 211.2 − 88 = 123.2.

## Task 4 (level 2)

a) **15.4 minutes.** 0.7 × 22 = 15.4.

b) **4 sides.** 60 ÷ 15.4 = 3.9. Round up to 4. After 3 sides the jig has saved 46.2 minutes, not yet 60. After 4 sides it has saved 61.6 minutes.

## Task 5 (level 2)

Open answer. At least 6 different ideas, not judged, with at least two prompts named. Examples:

- steel template plate with two holes, clamped on each clip (do it differently)
- drill press with a fence and an end stop, so each clip drops into the same spot (combine)
- a fixture that holds 4 clips at once (expand)
- mark one master clip perfectly and use it as a pattern (do it differently)
- ask the supplier to punch the holes (ask a question)
- move the drill to the clip instead of the clip to the drill, with a magnetic drill and a stop block (reverse)

## Task 6 (level 2)

Open answer. A good plan names a cheap first version (cardboard, MDF or scrap plywood), a measurable test (for example, "drill 3 scrap clips and measure hole spacing and edge distance"), and a clear pass mark (for example, "50 mm ± 0.5 mm on all 3").

## Task 7 (level 3)

a) **19 holes.** The zone is 750 − 150 = 600 mm. 600 ÷ 32 = 18.75, so 18 full gaps fit. 18 gaps make 19 holes. The span is 18 × 32 = 576 mm.

b) Space left over: 600 − 576 = 24 mm. Half at each end: 12 mm. First hole: 150 + 12 = **162 mm** from the bottom. Last hole: 750 − 12 = **738 mm**. Check: 162 + 576 = 738 ✓.

c) Open answer. One way: place a 140 mm spacer under the end of the jig so its first hole (22 mm from the end) lands at 162 mm. Drill 9 holes. Slide the jig up so its first hole sits over hole 9, with a 5 mm pin in that hole to lock it in place. Drill the next 8 holes. Move it once more the same way and drill the last 2 holes. 9 + 8 + 2 = 19.

## Task 8 (level 3)

a) **10 gaps.** Each should be **32 mm**. 10 × 32 = 320 mm.

b) **0.4 mm per gap.** Error: 324 − 320 = 4 mm. 4 ÷ 10 = 0.4 mm.

c) Open answer. Example: build the next version from plywood and drill the holes on a drill press with a stop, then measure first hole to last hole again. A small error in each gap adds up across the row, so measure the whole span, not just one gap.

## Task 10 (level 3)

Open answer. A strong pitch has all six parts and the numbers. Example:

"Marking 216 shelf holes by hand takes about 1.2 minutes each, so about 260 minutes on this job. I'd like to use a plywood jig with a fence, 9 holes on 32 mm centres, 37 mm from the edge. I tested a cardboard version. It was 3 mm off over the row, so I made the plywood one on the drill press, and it measures right. It took an hour to make, and it cuts the time to about 0.5 minutes a hole. That saves about 90 minutes on this job after the build time, and it pays for itself after 5 sides. We clamp it and the work every time, glasses on. Can I use it on the 12 sides this week?"
