Measure an improvement

Run a fair test of an old way and a new way, work out the average, percent time saved and break-even, and decide if the change is worth it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Apply your ideas; Develop your ideas

Task 1 (level 1)

Fastest old-way cut: trial 2, 47 seconds. Slowest new-way cut: trial 5, 22 seconds.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) Without the fixture: (95 + 88 + 102 + 91 + 94) ÷ 5 = 470 ÷ 5 = 94 seconds. With the fixture: (41 + 38 + 44 + 40 + 37) ÷ 5 = 200 ÷ 5 = 40 seconds.

b) Without: 102 − 88 = 14 seconds. With: 44 − 37 = 7 seconds.

Task 3 (level 2)

57.4%. (94 − 40) ÷ 94 × 100 = 54 ÷ 94 × 100 = 57.4468..., which rounds to 57.4%.

Task 4 (level 2)

The check is 608 to 612 mm.

  • Old way: 607 (fail), 612 (pass), 610 (pass), 615 (fail), 609 (pass). 3 of 5 pass.
  • New way: 610, 611, 609, 610, 612. 5 of 5 pass.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Saving per bracket: 94 − 40 = 54 seconds. 54 × 150 = 8100 seconds. 8100 ÷ 60 = 135 minutes.

b) 90 minutes. 135 − 45 = 90.

Task 6 (level 2)

20 pieces. 8 minutes = 8 × 60 = 480 seconds. 480 ÷ 24 = 20.

Task 7 (level 3)

Any three, each with a fix:

  1. Different people on different days and times. Fix: the same person does both methods, on the same day, in mixed order.
  2. Different material (1/2 inch and 5/8 inch). Fix: use the same thickness and sheet size for both.
  3. He dropped a slow trial with no real reason. Fix: keep every trial, or write down a real reason such as a broken blade.
  4. Different start points for the timing. Fix: time both methods from the same start (picking up the sheet) to the same end.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Saving per cut: 90 − 35 = 55 seconds. 55 × 400 = 22,000 seconds. 22,000 ÷ 3600 = 6.111..., about 6.11 hours.

b) $275.00. 22,000 ÷ 3600 × 45 = 275. (Using the rounded 6.11 hours gives $274.95. Accept either if the working is shown.)

c) $105. $60 material + 1 hour × $45 = $105.

d) Yes. Net saving: $275 − $105 = $170, and the jig can be used again.

Task 9 (level 3)

a) 46.4%. (28 − 15) ÷ 28 × 100 = 13 ÷ 28 × 100 = 46.43..., which rounds to 46.4%.

b) Not yet. It is faster, but 2 of 5 cuts (40%) are out by 3 mm when the job allows 1 mm. Those nipples need correction or replacement. Measure that extra time and material before claiming a net saving; the scenario does not say how much rework costs.

c) Find out why the cuts are long (for example, a stop that moves or a measuring error), fix it, and test 5 more cuts for both time and length.

Task 10 (level 2)

Open answer. Example: "I timed 5 cuts each way. Measuring with a tape averaged 51 seconds a cut, and the stop block averaged 20, which is about 61% less time. All 5 stop-block cuts were within 1 mm of 368 mm, but only 1 of the 5 tape cuts was. On 240 pieces that saves about 114 minutes after the 10-minute set-up."

Check yourself

1. Any three of: the person, the material and size, the tool and set-up apart from the change, and the start and stop points for timing. Level 1.

2. Because the saving is a part of the old time. The old time is what you started with. Level 2.

3. (40 − 30) ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%. Level 2.

4. The number of pieces after which the set-up time has been paid back. Level 2.

5. If the quality does not meet the requirement, the work needs correction or replacement. That adds time and material costs and can outweigh the speed saving. Level 2.
