Measure an improvement

Run a fair test of an old way and a new way, work out the average, percent time saved and break-even, and decide if the change is worth it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Apply your ideas; Develop your ideas

Session 11 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has a foreman ever said no to an idea you were sure was better? What would have changed their mind?" Take answers. Say: "Today you learn to prove an idea with a fair test and a few numbers. That is what turns 'I think' into 'I know'."

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • plan and run a fair test of an old and a new method (Apply your ideas)
  • work out averages, range and percent time saved with BEDMAS (Apply your ideas)
  • check quality as well as speed (Develop your ideas)
  • work out break-even and the saving on a whole job, and decide if a change is worth it (Apply your ideas)

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. Three trials took 12, 15 and 18 seconds. What is the average?
  2. A job took 50 minutes and now takes 40. What fraction of the time was saved?

Learning tasks

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening question. Share examples. Whiteboard 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Ask questions. Learner page 3 min
1.3 Pose the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. Paper 4 min
2.1 Teach the fair test, average, range, percent time saved and break-even. Stress "divide by the old time". Complete Task 7 in pairs. Learner page 10 min
2.2 Walk through Priya's worked example step by step. Have learners work each line on a calculator before you show it. Follow along. Complete Task 1. Learner page, calculators 12 min
2.3 Classroom test. Pairs time 5 trials of folding and taping a cardboard box with a ruler and pencil, then 5 with a cardboard template they make in 5 minutes. Same person, same box size, same start and stop points. Run the test and record times. Check each box against a size limit you set. Cardboard, rulers, pencils, tape, scissors, stopwatch or phone 20 min
2.4 Set Tasks 2, 3 and 4. Work out averages, range, percent and quality passes. Calculators 10 min
2.5 Set Tasks 5, 6 and 8. Work the dollar part of Task 8 on the board if needed. Scale up, break-even and cost. Calculators 12 min
2.6 Set Tasks 9 and 10. Read two result statements aloud. Decide on the pipe nipple method. Write a result statement. Learner page 6 min
3.1 Run the post-assessment. Answer the three questions. Paper 5 min
3.2 Summarize. Tell learners the last session is spotting waste on the job. Keep their test data. None 3 min

Safety. The classroom test uses only cardboard, tape and scissors. Close scissors when they are set down. No saws are used in this session. If you run a real stop-block test in the shop later, follow your shop's saw rules and have a qualified person supervise.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Old way 60 seconds, new way 45 seconds. Percent time saved? (25%)
  2. Set-up 5 minutes, saving 15 seconds a piece. Break-even? (20 pieces)
  3. Name one thing that would make a test unfair.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • running a fair test
  • averages, range and percent time saved
  • checking quality as well as speed
  • break-even and the saving on a whole job
  • deciding with numbers, then pitching with them
