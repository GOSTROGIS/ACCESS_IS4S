# Picturing yourself in a trade

Session 1 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 60 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Tell this short story, which is fictional: "Rae came to trades prep sure she wanted carpentry. During a group activity a classmate said, 'I can picture you in a pipe shop. You love puzzles and you never lose track of a fitting.' Rae had never thought about pipe trades. She asked questions, visited a mechanical shop with the class, and changed her plan." Ask: has anyone ever said something about you that made you see yourself differently?

Explain that creativity and innovation is one of the two skills that were new when the Government of Canada moved from the Essential Skills framework to the Skills for Success model. Today's creative work is about your own future: picturing possibilities before you decide.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- picture themselves in two or three work settings and ask questions about each (Use your imagination and curiosity)
- give a peer positive, specific feedback that helps them picture a possibility, and record others' ideas accurately (Facilitate a creative and innovative environment for yourself and others)

Levels: the Government of Canada page names three creativity and innovation levels, Entry, Intermediate and Advanced. These lessons use 1, 2 and 3 for them.

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. Name one trade you have never thought about doing. What do you think a day in it looks like?
2. Has feedback from someone else ever changed a choice you made? What made that feedback useful?

## Learning tasks

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Opening story and question. | Listen and share if they choose. | None | 5 min |
| 1.2 | State the objectives. | Ask any questions about the session. | Learner page | 3 min |
| 1.3 | Pose the two pre-assessment questions. Note who has a trade in mind already. | Answer aloud or on paper. | Paper | 5 min |
| 2.1 | Teach the key ideas: imagination, curiosity, trying an idea on, positive and specific feedback, choosing what to share. Read the worked example aloud. Model one helpful and one unhelpful comment. | Follow the worked example. Say why the unhelpful comment shuts a door. | Learner page, Key ideas | 7 min |
| 2.2 | Set Task 1. Circulate and prompt with "What would you be doing with your hands? Who is near you?" | Complete Task 1. | Learner page, pencil | 7 min |
| 2.3 | Run the career chair (Tasks 2 to 4). Ask for a volunteer, never assign one. Choose a scribe. Ask the group to sit quietly for two minutes and picture the person at work, then invite comments. Stop any negative comment and ask for it to be rephrased. Allow about five minutes per person, so three or four volunteers take part. Others can take a turn in a later session. | Take a turn in the chair if they wish (Task 2), give "I can picture you … because …" comments (Task 3), act as scribe (Task 4). | Chair at the front, paper for the scribe | 20 min |
| 2.4 | Set Task 5. Show the WorkBC career profiles and SkilledTradesBC Find Your Trade pages on the screen if available. | Complete Task 5 using scribe notes or their Task 1 list. | Learner page, projector (optional) | 6 min |
| 3.1 | Run the post-assessment. | Answer the three questions. | Paper | 5 min |
| 3.2 | Summarize the session. | Note one idea to keep open. | None | 2 min |

**Care during the career chair.** The original ACCESS lesson notes that this exercise "can be very powerful and even overwhelming for some participants." Keep it voluntary, keep comments positive, and let the person in the chair respond only if they want to. Learners do not have to share family, community or personal history. If a learner chooses to bring in a family or community goal, receive it without asking for more.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Give one example of a positive, specific comment you could make to a classmate about a trade you can picture them in.
2. What is one question you now want answered about a trade you had not considered before today?
3. Why is it useful to keep an idea open for a while instead of deciding right away?

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- imagination as picturing "what if" and curiosity as asking questions about it
- trying on a career idea before deciding
- giving feedback that is positive, specific and gives a reason
- recording other people's ideas so the person can use them later
- where to look up a trade: WorkBC career profiles and SkilledTradesBC trade profiles
