# Picturing yourself in a trade

## Why this matters on the job

Picking a trade is a big decision. Many people pick the first trade they hear about, or the one a relative works in. If you picture yourself in a few different settings first (a framing crew, a fabrication shop, a pulp mill maintenance crew, a mechanical contractor on a hospital job), you can test how each one fits you before you spend months training for it. Other people often notice strengths you do not see in yourself.

## Key ideas

**Imagination** means picturing "what if." What if I worked outdoors on a high-rise? What if I worked in a quiet shop on one machine?

**Curiosity** means asking questions about what you pictured. What does a day look like? Do they work alone or on a crew? What tools do they use?

**Trying an idea on.** You do not have to decide today. You picture an idea, notice how it feels, and keep it open while you learn more. The Skills for Success model calls this being "open to new ideas without judging and setting limitations."

**Useful feedback is positive and specific.** It says what the person sees and why.

- Helpful: "I can picture you as a millwright, because you always want to know how the machine works inside."
- Not helpful: "I can't see you doing anything with heights."

**You choose what to share.** You do not have to talk about your family, your community or your past to take part. You can pass on any turn.

### Worked example

Dale is a learner in a fictional trades prep class. He sits in the career chair and says, "I like building things, being outside and working with my hands." The group sits quietly for two minutes and pictures Dale at work.

The scribe writes down what the group says:

- "I see you on a framing crew, standing walls with three other people."
- "I see you in a fab shop, because you are careful with measurements."
- "I see you at a mill, fixing a conveyor. You like figuring out why things break."

Dale had never thought about mill work. He says the conveyor idea surprised him. He keeps the scribe's notes and writes three questions to find out more: What does an industrial mechanic (millwright) do in a day? Is it shift work? Where in BC are the mills?

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Write three work settings you are curious about. For each one, write one thing you picture yourself doing there.

1. Setting: ______________________ I picture myself: ______________________
2. Setting: ______________________ I picture myself: ______________________
3. Setting: ______________________ I picture myself: ______________________

**Task 2** (level 1) Take a turn in the career chair if you want to. Tell the group two or three kinds of work you are interested in. Then listen while the group says how they picture you. Do not answer until everyone has spoken. A scribe writes the ideas down for you.

**Task 3** (level 1) When someone else is in the career chair, picture them at work. Give one positive, specific comment that starts with "I can picture you … because …".

**Task 4** (level 1) When you are the scribe, write each idea in the speaker's own words, then hand the notes to the person in the chair.

**Task 5** (level 2) Choose one idea from your scribe notes that you had not thought of before. Write three questions you would need answered to know if it fits you. Name one place to look for answers, such as a WorkBC career profile (https://www.workbc.ca/plan-career/explore-careers/career-profiles), a SkilledTradesBC trade profile (https://skilledtradesbc.ca/find-your-trade) or a person who works in that trade.

1. ______________________________________________
2. ______________________________________________
3. ______________________________________________

Where I will look: ______________________________________________

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between imagination and curiosity?
2. Rewrite this comment so it is positive and specific: "You'd hate working in a shop."
3. Why does the person in the career chair wait until everyone has spoken before answering?
4. Name one way to check whether a trade you pictured really fits you.
