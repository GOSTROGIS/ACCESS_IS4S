# Combining two ideas into something new

## Why this matters on the job

Many new ideas in the trades are two old ideas put together: a tape measure with a magnet on the hook, a ladder with a tool tray. Putting two unrelated things together is one of the main ways people come up with something new. This lesson practises it with food. At a remote work camp, the kitchen feeds the same crew for weeks at a time. A good camp menu is healthy, works around allergies, and keeps a tired crew from getting bored. A fusion menu, which blends two food traditions, is one way to do that.

## Key ideas

**Combining.** Take two things that do not usually go together and look for a new mix. Write many combinations first, then judge them.

**Canada's food guide plate.** Health Canada's food guide uses a plate to show healthy proportions for a meal:

- half the plate vegetables and fruits
- one-quarter of the plate whole grain foods
- one-quarter of the plate protein foods, choosing protein foods that come from plants more often

The food guide also says to make water your drink of choice, to choose foods with little to no added sodium, sugars or saturated fat, and that culture and food traditions can be a part of healthy eating.

**Priority food allergens.** Health Canada lists these as the priority food allergens: eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame seeds, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, and wheat and triticale. A camp menu should mark which dishes contain them and offer a swap.

**Your traditions, your choice.** You may use food from your own family or community, food a friend has shared with you, or a style of cooking you like. You do not have to share anything personal. If you use a dish from someone else's tradition, say where it comes from, for example "my co-worker's family recipe for dal."

### Worked example

Silverline Camp Services (a fictional company) runs the kitchen at a road-building camp in northern BC. The crew of 30 says the menu is getting boring. Two crew members have allergies: one to peanuts, one to sesame.

Rosa, a camp cook, grew up with her family's Mexican cooking. Her co-worker Harpreet shared his mother's recipe for dal. Rosa lists foods from each:

| Rosa's family cooking | Harpreet's family dal |
|---|---|
| tacos, salsa, black beans, corn tortillas, cabbage slaw | red lentils, cumin, onion, tomato, spinach |

She writes six combinations without judging, then picks **lentil dal tacos**: spiced red lentil dal with spinach in whole grain corn tortillas, with cabbage slaw, salsa and a side of fruit.

Plate check:

- vegetables and fruits (half): slaw, salsa, spinach, fruit
- whole grains (one-quarter): whole grain corn tortillas
- protein (one-quarter): red lentils, a protein from plants
- drink: water on the table

Allergen check: no peanuts or sesame. If she adds a yogurt sauce, it contains milk, so she labels it and serves it on the side.
