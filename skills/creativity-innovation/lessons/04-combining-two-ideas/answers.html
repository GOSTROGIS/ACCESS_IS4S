Combining two ideas into something new

Plan a week of work camp meals that blends two food traditions and still follows Canada's food guide.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others; Develop your ideas; Apply your ideas

Task 1 (level 1)

Answers will vary. A good response names two traditions and lists at least five real foods or ingredients for each. If a tradition is not the learner's own, the source is named ("my friend's family", "Korean restaurant cooking").

Task 2 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good response:

  • has six or more dishes
  • uses something from both lists in each dish
  • includes at least one unusual pairing, since the generate phase is not for judging

Task 3 (level 2)

A good response, for each dish:

  • names what fills half the plate with vegetables and fruits, and adds a side such as salad, slaw or fruit where the dish falls short
  • names a whole grain food for one-quarter, such as brown rice, whole grain tortillas, whole grain bread, oats or barley
  • names a protein food for one-quarter, with a plant protein (beans, lentils, chickpeas, tofu, nuts) for at least one dish
  • puts water as the drink

Task 4 (level 2)

Answers depend on the dishes. A good response:

  • correctly names each priority allergen present, for example wheat in a flour tortilla or bread, milk in cheese or yogurt, eggs in a frittata, sesame in tahini or sesame oil, peanuts in a peanut sauce, fish or shellfish in seafood dishes, soy in tofu or soy sauce
  • for the peanut and sesame allergies, either removes those ingredients, swaps them (sunflower seeds for sesame in a topping, a tomato sauce for a peanut sauce) or labels the dish and offers a separate safe option

Task 5 (level 2)

Open task. A good menu:

  • fills all 15 slots
  • uses at least five of the learner's own fusion dishes
  • labels every dish that contains a priority allergen
  • avoids serving the same dish twice in the week
  • has dinners that could follow the food guide plate with a side added

Task 6 (level 2)

Open task. A good presentation:

  • names both traditions and where they come from
  • walks through one dinner using the plate proportions
  • explains the allergy plan for the peanut and sesame allergies
  • mentions variety or long shifts, such as hearty breakfasts or packed lunches that travel well
  • stays within 2 minutes

A good listener gives one specific, useful idea, such as "Thursday lunch has no vegetables. Add a slaw."

Check yourself

1. Half the plate. Level 1.

2. Any four of: eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame seeds, soy, sulphites, tree nuts, wheat and triticale. Level 1.

3. Any sensible example. Trades: a magnetic tape measure hook, a ladder with a tool tray, a corner stop with a hinged template. Food: lentil dal tacos, or any fusion dish from the lesson. Level 2.

4. Water. Level 1.
