Combining two ideas into something new

Plan a week of work camp meals that blends two food traditions and still follows Canada's food guide.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others; Develop your ideas; Apply your ideas

Session 4 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "What is the best meal you ever had at a camp, a job site lunch truck or a community event? What made it good?" Then ask: "What is a tool you use that is really two tools stuck together?" Take a few answers for each. Explain that combining two things that do not usually go together is one of the most common ways to make something new, and today's job is a fusion menu for a fictional work camp.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • generate many fusion dishes by combining foods from two traditions (Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others)
  • develop dishes into a weekly menu that follows Canada's food guide plate and marks priority food allergens (Develop your ideas)
  • present the menu and explain how it works for a crew (Apply your ideas)

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. What does a healthy plate look like to you? Sketch it and label the parts.
  2. Name any food allergies you know of that a camp kitchen has to plan around.

Learning tasks

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening questions. Share examples. None 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Ask questions. Learner page 3 min
1.3 Pose the pre-assessment questions. Sketch and answer. Paper 4 min
2.1 Teach the key ideas: combining, the food guide plate, water as the drink of choice, priority food allergens, choosing and crediting traditions. 3.2 Summarize the session. Keep the menu for the portfolio. None 2 min

Respect for food traditions. Learners decide what to bring. Do not ask anyone to speak for their Nation, community or culture. If a learner uses a dish from someone else's tradition, ask them to say where it came from. Do not present any dish as the traditional food of a Nation unless the learner who brings it says so from their own knowledge.

Health note. This is a menu-planning exercise, not dietary or medical advice. For real allergies or health conditions, a camp kitchen follows the employer's food safety plan and the advice of health professionals.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Your dinner is a big plate of pasta with meat sauce. What would you change or add to follow the food guide plate?
  2. A crew member has a fish allergy. Which of your dishes would you label, and what would you swap?
  3. Name one combination you came up with today that you would not have thought of without the two lists.

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • making something new by combining two unrelated things
  • Canada's food guide plate: half vegetables and fruits, one-quarter whole grains, one-quarter protein, with plant proteins more often and water as the drink of choice
  • Health Canada's priority food allergens and how to label or swap around them
  • crediting the traditions we draw on, and choosing what we share
  • presenting a menu and giving each other ideas
