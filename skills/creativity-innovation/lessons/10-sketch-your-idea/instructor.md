# Sketch your idea so others can build it

Session 10 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a small wooden block or bracket. Ask one volunteer to describe it in words while the group sketches it without looking. Compare the sketches. Say: "Words alone miss things, as you found in lesson 5. Today you learn the simple drawing rules the trades use, so anyone can build your idea."

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- sketch a simple object in isometric on dot paper (Develop your ideas)
- draw the front, top and right side views in third-angle layout with visible, hidden and centre lines (Develop your ideas)
- add the sizes a builder needs and test the drawing by having someone else build from it (Apply your ideas)
- picture an object from its views (Use your imagination and curiosity)

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. Have you ever built something from a drawing? What was hard about it?
2. What do you think a dashed line on a drawing means?

## Learning tasks (90 min)

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Describe-and-sketch warm-up. | Sketch from a spoken description. | A small block or bracket, paper | 5 min |
| 1.2 | State the objectives. | Ask questions. | Learner page | 3 min |
| 1.3 | Pose the pre-assessment questions. | Answer on paper. | Paper | 4 min |
| 2.1 | Teach isometric axes. Draw a box on the board on 30° axes. Show that true lengths go along the axes. | Complete Task 4 on isometric dot paper. | Isometric dot paper, pencils, erasers | 12 min |
| 2.2 | Teach the three views with a real block. Put it in a clear plastic box or a folded card "glass box" and unfold the sides to show where each view lands in third-angle layout. | Complete Task 1. | Block, clear box or folded card | 8 min |
| 2.3 | Teach the three line types. Show where the hidden lines for a hole come from. | Complete Task 2. | Learner page | 5 min |
| 2.4 | Walk through Kai's worked example and the three views. Check each view together. | Complete Tasks 3 and 7. | Learner page | 12 min |
| 2.5 | Set Task 5. Circulate and ask: "Can you see the bottom of the notch from the side?" | Draw three views of the notched box. | Square grid paper, rulers | 10 min |
| 2.6 | Set Task 6. Take answers from two learners. | Find the mistakes in Kai's first try. | Learner page | 4 min |
| 2.7 | Set Task 8 or Task 9. Pairs swap views and build cardboard models from them for Task 9. | Draw and test a sketch. | Grid paper, cardboard, tape, scissors | 15 min |
| 2.8 | Set Task 10 as a challenge for fast finishers or as homework. | Describe the pipe stand views. | Learner page | 4 min |

**Safety.** Learners use scissors only for cardboard in step 2.7. Close scissors when they are set down.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Draw where the top and right side views go around a front view in third-angle layout.
2. A hole goes through a block from top to bottom. How does it look in the top view? In the front view?
3. What are the angles of the two sloping isometric axes?

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- isometric sketching on 30° axes
- front, top and right side views in third-angle layout
- visible, hidden and centre lines
- adding sizes, and locating holes from an edge
- testing a drawing by having someone else build from it

## Sources

- Isometric drawing uses one vertical axis and two axes at 30° to the horizontal, and lengths are true only along the axes: Workforce LibreTexts, "Blueprint Reading and Technical Sketching" (Gentry), section 1.2 "Types of drawings", CC BY 4.0, https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Sacramento_City_College/Blueprint_Reading_and_Technical_Sketching_(Gentry)/01%3A_Introduction_to_Blueprints_and_Technical_Sketches/1.02%3A_Types_drawings, checked 2026-11-08.
- Third-angle projection, with the top view above the front view and the right view to the right of it, is the convention in the United States, Canada, Japan and Australia, and the rest of the world mostly uses first-angle: University of Washington Pressbooks, "Engineering Graphics and Design", "Multiview Layout and Orientation", https://uw.pressbooks.pub/enggraphics/chapter/multiview-drawing, checked 2026-11-08. Also: WTCS Pressbooks, "Blueprint Reading", chapter 2 "Views of an Object", https://wtcs.pressbooks.pub/blueprintreading/chapter/2-views-of-an-object, checked 2026-11-08.
- Visible (object) lines are thick and continuous, hidden lines are medium short dashes, centre lines are thin long-short-long lines used for hole centres: Ric Costin, "Basic Blueprint Reading for Welders", Chapter 1 "The Language of Lines", CC BY 4.0, https://openwa.pressbooks.pub/cloverpark/chapter/unit-1-the-language-of-lines, checked 2026-11-08.
- Creativity and innovation components 4 "Develop your ideas", 5 "Apply your ideas" and 1 "Use your imagination and curiosity": Government of Canada, "Skill components and proficiency levels", https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training/initiatives/skills-success/learning-steps.html, checked 2026-11-08.
