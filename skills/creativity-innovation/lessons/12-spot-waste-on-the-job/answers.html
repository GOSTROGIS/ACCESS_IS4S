Spot waste on the job

Walk a work area, spot waiting, extra trips, rework and extra stock, measure one waste, and pick the one most worth fixing.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify opportunities for you to innovate; Use your imagination and curiosity; Facilitate a creative and innovative environment for yourself and others

Task 1 (level 1)

  1. Extra trips
  2. Waiting
  3. Rework
  4. Extra stock
  5. Waiting
  6. Rework
  7. Extra trips (moving material more than once). Extra stock is also fair if the reason is that the blocks arrived too early.

Task 2 (level 1)

The van is parked outside, about 45 m from the work, near the front door. The storage room is empty. The unused reels sit in the corridor, near the front door.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 240 minutes a day. 3 × 5 × 2 = 30 minutes an hour. 30 × 8 = 240.

b) 20 hours. 240 × 5 = 1200 minutes. 1200 ÷ 60 = 20.

Task 4 (level 2)

180 minutes a day. New time: 3 × 5 × 0.5 × 8 = 60 minutes. Saving: 240 − 60 = 180. Or: 3 × 5 × (2 − 0.5) × 8 = 3 × 5 × 1.5 × 8 = 180.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Extra trips (11).

b) A bar chart with four bars: waiting 4, extra trips 11, rework 2, extra stock 3, with a labelled scale.

c) Each waste costs a different amount of time. For example, 11 short trips of 1 minute cost 11 minutes, but 2 reworks of 30 minutes each cost 60 minutes. Count the time, not only the tally.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) $108. 6 × $18 = $108.

b) 72 minutes. 6 × 12 = 72.

c) Open answer. Examples: check the cut list against the drawing before cutting, measure and cut one tread and test-fit it before cutting the rest, have a second person check the cut list.

Task 7 (level 3)

Open answer. Scores must have reasons. One reasonable scoring:

Waste Time lost How often Easy to fix Total
Waiting for the hoist 2 3 2 7
Screws run out 2 1 3 6
Sheets cut wrong 1 3 2 6
Mud on the wrong floor 3 3 3 9

Choice: move the mud to the fourth floor. It happens many times a day, costs a lot of walking and climbing, and is easy to fix by staging the day's buckets on the floor where the work is.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Open answer. Example: "How might we keep the day's mud close to the crew on the fourth floor?"

b) Open answer. Example: count trips to the first floor and time a round trip for one morning before the change, then do the same for one morning after. Compare the minutes, as in lesson 11.

Task 9 (level 2)

Open answer. Examples:

  1. "Set-up takes a long time because the tools are stored in three places."
  2. "Several pieces were cut wrong this week. Is the cut list clear, and does everyone have a good tape?"
  3. "There's no set place for tools and material, so things are hard to find."

Task 10 (level 3)

Open answer. A good plan names a small area and a set time, lists the four wastes, sets clear safety rules (PPE, stay out of machine areas, permission from the supervisor), names who to ask, and plans to share findings as process notes with the whole crew.

Check yourself

1. Waiting, extra trips, rework and extra stock. Level 1.

2. Watching an area for a set time and recording the waste you see, usually with a tally sheet. Level 1.

3. So people trust you and share what slows them down, and so the fix targets the real cause. Level 2.

4. 4 × 3 × 8 = 96 minutes. Level 2.

5. Write an opportunity statement, then measure the fix (before and after). Level 2.
