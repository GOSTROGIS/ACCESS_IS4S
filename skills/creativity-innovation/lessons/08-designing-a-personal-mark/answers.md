# Designing your own personal mark

## Task 1 (level 1)

Answers will vary. A good response lists five different choices, each with a one-word reason tied to how the learner works (level: exact, hook: strong, bridge: connects), and circles one. Choices taken from a company logo, a sports team or a Nation's crest do not meet the task.

## Task 2 (level 2)

Answers depend on the choice. A good response:

- for an animal or plant: covers attributes, habitat and range, role in the food chain and conservation status
- gives a conservation status using a COSEWIC category or a BC list status, taken from the Species at Risk Public Registry or the BC Species and Ecosystems Explorer
- for a tool or object: says what it is used for and gives one fact about how it is made or where it came from
- writes a source name, web address and date checked for every fact
- uses no facts from memory or unsourced websites

## Task 3 (level 2)

Open task. A good response has six or more sketches that differ in shape or idea (whole object, a part of it, combined with a letter, inside a shape, built from straight lines), not six colours of one drawing. All are the learner's own drawings.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good response:

- draws two sketches about 2 cm wide in black only
- writes what the partner actually said, even if it was not what the learner hoped
- makes a change that responds to it, such as removing fine detail, thickening lines or simplifying the outline

## Task 5 (level 2)

Open task. Good slides or a good poster:

- has 5 to 8 slides, or one clear poster
- includes a title slide or poster heading with name, date and mark
- shows research facts with sources
- explains how the mark relates to the learner and their work
- shows earlier sketches and the final mark, with what changed and why
- is readable: large text, few words per slide

## Task 6 (level 2)

Open task. A good presentation stays within 2 minutes and covers the choice, the research, the link to the learner's work and how the design changed. A good audience member gives one positive, specific comment ("The three-line heron reads clearly from the back of the room") and asks one real question.

## Check yourself

**1.** "Totem" comes from the Ojibwe word for clan, and on the Northwest Coast the animals on totem poles are crests that belong to specific family lineages. Clans and crests belong to the families and Nations they come from, so a classroom design should not use those names or copy those designs. Level 1.

**2.** Any three of: simple, readable small and from far away, works in one colour, original. Level 1.

**3.** The Species at Risk Public Registry and the BC Species and Ecosystems Explorer. Level 1.

**4.** A mark is often used small (a hard hat sticker, a tool tag) and seen from a distance (a truck door). If it only works large and up close, it fails where it is needed. Level 2.
