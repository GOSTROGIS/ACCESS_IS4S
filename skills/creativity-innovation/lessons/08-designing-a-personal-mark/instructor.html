Designing your own personal mark

Design an original symbol for your toolbox, business card or portfolio, research what it stands for, and present it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Use your imagination and curiosity; Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others; Develop your ideas

Session 8 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "How do you mark your tools so they come home from a shared site?" Take a few answers: paint, tape, engraving, initials. Then ask: "If you started your own trade business tomorrow, what would go on your truck door?" Explain that today each learner designs a personal mark, an original symbol of their own, and tests whether others read it the way they meant.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • choose a source for a personal mark and research it with reliable, cited sources (Use your imagination and curiosity)
  • sketch at least six different versions of the mark (Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others)
  • test the mark at small size, improve it and present the result (Develop your ideas)
  • explain why the lesson uses "personal mark" rather than "totem" or "crest"

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. Think of a company mark you recognize from a distance, on a truck or a hard hat. Without naming the company, what makes it easy to recognize?
  2. Where would you look to find out whether an animal in BC is at risk?

Respectful framing. The original ACCESS assignment asked learners to choose a "totem animal". This version keeps the research and branding parts and drops the totem framing, because clans and crests belong to specific families and Nations. Do not offer examples drawn from any Nation's crests or art style. If a learner raises their own family's clan or crest, let them lead. They are not required to share it, and it is not to be graded. Do not teach or explain any Nation's clan or crest system yourself beyond the sourced points on the learner page. If learners want to learn more, point them to the Nation's own cultural centre or knowledge keepers, or to the sources below.

Copying. A mark built from a company logo, clip art, a sports team symbol or another culture's art does not meet the task. Ask the learner to start again from their own sketches.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. What changed between your first sketch and your final mark, and why?
  2. Name one fact from your research and where it came from.
  3. A classmate wants to use a Northwest Coast-style raven they found online as their mark. What would you tell them?

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • why the lesson uses "personal mark" and not "totem" or "crest"
  • choosing something that says how you work
  • researching with official sources and recording the source and date
  • sketching many different versions
  • testing at small size and from a distance, then improving
  • presenting your mark and giving feedback

Across the first eight Creativity and Innovation lessons we covered all six components: imagination and curiosity, finding opportunities, generating ideas, developing ideas, applying ideas and learning from failure, and building a creative environment for yourself and others.

Access and offline options: Provide dated printouts from the named official sources for Task 2 when internet access is unavailable. Learners record the original page title, URL and printed date. For Task 4, allow an agreed viewing size and distance, magnification or a partner describing what is visible. Record the test conditions and assess how the learner uses feedback, not visual acuity. A paper poster meets Task 5.
