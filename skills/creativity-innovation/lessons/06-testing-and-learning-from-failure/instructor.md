# Testing an idea and learning from what fails

Session 6 of 12 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Tell me about a time something you built or fixed failed the first time. What did you learn from it?" Share one of your own if you are comfortable. Then hold up a sheet of cardboard and a paper cup and say: "Your team will build a bracket that sticks out past this table and holds a cup of washers. Most first versions fail. Today, failing well is the skill."

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- build, predict and load-test a bracket, and record what happened (Apply your ideas)
- tell an observation from a guess, and change one thing at a time between versions (Apply your ideas)
- improve a design using the results and the reverse prompt (Develop your ideas)
- work out averages and percent improvement with BEDMAS
- cut cardboard safely

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. You change the glue, the shape and the size of a part all at once, and it works better. What can you not tell?
2. Three tests gave 4, 6 and 8. What is the average?

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

**Extension.** Task 8 is a level 3 trade task for fast finishers or homework, with averages and percent improvement on a pipe support test. It is not timed in the table above.

**Safety.** You may pre-cut cardboard or limit cutting to scissors for any group. If utility knives are used, only one person at a time cuts, on a mat, with glasses on. Collect and count knives and blades at the end. Keep washer loads small and the floor under the test clear of feet.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Describe how your best version failed, using an observation, not a guess.
2. What one change made the biggest difference, and how do you know it was that change?
3. Version A averaged 10 washers and version B averaged 12. What is the percent improvement?

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- expecting failures and learning from them
- predicting, testing and recording what really happened
- keeping observations separate from guesses
- changing one thing at a time
- reversing an idea when the first approach fails
- averages and percent improvement with BEDMAS
- safe cutting with a utility knife or scissors
