Testing an idea and learning from what fails

Build a cardboard bracket, load-test it, and use each failure to make the next version better.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Apply your ideas; Develop your ideas

Session 6 of 8 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 90 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Tell me about a time something you built or fixed failed the first time. What did you learn from it?" Share one of your own if you are comfortable. Then hold up a sheet of cardboard and a paper cup and say: "Your team will build a bracket that sticks out past this table and holds a cup of washers. Most first versions fail. Today, failing well is the skill."

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • build, predict and load-test a bracket, and record what happened (Apply your ideas)
  • tell an observation from a guess, and change one thing at a time between versions (Apply your ideas)
  • improve a design using the results and the reverse prompt (Develop your ideas)
  • work out averages and percent improvement with BEDMAS
  • cut cardboard safely

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. You change the glue, the shape and the size of a part all at once, and it works better. What can you not tell?
  2. Three tests gave 4, 6 and 8. What is the average?

Learning tasks (90 min)

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening question and challenge. Share examples. Cardboard, cup 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Ask questions. Learner page 3 min
1.3 Pose the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. Paper 4 min
2.1 Teach the key ideas and the test rules. Demonstrate safe knife use step by step, wearing safety glasses, on a cutting mat. Show where blades go. 2.2 Form teams of 2 or 3. Set Task 1. Sketch and predict. Learner page 5 min
2.3 Hand out materials. Set Task 2. Circulate and check safety: glasses on when knives are in use, blades retracted when set down. Build version 1. Per team: corrugated cardboard about 30 cm by 40 cm, 1 m masking tape, paper cup, scissors, utility knife and cutting mat if used, safety glasses 12 min
2.4 Set Task 3. Put a box on the floor under each test spot. Test version 1 three times and record. Washers of one size, box to catch them, timer 8 min
2.5 Set Task 4. Ask two teams to read their observation and guess. Correct any guess written as an observation. Write observation, guess and check. Learner page 5 min
2.6 Set Task 5. Give each team a second piece of cardboard and tape. Check that each team changed one thing only. Build and test version 2, and version 3 if time. Second set of materials 18 min
2.7 Set Task 6. Calculate averages and percent improvement. Learner page, calculator 6 min
2.8 Set Task 7. Have teams pair up to swap one tip. Collect knives and count blades. Write the test report and share a tip. Learner page 7 min
3.1 Run the post-assessment. Answer the three questions. Paper 5 min
3.2 Summarize the session. Tell learners the next session is pitching an idea to a supervisor, using test results like these. Keep the test table and report. None 2 min

Safety. You may pre-cut cardboard or limit cutting to scissors for any group. If utility knives are used, only one person at a time cuts, on a mat, with glasses on. Collect and count knives and blades at the end. Keep washer loads small and the floor under the test clear of feet.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Describe how your best version failed, using an observation, not a guess.
  2. What one change made the biggest difference, and how do you know it was that change?
  3. Version A averaged 10 washers and version B averaged 12. What is the percent improvement?

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • expecting failures and learning from them
  • predicting, testing and recording what really happened
  • keeping observations separate from guesses
  • changing one thing at a time
  • reversing an idea when the first approach fails
  • averages and percent improvement with BEDMAS
  • safe cutting with a utility knife or scissors
