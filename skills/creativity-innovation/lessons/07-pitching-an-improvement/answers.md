# Pitching an improvement to a supervisor

## Task 1 (level 2)

Open task. A good pitch planner:

- problem: a specific observation with a number
- idea: one or two clear sentences
- test: something already tried, including what did not work
- cost and time: what it takes and what it saves
- safety: how it meets or improves safety and site rules, with nothing that works around a safety rule
- ask: a small, clear, time-limited request

## Task 2 (level 2)

Depends on the idea. Check that the learner:

- uses numbers from their problem and test
- works brackets first, for example (6 − 2) × 4 × 4 = 64 minutes a day saved
- converts to hours where asked, for example 64 × 5 = 320 minutes, and 320 ÷ 60 ≈ 5.3 hours a week
- uses units on every answer

For the lesson 3 marking jig, a sound saving is 40 × (2 − 0.5) = 60 minutes per batch of 40 plates.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Open task. A good pitch:

- covers all six parts in 2 minutes or less
- includes at least one number
- names any person who helped
- ends with a clear ask
- answers the supervisor's questions calmly, and says "I don't know yet, I'll find out" when needed
- tries on the supervisor's idea before defending the original

## Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good supervisor role:

- listens to the whole pitch without interrupting
- asks two realistic questions
- offers one idea that would improve the pitch or the idea
- stays in role but does not argue just to win

## Task 5 (level 3)

Answers will vary. A good response:

- accepts the "not yet" without arguing
- proposes a small, safe trial or a way to collect more evidence (time trips for a week, build and test the jig on 5 plates, get a photo of the setup)
- says what will be brought back (numbers, a sample part, a photo) and when

Example: "I'll time our trips for three days the old way and three days with the bin on the deck, and bring you the numbers on Friday."

## Task 6 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good response names a specific idea from the partner or supervisor and says how it improves the pitch, for example "Dana's idea to put the box on the second floor cuts even more trips."

## Check yourself

**1.** Problem, idea, test, cost and time, safety, ask. Level 1.

**2.** It is small, has an end date and is easy to say yes to. If it does not work, little is lost. A broad change sounds risky and needs more convincing. Level 2.

**3.** "I don't know yet. I'll find out and get back to you." Then follow through. Level 2.

**4.** The joint health and safety committee, which a BC workplace with 20 or more workers needs. Level 2.
