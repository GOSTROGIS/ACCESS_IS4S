# Many ideas before you choose

Session 3 of 8 in the Creativity and Innovation lessons. Total length 75 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a story stick, a stop block or any simple shop jig if you have one, and ask what it is for. Then ask: "Has anyone ever watched an old hand do a repeated job twice as fast as everyone else? What were they doing differently?" Tell learners that most of those tricks started as one idea out of many, and today they will practise getting many ideas out before choosing.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- generate at least 8 ideas for a repeated shop task without judging them, using prompts such as reverse and combine (Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others)
- expand and compare ideas against criteria and choose one to try (Develop your ideas)
- run or take part in a group idea round that keeps credit with each contributor (Facilitate a creative and innovative environment for yourself and others)
- work out hole positions and time saved, using BEDMAS

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. When a group is coming up with ideas, what happens if someone shoots down each idea as soon as it is said?
2. A plate is 160 mm long and a hole sits 30 mm in from each short end. How far apart are the hole centres?

## Learning tasks (75 min)

The table covers the whole session, including the opening, objective, pre-assessment, post-assessment and summary blocks.

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Opening with a jig or question. | Share examples. | Story stick or simple jig (optional) | 5 min |
| 1.2 | State the objectives. | Ask questions. | Learner page | 3 min |
| 1.3 | Pose the pre-assessment questions. | Answer on paper. | Paper | 4 min |
| 2.1 | Teach the key ideas: two phases, the five prompts, "Yes, and …", credit, what a jig is. Walk through the North Arm example and its arithmetic. Read the safety note aloud. | Follow along. Say which idea in the list surprised them. | Learner page, whiteboard | 8 min |
| 2.2 | Set Task 1 with a 2-minute timer. Keep it light and playful. Ask for the strangest use. | Complete Task 1. | Timer | 4 min |
| 2.3 | Set Task 2. Remind learners not to cross out. Prompt anyone stuck with one of the five prompts. | Generate 8 or more ideas alone. | Learner page, pencil | 10 min |
| 2.4 | Form groups of 3 or 4. Ask each group to pick a facilitator, or choose someone who has not led before. Watch for "No, but …" and redirect. | Run the idea round in Task 3. | Flip chart or large paper, markers | 12 min |
| 2.5 | Set Task 4. | Add two ideas from new prompts. | Learner page | 6 min |
| 2.6 | Announce the switch to the choose phase. Set Task 5. Ask each group to share its pick and one unknown. | Score and choose in Task 5. | Learner page | 10 min |
| 2.7 | Set Task 6. Check that learners subtract both end distances. | Complete Task 6. | Learner page, calculator | 6 min |
| 3.1 | Run the post-assessment. | Answer the three questions. | Paper | 5 min |
| 3.2 | Summarize the session. Tell learners the next session combines ideas from two different sources. | Keep their idea lists. | None | 2 min |

**Safety.** This session is sketching and talking only. If a group wants to build its jig, review the design first, and have it built in a later shop session under the shop's own safety orientation and tool rules.

**Group care.** Rotate the facilitator role across sessions so quieter learners get a turn. Some learners prefer to write ideas on sticky notes instead of saying them aloud. Both count.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Give one example of using the reverse prompt on a shop task of your choice.
2. Your group has 12 ideas. What do you do next, and how do you decide?
3. A partner gives an idea you think will not work. What do you say during the generate phase?

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- keeping the generate phase and the choose phase apart
- five prompts: do it differently, ask a question, combine, reverse, expand
- building on ideas with "Yes, and …" and keeping credit with each person
- choosing with criteria and naming what you do not know yet
- hole positions and time saved, worked with BEDMAS
