Creativity and innovation · Lesson 03 · Lesson 4: Combining two ideas into something new

Many ideas before you choose

Come up with a wide range of ideas for a repeated shop task, without judging them, then pick one for a reason.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others; Develop your ideas; Facilitate a creative and innovative environment for yourself and others

Task 1 (level 1)

Any list counts. A good response has many items and some unusual ones: hold a glue-up, hold a stop block, clamp a straightedge as a saw guide, hold a part in place for welding, a paperweight for drawings, a hook to hang a bucket. The aim is quantity and a relaxed start, not correct answers.

Task 2 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good response:

  • has 8 or more ideas
  • has ideas that differ in kind (templates, stops, master parts, changing who does the task, questioning the task), not 8 versions of the same template
  • has nothing crossed out
  • uses at most one idea from the worked example

Task 3 (level 2)

Open task. A good idea round:

  • starts with the opportunity read aloud and the rules stated
  • has every idea written with initials
  • gives every person a turn
  • uses "Yes, and …" to build ideas, with no judging
  • reaches 15 or more ideas

A good facilitator keeps the round moving, stops judging comments politely, and does not use the role to push their own ideas.

Task 4 (level 2)

Answers will vary. A good response names the prompt and gives an idea that fits it. Examples:

  • Reverse: instead of marking the plate, mark the bench, and slide each plate up to the marks.
  • Combine: a magnet strip and a template, so the template holds itself on the steel.
  • Ask a question: why mark the plate at all, if the holes could be located by a set-up?
  • Do it differently: one person marks all 40 while another sets up the drill.
  • Expand: add a handle to the template so it is easy to lift off.

Task 5 (level 2)

Open task. A good response:

  • scores three ideas against at least three named criteria
  • picks one and gives a reason that matches the scores or explains why the pick differs from the top score
  • names something still unknown, such as how long the template lasts, whether it slips, or whether the shop lead will allow the set-up

Task 6 (level 2)

a) 120 ÷ 2 = 60 mm from each long edge.

b) 200 − 30 − 30 = 140 mm between hole centres.

c) Brackets first: 40 × (2.5 − 0.5) = 40 × 2 = 80 minutes saved.

Check yourself

1. If you judge while generating, people stop offering ideas and the group settles on the first, familiar idea. Keeping them apart gets more and wider ideas to choose from. Level 2.

2. Any two, with an example that fits. Do it differently (change the tool or who does the task), ask a question (why do we mark these at all?), combine (a corner stop plus a template), reverse (move the plate to a fixed spot instead of moving the tape), expand (add a third stop). Level 2.

3. Reads the opportunity, states the rules, writes every idea with initials, makes sure everyone gets a turn and stops judging during the generate phase. Level 2.

4. 180 − 20 − 20 = 140 mm. Level 2.
