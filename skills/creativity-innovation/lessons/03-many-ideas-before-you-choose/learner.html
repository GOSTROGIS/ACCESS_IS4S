Creativity and innovation · Lesson 03 · Learner

Many ideas before you choose

Come up with a wide range of ideas for a repeated shop task, without judging them, then pick one for a reason.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Generate ideas that are novel to yourself or others; Develop your ideas; Facilitate a creative and innovative environment for yourself and others

Why this matters on the job

Shops and sites are full of repeated tasks: marking the same hole on 40 plates, cutting 60 pieces of blocking, bending 30 identical brackets. Measuring each one from scratch is slow, and every measurement is a new chance for a mistake. Tradespeople solve this with jigs, templates, stops and story sticks. The first idea that comes to mind is often not the best one. Crews that list many ideas first, then choose, end up with better fixes.

Key ideas

Two phases. Keep them apart.

  1. Generate: get as many ideas out as you can. Do not judge any of them yet. Silly ideas are welcome, because they often lead to good ones.
  2. Choose: now judge. Compare the ideas against what the job needs and pick one to try.

If you judge while you generate, people stop offering ideas and you end up with the first, most familiar one.

Prompts that help you think differently. These come from the Skills for Success description of creativity and innovation:

  • Do it differently: what if we changed the usual process, the tool, or who does the task?
  • Ask a question with no obvious answer: why do we mark these at all?
  • Combine: put two unrelated things together (a bench stop and a template).
  • Reverse: flip the idea around to see if the opposite works. Instead of moving the tape to each plate, what if the plate moved into a fixed spot?
  • Expand: take an idea and add to it.

Building on each other's ideas. In a group, say "Yes, and …" to add to someone's idea instead of "No, but …". Write every idea down with the initials of the person who gave it, so credit stays with them.

A jig or template is anything you make once so a repeated task comes out the same every time.

Worked example

North Arm Fabrication (a fictional shop) must mark two hole centres on 40 steel plates. Each plate is 100 mm wide and 150 mm long. Each hole is on the centreline of the width, 25 mm in from each short end. Right now a worker measures every plate with a tape and square. It takes about 2 minutes a plate, and a few marks have been off.

Where the holes are:

  • across the width: 100 ÷ 2 = 50 mm from either long edge
  • between the two hole centres: 150 − 25 − 25 = 100 mm

Ideas from a 10-minute generate round, with no judging:

  1. Sheet metal template the size of the plate, with two holes. Lay it on and mark through the holes.
  2. Cardboard template. Quick to make, wears out.
  3. Mark one plate very carefully and use it as a master on top of the others.
  4. Two strips on the bench at a right angle, so every plate sits in the same corner.
  5. Combine 1 and 4: a template hinged to the corner stop that flips down onto each plate.
  6. Reverse: set the measurement once. Lock a combination square at 25 mm instead of reading a tape 80 times.
  7. Story stick with notches at 25 mm and 125 mm.
  8. Do it differently: ask the supplier to send the plates already marked.
  9. Ask a question: can the holes be located by a set-up on the machine instead of by marks? (A question for the shop lead.)
  10. Expand 4: add a stop on the third side so the plate cannot slide.

Then the crew chose, using criteria:

Idea Same every time (1 to 3) Fast (1 to 3) Uses shop scrap (1 to 3) Total
1. Steel template 3 2 3 8
5. Hinged template on corner stop 3 3 2 8
6. Locked combination square 2 2 3 7

Two ideas tied. The crew picked idea 5 to try first, because it locates the plate and marks it in one move. They wrote down what they did not know yet: whether the hinge would wear and let the template move.

Time check: if the new method takes about 0.5 minutes a plate, the time for 40 plates goes from 40 × 2 = 80 minutes to 40 × 0.5 = 20 minutes. That saves 80 − 20 = 60 minutes.

Safety. In this lesson you only sketch and talk. Nothing gets cut, drilled or punched. Any jig you want to build later is checked by your instructor first and built under your shop's safety rules.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Warm-up. In 2 minutes, list as many uses as you can for a C-clamp. Any use counts.

Task 2 (level 2) On your own, generate at least 8 ideas for marking the North Arm plates faster and the same every time. Use words, sketches or both. Do not judge or cross anything out. You may use one idea from the worked example, but the rest must be your own.

Task 3 (level 2) In a group of 3 or 4, run an idea round. One person is the facilitator. The facilitator:

  • reads out the opportunity: "How might we mark 40 plates the same every time, faster?"
  • reminds the group of the rules: all ideas count, no judging yet, build with "Yes, and …"
  • writes every idea with the initials of the person who gave it
  • makes sure everyone gets a turn

Aim for at least 15 ideas as a group.

Task 4 (level 2) Use two prompts you have not used yet (do it differently, ask a question, combine, reverse, expand). Add one new idea for each prompt and name the prompt you used.

  1. Prompt: __ Idea: ________
  2. Prompt: __ Idea: ________

Task 5 (level 2) Now choose. Pick your group's top 3 ideas. Score each from 1 to 3 against at least three criteria, such as same every time, fast, uses shop scrap, safe, easy to check. Pick one to try first and write one thing you do not know yet about it.

Idea Total

Idea we picked: ___ Why: ___

What we do not know yet: __________

Task 6 (level 2) A new order comes in. The plates are 120 mm wide and 200 mm long. Each hole is on the centreline of the width, 30 mm in from each short end.

a) How far is each hole from a long edge? __

b) How far apart are the hole centres? __

c) Measuring each plate takes 2.5 minutes. A template takes 0.5 minutes. How many minutes does the template save on 40 plates? Use BEDMAS. __

Task 7 (level 3) Northline Electric (a fictional company) must mount 48 receptacle boxes in a new daycare. The drawings call for the bottom of every box to be 450 mm above the finished floor, and the floor is not finished yet. Measuring each box from the subfloor with a tape is slow, and two boxes on the last job were set at the wrong height.

a) Generate at least 6 ideas for setting every box at the same height, faster. Use at least two of the five prompts.

b) Score your top 3 ideas from 1 to 3 against three criteria you choose. Pick one to try first.

c) Write one thing you do not know yet that could change your choice. Who would you ask?

Check yourself

  1. Why do you keep generating ideas separate from choosing ideas?
  2. Name two of the five prompts and give a one-line example of each.
  3. What does the facilitator do in an idea round?
  4. A plate is 180 mm long. Its holes are 20 mm in from each short end. How far apart are the hole centres?
