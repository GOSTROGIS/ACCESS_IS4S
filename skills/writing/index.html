Writing

Writing helps a reader understand, decide, remember or act. A useful piece of writing might be a two-line request, a completed form, a clear label, a table or a longer explanation. Its success depends on the task. A short note can be effective when it includes exactly what the reader needs. A longer document can fail when its important point is difficult to find.

Lessons

9 ACCESS lessons for writing, each with a learner page, an instructor lesson plan and answers.

1Keep a daily work log

Write short, factual log entries that tell the next person what was done, when, by whom and what still needs doing. 60 min · Level 1-2.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

2Write sentences that say who does what

Build clear sentences with a doer, an action and an object, choose active or passive voice on purpose, and keep pronouns and verb tenses from confusing the reader. 90 min · Level 1-2.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

3Write steps and conditions a crew can follow

Turn a task into numbered steps, put each condition before the action it controls, and use words like if, unless, and, or and all so they mean exactly what you intend. 90 min · Level 2-3.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

4Fill in a workplace form correctly

Read a form before you write, put each fact in the right box, calculate hours with BEDMAS, report facts without accusing anyone, and check before you sign. 75 min · Level 1-2.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

5Report a workplace injury in BC

Know who writes which injury report in BC, and write a clear, factual description of how an injury happened. 90 min · Level 2-3.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

6Text and email a foreman or client

Choose text or email, lead with the point, write a subject line that says what the message is about, match your tone to the reader, and confirm spoken agreements in writing. 90 min · Level 1-3.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

7Check writing made with a phone or AI tool

Use voice typing and AI writing tools without losing control of your message. Check every word against the facts, keep private information out, and write so any reader or tool keeps the key facts. 60 min · Level 2-3.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

8Write a trades résumé and cover letter

Build a one-page résumé that shows your skills to a trades employer, choose a format that fits your experience, and write a short cover letter tailored to one job posting. 120 min · Level 2-3.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers

9Use word roots to spell and explain trade words

Break long trade words into Latin and Greek roots to remember their meaning and spelling, use Canadian spelling, and explain technical words in plain language when the reader needs it. 60 min · Level 1-2.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
