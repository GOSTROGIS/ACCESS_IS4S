Take meeting notes and keep an issue log

Write meeting notes as decisions and action items with one owner and a due date, move open problems into an issue log, and keep the log up to date until each issue is closed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Why this matters on the job

On most jobs the trades meet once a week, or every morning, to sort out who goes where and what is in the way. People leave the meeting sure they agreed. Three days later the framer thinks the electrician was calling the inspector, and the electrician thinks the super was. Good notes fix that. They say what was decided, who will do what by when, and which problems are still open. An issue log keeps those open problems on one page until someone closes them. Taking the notes is often a job for an apprentice or a lead hand, and it is a good way to be noticed.

Key ideas

1. Notes are not a word-for-word record

You do not write down everything people say. You listen for three things:

Listen for It sounds like Example
Decision: something agreed "OK, let's do that." "Drywall starts Thursday." Drywall starts Thursday, November 19.
Action item: someone will do something "I'll call them." "Can you book it?" Leo Brandt to call the door supplier today.
Issue: a problem not solved in the meeting "Nobody knows yet." "That's still a problem." The back door does not lock.

Jokes, stories and repeated points do not go in the notes.

2. The header

Meeting: Weekly coordination, Unit 104 fit-out Date and time: 2026-11-16, 7:30 am Place: Site office Present: Leo Brandt (GC project manager), Tasha Bell (framing foreman), Mina Cho (electrician), Kofi Mensah (drywall foreman) Notes by: your name

3. Every action item has one owner and a due date

Weak Clear
Someone will look at the door. Leo Brandt: call the door supplier about the back door lock, by end of day Monday, November 16.
The crew to clean up. Tasha Bell: clear the framing offcuts from Room 105, by noon Tuesday, November 17.

"Everyone", "the crew" and "someone" are not owners. Write a person's name. If nobody takes it, write it as an issue.

4. Open problems go into an issue log

An issue log is a table that lists every open problem on the job:

No. Date raised Issue Raised by Owner Due Status Update
  • No.: give each issue a number and never reuse it.
  • Owner: one person who will get it closed.
  • Status: Open, In progress or Closed.
  • Update: what changed, and when.

5. Keep the log alive

  • Update it after every meeting, and when a text or call changes something.
  • Never erase an issue. When it is fixed, mark it Closed and write the date.
  • Bring the log to the next meeting and go through the open issues first.

6. Send the notes the same day

Send or post the notes the day of the meeting. End with: "Please tell me by noon tomorrow if anything here is wrong." Now nobody can say later that they did not agree.

Worked example

Weekly coordination meeting, Unit 104 fit-out at Brightwater Plaza (a fictional job). What was said:

Leo (GC project manager): Morning. Three things. First, drywall delivery is Wednesday at 7. Kofi, can your crew start Thursday? Kofi (drywall foreman): Yes, if the framing in Room 105 is done. Tasha (framing foreman): 105 will be done Tuesday by noon. Leo: Good. Second, electrical rough-in inspection. Mina? Mina (electrician): Booked for Tuesday at 1 pm. I need the ceiling in 105 left open till then. Kofi: We won't board 105 before Thursday anyway. Leo: Third, the back door still won't lock. Tasha: That's not ours. The door supplier installed it. Leo: I'll call them today. And coffee's on me Friday.

The notes:

Meeting: Weekly coordination, Unit 104 fit-out Date and time: 2026-11-16, 7:30 am Place: Site office Present: Leo Brandt, Tasha Bell, Mina Cho, Kofi Mensah Notes by: Jordan Paul

Decisions 1. Drywall delivery Wednesday, November 18, at 7:00 am. Drywall starts Thursday, November 19. 2. The Room 105 ceiling stays open until the electrical inspection on Tuesday, November 17.

Action items

Action Owner Due
Finish framing in Room 105 Tasha Bell Tue Nov 17, noon
Electrical rough-in inspection Mina Cho Tue Nov 17, 1:00 pm
Call the door supplier about the back door lock Leo Brandt Mon Nov 16

Open issues: see issue log, No. 1.

Please tell me by noon tomorrow if anything here is wrong.

The issue log:

No. Date raised Issue Raised by Owner Due Status Update
1 2026-11-16 Back door does not lock Leo Brandt Leo Brandt 2026-11-17 Open

The coffee on Friday did not go in the notes.

Practice

Use this second meeting for Tasks 1 to 3.

Weekly coordination, Unit 104 fit-out, Thursday, November 19, 7:30 am, site office. Present: Leo Brandt, Tasha Bell, Mina Cho, Kofi Mensah.

  1. Leo: Drywall is going up in Room 104 today. Kofi, what about 105?
  2. Kofi: We'll start 105 tomorrow, Friday.
  3. Leo: OK, 105 starts Friday.
  4. Mina: The inspector passed rough-in on Tuesday, but he wants a cover plate on the junction box above the back door.
  5. Mina: I'll put it on today.
  6. Tasha: Somebody keeps parking in front of the garbage bin. The bin truck couldn't get in yesterday.
  7. Leo: I'll put up a no-parking sign by the bin by Friday.
  8. Kofi: Did anyone watch the game last night?
  9. Leo: The door supplier says the new lock comes Monday.
  10. Tasha: The header in Room 106 is 50 mm lower than the drawing shows. I need an answer before Monday or my crew stops in 106.
  11. Leo: I'll send an RFI to the architect today.

Task 1 (level 1) Mark each line D (decision), A (action item), I (issue or issue update) or X (does not go in the notes). Some lines can have two letters.

1 ___ 2 ___ 3 ___ 4 ___ 5 ___ 6 ___ 7 ___ 8 ___ 9 ___ 10 ___ 11 ___

Task 2 (level 2) Write the notes for the November 19 meeting: the header, the decisions and an action items table with Action, Owner and Due. Write dates in full.

Task 3 (level 2) Copy the issue log from the worked example. Update issue 1 with the news from the November 19 meeting. Add the new issues from that meeting as issues 2, 3 and 4.

Task 4 (level 2) These messages arrive after the meeting. Update your issue log. Close any issue that is fixed, with the date.

  • Thursday, November 19, 3:10 pm, text from Mina: "Cover plate is on the back door junction box."
  • Friday, November 20, 10:00 am, text from Leo: "No-parking sign is up at the bin."
  • Friday, November 20, 2:30 pm, email from Leo: "Architect replied to the RFI. The Room 106 header is OK where it is. Carry on."
  • Monday, November 23, 11:00 am, text from Tasha: "New lock is on the back door. It works."

Task 5 (level 1 to 2) Rewrite each action item so it has one owner, a clear action and a due date. Use the names from this lesson. Say what you added.

a) "Someone to sort out the bin." b) "Kofi to check stuff." c) "Order more screws."

Check yourself

  1. Name the three things you listen for when you take meeting notes.
  2. Why is "the crew" not an owner for an action item?
  3. What do you do with an issue in the log once it is fixed?
  4. What should you write at the end of your notes, and why?
  5. Name four columns in an issue log.
