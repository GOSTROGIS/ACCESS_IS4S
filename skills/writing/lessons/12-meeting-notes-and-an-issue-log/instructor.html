Take meeting notes and keep an issue log

Write meeting notes as decisions and action items with one owner and a due date, move open problems into an issue log, and keep the log up to date until each issue is closed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Session length: 75 min. All times below are part of the 75 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Before class

Pick four learners to read the parts of Leo, Tasha, Mina and Kofi. The worked example and the November 19 meeting are read aloud as short role plays, so the rest of the class listens and takes notes as they would on site. Print a blank issue log (the eight columns from key idea 4) on a page turned sideways, one for each learner.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Have you ever left a meeting or a morning huddle sure of what was agreed, and found out later that someone else heard it differently?" Take two stories. Then ask: "If nobody writes it down, whose memory wins?" Say: "Today you learn to write the notes that settle it."

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • listen for decisions, action items and issues and leave out the rest (Plan the writing task)
  • write action items with one owner, a clear action and a due date (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • use a notes header, an action table and an issue log (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)
  • update and close issues as news comes in (Review and revise your writing)

Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

  1. "What is the difference between a decision and an action item?"
  2. "Rewrite: 'Someone will call about the door.'"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Ask the opening questions. Share a story of a meeting that was remembered differently. none 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Listen, ask questions. Board 2 min
1.3 Ask the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. none 3 min
2.1 Teach key ideas 1 to 3. Have the four readers act out the worked example meeting while the class jots notes. Then show the model notes and compare. Point out that the coffee is left out. Take rough notes during the role play, compare with the model. learner.md, four readers 10 min
2.2 Teach key ideas 4 to 6: the issue log, keeping it alive, sending notes the same day. Fill issue 1 on the blank log. printed issue log 8 min
2.3 Have the readers act out the November 19 meeting. Assign Task 1. Label each line D, A, I or X. learner.md, four readers 8 min
2.4 Assign Task 2. Circulate and check that every action has a person, not "the crew". Write the November 19 notes. learner.md 8 min
3.1 Assign Task 3. Check that issue numbers are not reused and that issue 1 gets an update, not a new row. Update and add to the issue log. printed issue log 10 min
3.2 Assign Task 4. Read each message aloud as if it had just come in on your phone. Update the log and close issues. printed issue log 8 min
3.3 Assign Task 5. Rewrite the vague action items. learner.md 4 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer in writing. none 5 min
4.2 Give the summary. Listen, ask final questions. none 4 min

Total: 5 + 2 + 3 + 10 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 8 + 4 + 5 + 4 = 75 min.

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. In a meeting the super says, "Ana, can you get the lift charged before Wednesday?" and Ana says, "Sure." Write the action item.
  2. An issue was fixed yesterday. What do you change in the issue log?
  3. Name the five lines of a notes header.

Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • listening for decisions, action items and issues
  • the notes header
  • action items with one owner, a clear action and a due date
  • the issue log: numbers, owners, status and updates
  • closing issues with a date, never erasing them
  • sending notes the same day and asking for corrections
