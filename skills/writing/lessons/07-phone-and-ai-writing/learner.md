# Check writing made with a phone or AI tool

## Why this matters on the job

On site you might talk a text into your phone with gloves on, or ask an AI tool to clean up an email to a client. These tools save time, but they also mishear words, change trade terms and sometimes make up facts. The message goes out under your name. You are the one who has to check it.

## Key ideas

### 1. You are the author

A tool can draft or fix your writing. You decide what is sent, and you are responsible for it. The Government of Canada's advice to its own staff says the same thing: take responsibility for what you prepare using AI, review it for accuracy, and do not assume the output is correct.

### 2. Voice typing: say the punctuation, then read it back

Most phones can type what you say.

- **iPhone Dictation:** turn it on in Settings > General > Keyboard > Enable Dictation, then tap the microphone on the keyboard. You can say punctuation such as "comma" or "question mark". In supported languages it also adds some punctuation for you. Apple's guide: https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/dictate-text-iph2c0651d2/ios
- **Gboard (Android or iPhone):** tap the microphone on the keyboard and speak. You can say "period", "comma", "question mark" and "new line". Google's guide: https://support.google.com/gboard/answer/2781851

Voice typing often gets these wrong. Check them every time:

- words that sound alike: tee and tea, two, to and too, four and for, weld and well
- sizes and numbers: "three quarter inch" may come out as 3 quarter inch, 3/4 inch or 0.75
- names of people, streets and products
- trade terms the phone does not know

### 3. AI writing tools: check every fact against the source

An AI tool can write smooth sentences that are wrong. It may change a date, invent a form number, quote a law that does not say that, or promise something you never agreed to. The BC Information and Privacy Commissioner's tips say to confirm information you get from a generative AI tool before using it, and, if the tool cites a law, to look at the law itself.

Before you send anything an AI tool helped write:

1. Put your source facts beside the draft.
2. Check every name, date, time, number, size, address and price.
3. Check every promise. Remove any you did not agree to.
4. Check the tone for your reader.

### 4. Keep private information out of public AI tools

The BC Information and Privacy Commissioner warns that information you type into a generative AI tool may be stored, used or shared by the company that owns it, and you may not be able to get it back or have it deleted. The Government of Canada tells its staff not to put personal, sensitive or protected information into public AI tools.

On the job, that means do not paste in:

- anyone's Social Insurance Number, Personal Health Number, address or phone number
- details of a co-worker's injury or health
- your employer's prices, bids, drawings or client lists, unless your employer has approved the tool for that use

If you want help with wording, replace names and private details with placeholders like [client] or [address] before you paste.

### 5. Write so the key facts survive

Your email may be read on a small screen, skimmed, forwarded, found later by a search, or summarized by the reader's own AI assistant. A vague message loses its meaning fast. To keep the key facts:

- put the main point and date in the subject line and first sentence
- name things: "the Unit 4 water heater", not "it"
- write full dates: "Thursday, November 12", not "Thursday" or "next week"
- write key facts in the message, not only in a photo or attachment

### Worked example

What a plumber said into the phone:

> "Hi Marcus I need to three quarter inch tees and for elbows for the unit for kitchen can you bring them by ten"

What the phone typed:

> Hi Marcus I need to 3 quarter inch teas and for elbows for the unit for kitchen can you bring them by 10

Checked and fixed:

> Hi Marcus, I need two 3/4" tees and four elbows for the Unit 4 kitchen. Can you bring them by 10:00 am?

Fixes: "to" to "two", "teas" to "tees", "for" to "four", "unit for" to "Unit 4", the size written as 3/4", punctuation added, and the time made clear.

Source facts:
- Client: Ms. Grace Thomas. Address: 2214 Birch Lane.
- Furnace install moved from Thursday, November 12, to Tuesday, November 17, 2026, because the furnace will arrive on November 16.
- Crew arrives 8:00 to 8:30 am, finishes about 4:00 pm. Heat off during the work.

>
> We are pleased to confirm your furnace installation at 2214 Birch Lane has been rescheduled to Tuesday, November 18. As required by WorkSafeBC, our licensed crew will arrive at 7:30 am. The installation will be completed by noon, and we will provide a 10% discount for the inconvenience.

## Check yourself

1. Who is responsible for a message that an AI tool helped write?
2. Name two kinds of words that voice typing often gets wrong.
3. What should you do if an AI tool tells you a law or form requires something?
4. Give two kinds of information you should not paste into a public AI tool.
5. Why is "about Thursday" weaker than "about Thursday, November 12"?
