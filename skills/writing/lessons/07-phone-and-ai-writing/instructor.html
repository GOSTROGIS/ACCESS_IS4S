Writing · Lesson 07 · Instructor

Check writing made with a phone or AI tool

Use voice typing and AI writing tools without losing control of your message. Check every word against the facts, keep private information out, and write so any reader or tool keeps the key facts.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Session length: 60 min. All times below are part of the 60 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Before class

Check that voice typing works on at least one phone you can show on a projector or pass around. Check the Apple and Google help pages listed in Sources, because menus change. If you show an AI tool, use only an account your organization has approved, and use only the fictional facts in the lesson. Learners do not need to own a phone or an AI account to complete the tasks.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Say a short, trade-heavy sentence into a phone with voice typing, for example "Grab two three quarter inch tees and four elbows for Unit 4." Show the result. Ask: "Would you send that to your foreman as it is? What would go wrong if you did?"

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • correct words, numbers, sizes and names that voice typing gets wrong (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • adjust an AI draft so the tone suits the reader and promises nothing extra (Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task)
  • check every fact in a phone or AI draft against the source before sending (Review and revise your writing)

Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

  1. "Have you used voice typing or an AI tool to write a message? What went wrong, if anything?"
  2. "If an AI tool writes something wrong and you send it, whose mistake is it?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Do the voice typing demo and ask the opening question. Point out the errors. Phone, projector 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Listen, ask questions. Board 2 min
1.3 Ask the pre-assessment questions. Answer aloud. none 3 min
2.1 Teach key ideas 1 and 2. Show where Dictation or voice typing is turned on. Say a sentence with "comma" and "question mark" in it. Walk through the worked example. Try voice typing on their own phone if they have one, or follow on screen. learner.md, phone 8 min
2.2 Assign Task 1. Fix the dictated text. learner.md 7 min
2.3 Teach key ideas 3 and 4. Read the BC Information and Privacy Commissioner's two warnings aloud. Ask questions, give examples of private information from their trade. learner.md 7 min
2.4 Assign Task 2. Ask learners to put the source facts and draft side by side and tick each fact. Find the errors and rewrite the email. learner.md 10 min
2.5 Assign Task 3. Discuss answers quickly. Sort the five items. learner.md 5 min
3.1 Teach key idea 5. Assign Task 4. Rewrite the vague message. learner.md 7 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer in writing. none 4 min
4.2 Give the summary. Listen, ask final questions. none 2 min

Total: 5 + 2 + 3 + 8 + 7 + 7 + 10 + 5 + 7 + 4 + 2 = 60 min.

Post-assessment (4 min, step 4.1)

  1. Voice typing wrote "were out of to buy for studs". Write what the worker most likely meant.
  2. An AI draft says "As required by the BC Building Code, we will finish by Friday." What do you check before sending?
  3. Rewrite "Can you fix it Tuesday?" so the reader knows what and when.

Summary (2 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • you are the author of anything a tool helps you write
  • saying punctuation and checking sound-alike words, numbers, sizes and names in voice typing
  • checking every fact and promise in an AI draft against the source
  • keeping personal and confidential information out of public AI tools
  • writing so key facts survive skimming, forwarding and summaries
