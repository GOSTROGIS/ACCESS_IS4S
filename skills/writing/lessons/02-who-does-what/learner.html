Write sentences that say who does what

Build clear sentences with a doer, an action and an object, choose active or passive voice on purpose, and keep pronouns and verb tenses from confusing the reader.

  • Components: Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Why this matters on the job

"The valve was left open." Who left it open? When? A sentence like this can start an argument on site, or stall a safety investigation. In logs, reports, texts and emails, the reader needs to know who did what, to what, and when. A clear sentence protects you, your crew and the people reading it later.

Key ideas

1. Most sentences have a doer, an action and an object

Doer (subject) Action (verb) Object
The crew laid the pipe.
The gas fitter is connecting the meter.
The apprentice will clean the welding booth.

To find each part, ask questions:

  • Who or what did it? That is the subject.
  • Did what? That is the verb. Look for the action word.
  • To what or whom? That is the object.
  • Where, when, how, how many? These add detail: "...laid the pipe in the trench on Tuesday."

2. Kinds of words

Kind of word What it does Trade examples
Noun names a person, thing or place welder, flange, shop, Bay 3
Verb shows an action or state cut, torque, is, will check
Adjective describes a noun cracked guard, 3/4" pipe
Adverb describes how, when or where an action happens tighten evenly, reported immediately

3. Pronouns must point to one clear thing

Pronouns are short words like it, they, he, she, this, that. They save space, but only if the reader knows what they stand for.

  • Unclear: "The forklift hit the trailer. It was damaged." (The forklift or the trailer?)
  • Clear: "The forklift hit the trailer. The trailer's rear light was damaged."

When something matters, name it. "Unit 14, the yellow skid steer" is clearer than "the machine", which is clearer than "it".

4. Active and passive voice

Active voice puts the doer first. Passive voice puts the object first and often drops the doer.

Active Passive
Ken shut the valve. The valve was shut (by Ken).
The crew is excavating at Bear Creek. Excavation is being done at Bear Creek.
We will charge a late fee. A late fee will be charged.

Passive voice uses a form of be (is, was, were, has been, will be) plus the action word.

When to use which:

  • Use active when the reader needs to know who did it. In incident reports, logs and messages about your own work, this is almost always.
  • Passive is fine when the doer is unknown or does not matter: "The slab was poured on 2026-10-14."
  • Passive can sound like you are hiding something. "Mistakes were made" sounds worse to a foreman than "I measured from the wrong line. I've re-marked it."

5. Keep the time steady, and match the doer to the verb

  • A report about what happened is in the past tense: "I checked the gauge, saw 0 psi and opened the valve." Do not switch to "I check... I see..." halfway.
  • One doer takes the singular form, more than one takes the plural form: "The bolt is loose." "The bolts are loose." "One of the bolts is loose." (The doer is "one", not "bolts".)

Worked example

A lead hand at Salmonberry Framing (a fictional contractor) wrote this note:

The ladder was moved and the area was not taped off. Then the box was dropped and it broke.

These are the facts from the site:

  • At 10:20 am, Sam moved the ladder to the north wall.
  • Nobody taped off the area under the scaffold.
  • At 10:25 am, a box of fittings fell from the second level and split open on the ground. Nobody was hurt.

Rewritten with doers, past tense and clear nouns:

At 10:20 am, Sam moved the ladder to the north wall. Nobody had taped off the area under the scaffold. At 10:25 am, a box of fittings fell from the second level. The box split open on the ground. Nobody was hurt.

Notice: "the box was dropped" became "a box of fittings fell", because the facts do not say anyone dropped it. "It broke" became "The box split open", so the reader is not left guessing. The writer did not add blame that the facts do not support.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) For each sentence, write the subject (S), verb (V) and object (O).

a) The millwright aligned the coupling. b) Our crew will frame the east wall tomorrow. c) The supplier delivered forty studs. d) Maria is grinding the welds on the handrail. e) The inspector checked the firestopping in Room 204.

Task 2 (level 1) Each sentence has a pronoun that could mean two things. Rewrite it so the meaning is clear. Make your own choice where needed and say which you chose.

a) The pipe rolled into the trench box and it was dented. b) Jordan told Lee that he left the van keys in the shop. c) The grinder and the extension cord were both on the floor. Put it away. d) The client called about the invoice and the schedule. This needs an answer by Friday.

Task 3 (level 2) Change each active sentence to passive and each passive sentence to active. For passive sentences with no doer, use the doer in brackets. Then circle the version you would use in an incident report, and say why.

a) The apprentice left the guard off the bench grinder. b) The scaffold was inspected this morning. (doer: the foreman) c) Someone moved the barricade. d) The trench was entered before it was shored. (doer: two labourers) e) I tagged the saw out of service. f) The delivery was signed for. (doer: Priya)

Task 4 (level 2) Fix the verb tense and the doer-verb matches in this log entry. There are five errors.

At 8:00 am I check the tank level and it was low. I call the supplier. The trucks is coming at noon. One of the fill caps are cracked, so I tag it and told the foreman.

Task 5 (level 2) Rewrite this note in active voice using the facts below it. Use past tense and clear nouns. Do not add anything the facts do not say.

The breaker was turned off and the panel was opened. The work was started before the lock was put on.

Facts: At 1:15 pm Darren switched off breaker 12. At 1:17 pm Darren opened panel P2. At 1:18 pm Kim started pulling wire. Darren put his lock on breaker 12 at 1:25 pm.

Check yourself

  1. In "The apprentice swept the shop floor", what is the subject, the verb and the object?
  2. Change "The forms were stripped" into active voice, using "the carpenters" as the doer.
  3. Give one situation where passive voice is fine to use.
  4. Which is correct: "One of the welds are cracked" or "One of the welds is cracked"? Why?
  5. Why can the word "it" cause problems in a report?
