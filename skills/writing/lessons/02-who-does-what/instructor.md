# Write sentences that say who does what

Session length: 90 min. All times below are part of the 90 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Read two versions of the same story aloud, adapted from the ACCESS active and passive lesson.

Version A: "I show up Monday and what do I see? Nobody swept the deck. Nobody stacked the plywood. The generator's out of gas, because if I don't say something, it sits there empty all week. I'm busting my back all day, and what are they doing? Nothing."

Version B: "Upon arrival it was noted that the deck had not been swept, the plywood had not been stacked and the generator had not been refuelled. Repeated reminders appear to be required."

Ask: "Same facts. Why do they feel so different? Which one tells you who should do what?"

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the subject, verb and object in a sentence and name the kind of each word (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
- replace unclear pronouns with clear nouns (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
- choose active or passive voice to suit the reader and purpose (Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task)
- correct verb tense and doer-verb agreement in their own writing (Review and revise your writing)

## Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.3)

1. "In 'The crew laid the pipe', who did the action?"
2. "What is the difference between 'Ken shut the valve' and 'The valve was shut'?"

Write learners' words on the board. Come back to them in step 2.4.

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

1. Mark the subject, verb and object: "The pipefitter threaded the nipple."
2. Rewrite in active voice: "The lockout was removed early." (doer: the night shift electrician)
3. Fix the sentence: "The crew check the anchors yesterday and finds two loose."

## Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- subject, verb and object, and the questions that find them
- nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs
- pronouns that point to one clear thing
- active voice, passive voice and when to use each
- past tense in reports, and matching the doer to the verb
