Report a near miss in writing

Know when something counts as a near miss in BC, describe acts and conditions instead of blaming people, and write a near-miss report that helps your employer find and fix the cause before someone gets hurt.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Task 1 (level 1)

a) NM. Nobody hurt, but a falling 2x10 could seriously injure someone. b) INJ. Stitches at a clinic are medical treatment, so it is reported as an injury. c) NM. A fall from a ladder could cause a serious injury. d) NM. Nobody was near, but a falling pipe could seriously injure someone walking past. e) N. A broken microwave is a maintenance request, not a safety incident.

Steps: ask "Was anyone hurt badly enough to need medical treatment?" If yes, it is an injury. If no, ask "Could it have caused a serious injury?" If yes, it is a near miss.

Task 2 (level 2)

  1. F
  2. O. Blame. Leave it out. The fact is the act: "The top limit switch was wedged closed."
  3. F. It is a time someone can check.
  4. O. A generalization. Leave it out, or write the specific fact: "The repair report was not followed. New bolts were put in the damaged threads."
  5. F

Task 3 (level 2 to 3)

Sample:

When: 2026-11-03, 1:20 pm Where: Harbour Lane townhouses, Building C, east edge of the second-floor deck Who was there: Ana Silva (framer, on the deck), Chris Doyle (labourer, on the ground below), Sky Morgan (apprentice framer, on the deck, witness) What was happening: Ana was setting a 2x10 header down near the east edge of the deck. Chris was stacking blocking on the ground below. What happened: At 11:30 am a 2.4 m section of toe board at the east edge was taken off so joists could be slid up. It was not put back. At 1:20 pm the header slid off the deck through the gap and fell about 3 m. It landed about 1 m from Chris. Nobody was hurt. What could have happened: The header could have hit Chris on the head or shoulders. What was done right away: At 1:22 pm Ana and I stopped work at the east edge. Chris moved away and taped off the area below. At 1:25 pm I told Leah Ross, the foreman. Ana put the toe board back at 1:40 pm. What is needed: Put toe boards back as soon as loading is finished. Tape off the area below any edge where a toe board is off. Reported by: Sky Morgan, apprentice framer, Salmonberry Framing, 2026-11-03, 2:00 pm

A good answer:

  • keeps the events in time order, from 11:30 am to 2:00 pm
  • gives the distances (3 m fall, 1 m from Chris) and the 2.4 m gap
  • says what could have happened
  • names the people as witnesses, not as people to blame
  • says what was done right away, with times
  • suggests a fix to the cause, not "be more careful"

Task 4 (level 3)

Sample email:

Subject: Building C north deck edge: toe board off, 2026-11-06

Hi Leah,

At 8:00 am today the toe board at the north edge of the second-floor deck in Building C was off. Nobody was working at that edge. This is the same kind of gap as the near miss I reported on November 3, when a header fell through the east edge and landed 1 m from Chris.

I taped off the ground below the north edge and told the crew on site. Can you let me know by the end of today who will put the toe board back, and whether we can make it a rule to replace toe boards as soon as loading is done?

Thanks, Sky Morgan

Two more steps (any two): tell the worker member of the joint health and safety committee or the worker health and safety representative, call WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line (604.276.3100 in the Lower Mainland, 1.888.621.7233 toll-free), or use WorkSafeBC's online form for reporting unsafe work.

A good answer is factual and polite, links to the earlier report, says what the writer did, and asks for a named action and a time.

Task 5 (level 3)

a) Any three:

  • The operator could have reported the limit switch problem instead of wedging it.
  • Anyone who saw the die drop could have written a near-miss report that day, which would have started an investigation.
  • Lee's repair report could have been followed, or the decision to overrule it could have been written down with the reason, so someone had to answer for it.
  • Lee could have followed up in writing when the quick fix was chosen, and taken it to the joint committee if nothing changed.

b) Sample:

When: the date and time the die dropped Where: the stamping press, by its shop number Who was there: the setup operator, Lee (maintenance technician) What was happening: The press was being set up for a production run. What happened: The top limit switch was found wedged closed. The press drove the top die past its stop. The bolts holding the die support pulled out of their threads and the die dropped. Nobody was under it. What could have happened: Anyone near the press could have been crushed or struck by the die. What was done right away: Lee locked out the press and tagged it out of service. The supervisor was told. What is needed: Re-machine the support threads properly before the press runs again. Find out why the limit switch kept stopping the machine and fix it. Remind operators that safeguards must not be bypassed. Reported by: Lee, maintenance technician, with the date and time.

A good answer describes the wedged switch as a fact, not as one person's fault, says what could have happened, and asks for a fix to the cause.

Check yourself

  1. When nobody needed medical treatment, but what happened could have caused a serious injury to a worker (level 1).
  2. 48 hours (level 1).
  3. Sample: "The toe board was taken off and not put back." Add the time, place and length if you know them, for example "At 11:30 am a 2.4 m section of toe board was taken off the east edge and not put back." (level 2)
  4. A person must not intentionally remove, impair or make ineffective a safeguard that protects workers, except as the Regulation allows (level 2).
  5. 604.276.3100. Yes, you can report anonymously (level 1).
