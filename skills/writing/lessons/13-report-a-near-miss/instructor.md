# Report a near miss in writing

Session length: 90 min. All times below are part of the 90 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

## Before class

Open these pages on the projector and check them again on the day:

- BC Laws, Workers Compensation Act, Part 2, Division 10 (sections 68 to 73): https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/19001_02
- WorkSafeBC, Health and safety assistance (Prevention Information Line and the online form for reporting unsafe work): https://www.worksafebc.com/en/contact-us/departments-and-services/health-safety-prevention

The story in key idea 6 ends with a serious injury. It is told by an ACCESS instructor from their own work as a maintenance technician, with the names and the company removed. Tell learners it is a real event before you read it. Some learners may have been hurt at work or seen someone hurt. Invite them to share only if they want to, and do not ask for details of anyone's injury. Keep the discussion on what was written, or not written, at each point.

This lesson follows [Report a workplace injury in BC](../05-injury-report/learner.html). If learners have not done lesson 05, spend two extra minutes in step 2.2 on writing in time order, past tense and active voice.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Say: "A bundle of rebar slips from a sling and lands one metre from a worker. Nobody is hurt. The crew says, 'No harm done, let's keep going.'" Ask: "Is there harm done? What is different tomorrow if nobody writes it down?" Take answers. Say: "Today you write the report that stops tomorrow's injury."

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- recognize when an incident is a near miss that a BC employer must investigate (Identify the task that requires you to write)
- put the facts of a near miss in time order and decide what could have happened (Plan the writing task)
- describe acts and conditions without blaming people (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
- use the boxes of a near-miss report and a follow-up email (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)
- remove blame and guesses from a report before handing it in (Review and revise your writing)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

1. "What is a near miss?"
2. "Who would you tell first if you saw one on site?"

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

1. A forklift tips a pallet of blocks off the forks. The blocks land where a worker was standing a minute before. Nobody is hurt. Is this a near miss the employer must investigate? Why?
2. Within how many days must the employer send the full investigation report to WorkSafeBC?
3. Rewrite as a fact: "The forklift driver is a menace."

## Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- what counts as a near miss under the BC Workers Compensation Act, section 69
- how your report leads to the employer's 48-hour preliminary and 30-day full investigations
- the boxes of a near-miss report, including what could have happened
- describing acts and conditions, not people
- safeguards (section 4.12) and tagging out unsafe equipment (section 12.10)
- following up in writing, and WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line
- protection from being discouraged or punished for reporting
