# Report a near miss in writing

## Why this matters on the job

A board slides off a deck and lands a metre from someone. A grinder disc shatters and the pieces hit the wall. Nobody is hurt, so the crew says, "No harm done," and keeps going. But the same thing can happen tomorrow with someone standing a metre closer. A near miss is a free warning. In BC, the law says the employer must investigate a near miss that could have seriously hurt someone, and a clear written report from the people who saw it is where that investigation starts. This lesson builds on [Report a workplace injury in BC](../05-injury-report/learner.html) and on Communication lesson 11, [Report up the chain](../../../communication/lessons/11-report-up-the-chain/learner.html), where you reported a near miss out loud.

## Key ideas

### 1. What counts as a near miss in BC

The BC Workers Compensation Act, section 69, says an employer must investigate an incident that:

- did not hurt anyone, or caused only a minor injury that did not need medical treatment,
- **but could have caused a serious injury** to a worker.

That is a near miss. If someone needed medical treatment, it is an injury, and you report it as in lesson 05.

### 2. Where your report goes

Lesson 05 ([Injury report](../05-injury-report/learner.html)) explains these steps for injuries. The same rules cover a near miss.

| Step | Who | What the law says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You, or anyone who sees it | Report the unsafe condition or act as soon as possible to your supervisor or employer (OHS Regulation, section 3.10). |
| 2 | The person who gets your report | Investigates and makes sure it is fixed without delay (section 3.10). |
| 3 | Your employer | Starts a preliminary investigation right away and completes its report within 48 hours (Act, section 71). |
| 4 | Your employer | Does a full investigation and sends the report to WorkSafeBC within 30 days (Act, section 72). |

Both investigations look for the unsafe conditions, acts or procedures that led to the incident, and the fix. Your written report gives them the facts while they are fresh.

### 3. What goes in a near-miss report

| Box | What to write |
|---|---|
| When | Date and time |
| Where | Site, building, floor, room or machine |
| Who was there | Names of the people involved and the witnesses |
| What was happening | The task, the tool or material |
| What happened | Events in time order, past tense, active voice |
| What could have happened | The realistic worst outcome: "The header could have hit Chris on the head." |
| What was done right away | Work stopped, area taped off, tool tagged out, supervisor told at what time |
| What is needed | What would stop it happening again |
| Reported by | Your name, the date and time you wrote it |

### 4. Describe the act, not the person

Name who was there, because the investigators need to talk to them. But describe what was done, not what kind of person did it.

| Blame | Fact |
|---|---|
| "Kyle is reckless." | "The top limit switch was wedged closed with a wood shim." |
| "The framers are lazy about safety." | "A 2.4 m section of toe board was off the east deck edge from 11:30 am to 1:40 pm." |
| "Nobody cares here." | "I reported the missing toe board to Leah Ross at 1:25 pm on November 3." |

### 5. Safeguards and unsafe equipment

- Under the OHS Regulation, section 4.12, a person must not intentionally remove, impair or make ineffective a safeguard that protects workers, except as the Regulation allows. Guards, toe boards, interlocks and limit switches are safeguards.
- Under section 12.10, an unsafe tool, machine or piece of equipment must be taken out of service and marked so nobody uses it until it is safe. That is why you tag it out.

### 6. Hiding it is the real danger

This story is based on a real event that an ACCESS instructor saw as a maintenance technician in a machine shop. The names and some details are changed.

> A press operator was setting up a stamping press. The top limit switch kept stopping the machine, so he wedged it closed to keep running. He told no one, because he was afraid of being written up. The press drove the top die past where it should stop. The bolts holding its support pulled out of their threads and the die dropped. Nobody was under it. That was the near miss.
>
> The maintenance technician, Lee, locked out the press and found the threads in the support stripped. Lee wrote a repair report that said the holes had to be properly re-machined before the press ran again. A senior machinist called the report "too theoretical". The supervisor chose a quick fix: new bolts in the damaged threads. The press was running again that afternoon.
>
> Weeks later the bolts pulled out again. A part broke loose, went through the guard, and broke an operator's collarbone.

Two things went wrong in writing. The bypass was never reported. And the written repair report was overruled with no record of why. If the near miss had been written up that day, the investigation would have had to find the wedged switch and the stripped threads.

### 7. If nothing gets fixed

1. Follow up **in writing** with your supervisor. Say what you reported, when, and what is still not fixed. Keep a copy.
2. Tell the worker member of your joint health and safety committee, or your worker health and safety representative.
3. Contact **WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line**: 604.276.3100 in the Lower Mainland, or 1.888.621.7233 toll-free. It takes reports of unsafe work conditions 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and you can report anonymously. WorkSafeBC also has an online form for reporting unsafe work.

The Workers Compensation Act protects you. Under section 73, an employer or supervisor must not try to discourage you from reporting a hazardous condition to WorkSafeBC. Under section 48, an employer must not punish or threaten you for using your rights or carrying out your duties under the health and safety rules.

### Worked example

A weak near-miss report:

> Grinder blew up. Could of been bad. Need better grinders.

A clear one (fictional case at **Fraser Bend Fabrication**):

> **When:** 2026-11-04, 10:15 am
> **Where:** Fraser Bend Fabrication, Bay 1, grinding station
> **Who was there:** Rena Thomas (operator), Sam Ortiz (witness, at the saw 3 m away)
> **What was happening:** Rena was grinding a weld on a steel bracket with a 4-1/2 in angle grinder.
> **What happened:** The grinding disc broke apart. Pieces hit the steel wall behind the station. One piece cracked Rena's face shield. Rena was not injured.
> **What could have happened:** A piece could have hit Rena or Sam in the face or neck.
> **What was done right away:** Rena stopped work and unplugged the grinder. I tagged the grinder out of service and told Dale Okafor at 10:20 am. The rest of the box of discs was set aside.
> **What is needed:** Check the grinder and the box of discs before anyone uses them. Replace Rena's face shield.
> **Reported by:** Sam Ortiz, 2026-11-04, 10:40 am
