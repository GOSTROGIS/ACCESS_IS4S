# Write a site visit report with photos

Session length: 120 min. All times below are part of the 120 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

## Before class

Choose three areas at ACCESS for the site walk in Task 3, for example the shop, the tool crib and the yard. Ask the staff who look after those areas if the class may walk through and take photos, and when. Agree on PPE for each area. Check that nothing private (files, screens, other people's belongings) will be in the shots.

Learners use their own phones if they choose to. Have paper and clipboards ready for anyone who would rather sketch. Remind learners not to photograph other people without asking, and to delete any photo that shows a face or private information. Photos stay on the learner's phone or go to the instructor only. Nothing is posted online.

This lesson uses the memo heading and the short report plan from [Write a site memo and a short report](../10-site-memo-and-short-report/learner.html). If learners have not done lesson 10, spend two extra minutes in step 2.2 on To, From, Date and Subject.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Hold up your phone and say: "A painting crew of four drives an hour to a job. They get there and the walls aren't sanded. Nobody can paint. What did that day cost?" Take answers: four people's wages, fuel, a lost day. Ask: "What would have saved it?" Lead to: someone visiting first and writing it up.

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name the question a site visit has to answer and who will read the report (Identify the task that requires you to write)
- plan a visit and gather photos, measurements and notes (Plan the writing task)
- write captions, factual observations and steps that start with a verb (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
- use the report skeleton: cover, distribution, summary, visit details, area sections and an actions table (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)
- check a partner's report against a checklist and make one fix to their own (Review and revise your writing)

## Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

1. "What makes a work photo useful to someone who was not there?"
2. "What is the difference between To and CC on an email?"

## Learning tasks

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Tell the wasted-trip story and ask the opening questions. | Say what the day cost and what would have saved it. | none | 5 min |
| 1.2 | State the objectives. | Listen, ask questions. | Board | 2 min |
| 1.3 | Ask the pre-assessment questions. | Answer on paper. | none | 3 min |

| 2.2 | Teach key ideas 4 to 6. Walk through Nadia's report part by part. Ask: "If Leo reads only the summary, does he know what to do?" | Find each part of the skeleton in the example. | learner.md | 12 min |
| 2.3 | Assign Task 2. | Turn rough notes into facts and steps. | learner.md | 8 min |
| 3.1 | Name the three ACCESS areas. Assign Task 3 part a. Check PPE. | Fill in the planning questions. | learner.md | 8 min |

Total: 5 + 2 + 3 + 12 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 20 + 30 + 10 + 6 + 4 = 120 min.

## Post-assessment (6 min, step 4.1)

1. Write a caption for a close-up photo of a leaking valve under the sink in Unit 2, looking north, taken 2026-11-30 at 10:15 am.
2. Rewrite as a step: "Somebody should probably check the breaker before the electrician comes back."
3. Name the six parts of the report skeleton in order.

## Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- planning a site visit: the question, the readers, the areas, what to bring
- taking a wide shot and a close-up, with scale, and keeping faces and private information out
- captions that say what, where, which way and when
- the report skeleton: cover, distribution, summary, visit details, area sections, actions
- writing observations as facts and steps as commands
- checking a report against a list before it goes out
