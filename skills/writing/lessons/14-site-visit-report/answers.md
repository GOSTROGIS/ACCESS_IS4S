# Write a site visit report with photos

## Task 1 (level 2)

Samples:

a) "Photo 1. Loading door, Fraser Bend Fabrication, from inside the shop looking west, 2026-11-25, 7:40 am."
b) "Photo 2. Cracked weld on the top hinge of the loading door, direction not recorded, 2026-11-25, 7:42 am. Tape shows 40 mm."
c) "Photo 3. Stack of steel plate on the floor in front of Bay 3, looking south, 2026-11-25, 7:50 am."

A good caption has a number, says what is in the photo, where, which way the camera faces, and the date and time. The close-up mentions the tape for scale. Its camera direction is not given; do not borrow the wide shot's direction.

## Task 2 (level 2)

Sample:

> **What I saw:** The corner bead on the washroom wall beside the door is dented for an estimated 300 mm, at about hip height. There is dust on the floor. The length is an estimate from the rough notes, not a verified measurement.
>
> **Steps:**
> 1. Remove the dented section of corner bead.
> 2. Install a new section of corner bead.
> 3. Apply mud, let it dry and sand it smooth.
> 4. Vacuum the dust from the floor.
> 5. Tell the painting crew when the corner is ready.

A good answer:

- keeps the facts (where, how long, how high, the dust)
- leaves the cart hypothesis out of the facts-only paragraph and does not identify the dust as drywall dust without evidence
- writes each step as a command that starts with a verb
- ends with a step that hands the work on to the next trade

Learners who do not know the drywall repair steps can write fewer, simpler steps ("Repair the dented corner bead before painting.") as long as each starts with a verb.

## Task 3 (level 3)

Answers depend on the areas chosen. A good plan answers all five questions: the question the visit answers, the readers, the three areas, what to bring (including PPE) and who to sign in with. A good set of photos has a wide shot and a close-up for each area, a caption for each, and no faces or private information. Sketches are fine in place of photos if they are labelled.

## Task 4 (level 3)

Answers depend on the site walk. A good report has:

- a cover with the project (the ACCESS areas and the address), the visit date, prepared by and prepared for
- To (the instructor) and CC (one classmate)
- a summary of three or four sentences that says why the visit happened, the overall condition, the main problems and what can go ahead
- visit details: date and time, conditions, who was there, how they got in
- one section per area with numbered photos and captions, a What I saw paragraph of facts, and numbered steps that start with verbs
- an actions table where each action has one person and a date
- dates written as YYYY-MM-DD

## Task 5 (level 2)

Answers depend on the partner's report. A good check ticks only the lines that are really met, and names one specific fix, for example "Photo 4 has no time" or "Step 2 starts with 'Somebody should'. Start it with a verb."

## Check yourself

1. The wide shot shows the reader where you are, so the close-up makes sense (level 1).
2. What is in the photo, where it is, which way you are facing, and when it was taken (level 1).
3. To: the people who must act on the report. CC: the people who only need to know (level 2).
4. You only know what to say in the summary after you have written up every area. It goes near the top because many readers read only the summary (level 2).
5. Sample: "1. Put down floor protection before anyone starts work." (level 2)
