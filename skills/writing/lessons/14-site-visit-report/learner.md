# Write a site visit report with photos

## Why this matters on the job

Before a crew starts on a job, someone walks the site to see if it is ready. After a leak, a delivery problem or a bad inspection, someone goes to look. That person writes a site visit report. It records what was there on that day, tells people who were not there what they need to know, and gives the boss what they need to decide: can we start, what needs fixing first, and who fixes it. Photos do a lot of the work, but only if each one has a caption. Lead hands, estimators and foremen write these reports, and a clear one can save a wasted trip for a whole crew.

## Key ideas

### 1. Plan the visit before you go

Answer these on a notepad before you leave:

| Question | Example |
|---|---|
| What question does the visit answer? | Are the walls in Unit 104 ready to paint? |
| Who will read the report? | The GC's project manager and our office |
| Which areas will I look at? | Reception and hallway, meeting room, washroom |
| What do I bring? | Phone or camera, tape measure, notepad, pen, hard hat, safety boots, vest |
| Who do I meet? | Sign in with the site contact when you arrive. |

Ask the site contact before you take photos. Keep people's faces, licence plates and private papers out of your shots.

### 2. Take photos that show what matters

- **Wide shot first**, so the reader knows where you are. **Then a close-up** of the problem.
- Put a tape measure or another object of known size in the close-up to show scale.
- One problem per photo.
- Take the photo straight on, in good light. Turn on a work light if you need it.
- Number the photos in the order you take them, and note the area for each one in your notepad.

### 3. Caption every photo

A caption says **what, where, which way you are facing, and when**.

- Weak: "Wall."
- Clear: "Photo 3. Drywall patch at the third receptacle from the north end, meeting room east wall, looking east, 2026-11-24, 8:50 am. Tape shows 150 mm."

### 4. The report skeleton

1. **Cover**: project name and address, date of visit, prepared by, prepared for.
2. **Distribution**: **To** the people who must act. **CC** the people who need to know.
3. **Summary**: three or four sentences. Why you went, the overall condition, the main problems, and whether work can start.
4. **Visit details**: date and time, weather, who was there, how you got in.
5. **One section per area**: the photos with captions, **What I saw** (facts and measurements), and **Steps** (a numbered list).
6. **Actions**: a table of Action, Who, By when.

Many readers read only the summary. Write it last, after you know what you found, and put it near the top.

### 5. Write steps as commands

Start each step with a verb, so the reader knows exactly what to do.

| Weak | Clear |
|---|---|
| The patches should probably be sanded by somebody. | 1. Sand the four patches smooth. |
| Dust needs dealing with. | 2. Vacuum the dust from the floor and the receptacle boxes. |

### 6. Facts, measured where you can

Write what you saw and measured. If you are not sure of a cause, say so: "Possible cause: a door handle hit the corner. Not confirmed."

### Worked example

**Lakeview Painting** (a fictional company) has been hired to paint the office fit-out at Unit 104, Brightwater Plaza. Its lead hand, **Nadia Fox**, visits on Tuesday, November 24, to see if the painters can start Thursday. Here are parts of her report.

> **Cover.** Site visit report, Unit 104 office fit-out, Brightwater Plaza. Visit date: 2026-11-24. Prepared by: Nadia Fox, Lead Hand, Lakeview Painting. Prepared for: Leo Brandt, Project Manager, general contractor.
>
> **Distribution.** To: Leo Brandt (GC project manager), Kofi Mensah (drywall foreman). CC: Lakeview Painting office.
>
> **Summary.** I visited Unit 104 on November 24 to check whether the walls are ready to paint. The reception area and hallway are ready. The meeting room east wall has four drywall patches that are not sanded, and the washroom has a dented corner bead. Painting can start in reception and the hallway on Thursday, November 26. The meeting room and the washroom can start when the drywall fixes are done.
>
> **Visit details.** 2026-11-24, 8:30 to 9:30 am. Dry, cold. Present: Nadia Fox, Leo Brandt. Signed in at the GC site office. Leo let us in with the front door key.
>
> **Area 1: Reception and hallway**
> Photo 1. Reception, looking north from the front door, 2026-11-24, 8:35 am.
> What I saw: All walls are taped and sanded. No ridges or marks show under a work light. The floor is covered.
> Steps:
> 1. Cover the door frames and the front window.
> 2. Wipe the walls with a damp cloth to remove dust.
> 3. Prime all walls.
> 4. Check the primed walls under a work light and mark any flaws.
> 5. Apply two finish coats.
> 6. Remove the covers and clean up.
> 7. Record the paint used against the estimate.
>
> **Area 2: Meeting room east wall**
> Photo 2. Meeting room east wall, looking east, 2026-11-24, 8:48 am.
> Photo 3. Patch at the third receptacle from the north end, looking east, 2026-11-24, 8:50 am. Tape shows 150 mm.
> What I saw: Four drywall patches around new receptacles, each about 150 mm × 150 mm. The mud is on but not sanded. The edges show as ridges under a work light.
> Steps:
> 1. Sand the four patches smooth.
> 2. Apply a thin finish coat where the edges still show.
> 3. Sand again when dry.
> 4. Vacuum the dust from the floor and the receptacle boxes.
> 5. Tell Lakeview Painting that the wall is ready.
>
> **Actions**
>
> | Action | Who | By when |
> |---|---|---|
> | Finish and sand the four patches, meeting room east wall | Kofi Mensah | Fri Nov 27 |
> | Fix the dented corner bead, washroom | Kofi Mensah | Fri Nov 27 |
> | Start painting reception and hallway | Nadia Fox | Thu Nov 26 |

(Area 3, the washroom, follows the same pattern.)

## Check yourself

1. Why do you take a wide shot before a close-up?
2. What four things does a good caption say?
3. Who goes on the To line, and who goes on the CC line?
4. Why do you write the summary last but put it near the top?
5. Rewrite as a step that starts with a verb: "The floor protection should be put down before anybody starts."
