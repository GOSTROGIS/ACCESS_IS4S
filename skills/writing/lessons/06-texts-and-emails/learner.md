# Text and email a foreman or client

## Why this matters on the job

Much of the writing you do in the trades is on your phone. You text your foreman that a delivery is short. You email a homeowner that the install date has moved. You confirm a change the super asked for on the phone. These messages are read fast, often on a small screen, and sometimes forwarded to people you never meant to see them. A clear, polite message gets the answer you need and keeps a record if there is a disagreement later.

## Key ideas

### 1. Pick the right channel

| Use | When |
|---|---|
| A text | Short, quick, today: "Delivery is here", "Running 10 min late". |
| An email | More detail, attachments, clients, anything you may need a record of later. |
| A phone call, then a written note | Something urgent or complicated. After the call, send a short text or email saying what was agreed. |

### 2. Lead with the point

Put what you need, and by when, in the first line. The reader may only see the first line in a notification.

- Weak: "Hey so I got to the site and was looking around and the guys from yesterday..."
- Clear: "Need 6 more 2x6x10 by noon. Only 14 of 20 came on today's load."

### 3. Give the details the reader needs to act

Who, what, where, when, how many. Name the job or address. Keep one topic per message, so replies don't get mixed up.

### 4. Write a subject line that says what the email is about

- Weak: "Question", "Update", "Hi"
- Clear: "Job 26-118: drywall delivery moved to Thursday 2026-11-12"

### 5. Match the tone to the reader

| Reader | Tone |
|---|---|
| Your crew or foreman | Short is fine. Use the shortcuts your crew already uses. Stay respectful. |
| A client, inspector or office | Greeting, full sentences, no slang, no ALL CAPS, sign with your name and company. |

Write as if your message could be forwarded to your boss, the client and a lawyer. If you are angry, wait before you send.

### 6. Photos need words

A photo of a cracked fitting means nothing without a caption: "Cracked 1/2" tee, kitchen ceiling, Unit 4. Photo taken 9:20 am."

### 7. Confirm spoken agreements in writing

If someone tells you on the phone or on site to change the work, send a short note: "As we discussed at 2:15 pm today, ... Please reply to confirm." Now there is a record.

### 8. Never text while you drive

In BC, the Motor Vehicle Act, section 214.2, says a person must not use an electronic device while driving, and must not send text-based messages or email from one. The penalty is a $368 fine and 4 penalty points. Pull over and park safely before you read or write.

### Worked example

A vague text to the foreman:

> hey no pipe

A clear text:

> Hi Marcus, the 3/4" PEX for Unit 4 isn't in the cage. Need it to start rough-in. Can you check with the supplier? I'm on hose bibs at Unit 2 until you let me know.

An email to a client from **Cedar Ridge Mechanical** (a fictional company):

> **Subject:** Your furnace install at 2214 Birch Lane moved to Tuesday, November 17
>
> Hello Ms. Thomas,
>
> The new furnace for your home will be installed on **Tuesday, November 17**, instead of Thursday, November 12. The supplier has told us the furnace will not arrive until November 16.
>
> Our installers will arrive between 8:00 and 8:30 am and expect to finish by 4:00 pm. The heat will be off during that time.
>
> Could you please reply by Friday to confirm that someone will be home to let us in?
>
> Thank you,
> Jordan Paul
> Service Coordinator, Cedar Ridge Mechanical
