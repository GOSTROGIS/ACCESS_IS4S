# Text and email a foreman or client

## Task 1 (level 1)

Samples. Any added details are fine if the learner says what they added.

a) "Hi Marcus, I can't make it in today. I have a fever. I'll text you tomorrow by 6:30 am to say if I'm back." (Added: who, the reason, when you will update.)

b) "The chop saw at the Unit 4 cut station won't start again. I've tagged it. Can we get the spare from the shop?" (Added: which tool, where, what you did, what you need.)

c) "Can you call me before 10 am? I need to know which wall the panel goes on in Unit 6." (Added: when and why.)

Steps: ask "Could the reader act on this without calling me?" If not, add what, where, when and what you need.

## Task 2 (level 1 to 2)

Sample (about 40 words):

> Hi Priya, the boom lift at the Surrey warehouse (job 26-120) has a dead battery and won't start. I can't start the high work. Should I charge it, or is there another lift? I'll do floor prep until I hear back.

A good answer names the site or job, the machine, the problem, the effect on the work, asks a clear question, and says what you will do in the meantime.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Sample:

> **Subject:** Your water heater replacement at 77 Cedar Court moved to Thursday, November 12
>
> Hello Mr. Arcand,
>
> We need to move your water heater replacement from Monday, November 9, to **Thursday, November 12**. Our crew lead is sick, and November 11 is a statutory holiday.
>
> The crew will arrive between 9:00 and 9:30 am. The job takes about 4 hours. You will have no hot water from about 9:30 am to 1:30 pm.
>
> Please reply to confirm the new date, or call our office if it doesn't work for you.
>
> Thank you,
> Sam Ortiz
> Cedar Ridge Mechanical

A good answer has: a subject line with the address and new date, a greeting, the change in the first sentence, the reason in plain words without private details about the sick worker, arrival time, length of job, the no-hot-water window, a clear request, and a signature with name and company. The date is Thursday, November 12, not November 11.

## Task 4 (level 3)

Sample:

> **Subject:** Willow Creek Unit 6: confirming 2 extra hose bibs, north wall
>
> Hi Dan,
>
> As we discussed on the phone at 2:15 pm today, you asked me to add two extra hose bibs on the north wall of Unit 6, at the same height as the existing hose bibs.
>
> Please reply to confirm this is correct before I order the parts.
>
> Thanks,
> Sam Ortiz

A good answer states when the request was made, by whom, what was asked, where and at what height, and asks for a reply. It does not add details Dan did not give, such as the exact spacing.

## Task 5 (level 2)

Problems (any three):

- ALL CAPS in the subject and body reads as shouting.
- The subject line does not say what the email is about.
- No greeting, no order number, no signature.
- It does not say what fix is needed, or by when.
- "you guys" and "FIX IT" are rude, which can slow down the fix.

Sample rewrite:

> **Subject:** Order 5520: received 3/4" fittings, need 1/2" by tomorrow
>
> Hello,
>
> Today's delivery for order 5520 had 3/4" fittings, but we ordered 1/2". This is the third time this has happened on this order. My crew can't continue the rough-in until the right fittings arrive.
>
> Could you send the 1/2" fittings by 10:00 am tomorrow and arrange pickup of the 3/4" ones? Please reply to confirm.
>
> Thank you,
> Sam Ortiz, Cedar Ridge Mechanical

(The order number is an example. Use the real one.)

## Check yourself

1. The reader may only see the first line in a notification, and needs to know what to do straight away (level 1).
2. Any specific subject, for example "Job 26-118: drywall delivery moved to Thursday, November 12" (level 1).
3. Any two of: a subject line, a greeting, full sentences without slang, a sign-off with your name and company (level 2).
4. Send a short text or email saying what was agreed, when, and with whom, and ask them to reply to confirm (level 2).
5. A person must not use an electronic device while driving, and must not send texts or email from one. The penalty is a $368 fine and 4 penalty points (level 1).
