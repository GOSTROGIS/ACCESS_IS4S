Writing · Lesson 03 · Instructor

Write steps and conditions a crew can follow

Turn a task into numbered steps, put each condition before the action it controls, and use words like if, unless, and, or and all so they mean exactly what you intend.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Session length: 90 min. All times below are part of the 90 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Ask: "How did you qualify for this program? What did you have to have or do?" List the answers on the board as learners give them. Then ask: "Were all of these must-haves, or could you meet some of them in more than one way? What if you were missing just one?"

Point out that learners have just sorted conditions, options and must-haves. That is today's skill, in writing.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • plan a procedure by listing actions and the conditions that control them (Plan the writing task)
  • use if, unless, and, or, only, except and all with their exact meaning (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • choose a numbered list, bullets and a title to organize steps (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)
  • test steps with a reader and revise them (Review and revise your writing)

Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.3)

  1. "A sign says 'Boots and hard hat required.' Do you need one or both?"
  2. "Is this true: 'All vehicles have four wheels'? How do you know?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Run the opening brainstorm on program entry conditions. Call out conditions and options. Board 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Listen, ask questions. Board 2 min
1.3 Ask the pre-assessment questions. Answer aloud. none 5 min
2.1 Teach "one action per step" and "condition before action". Read the rough Cedar Ridge paragraph aloud, then build the numbered version on the board with learners. Suggest where to split steps and where each condition belongs. learner.md, board 12 min
2.2 Teach the condition-word table. Stress that "and" means all, "or" means any one. Give two quick oral examples from learners' own trades. Give an example sentence for "unless" and "except". learner.md 8 min
2.3 Assign Task 1. Complete Task 1 in pairs. learner.md 8 min
2.4 Assign Task 2. Take one answer for each sign. Rewrite the signs as if-then sentences. learner.md 7 min
2.5 Teach "all" and counter-examples. Use a folding ruler or tape to sketch a square, a rectangle and a parallelogram on the board. Ask which statements about them are true. Assign Task 3. Complete Task 3, give counter-examples. Board, learner.md 10 min
3.1 Assign Task 4. Circulate. Check that each step starts with a verb and that conditions come first. Write the end-of-shift procedure. learner.md 15 min
3.2 Run Task 5. Partners act out each other's steps exactly as written. Test, note questions, revise. learner.md 10 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer in writing. none 5 min
4.2 Give the summary. Listen, ask final questions. none 3 min

Total: 5 + 2 + 5 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 5 + 3 = 90 min.

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. Rewrite as numbered steps with the condition first: "Clean the brush and put the lid on the can, and if the can is nearly empty write it on the order board."
  2. "Bring your BC Services Card or your passport." How many of these do you need to bring?
  3. Give a counter-example to "All saws cut wood."

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • one action per step, starting with a verb
  • conditions placed before the action they control
  • the exact meaning of if, unless, and, or, only and except
  • reading short signs as full conditions
  • "all" statements and counter-examples
  • testing steps with a real reader
