Write steps and conditions a crew can follow

Turn a task into numbered steps, put each condition before the action it controls, and use words like if, unless, and, or and all so they mean exactly what you intend.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Why this matters on the job

Shops and sites run on written steps: how to sign out a tool, how to close up at the end of a shift, what to do if a machine makes a strange noise. When steps are out of order, or a condition is hidden at the end of a sentence, people do the action first and read the warning second. Clear steps let a new apprentice do the job right the first time.

Key ideas

1. One action per step, in order, starting with the action word

  • Weak: "The grinder should be unplugged and checked and the wheel looked at for cracks."
  • Clear: 1. Unplug the grinder. 2. Check the wheel for cracks. 3. Check that the guard is in place.

Start each step with a verb (unplug, check, sign, return). Number the steps when the order matters. Use bullets when it does not.

2. Put the condition before the action it controls

People act on the first words they read.

  • Risky: "Give the tool to the crib attendant if it is damaged."
  • Better: "If the tool is damaged, give it to the crib attendant."

3. Condition words have exact meanings

Word Meaning Example
if ... then when the first part is true, do the second If the cord is cut, then tag the tool out.
unless do this, except when the condition is true Lock the gate unless the foreman is still on site.
and every part must be true You need boots and a hard hat. (Both.)
or any one part is enough Bring a pay stub or a letter from your employer. (Either one.)
only this and nothing else Crib staff only.
except everything, leaving one out Return all tools except your own hand tools.

"And" is all-or-nothing. If a job posting asks for three things joined by "and", you need all three. If it says "or", one is enough.

4. Short signs are conditions too

A sign packs a whole sentence into a few words.

  • "Hard hats required beyond this point" means "If you go past this point, wear a hard hat."
  • "Authorized personnel only" means "If you are not authorized, do not enter."

5. "All" means every single one

A statement with "all" is false if you can find even one case that does not fit. That case is called a counter-example.

  • "All the pipe in this building is copper." One PEX line proves it false.
  • If you are not sure every case fits, write "most", "some" or the exact number.

Worked example

A lead hand at Cedar Ridge Mechanical (a fictional company) wrote this for new hires:

Tools come from the crib and you sign the sheet with your name and the tool number and the time, and if it's broken when you bring it back tell the attendant, and everything has to be back by 3:15 unless you're on overtime, then leave it in the overnight bin.

Rewritten as steps:

Signing tools out and in

  1. Take the tool from the crib.
  2. Write your name, the tool number and the time on the sign-out sheet.
  3. Return the tool by 3:15 pm. - If you are on overtime, put the tool in the overnight bin instead.
  4. If the tool is damaged when you return it, tell the crib attendant.

What changed: one action per step, each step starts with a verb, the two conditions ("on overtime", "damaged") are placed where the reader will need them, and "everything" became "the tool", because the steps are about one tool at a time.

Practice

Task 1 (level 2) Read this fictional job notice. Then answer the questions.

Cedar Ridge Mechanical: Shop helper (fictional posting) To apply, you must be 18 or older and live in Surrey or Langley. You must have a valid BC driver's licence or your own way to get to our Surrey and Langley shops. You need steel-toed boots. If you have not taken a fall protection course, we will provide one in your first week.

a) List every condition an applicant must meet.

b) Which requirement can be met in two different ways? What are the two ways?

c) An applicant is 19, lives in Langley, has no driver's licence, takes the bus to both shops and owns steel-toed boots. She has never taken a fall protection course. Does she qualify? Explain.

Task 2 (level 2) Rewrite each sign as a full "If ... then" sentence.

a) No hot work without a permit. b) Ear protection required when the planer is running. c) Visitors must sign in at the office. d) Do not enter. Confined space.

Task 3 (level 2) Mark each statement True or False. For each false statement, give one counter-example.

a) All fasteners are made of steel. ___ b) Some fittings have threads. ___ c) All squares are rectangles. ___ d) All rectangles are squares. ___ e) All apprentices are under 25 years old. ___

Task 4 (level 3) Use these notes from the shop foreman at Fraser Bend Fabrication (fictional) to write a numbered end-of-shift procedure. Give it a title. Put each condition before its action.

Notes: sweep your bay. Put offcuts in the scrap bin, but aluminum goes in the blue bin, not the steel bin. Shut off the welder and close the gas cylinder. If you used the plasma table, empty the dross tray if it's more than half full. Coil the extension cords and hang them. Last person out turns off the compressor and locks the side door. Write anything broken in the shop log.

Task 5 (level 3) Give your Task 4 procedure to a partner. Your partner reads it aloud and acts out each step exactly as written, without guessing. Note every place where they had to stop and ask. Revise your procedure so those questions do not come up.

Check yourself

  1. Rewrite with the condition first: "Shut down the pump if the pressure drops below the red line."
  2. A sign says "Gloves and safety glasses required." Do you need one or both?
  3. What is a counter-example?
  4. Why should each step begin with an action word?
  5. When should you use a numbered list instead of bullets?
