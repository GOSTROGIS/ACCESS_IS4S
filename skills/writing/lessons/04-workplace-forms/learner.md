# Fill in a workplace form correctly

## Why this matters on the job

Timesheets, tool sign-outs, material requests and report forms decide whether you get paid right, whether the right parts show up, and whether a problem gets fixed. A form is a piece of writing with the structure already built for you. Your job is to put the right fact in the right box, clearly, so the person who reads it does not have to come and find you.

## Key ideas

**1. Read the whole form before you write anything.** Look for:

- who fills in each part. Boxes marked "Supervisor" or "Office use only" are not for you.
- the format each box wants. WorkSafeBC forms, for example, print **(yyyy-mm-dd)** beside date boxes.
- where your signature goes, and what you are signing for.

**2. Fill every box that applies to you.** If a box does not apply, write **N/A** (not applicable) so the reader knows you did not skip it.

**3. Print so a stranger can read it.** If you write by hand, use pen and print clearly. If the form asks for capital letters, use them.

**4. Numbers need units, and the maths must check.** Write "38.5 h", "3 coils", "20 m". Then check every total.

**5. Calculate hours with BEDMAS.** Hours worked = (finish time − start time) − unpaid break. Do the bracket first.

> Finish 5:00 pm, start 7:00 am, 30 min unpaid lunch.
> Change 5:00 pm to 17:00 so you can subtract.
> (17.0 − 7.0) − 0.5 = 10.0 − 0.5 = **9.5 h**

Common mistake: doing the break first. 17.0 − (7.0 − 0.5) = 10.5 h is wrong. BEDMAS says brackets first, so write the bracket where it belongs.

**6. Report facts, not accusations.** Follow your workplace's reporting rule. The fictional company in this lesson has this one: report a problem to your supervisor, do not confront anyone yourself, and let the supervisor investigate. On a report form, write what you saw. Leave out who you think did it unless you saw it happen.

**7. Check, then sign.** Your signature says the form is true and complete. Never sign a blank form or one you have not read. Keep a copy or a photo if your workplace allows it.

### Worked example

**Cedar Ridge Mechanical** (a fictional company) uses this timesheet. Here is Monday for an apprentice:

| Day | Date (yyyy-mm-dd) | Job # | Start | Finish | Unpaid break | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 2026-11-02 | 26-118 | 7:00 am | 3:30 pm | 0.5 h | 8.0 |

Working: 3:30 pm is 15:30, or 15.5 in decimal hours. (15.5 − 7.0) − 0.5 = 8.5 − 0.5 = 8.0 h.
