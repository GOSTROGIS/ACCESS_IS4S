Say no and ask for something in writing

Turn down a request in writing without losing the relationship, and write a formal request letter that answers the reader's worries and makes it easy for them to say yes.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Task 1 (level 1)

3 "We can't take on the extra unit this week." 1 "Thanks for asking us about Unit 6." 5 "Hope the move-in goes well, and thanks again." 4 "We could do it on Monday, November 30, as a separate job." 2 "Our crew is booked on Units 2 to 4 until Friday."

Steps: thanks, reason, the no, the alternative, the friendly close.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) Any three:

  • It never says no. "I'll see if we can squeeze it in" sounds like yes.
  • It over-explains: busy, tired, the office, the GC, the mud. Each excuse invites an argument.
  • "The guys are tired" and "slammed" are too casual for a tenant and may be repeated to the building manager.
  • It offers no real alternative and no thanks.

b) Sample:

Hi, thanks for asking about the holes in your unit. Our crew is only booked for the hallways in this building, so we can't patch your unit as part of this job. If you'd like a price to have it done, I can give you our office number to arrange a price. Thanks, and sorry for the noise this week.

A good answer has thanks, a short true reason, a clear no, the offer of a price through the office, and a friendly close. It does not blame the tenant or complain about the crew.

Task 3 (level 2 to 3)

Sample:

Subject: PO for 40 lengths of 2 in square tube: no substitute, please

Hi Marco,

Thanks for letting us know about the delay.

We can't take the 1-1/2 in square tube. The shop drawings call for 2 in, and we can't change the size without our customer's approval.

Can you send any 2 in square tube you have in stock now, or check whether another branch can ship it sooner? Even part of the order this week would let us start the first frames.

Please let me know by tomorrow at noon what you can do.

Thanks, (learner's name) Fraser Bend Fabrication

A good answer thanks him, gives one short reason, says no to the substitute clearly, asks for 2 in tube sooner, and gives a time for his answer. It does not accept the smaller size "for now".

Task 4 (level 3)

Sample:

Question Answer
Who is the reader, and what is their job? Ruth Adeyemi, Operations Manager. She runs the crews and the schedule.
What do they care about? Keeping crews productive, material arriving on time, the cost of short orders
What will they likely object to? Losing 10 hours of an apprentice's time on the tools across December and January
What is in it for them? An apprentice who can help check takeoffs, so orders come in short less often

Task 5 (level 3)

Sample:

(Learner's name) Apprentice, Coastal Spark Electric

2026-11-25

Ruth Adeyemi Operations Manager, Coastal Spark Electric

Re: Request to learn material takeoffs with Paul Singh, four Wednesday afternoons

Dear Ms. Adeyemi,

I am asking to spend four Wednesday afternoons, from 1:00 to 3:30 pm, with Paul Singh to learn how to do material takeoffs. The dates would be December 2, 9 and 16, 2026, and January 6, 2027.

This fall I saw two jobs lose time when material orders came in short. I want to understand how a takeoff is done so I can catch a short count before the order goes in. Paul has agreed to teach me if you approve.

The four afternoons add up to 10 hours away from the tools. On those afternoons my crew is doing trim-out at one site, and Mina Cho has said she can cover the work with the rest of the crew.

Could you let me know by Friday, November 27, so Paul can plan the first afternoon? I am glad to talk about it any time.

Sincerely,

(signature)

(Learner's name) Apprentice

A good answer:

  • uses block style with the date, the reader's name and title, a Re: line, a greeting, a closing and a typed name
  • puts the request and the dates in the first paragraph
  • gives the reason with a fact (two jobs with short orders)
  • answers the objection with the 10 hours and Mina's cover
  • asks for a decision by Friday, November 27
  • stays polite and confident, with no begging and no pressure

Check yourself

  1. Thanks or acknowledgement, a short reason, a clear no, an alternative, a friendly close (level 1).
  2. The reader may hear it as yes or maybe and keep waiting (level 2).
  3. In the first paragraph, in the first sentence or two (level 1).
  4. If you answer it first, the reader does not have to come back with a no, and you show you have thought about their side (level 2).
  5. Date, reader's name and title, Re: line, greeting, closing (level 1).
