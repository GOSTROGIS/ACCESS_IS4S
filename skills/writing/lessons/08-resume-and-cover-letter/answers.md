# Write a trades résumé and cover letter

## Task 1 (level 2)

- Contact information: f
- Summary: c
- Skills: e
- Work experience: d, with g as an achievement line under it
- Education and training: a
- References: b

Steps: ask what each line describes. A place and dates for a job is work experience. A thing you did well in that job goes under it. Tools you can use are skills.

## Task 2 (level 2)

Answers vary. A good answer names a real skill an employer can use and backs it with a fact. Example:

| Activity | Skill | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Fixing my own truck | Troubleshooting | Found and replaced a failed alternator myself |
| Fixing my own truck | Tool use | Own and use a socket set, torque wrench and multimeter |
| Coaching youth soccer | Reliability | Ran 2 practices a week for 3 seasons without missing one |
| Coaching youth soccer | Communication | Explained drills to 14 players aged 10 to 12 |

## Task 3 (level 2)

Samples use fictional practice numbers; they are not facts supplied by the duties.

a) "Ran the tool crib for a 20-person crew, signing out and checking in tools every shift."
b) "Loaded 4 to 6 delivery trucks a day, checking each load against the order."
c) "Completed 1 pre-use safety check on the forklift at the start of every shift."

A good line starts with a past-tense action verb, includes a number, and has no "I". Numbers can be estimates if they are honest.

## Task 4 (level 3)

No single answer. Suggested formats:

- **Sky Morin:** combined. Short work history, but strong skills (tool use, small engine repair, physical stamina, first aid).
- **Lou Antoine:** functional or combined. He is changing careers, so leading with transferable skills (training staff, leading a team, working to deadlines, building a deck) works better than a list of kitchen jobs.
- **Marnie Paul:** chronological or combined. One steady job that already matches the posting (forklift, hardware, reliability).

A good résumé for posting FBF-26-14:

- fits on one page and has contact information
- has a summary that names the shop helper job
- matches the posting requirements to evidence the character actually has; identifies missing information rather than inventing qualifications (forklift experience is an asset, not a requirement)
- uses achievement lines with numbers and action verbs, without "I"
- lists education and certificates with dates
- has no SIN
- has no spelling errors

## Task 5 (level 3)

Sample for Marnie Paul (about 170 words):

> Hiring Manager, Fraser Bend Fabrication
>
> Dear Hiring Manager,
>
> I am applying for the shop helper position, posting FBF-26-14, which I saw on your company's job posting.
>
> Your posting asks for reliable attendance, basic tool skills and forklift experience. For two years I have worked part time at a hardware store, and I have not missed a scheduled shift. After training at the store, I drove a forklift in the yard to load customer orders. In high school metalwork I used a band saw, a drill press and grinders, and I can read a tape measure accurately. I own steel-toed boots.
>
> I am available to start on December 1 and can work Monday to Friday, 7:00 am to 3:30 pm. You can reach me at 604-555-0119 or marnie.paul@example.com. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Sincerely,
> Marnie Paul

Marnie and her contact details are fictional. The sample also adds illustrative details not supplied in her profile: specific metalwork tools, tape-measure ability, boots and availability. Label these additions in class; confirm such details before using them in a real application.

A good letter follows the three-part plan, names the job and posting number, matches two or three requirements with proof, gives the start date and contact information, uses 150 to 250 words, and uses "I" naturally (a cover letter, unlike a résumé, is written in the first person).

## Task 6 (level 2)

No single answer. A good check marks each item on the list as present or missing, and points to at least one specific fix.

## Check yourself

1. Any four of: contact information, summary or objective, skills, tickets and certificates, work experience, education and training, references (level 1).
2. Functional (level 1).
3. Any two of: keep it clear and short, quantify achievements, use simple words and action verbs, limit sections to 5 to 7 bullets, show achievements not just duties (level 1).
4. No. Service Canada says you do not need to give your SIN to fill out a job application. An employer needs it only after you are hired and start earning income (level 2).
5. The job you are applying for, the posting number, and where you saw it (level 1).
