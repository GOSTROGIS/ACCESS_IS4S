Write a trades résumé and cover letter

Build a one-page résumé that shows your skills to a trades employer, choose a format that fits your experience, and write a short cover letter tailored to one job posting.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 120 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Why this matters on the job

Before a shop or contractor meets you, they meet your résumé. Job Bank, the Government of Canada's job site, says an employer takes an average of 30 seconds to skim a résumé. Many people have the skills a job needs but do not get called, because the résumé does not show those skills clearly, or does not show them at all. A short, tailored résumé and cover letter get you to the interview.

Key ideas

1. The parts of a résumé

Part What goes in it
Contact information Name, phone, email, city
Summary or objective Two or three lines: the job you want and what you bring
Skills Tools, equipment and abilities the job asks for
Tickets and certificates Safety tickets, licences, course certificates, with dates
Work experience Job title, employer, city, dates, then achievement lines
Education and training Programs, schools, courses, with dates
References People who can speak about your work, with their permission. You can write "Available on request" and bring a list to the interview.

Leave out your Social Insurance Number. Service Canada says you do not have to give your SIN to fill out a job application. An employer needs it only after you are hired and start earning income.

2. Choose a format

Three common formats:

  • Chronological: jobs listed newest first. Good when you have steady work history in the same field. The Government of Manitoba's job tips say a chronological résumé may work best when you are answering an advertised position.
  • Functional: grouped by skills (for example "Tools and equipment", "Safety", "Teamwork") with a short job list at the end. Useful when you are changing careers or have gaps in your work history.
  • Combined: a skills section first, then your jobs newest first.

3. Job Bank's do's and don'ts

From Job Bank's "How to write a good resume":

  • Keep it clear and short.
  • Quantify your achievements. Use real numbers.
  • Use simple words and action verbs.
  • Limit each section to 5 to 7 bullet points.
  • Don't use "I", "my" or "me".
  • Don't just list duties. Show what you did well, with examples.
  • Leave hobbies out unless they relate to the job.
Weak (duty) Strong (achievement)
Responsible for cleaning the kitchen. Cleaned and sanitized a 40-seat kitchen to health inspection standard every closing shift.
I helped with deliveries. Unloaded and checked 3 to 5 supplier deliveries a day against packing slips.

4. Find your hidden skills

Skills from outside paid work count. Fixing your own truck uses troubleshooting and tool skills. Coaching a youth team uses planning, reliability and communication. Helping run a community event uses organizing, timekeeping and teamwork. Write the skill, then one fact that proves it.

5. The cover letter

Job Bank says a cover letter is your introduction to an employer. It should be short, well written and tailored to the company and the job. You may need a different letter for each job. The Government of Manitoba's tips add: name the position and the posting number, and give an email and phone number where you can be reached.

A simple plan, about 150 to 250 words:

  1. Opening: the job you are applying for, the posting number, and where you saw it.
  2. Middle: two or three things the posting asks for, and proof that you have each one.
  3. Closing: when you can start, how to reach you, and thanks.

Some employers invite applicants to self-identify, for example as part of an employment equity program. Whether you do is your choice.

6. Review before you send

Read it aloud. Check every date, phone number and spelling. Ask someone else to read it. A résumé with typing errors can lose you the interview before anyone reads your skills.

Worked example

Two versions of the same person's résumé, for a fictional shop helper job.

Version 1:

Rowan Pierre I am a hard worker looking for any job. Work: Kitchen at Riverside Diner 2022-2026. I did dishes and cleaning and prep. School: high school

Version 2:

Rowan Pierre Langley, BC | 604-555-0182 | rowan.pierre@example.com

Summary Reliable, safety-minded worker with 4 years in a fast-paced kitchen, now training for the metal fabrication trade.

Skills - Measure and cut to 1/16" with a tape measure and chop saw (trades prep program) - Safe use of grinders, drills and hand tools - Follow written procedures and checklists

Work experience Prep Cook, Riverside Diner, Langley, 2022 to 2026 - Prepped food for a 40-seat restaurant on 5 to 6 shifts a week - Trained 3 new dishwashers on safe chemical use - Did not miss a scheduled shift in 2025

Education and training Trades preparation program, 2026 Grade 12, 2021

References Available on request

Rowan, the diner and the contact details are fictional.

Practice

Use this fictional job posting for Tasks 4 and 5.

Fraser Bend Fabrication, Langley: Shop helper (fictional posting FBF-26-14) We are a small structural steel shop. We need a shop helper to move and prepare material, run the band saw and drill press, grind and clean parts, and keep the shop tidy. You have: reliable attendance, the ability to read a tape measure, basic hand and power tool skills, and steel-toed boots. Forklift experience is an asset. Monday to Friday, 7:00 am to 3:30 pm. Start date: December 1.

Task 1 (level 2) Put each line under the right résumé heading: Contact information, Summary, Skills, Work experience, Education and training, or References.

a) Grade 12, graduated 2023 b) Available on request c) Hardworking apprentice-ready helper with framing and concrete experience d) Labourer, Salmonberry Framing, Surrey, 2024 to 2026 e) Use of circular saw, nail gun and laser level f) Abbotsford, BC, phone and email g) Stripped and cleaned forms for 12 townhouse foundations

Task 2 (level 2) List three things you do outside paid work. You may use a made-up person instead of yourself. For each, write two skills it uses and one fact that proves each skill.

______ ______ ______

Task 3 (level 2) Rewrite each duty as an achievement line. Start with an action verb, add a number, and do not use "I".

a) I was in charge of the tool crib. b) Responsible for loading trucks. c) I did safety checks on the forklift.

Task 4 (level 3) In a group of two or three, choose one character. Choose a résumé format and say why. Write a one-page résumé for the character for posting FBF-26-14. Present it to the class.

  • Sky Morin, 24. Finished a trades preparation program in 2026. Two summers at a tree-planting camp (planted about 2,000 trees a day by the second summer). Fixes small engines for family and neighbours. Has a first aid certificate.
  • Lou Antoine, 41. Twelve years as a line cook, the last four as kitchen lead, trained new staff. Built his own backyard deck. Coached a youth soccer team for three seasons. Wants to change careers into the trades.
  • Marnie Paul, 19. Grade 12, 2025. Took metalwork in high school. Two years part time at a hardware store, where she drove a forklift in the yard after training. Has never missed a shift.

Task 5 (level 3) Write a cover letter of 150 to 250 words for posting FBF-26-14, for your Task 4 character or for yourself. Follow the three-part plan. Address it to "Hiring Manager, Fraser Bend Fabrication".

Task 6 (level 2) Swap your Task 5 letter with another group. Check it against this list and mark anything missing:

  • the posting number and job title
  • two or three requirements matched with proof
  • start date and contact information
  • no spelling errors, no "I am a hard worker" without proof
  • under 250 words
