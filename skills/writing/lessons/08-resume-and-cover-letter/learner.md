# Write a trades résumé and cover letter

## Why this matters on the job

Before a shop or contractor meets you, they meet your résumé. Job Bank, the Government of Canada's job site, says an employer takes an average of 30 seconds to skim a résumé. Many people have the skills a job needs but do not get called, because the résumé does not show those skills clearly, or does not show them at all. A short, tailored résumé and cover letter get you to the interview.

## Key ideas

**Tool:** you can build your résumé and cover letter in the ACCESS [Résumé and cover letter builder](../../resume-builder/). It checks your résumé against a job posting and downloads a Word file or a PDF. What you type stays in your browser.

### 1. The parts of a résumé

| Part | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Contact information | Name, phone, email, city |
| Summary or objective | Two or three lines: the job you want and what you bring |
| Skills | Tools, equipment and abilities the job asks for |
| Tickets and certificates | Safety tickets, licences, course certificates, with dates |
| Work experience | Job title, employer, city, dates, then achievement lines |
| Education and training | Programs, schools, courses, with dates |
| References | People who can speak about your work, with their permission. You can write "Available on request" and bring a list to the interview. |

**Leave out your Social Insurance Number.** Service Canada says you do not have to give your SIN to fill out a job application. An employer needs it only after you are hired and start earning income.

### 2. Choose a format

Three common formats:

- **Chronological:** jobs listed newest first. Good when you have steady work history in the same field. The Government of Manitoba's job tips say a chronological résumé may work best when you are answering an advertised position.
- **Functional:** grouped by skills (for example "Tools and equipment", "Safety", "Teamwork") with a short job list at the end. Useful when you are changing careers or have gaps in your work history.
- **Combined:** a skills section first, then your jobs newest first.

### 3. Job Bank's do's and don'ts

From Job Bank's "How to write a good resume":

- Keep it clear and short.
- Quantify your achievements. Use real numbers.
- Use simple words and action verbs.
- Limit each section to 5 to 7 bullet points.
- Don't use "I", "my" or "me".
- Don't just list duties. Show what you did well, with examples.
- Leave hobbies out unless they relate to the job.

| Weak (duty) | Strong (achievement) |
|---|---|
| Responsible for cleaning the kitchen. | Cleaned and sanitized a 40-seat kitchen to health inspection standard every closing shift. |
| I helped with deliveries. | Unloaded and checked 3 to 5 supplier deliveries a day against packing slips. |

### 4. Find your hidden skills

Skills from outside paid work count. Fixing your own truck uses troubleshooting and tool skills. Coaching a youth team uses planning, reliability and communication. Helping run a community event uses organizing, timekeeping and teamwork. Write the skill, then one fact that proves it.

### 5. The cover letter

Job Bank says a cover letter is your introduction to an employer. It should be short, well written and tailored to the company and the job. You may need a different letter for each job. The Government of Manitoba's tips add: name the position and the posting number, and give an email and phone number where you can be reached.

A simple plan, about 150 to 250 words:

1. **Opening:** the job you are applying for, the posting number, and where you saw it.
2. **Middle:** two or three things the posting asks for, and proof that you have each one.
3. **Closing:** when you can start, how to reach you, and thanks.

Some employers invite applicants to self-identify, for example as part of an employment equity program. Whether you do is your choice.

### 6. Review before you send

Read it aloud. Check every date, phone number and spelling. Ask someone else to read it. A résumé with typing errors can lose you the interview before anyone reads your skills.

### Worked example

Two versions of the same person's résumé, for a fictional shop helper job.

Version 1:

> Rowan Pierre
> I am a hard worker looking for any job.
> Work: Kitchen at Riverside Diner 2022-2026. I did dishes and cleaning and prep.
> School: high school

Version 2:

> **Rowan Pierre**
> Langley, BC | 604-555-0182 | rowan.pierre@example.com
>
> **Summary**
> Reliable, safety-minded worker with 4 years in a fast-paced kitchen, now training for the metal fabrication trade.
>
> **Skills**
> - Measure and cut to 1/16" with a tape measure and chop saw (trades prep program)
> - Safe use of grinders, drills and hand tools
> - Follow written procedures and checklists
>
> **Work experience**
> Prep Cook, Riverside Diner, Langley, 2022 to 2026
> - Prepped food for a 40-seat restaurant on 5 to 6 shifts a week
> - Trained 3 new dishwashers on safe chemical use
> - Did not miss a scheduled shift in 2025
>
> **Education and training**
> Trades preparation program, 2026
> Grade 12, 2021
>
> **References**
> Available on request

Rowan, the diner and the contact details are fictional.

> We are a small structural steel shop. We need a shop helper to move and prepare material, run the band saw and drill press, grind and clean parts, and keep the shop tidy.
