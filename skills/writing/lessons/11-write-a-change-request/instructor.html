Write a change request before you build it

Turn a "can you just..." into a written change request that says what is asked, why, and what it does to the scope, days, cost, crew and safety, and get it signed before the work starts.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task

Session length: 75 min. All times below are part of the 75 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Before class

Print a blank change request for each learner with the parts from key idea 4 as boxes, and the approval line at the bottom. Learners use it for Task 3. Have calculators ready for Task 2.

This lesson builds on Text and email a foreman or client (confirming a spoken change) and on Communication lesson 12, Ask a good RFI question. If learners have not done those, spend one extra minute in step 2.1 on what an RFI is.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Tell this short story: "A framer is asked by the super to 'just move that wall a foot'. He does it. It takes two of them half a day. At the end of the month the framing company bills for it. The GC says, 'We never approved that.' Nobody can prove who asked." Ask: "Who pays for the half day? What could the framer have done?" Take answers.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell normal work, a question for an RFI and a change apart (Identify the task that requires you to write)
  • gather the facts, the cost and the days before writing (Plan the writing task)
  • describe a change so a stranger can price and build it, with the math shown (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • use a change request form and a change log (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)

Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

  1. "Has a client or boss ever asked you to do something extra on the spot? What happened about the money?"
  2. "What is 3 × 4 × $50 + $200?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Tell the moved-wall story and ask the opening questions. Say who pays and what could have been done. none 5 min
1.2 State the objectives. Listen, ask questions. Board 2 min
1.3 Ask the pre-assessment questions. Answer on paper. none 3 min
2.1 Teach key ideas 1 to 3: normal work, RFI or change, confirming versus approving, and who can sign. Assign Task 1. Sort the five items. learner.md 8 min
2.2 Teach key ideas 4 and 5. Walk through Jesse's change request box by box. Work the cost on the board with BEDMAS: multiply first, then add. Follow the worked example, check the math. learner.md, board 10 min
2.3 Assign Task 2. Work the three costs. learner.md, calculator 7 min
2.4 Assign Task 3 on the printed form. Ask first: "Who asked, and who can sign?" Circulate and check that the drywall patch is named as work by others. Write the CR-03 change request. printed form, learner.md 18 min
3.1 Assign Task 4. Take answers from three learners. List the problems and the fixes. learner.md 6 min
3.2 Teach key ideas 6 and 7: no signature, no start, and the change log. Assign Task 5. Ask two learners to read their texts aloud. Write the text and the log line. learner.md 7 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer in writing. none 5 min
4.2 Give the summary. Listen, ask final questions. none 4 min

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. A homeowner asks a plumber to add an outdoor tap that is not in the contract. Is this an RFI or a change request?
  2. Work out the cost: 1 plumber × 3 h × $95/h + $120 material.
  3. Name the person who must sign a change request.

Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • the difference between normal work, an RFI and a change
  • why confirming a change is not the same as getting it approved
  • the parts of a change request: description, reason, impact on scope, schedule, cost, crew and safety, and approval
  • costing a change with BEDMAS and showing the math
  • no signature, no start, unless something must be made safe now
  • keeping a change log
