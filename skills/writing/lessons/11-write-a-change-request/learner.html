Write a change request before you build it

Turn a "can you just..." into a written change request that says what is asked, why, and what it does to the scope, days, cost, crew and safety, and get it signed before the work starts.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task

Why this matters on the job

"While you're here, can you just..." Every trade hears it. A homeowner wants a second set of stairs. A tenant wants two more outlets after the drywall is up. A super wants a wall moved 300 mm. If you say "sure" and start, the extra hours and material often go unpaid, the schedule slips, and nobody can prove later who asked for what. A written change request stops that. It says what is being asked, what it will cost in money and days, and it gets signed by the person who can approve it before anyone picks up a tool.

Key ideas

1. Question, change or normal work?

It is When What you write
Normal work It is already in the drawings, specs or contract. Nothing extra. Build it.
A question (RFI) The drawings are unclear, wrong or missing something. A request for information. See Ask a good RFI question.
A change Someone wants something different from what was agreed. A change request.

An RFI asks a question. A change request asks for approval. On many jobs, an approved change request becomes a change order.

2. Confirming is not the same as approving

In Text and email a foreman or client you confirmed a spoken change by email. That gives you a record. A change request goes further: it puts a price and a number of days on the change and asks for a signature.

3. Find out who can sign

The person asking is not always the person who can approve. A tenant's office manager, a homeowner's neighbour or a crew lead from another trade may ask for a change, but only the person named in the contract, or the person your boss names, can approve it. If you are not sure who that is, ask your foreman before you write.

4. The parts of a change request

Part What goes in it
Heading Job name, change request number (CR-01, CR-02 ...), date, requested by, written by
Description What is being changed, where, how many, what size. Name the drawing or room.
Reason Why the change is wanted, in one or two sentences
Impact on scope What is added, removed or different
Impact on schedule How many working days it adds, and what it pushes back
Impact on cost Labour, material and total, with the math shown
Impact on crew and other trades Who does the work, and which other trade is affected
Safety Any new hazard and how it will be controlled
Approval Name, title, signature, date, approved or rejected

5. Cost the change with BEDMAS

For pricing a change, see Numeracy lessons 22 (Markup, margin and pricing a job) and 26 (What a machine or crew costs per hour).

Labour = people × hours × rate. Then add the material. Multiply before you add.

2 carpenters × 7 h × $80/h + $480 material = $1,120 + $480 = $1,600

Use the labour rate your company gives you for the job. Show the math so the reader can check it. For more practice pricing work, see the Numeracy Hub, Construction Operations tab.

6. No signature, no start

Unless the change is needed right now to make something safe, wait for the signed approval before you start. If something must be made safe, make it safe, tell your foreman, and write up the change the same day.

7. Keep a change log

A change log is a list of every change request on the job, so nothing gets lost.

CR Date Description Amount Status
CR-01 2026-11-12 Add east stairs $1,600 Approved 2026-11-13
CR-02 2026-11-16 Cedar fascia in place of painted fascia $420 Waiting for signature

Worked example

Alder Creek Decks (a fictional company) is building a 12 ft × 16 ft deck at 418 Hemlock Street. The contract includes one set of stairs on the south side. On Thursday, November 12, the homeowner, Carol Grant, asks the carpenter, Jesse Paul, for a second set of stairs on the east side down to the garden.

Jesse does not say "sure". He says, "I can write that up with a price today. We start it once you sign." His change request:

Job: Grant deck, 418 Hemlock Street CR number: CR-01 Date: 2026-11-12 Requested by: Carol Grant, homeowner Written by: Jesse Paul, carpenter, Alder Creek Decks

Description: Add a second set of stairs on the east side of the deck, down to the garden: 4 steps, 36 in wide, with a handrail that matches the south stairs.

Reason: The homeowner wants direct access from the deck to the garden.

Scope: Adds one set of stairs and a handrail. Removes 36 in of the east guard rail for the stair opening.

Schedule: Adds 1 working day. Completion moves from Friday, November 20, to Monday, November 23.

Cost: Labour 2 carpenters × 7 h × $80/h = $1,120. Material $480. Total $1,600.

Crew and other trades: Same 2-person crew. No other trades affected. The office will check whether the permit drawings need to change.

Safety: The east guard rail stays in place until the stair is built, so there is no open edge.

Approval: Name _ Title _ Signature _ Date _ Approved / Rejected

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Mark each item N (normal work), RFI (a question) or CR (a change request).

a) The drawings show a 3-ply beam, but the beam schedule says 2-ply. ___ b) After the wall is drywalled, the client asks to move a light switch to the other side of the door. ___ c) Install the 12 joists shown on the framing plan. ___ d) The owner wants cedar decking in place of the pressure-treated decking in the contract. ___ e) Detail 4 on sheet A3 has no dimension for the stair landing. ___

Task 2 (level 2) Work out the cost of each change. Show each step with BEDMAS.

a) 1 electrician for 5 h at $90/h, plus $160 of material

b) 2 labourers for 3 h at $45/h, plus a $75 dump fee

c) 3 framers for 4 h at $80/h, plus $380 of material

Task 3 (level 2 to 3) Write a change request using the parts in key idea 4. Use these facts.

  • Company: Coastal Spark Electric (fictional). You are the electrician, Mina Cho.
  • Job: office fit-out, Unit 104, Brightwater Plaza. This is change request CR-03.
  • On Tuesday, November 17, at 2:00 pm, the tenant's office manager, Priya Nair, asked you to add 4 duplex receptacles on the east wall of the meeting room, spaced evenly, at the same height as the existing receptacles. She wants to plug in a row of laptops at the table.
  • The east wall is already drywalled and taped.
  • Labour: 1 electrician, 6 h, at $90/h. Material: $140.
  • It adds 1 working day for your crew. The drywall trade will need to patch 4 openings. That work is not in your price.
  • Cutting into finished drywall makes dust. The meeting room carpet is already down.
  • Only the general contractor's project manager, Leo Brandt, can approve changes on this job.

Task 4 (level 3) List four problems with this change request. For each one, say what should be there instead.

Job: 5th St reno CR: ___ Date: ___ Description: Move wall a bit. Cost: TBD Requested by: the super Approval: _____

Task 5 (level 3) On the Harbour Lane job, the superintendent says to you on site: "Just move that partition 300 mm north. We'll sort out the paperwork later." You have priced it at $860 and 1 extra day. Write a text to the superintendent (under 50 words) that keeps the relationship good and gets the change approved before you start. Then write the line you add to your change log.
