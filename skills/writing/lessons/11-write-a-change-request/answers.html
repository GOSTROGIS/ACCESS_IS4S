Write a change request before you build it

Turn a "can you just..." into a written change request that says what is asked, why, and what it does to the scope, days, cost, crew and safety, and get it signed before the work starts.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task

Task 1 (level 1)

a) RFI. The drawings disagree, so it is a question for the designer. b) CR. The client wants something different from what was built. c) N. It is on the framing plan. d) CR. A different material from the contract. e) RFI. A dimension is missing.

Steps: ask "Is it already in the drawings or contract?" If yes, it is normal work. If the drawings are unclear or wrong, it is an RFI. If someone wants something different, it is a change request.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) 1 × 5 × $90 + $160 = $450 + $160 = $610 b) 2 × 3 × $45 + $75 = $270 + $75 = $345 c) 3 × 4 × $80 + $380 = $960 + $380 = $1,340

Steps: multiply people × hours × rate first, then add material or fees (BEDMAS).

Task 3 (level 2 to 3)

Sample:

Job: Office fit-out, Unit 104, Brightwater Plaza CR number: CR-03 Date: 2026-11-17 Requested by: Priya Nair, tenant's office manager, on site at 2:00 pm Written by: Mina Cho, electrician, Coastal Spark Electric

Description: Add 4 duplex receptacles on the east wall of the meeting room, spaced evenly, at the same height as the existing receptacles.

Reason: The tenant wants to plug in a row of laptops at the meeting table.

Scope: Adds 4 receptacles and the wiring to them. Needs 4 openings cut in the finished drywall.

Schedule: Adds 1 working day for the electrical crew. The drywall patch adds time for the drywall trade, which their foreman must confirm.

Cost: Labour 1 electrician × 6 h × $90/h = $540. Material $140. Total $680. The drywall patching is by others and is not in this price.

Crew and other trades: 1 electrician. The drywall trade must patch 4 openings after the receptacles are in.

Safety: Cutting finished drywall makes dust. Cover the carpet and use a vacuum at the cut. Keep the circuit locked out while wiring.

Approval: Leo Brandt, Project Manager, general contractor. Signature __ Date ____ Approved / Rejected

A good answer:

  • names Priya Nair as the person who asked and Leo Brandt as the person who approves
  • gives the wall, the room, the number and the height
  • shows the cost math: $540 + $140 = $680
  • states that the drywall patch is by others and not in the price
  • names the dust hazard and a control
  • leaves the approval line for a signature

Task 4 (level 3)

Any four:

  • No CR number or date. Give it the next number in the change log and the date.
  • "Move wall a bit" is vague. Say which wall, which room or grid line, which way and how far, for example "Move the partition between Rooms 104 and 105 300 mm north."
  • "Cost: TBD" gives the approver nothing to approve. Show labour, material and total.
  • "The super" is not a name. Give the person's name and title, and when they asked.
  • No reason, no schedule impact, no safety note.
  • The approval line has no name or title for the person who can sign.

Task 5 (level 3)

Sample text:

Hi Marta, happy to move the partition 300 mm north. Price is $860 and 1 extra day. I'll bring the change request to the site office at 1:00 so you can sign it, then we'll start right after.

Sample change log line:

CR Date Description Amount Status
CR-04 2026-11-18 Move partition 300 mm north, requested by Marta Kowal on site $860, 1 day Waiting for signature

A good answer is polite, says yes to the work, gives the price and the days, asks for a signature before starting and makes signing easy. Any date and CR number that fit the learner's log are fine.

Check yourself

  1. An RFI asks a question about unclear or wrong drawings. A change request asks for approval to do something different from what was agreed, with its cost and days (level 2).
  2. Any four of: scope, schedule, cost, crew or other trades, safety (level 1).
  3. 2 × 5 × $60 + $250 = $600 + $250 = $850 (level 2).
  4. Only when something must be made safe right now. Then tell your foreman and write it up the same day (level 2).
  5. It lists every change request on the job, with its amount and status, so none get lost or go unpaid (level 1).
