Keep a daily work log

Write short, factual log entries that tell the next person what was done, when, by whom and what still needs doing.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Why this matters on the job

On a BC job site or in a shop, the person who reads your log is often not there when you write it: the next shift, your foreman, the office that does payroll, or you, three weeks later. A good log says what happened in plain facts. It backs up your timesheet, warns the next crew about a problem, and gives a clear record if anyone asks later what was done.

Key ideas

1. Write it soon. Memory fades fast. Write your log at breaks or at the end of the shift, not the next morning.

2. Answer the reporter's questions. Each line should answer as many of these as it can:

Question Example
When? 10:15 am
Where? Bay 3, east wall
Who? J. Moon and I
What was done or seen? cut and tacked 6 brackets
How much or how many? 6 of 10 brackets
What is still open? 4 brackets left, waiting on 3/8" flat bar

3. Facts first. Keep guesses and opinions out, or label them. A fact is something you saw, heard, measured or did. An opinion or guess is what you think about it.

  • Fact: "Grinder guard on grinder #2 is cracked. Tagged it out of service at 1:40 pm."
  • Opinion: "Somebody wrecked the grinder again."

If you need to give a reason you are not sure of, say so: "Possible cause: guard was hit during the lunch move. Not confirmed."

4. Use the date and time formats your workplace uses. For all-number dates, the Government of Canada's Language Portal recommends year-month-day with hyphens: 2026-11-06 means November 6, 2026. It cannot be confused with June 11.

5. Short lines are fine. A log is not an essay. Each line can be a short sentence or a phrase, but every line must make sense to someone who was not there. "Done" is not enough. "Pump base bolts torqued and marked, Bay 2" is.

6. Read it once as a stranger. Before you close the log, read it as if you were the next shift. Can they start work from it? Is anything only clear inside your own head?

Worked example

These are an apprentice's rough notes for one shift at Fraser Bend Fabrication (a fictional shop):

brackets bay 3 / 6 done / flat bar out / grinder guard busted, tagged / told Dale / Moon helped am

Here is the same information as a log entry:

Date: 2026-11-06 Shift: 7:00 am to 3:30 pm Written by: R. Thomas

  • 7:15 am. Bay 3. J. Moon and I cut and tacked 6 of 10 wall brackets.
  • 11:30 am. Ran out of 3/8" flat bar. 4 brackets not started.
  • 1:40 pm. Grinder #2 guard cracked. Tagged out of service. Told Dale (lead hand) at 1:45 pm.
  • Still open: 4 brackets, flat bar order, grinder #2 repair.

What changed:

  • The date, times and names are added.
  • "Busted" became what was actually seen.
  • "Told Dale" now says when, and who Dale is.
  • The open items are listed at the end, so the next shift finds them fast.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Rewrite each rough note as one clear log line. Use the time, place and details in the brackets.

a) "pipe rack done" (2:10 pm, Unit 4 mechanical room)

b) "leak again" (9:05 am, hose bib on the north side, small drip at the fitting)

c) "boss says wait" (10:40 am, foreman M. Joe said not to pour until the inspector signs off)

Task 2 (level 1) Mark each line F (fact) or O (opinion or guess). Then rewrite the two opinion lines as facts, using only what the line tells you, or say what you would need to find out.

  1. The east stairs were wet at 6:50 am. ___
  2. The night crew is lazy and never cleans up. ___
  3. Delivered: 40 sheets of 5/8" drywall, stacked in Room 112. ___
  4. The forklift is probably going to break down soon. ___
  5. Forklift alarm sounded twice when reversing, then stopped working at 2:20 pm. ___

Task 3 (level 2) During today's class, something will happen that you are not told about in advance. When your instructor asks, write a log entry about it. Include the time, where in the room it happened, who was involved (describe them if you do not know their name), what they did, what they said, and anything they brought in or took out. Write only what you saw and heard.

Task 4 (level 2) Use the worked example log above. Write a handover note of three to five sentences to the next shift lead, Tanya. Tell her what is finished, what is not, and what she needs to know about the grinder. Do not add facts that are not in the log.

Check yourself

  1. Write November 6, 2026 as an all-number date in the order the Language Portal of Canada recommends.
  2. Why is "Done" a weak log line?
  3. Is "The welder looked tired" a fact or an opinion? How could you write what you actually saw?
  4. Name three readers, other than yourself, who might read your work log.
  5. Where in a log entry should you put the jobs that are still open, and why there?
