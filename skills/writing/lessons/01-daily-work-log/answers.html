Keep a daily work log

Write short, factual log entries that tell the next person what was done, when, by whom and what still needs doing.

  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Task 1 (level 1)

Sample answers. Wording can differ if the time, place and action are all there.

a) "2:10 pm. Unit 4 mechanical room. Pipe rack installed and complete."

b) "9:05 am. Hose bib, north side. Small drip at the fitting. Leak has come back."

c) "10:40 am. Foreman M. Joe said not to pour until the inspector signs off. Pour on hold."

Steps: find the time and place in the brackets, put them first, then say what was done or seen in full words. Replace slang ("boss") with the person's role and name.

Task 2 (level 1)

  1. F
  2. O
  3. F
  4. O
  5. F

Rewrites of the opinion lines:

  • Line 2: The line gives no facts about the night crew. A factual version describes what was seen, for example: "6:50 am. Offcuts and empty boxes left on the floor in Room 112." If you did not see anything specific, leave it out.
  • Line 4: "Probably going to break down" is a guess. Use the fact from line 5 instead: "Forklift reverse alarm sounded twice, then stopped working at 2:20 pm. Reported to foreman."

Steps: ask "Did someone see, hear, measure or do this?" If yes, it is a fact. Words like "lazy", "probably", "never" and "always" usually signal opinion.

Task 3 (level 2)

Answers depend on the walk-in your instructor arranged. A good entry:

  • starts with the time (to the nearest few minutes)
  • says where in the room it happened (door, board, front table)
  • describes the person by what could be seen (for example, "a person in a yellow vest") or by name if known
  • lists actions in order (came in, pinned a paper to the board, left)
  • quotes or closely reports anything said
  • names items brought in or taken out
  • contains no guesses about why, or labels them clearly ("reason not known")

Task 4 (level 2)

Sample answer:

"Hi Tanya. Today in Bay 3, J. Moon and I cut and tacked 6 of the 10 wall brackets. The other 4 are not started because we ran out of 3/8" flat bar, so that needs ordering. Grinder #2 has a cracked guard. I tagged it out of service at 1:40 pm and told Dale at 1:45 pm. Please don't use it until it's repaired."

A good note covers: what is finished (6 brackets), what is not (4 brackets), why (no flat bar), and the grinder status (tagged out, Dale told). It adds no new facts.

Check yourself

  1. 2026-11-06 (level 1). Year first, then month, then day, each with leading zeros and hyphens between.
  2. "Done" does not say what was done, where, by whom or when, so the next reader cannot act on it (level 1).
  3. It is an opinion (level 1). Write what you saw instead, for example "The welder sat down twice during the morning and said he had a headache." Only write what you saw or heard.
  4. Any three of: the next shift, the foreman or supervisor, the payroll or office staff, a safety committee or investigator, the client or inspector, the employer (level 1).
  5. At the end, in a "Still open" line (level 2). The next reader often reads the end first to find out what they must do, and a separate list keeps open items from getting lost in the middle.
