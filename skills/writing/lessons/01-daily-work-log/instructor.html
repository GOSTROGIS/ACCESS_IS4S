Keep a daily work log

Write short, factual log entries that tell the next person what was done, when, by whom and what still needs doing.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Session length: 60 min. All times below are part of the 60 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Tell a short story: a millwright comes in on Monday to finish a pump job. The pump is half assembled on the bench. There are no notes. Nobody knows which bolts were torqued, whether the seal kit was the right one, or who had the job on Friday. The crew loses two hours checking work that was already done.

Ask: "What one page of writing would have saved those two hours?" Write the answers on the board. Lead to the idea of a daily work log.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • name who reads a work log and what they need from it (Identify the task that requires you to write)
  • sort notes into facts, open items and guesses before writing (Plan the writing task)
  • write log lines with time, place, people, action and quantity (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • reread an entry as a stranger and fix gaps (Review and revise your writing)

Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

  1. "Have you ever kept a logbook, journal or record at work or in school? What went in it?"
  2. "Is 'The welder looked tired' a fact or an opinion?"

Note who already separates fact from opinion. Pair them with learners who do not for Task 2.

Before class

Arrange a walk-in for step 2.3 with a colleague. Do not tell learners. Choose one of these, adapted from the ACCESS walk-in scenarios:

  • The person walks in during instruction, says nothing, pins a sheet of paper to the board and leaves.
  • The person walks in, puts one item down (for example, a box of gloves), picks up a different item (for example, a tape measure) and leaves.
  • The person walks in, asks you an office question (for example, "Where are the spare timesheets?"), gets an answer, and leaves with an item.

Ask the colleague to wear one item learners can describe (a coloured hat or vest). Keep the walk-in calm and short. Do not stage arguments, threats or injuries.

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Deliver the opening story and question. Answer the question, suggest what a note should contain. Board, markers 5 min
1.2 State the objectives in plain words. Listen, ask questions. Board 2 min
1.3 Ask the two pre-assessment questions. Answer aloud or on paper. none 3 min
2.1 Teach the key ideas: write it soon, the reporter's questions, fact versus opinion, YYYY-MM-DD dates, short lines, read it as a stranger. Walk through the worked example line by line. Follow in learner.md, point out what changed between the rough notes and the log entry. learner.md, board 10 min
2.2 Assign Tasks 1 and 2. Circulate. Push for times, places and quantities. Complete Tasks 1 and 2 in pairs. learner.md 10 min
2.3 Continue teaching or checking answers while the colleague's walk-in happens. Do not comment on it. Carry on with the class. Colleague, prop items 2 min
2.4 Ask learners to write Task 3 alone, without talking. Write a log entry about the walk-in. learner.md 10 min
2.5 Bring the colleague back if possible, or describe exactly what happened. Compare entries: who got the time, the clothing, the words said, the items? Point out guesses that crept in. Compare their entry with the facts, mark what they missed or guessed. Board 6 min
3.1 Assign Task 4. Remind learners to use only facts in the log. Write the handover note to Tanya. Swap with a partner, who checks it against the log. learner.md 7 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer in writing. none 3 min
4.2 Give the summary. Listen, ask final questions. none 2 min

Total: 5 + 2 + 3 + 10 + 10 + 2 + 10 + 6 + 7 + 3 + 2 = 60 min.

Post-assessment (3 min, step 4.1)

  1. Rewrite "fixed it" as a full log line for this fact: at 2:30 pm in Bay 1 you replaced a worn V-belt on the exhaust fan.
  2. Write today's date as an all-number date in YYYY-MM-DD order.
  3. Give one reason a log should keep guesses out or label them.

Summary (2 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • who reads a work log, and why they need facts
  • the reporter's questions: when, where, who, what, how much, what is still open
  • writing facts and labelling guesses
  • all-number dates in year-month-day order
  • reading your own log as a stranger before you close it
