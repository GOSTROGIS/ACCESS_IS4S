Write a site memo and a short report

Write a one-page memo that puts the point first and lists who does what by when, then a short report that sets out what you found, two options and a recommendation.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to write; Plan the writing task; Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate format for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Task 1 (level 1)

4 "1. Park in the north lot. 2. Tell anyone who rides with you." 1 "Subject: No parking in the back lane..." 5 "Questions: see Dale in the shop office." 2 "Starting Monday, November 23, nobody may park..." 3 "Steel trucks need the whole lane to unload."

Steps: the heading comes first, then the point, then the reason, then the numbered actions, then the contact.

Task 2 (level 2)

Samples:

a) "The site gate was found open after 3:30 pm on 4 of 5 days, November 2 to 6." b) "The drywall delivery booked for 7:00 am on November 10 arrived at 10:40 am. Three tapers waited for it." c) "We used 60 grinding discs in October, double the 30 we used in September."

A good answer uses only the facts given, with numbers, dates and places, and adds no guesses about why.

Task 3 (level 2)

Sample:

To: All Fraser Bend Fabrication staff From: Dale Okafor, Shop Manager Date: 2026-11-16 Subject: No parking in the back lane from 7 to 9 am, starting Monday, November 23

Starting Monday, November 23, nobody may park in the back lane between 7:00 and 9:00 am.

Steel trucks come on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays and need the whole lane to unload. Trucks parked in the lane have been making them wait.

  1. Park in the north lot between 7:00 and 9:00 am.
  2. Tell anyone who rides in with you.

Questions: see me in the shop office.

A good answer has all four heading lines, the date and the rule in the first sentence, a reason of one or two sentences, numbered actions and a contact. It leaves out "as most of you probably know", "I mean" and other filler. It applies to every day, not only delivery days, because the original says "from now on". A learner who limits it to Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays has changed the rule.

Task 4 (level 3)

Math (with BEDMAS):

  • Sheets damaged: 9 + 12 + 6 = 27
  • Sheets delivered: 120 + 140 + 100 = 360
  • Percent damaged: 27 ÷ 360 × 100 = 7.5%
  • Cost of damaged sheets: 27 × $18 = $486, which is $486 ÷ 3 = $162 a week on average
  • Option A: 2 labourers × 1 hour × $45 = $90 a delivery. Four weekly deliveries cost 4 × $90 = $360.
  • Option B: first month $180 + $150 = $330. Each month after that, $180.

Sample report:

To: Marta Kowal, Site Superintendent From: Sam Ortiz, Lead Hand Date: 2026-11-23 Subject: Damaged drywall at Harbour Lane: findings and recommendation

Purpose. On November 9 you asked me to find out why drywall is getting damaged on site and to recommend a fix.

What I found. In the three weeks starting November 2, 27 of the 360 sheets delivered were damaged, which is 7.5%. At $18 a sheet, that cost $486, about $162 a week. The sheets are stored outside at the north end under one tarp. The tarp blew off twice, and it rained in two of the three weeks.

Options. A. Store the drywall in the parkade. Moving each delivery takes 2 labourers 1 hour, which is $90 a delivery, or about $360 a month. B. Rent a storage container: $330 for the first month, including delivery, then $180 a month.

Recommendation. Rent the container (option B). It keeps the sheets dry and costs less than option A from the first month. Both options cost less than the damage we are paying for now.

Next step. Please approve the rental by Wednesday, November 25, so the container arrives before the next delivery.

A good answer:

  • has the four heading lines and the five report headings
  • shows the math, and the numbers in the report match it
  • states facts about storage, the tarp and the weather with no blame
  • makes a recommendation that follows from the numbers. A learner who picks option A is also correct if they give a reason from the facts, for example that the parkade is already available and needs no rental.
  • asks for a decision by a date

Check yourself

  1. To, From, Date, Subject (level 1).
  2. What is changing, who it applies to, and from when (level 2).
  3. Each action is easy to find and check, and each one can say who does what by when (level 2).
  4. Sample: "On November 9, 2 of the 8 ladders in the tool crib had cracked side rails." (level 2)
  5. Purpose, What I found, Options, Recommendation, Next step (level 1).
