# Write a site memo and a short report

Session length: 90 min. All times below are part of the 90 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

## Before class

Print the two worked examples on separate sheets so learners can mark them up. Bring a real memo board item if you have one that names no real person or company (a posted shop notice, a sign-in board notice). Have a calculator for each pair for Task 4.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Read the superintendent's first try aloud: "Hey all, so it's come to my attention that there's been garbage around..." Ask: "You read this on the sign-in board at 6:55 am. What are you supposed to do, starting when?" Take answers. Most will say "nothing clear". Then ask: "Who on a crew writes notices like this?" Lead hands, foremen, superintendents, shop managers. Say: "Today you learn the two documents that people write once they start running work: the memo and the short report."

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- decide whether a message should be a text or email, a memo or a short report (Identify the task that requires you to write)
- gather the facts, numbers and options before writing (Plan the writing task)
- write specific lines with dates, places and numbers in place of vague ones (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
- use a memo heading, numbered actions and the five report headings (Choose the appropriate format for the writing task)
- cut a rambling message down to the point and the actions (Review and revise your writing)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

1. "What is the difference between a memo and an email?"
2. "Your foreman asks you to find out why a job keeps running out of screws. What would you put in your answer?"

## Post-assessment (6 min, step 4.1)

1. Write a subject line for a memo telling all crews that the site gate will close at 4:00 pm instead of 5:00 pm, starting Monday, November 30.
2. Rewrite as a specific line: "The compressor is acting up." Use these facts: the compressor in Bay 1 shut off twice on November 18, at 9:10 am and 1:45 pm.
3. In a short report, where does your recommendation go, and what must it be based on?

## Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- when to write a memo, an email or a short report
- the memo heading: To, From, Date, Subject
- putting the point first, a short reason, and numbered actions with who and when
- writing specific lines with numbers, places and dates
- the short report plan: purpose, what I found, options, recommendation, next step
