# Write a site memo and a short report

## Why this matters on the job

Texts and emails reach one or two people. A memo goes to a whole crew, a shop or every trade on a site. It gets posted in the site office, pinned to the sign-in board or attached to an email. A short report goes up the chain: your foreman or the shop manager asks you to look into a problem, and you write back what you found and what you think should happen. Lead hands, foremen and estimators write both. If your memo is clear, people do what it asks. If your report is clear, the boss can say yes to your fix.

## Key ideas

### 1. Memo, email or report?

| Write a | When |
|---|---|
| Text or email | One or two readers, a quick question or update. See [Text and email a foreman or client](../06-texts-and-emails/learner.html). |
| Memo | Many readers inside the company or on the site. A rule, a change, a reminder everyone must follow. One page. |
| Short report | Someone asked you to look into a problem. You give what you found, the options and what you recommend. One or two pages. |

### 2. The memo heading

Every memo starts with four lines. Readers look here first.

> **To:** who must read it
> **From:** your name and job title
> **Date:** 2026-11-09
> **Subject:** what it is about and what changes, in a few words

Write the date the same way as in your [daily work log](../01-daily-work-log/learner.html): year, month, day.

### 3. Put the point first

The first sentence says what is changing, who it applies to and from when. Many people read only the first two lines on a sign-in board.

- Weak: "As many of you may already be aware, there have been some ongoing concerns lately regarding..."
- Clear: "Starting Tuesday, November 10, every crew must put garbage in the new bins by the main gate."

### 4. Give the reason in one or two sentences

People follow a rule better when they know why. Keep the reason short and true. Leave out blame.

### 5. Number the actions

Use a numbered list for anything people must do. Each action says **who**, **what** and **by when**.

### 6. Be specific, not vague

A vague line starts arguments. A specific line gives people the facts.

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "People never clean up." | "The lane behind Building B had nails and lunch garbage in it on 3 of 5 days this week." |
| "Deliveries are always late." | "The drywall delivery booked for 7:00 am on November 10 arrived at 10:40 am. Three tapers waited." |

### 7. The short report plan

A short report answers the question someone asked you. Use these headings:

1. **Purpose**: who asked, what they asked, when.
2. **What I found**: facts, numbers, places and dates.
3. **Options**: two choices, each with its cost and what it takes.
4. **Recommendation**: which option, and why, in two or three sentences.
5. **Next step**: what you need from the reader, and by when.

Use the memo heading (To, From, Date, Subject) at the top. Keep each section short.

### Worked example: a memo

**Tidewater Builders** (a fictional company) has had complaints from neighbours. The superintendent's first try:

> Hey all, so it's come to my attention that there's been garbage around and some of it's ending up out back and the neighbours aren't too happy, so let's all try to do better going forward on that, thanks.

The memo she posted instead:

> **To:** All Harbour Lane crews and trades
> **From:** Marta Kowal, Site Superintendent
> **Date:** 2026-11-09
> **Subject:** New garbage bins and a 10-minute end-of-shift sweep, starting Tuesday, November 10
>
> Starting Tuesday, November 10, every crew must use the new bins by the main gate and sweep its work area at the end of each shift.
>
> Neighbours on Pine Lane phoned the office three times last week about lunch garbage and nails in the lane behind Building B. Nails in the lane can puncture tires, and the complaints hurt our name in this neighbourhood.
>
> 1. Everyone: put food and lunch garbage in the green bins by the main gate. Put offcuts, nails and screws in the metal bin beside them.
> 2. Everyone: at the end of each shift, spend 10 minutes picking up your work area, including the lane side of Building B.
> 3. Foremen: check your area after the sweep and initial the clean-up sheet in the site office.
> 4. Everyone: sign the sign-off sheet in the site office by Friday, November 13, to show you have read this memo.
>
> Questions: see me in the site office.

What changed: a heading, the point first, a short reason with facts, numbered actions with who and when, and a way to ask questions.

### Worked example: a short report

**Fraser Bend Fabrication** (fictional) has been losing tools. The shop manager asked the lead hand to find out why.

> **To:** Dale Okafor, Shop Manager
> **From:** Rena Thomas, Lead Hand
> **Date:** 2026-11-16
> **Subject:** Missing tools in Bay 2: findings and recommendation
>
> **Purpose.** On November 2 you asked me to find out why tools keep going missing from Bay 2 and to suggest a fix.
>
> **What I found.** From September 1 to October 30, six tools went missing from Bay 2: two grinders, one drill, one impact driver and two levels. Replacing them cost $960. Five of the six were last seen on the tool cart at the end of a shift. Bay 2 has no sign-out sheet, and the cart stays outside the tool crib overnight.
>
> **Options.**
> A. Put a sign-out sheet on the crib door. The lead hand checks the cart against the sheet at 3:15 pm each day. This takes about 10 minutes a day and costs nothing to buy.
> B. Buy a lockable cabinet for the cart for $1,450 and use the sign-out sheet as well.
>
> **Recommendation.** Start option A on Monday, November 23. It costs nothing to buy and deals with the main cause, tools left on the cart overnight. If any tool goes missing before Friday, December 18, buy the cabinet.
>
> **Next step.** Please tell me by Friday, November 20, if I can start option A.
