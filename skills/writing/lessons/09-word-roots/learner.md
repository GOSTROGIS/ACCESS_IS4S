# Use word roots to spell and explain trade words

## Why this matters on the job

Trade work is full of long words: hydraulic, pneumatic, perpendicular, circumference, isometric. You have to read them on drawings and in manuals, and spell them on work orders and in reports. If you know the small parts inside a long word, you can often work out what it means, remember how to spell it, and explain it to a client who has never heard it.

## Key ideas

### 1. Long words are built from parts

Many trade words came into English from Latin and Greek, often through French. A **root** carries the main meaning. A **prefix** comes at the front. Put the parts together and the word often explains itself.

> **thermometer** = Greek *thermos* (hot) + Greek *metron* (measure) = an instrument that measures heat
>
> **hydraulic** = Greek *hydor* (water) + Greek *aulos* (pipe or tube). The Greek word first named a water organ, a musical instrument.

### 2. Some roots worth knowing

| Root or prefix | From | Meaning | Trade words |
|---|---|---|---|
| hydr-, hydro- | Greek *hydor* | water | hydraulic, hydrogen |
| pneum- | Greek *pneuma* | wind, breath, air | pneumatic |
| therm- | Greek *thermos* | hot | thermometer |
| -meter, metr- | Greek *metron* | measure | diameter, perimeter, isometric |
| dia- | Greek | across, through | diameter |
| peri- | Greek | around | perimeter |
| iso- | Greek *isos* | equal | isometric |
| kilo- | Greek *khilioi* | thousand | kilometre, kilowatt |
| manu- | Latin *manus* | hand | manual |
| plumb- | Latin *plumbum* | lead (the metal) | plumb, plumber |
| torqu- | Latin *torquere* | to twist | torque |
| ferr- | Latin *ferrum* | iron | ferrous, non-ferrous |
| circum- | Latin | around | circumference |
| tang- | Latin *tangere* | to touch | tangent |
| milli- | Latin *mille* | thousand, thousandth part | millimetre |
| centi- | Latin *centum* | hundred, hundredth part | centimetre |

Some trade words have a story inside them:

- A **plumber** was first a worker in lead, from Latin *plumbum*. Lead was used for roofs, gutters and pipes.
- **Perpendicular** comes from Latin *perpendiculum*, a plumb line. A plumb line hangs straight down, at a right angle to level ground.
- **Wrench** is much older English. It comes from Old English *wrencan*, "to twist".

### 3. Use the root to guess, then check

Roots give you a good guess, not a guarantee. Check the meaning in a dictionary. The Language Portal of Canada recommends a reliable Canadian dictionary, such as the *Canadian Oxford Dictionary*.

### 4. Canadian spelling

The Language Portal of Canada gives these Canadian preferences:

- **metre** for the unit of length, **meter** for a measuring device: "The pipe is 3 metres long." "Read the water meter."
- **litre**, not liter
- **colour**, **labour** (but **laborious**)
- **centre**, **fibre**

### 5. Explain a technical word when the reader needs it

A crew member knows "perpendicular". A homeowner may not. Add a plain explanation the first time: "The pipe runs perpendicular to the joists (at a right angle to them)."

### Worked example

A new apprentice sees "isometric" on a piping drawing.

1. Split the word: **iso** + **metr** + ic.
2. Look up the parts: *isos* means equal, *metron* means measure.
3. Guess: "equal measure", a drawing where measurements along the main directions are shown at the same scale.
4. Check the dictionary and ask the journeyperson.
5. Spell it from its parts: i-s-o, m-e-t-r, i-c.
