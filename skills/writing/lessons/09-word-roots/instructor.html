Writing · Lesson 09 · Lesson 8: Write a trades résumé and cover letter

Use word roots to spell and explain trade words

Break long trade words into Latin and Greek roots to remember their meaning and spelling, use Canadian spelling, and explain technical words in plain language when the reader needs it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task; Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task; Review and revise your writing

Session length: 60 min. All times below are part of the 60 minutes and match the steps in the learning tasks table.

Before class

Have at least one dictionary available for every two learners: a print Canadian dictionary, or a dictionary on phones or computers. The Online Etymology Dictionary (https://www.etymonline.com) is useful for showing where a word comes from.

Invite learners who speak another language, including an Indigenous language, to share a trade word from that language if they want to. Do not ask anyone to translate or explain their language for the class.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Write on the board: plumber. Ask: "What does a plumber work with?" Take answers. Then tell them the word first meant a worker in lead, from the Latin word for lead, plumbum, because lead was used for roofs, gutters and pipes. Ask: "What other long words from your trade do you think have a story inside them?" List them on the board for step 3.2.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • split a trade word into its parts and use them to spell it (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • use Canadian spelling for common trade and measurement words (Use written words and phrases so you can achieve the purpose of the writing task)
  • add a plain explanation after a technical word for a reader who needs it (Choose the appropriate language and style for the writing task)
  • check a guessed meaning in a dictionary and correct it (Review and revise your writing)

Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

  1. "Spell 'perpendicular' and 'circumference' without looking."
  2. "Which is the unit of length in Canadian spelling: metre or meter?"

Learning tasks

Post-assessment (3 min, step 4.1)

  1. Split "thermometer" into its parts and give the meaning of each.
  2. Correct the spelling: "The tank is 2 meters wide and holds 500 liters."
  3. Add a plain explanation for a client: "The pump is pneumatic."

Summary (2 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • roots and prefixes from Latin and Greek, and what they mean
  • using the parts of a word to remember its spelling
  • checking a guessed meaning in a dictionary
  • Canadian spelling: metre and meter, litre, colour, labour, centre
  • plain explanations for readers who do not know the term
