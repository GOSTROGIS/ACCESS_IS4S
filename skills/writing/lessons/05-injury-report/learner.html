Report a workplace injury in BC

Know who writes which injury report in BC, and write a clear, factual description of how an injury happened.

Why this matters on the job

If you are hurt at work in BC, several people may have to write about it: you, the first aid attendant and your employer. What you write affects your WorkSafeBC claim, and it helps the employer find out what went wrong so the next worker does not get hurt. A clear, factual description of how it happened is one of the most important things you will ever write at work.

Key ideas

1. Who writes what (WorkSafeBC, checked November 2026)

Who What they write or do
You, the worker Tell your employer as soon as it is safe to do so. If you get medical care, tell the clinic or hospital you were hurt at work. Report the injury to WorkSafeBC online (24 hours a day, 7 days a week) or by phone through Teleclaim: 604.231.8888 in the Lower Mainland, 1.888.967.5377 toll-free in Canada, Monday to Friday, 8 am to 6 pm. The paper Form 6 for fax or mail is no longer available. You have up to one year from the date of the injury to report, but WorkSafeBC asks you to report right away.
You, if your employer asks Form 6A, Worker's Notice to Employer of Injury or Occupational Disease. You give it to your employer, not to WorkSafeBC.
The first aid attendant (or the person who gave first aid) A first aid record for every first aid treatment. WorkSafeBC offers Form 55B23, or the employer can use its own template. Records are kept at least 3 years and are confidential.
The employer Form 7, Employer's Report of Injury or Occupational Disease. The employer must report within 72 hours (three days) if the worker got medical treatment beyond first aid or lost time from work.
The employer An incident investigation if the injury was serious or needed medical treatment: a preliminary investigation and report within 48 hours, and a full investigation report to WorkSafeBC within 30 days. WorkSafeBC's template is Form 52E40.
Anyone who sees a hazard Under section 3.10 of the BC Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, a person who sees what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition or act must report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer.

When you report to WorkSafeBC, have this ready: your contact information and your employer's, your Personal Health Number if you have one, the date, place and how you were hurt, details of your first medical visit, and, if you missed work, your earnings for the past 12 weeks and your Social Insurance Number.

2. Investigations look for causes

An employer's investigation looks for the unsafe conditions, acts or procedures that led to the incident, so they can be fixed. The BC government's training handbook for joint health and safety committees says the outcome of an investigation should never be the allocation of blame. Write so your report helps find the cause.

3. How to describe what happened

Most injury forms have a box like "Describe how the incident happened." A good description:

  • follows the order things happened
  • is in the past tense and active voice: "I slipped", "the board hit my shin"
  • says what you were doing, with what tool or material, and where
  • names the body part and the side (left, right, both)
  • describes conditions you saw: wet, dark, missing guard, missing cleat
  • names witnesses
  • leaves out blame and guesses ("the foreman never fixes anything", "I wasn't careful")
Fact Opinion or blame
The ramp was wet. Nobody cares about this ramp.
Two cleats were missing from the lower part of the ramp. The foreman should have fixed it.
I felt pain in my right knee right away. It was my own stupid fault.

4. Protect personal information

Injury forms ask for your Social Insurance Number and Personal Health Number. Give them only on the real form to the right person. In class, use only the fictional details in the case.

Worked example

A weak description:

Was carrying stuff and fell on the ramp. Hurt my leg. Ramp is bad.

A clear description (fictional case, written by the worker):

At about 9:15 am I was walking down the plank ramp at the east entrance, carrying a box of drywall screws in both hands. My right foot slipped on mud on the ramp and I fell onto my left hip and left elbow. I felt pain in my left elbow right away. Kai Larsen was at the bottom of the ramp and saw me fall. I reported it to the first aid attendant at 9:20 am.

Practice

Use this case for Tasks 2 to 5.

Case (fictional). Dakota Joe is an apprentice carpenter with Salmonberry Framing at the Willow Creek duplex site. On 2026-10-28 it had been raining since 7:00 am. At about 10:40 am Dakota was carrying a sheet of 3/4" plywood down the temporary ramp on the north side of the building. Two cleats were missing from the lower part of the ramp. His left boot slipped on the wet ramp. He fell onto his right knee and right hand, and the edge of the plywood hit his left shin. Ana Silva, a carpenter, was cutting at the saw station and saw him fall. At 10:50 am first aid attendant Ray Morin cleaned and bandaged a 3 cm cut on Dakota's left shin and put ice on his right knee. Dakota went back to work on light duty at 11:30 am. By 3:00 pm his right knee was swollen. After work he went to a walk-in clinic. The doctor told him to stay off work the next day.

Task 1 (level 1) Match each report to who writes or does it: W (worker), F (first aid attendant) or E (employer).

a) First aid record ___ b) Form 7 ___ c) Report to WorkSafeBC online or by Teleclaim ___ d) Form 6A, if asked ___ e) Preliminary investigation within 48 hours ___

Task 2 (level 2) Mark each statement F (fact) or O (opinion or blame). For each O, write what you could say instead, or say it should be left out.

  1. It had been raining since 7:00 am. ___
  2. Dakota should have been more careful. ___
  3. Two cleats were missing from the lower part of the ramp. ___
  4. The plywood edge hit Dakota's left shin. ___
  5. This company always cuts corners on safety. ___

Task 3 (level 2 to 3) You are Dakota. Write the answer to "Describe how the incident happened" for Form 6A. Use 80 to 120 words. Write in the first person ("I"), past tense and active voice. Include the time, place, what you were carrying, what you saw about the ramp, the body parts and sides, and the witness.

Task 4 (level 2) You are Ray Morin. Write three short entries for the first aid record:

a) Description of the injury: __________

b) First aid treatment given: __________

c) Actions taken after treatment: __________

Task 5 (level 3) Before Dakota fell, a labourer named Chris had noticed the missing cleats at 8:00 am. Write the message Chris should have sent to the site foreman, Leah Ross, under section 3.10 of the OHS Regulation. Use three to five sentences. Say what the hazard is, where it is, when you saw it, and what you did to warn others.

Check yourself

  1. Who fills in a first aid record?
  2. Within how many hours must an employer report an injury to WorkSafeBC when the worker got medical treatment beyond first aid?
  3. Where do you hand in Form 6A?
  4. Why should an injury description leave out blame?
  5. Give two ways a worker can report an injury to WorkSafeBC.
