# Report a workplace injury in BC

## Task 1 (level 1)

a) F, first aid attendant
b) E, employer
c) W, worker
d) W, worker
e) E, employer

Steps: use the "who writes what" table. Form 6A is filled in by the worker and given to the employer. Form 7 and the investigation belong to the employer.

## Task 2 (level 2)

1. F
2. O. Blame. Leave it out. Use only a supplied fact instead, for example "Dakota was carrying a sheet of 3/4-inch plywood down the temporary ramp." The case does not say he used both hands.
3. F
4. F
5. O. Blame and a generalization. Leave it out. If there is a specific hazard, describe it: "Two cleats were missing from the lower part of the ramp."

## Task 3 (level 2 to 3)

Sample answer (about 100 words):

> On 2026-10-28 at about 10:40 am I was walking down the temporary ramp on the north side of the Willow Creek duplex, carrying a sheet of 3/4" plywood. It had been raining since 7:00 am and the ramp was wet. Two cleats were missing from the lower part of the ramp. My left boot slipped and I fell onto my right knee and right hand. The edge of the plywood hit my left shin. Ana Silva saw me fall from the saw station. Ray Morin gave me first aid at 10:50 am.

A good answer:

- keeps events in time order
- uses "I" and past tense throughout
- names the place, the material and the ramp conditions
- gives each body part with its side: left boot, right knee, right hand, left shin
- names the witness
- contains no blame or guesses

## Task 4 (level 2)

Sample answers:

a) Injury: 3 cm cut on the left shin. The case says Dakota landed on his right knee, but does not give Ray's knee assessment or a report of pain. Do not add symptoms or findings that are not supplied.
b) Treatment: cleaned and bandaged the cut on the left shin at 10:50 am. Applied ice to the right knee.
c) After treatment: worker returned to light duty at 11:30 am.

A good answer separates the treatment stated in the case from any assessment findings that are missing. It describes only what Ray saw, was told and did. Ray does not know about the swelling at 3:00 pm or the clinic visit at the time he writes the record, so those are not in his entry.

## Task 5 (level 3)

Sample answer. The cone and warning below are chosen hypothetical actions for this exercise; they are not facts supplied in the case. In a real report, say only what you actually did:

> Leah, at 8:00 am I saw that two cleats are missing from the lower part of the temporary ramp on the north side. The ramp is wet from the rain and slippery. I put a cone at the top of the ramp and told the two carpenters working nearby. Can the cleats be replaced before anyone carries material down it?

A good answer says what the hazard is, where it is, when it was seen, what was done to warn others, and what action is needed. It goes to the foreman as soon as possible.

## Check yourself

1. The first aid attendant, or the person who gave the first aid (level 1).
2. 72 hours, which is three days (level 1).
3. To your employer, not to WorkSafeBC (level 1).
4. Investigations are meant to find causes so they can be fixed. The BC government's joint committee handbook says the outcome should never be the allocation of blame. Facts about conditions and actions are what help find the cause (level 2).
5. Online through WorkSafeBC's online services, any time, or by phone through Teleclaim during its hours (level 1).
