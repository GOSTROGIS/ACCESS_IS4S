Using Microsoft Copilot

Tell the personal Copilot app apart from Copilot in a work account for Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams, find its privacy settings, and use it for three trades tasks, checking each answer.

Product facts on this page were checked against Microsoft's own pages (microsoft.com, support.microsoft.com and learn.microsoft.com) on 2026-11-06. Why this matters on the job

Microsoft Copilot is the AI assistant built into Microsoft products. If your employer uses Outlook, Teams, Word or Excel, you may already have a Copilot button at work. The Copilot you use at home and the Copilot in a work account are set up differently, with different data rules. Knowing which one you are in matters before you type anything about a job, a client or your employer.

Key ideas

Who makes it and how to get it

One name, two kinds of Copilot. The account you sign in with decides which one you get.

Personal Copilot Work or school Copilot
Sign in with A personal Microsoft account. On the web, phone and Mac, Microsoft also accepts an Apple ID or a Google account. The Windows app needs a Microsoft account. A work or school account your employer or school set up. Microsoft calls it a Microsoft Entra account.
Where you find it The Microsoft Copilot app: copilot.microsoft.com, and apps for Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android Copilot in a browser signed in with your work account, inside the Microsoft 365 app, Teams and Outlook, and the Microsoft Copilot phone app
What it can use Your own content, such as OneDrive files and Outlook.com email, plus the web Your organization's chats, email and files that you already have permission to see, plus the web
Rules that apply Microsoft's Copilot Terms of Use and Services Agreement Your employer's agreement with Microsoft and your employer's policies
  • Minimum age for personal Copilot: 13, and older in some countries.
  • Price: Copilot inside Word, Excel and other desktop apps at home comes with some Microsoft 365 subscriptions (see below). Check prices for Canada on microsoft.com. You do not need to buy anything for this lesson.

Official help page: Microsoft Support, Get started with Microsoft Copilot, https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/get-started-with-microsoft-365-copilot-chat

What it can do

With a personal account, Microsoft's pages describe these features in the Copilot app:

  • write and explain text in a chat
  • search the web for current information
  • Copilot Voice: talk with Copilot and hear it answer
  • Copilot Vision and image upload: show Copilot a picture or a file and ask about it
  • create images

Features depend on your plan, your device and where you live. If you do not see a feature, check the help page.

Where you may meet it at work

Work names (renamed in 2026):

  • Microsoft Copilot Chat (formerly Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat). Included with eligible Microsoft 365 work subscriptions at no extra cost. Admins can pin it in Teams, Outlook and the Microsoft 365 app, and that setting is on by default.
  • Microsoft Copilot (formerly Microsoft 365 Copilot). An add-on licence your employer buys. It gives the full Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote, and lets Copilot work with your organization's data.

Check what your work account has. Sign in to Microsoft 365 with your work account. Scroll to the bottom of the home page. Under your name you see one of these labels:

Label What it means
Copilot Chat (Basic) No add-on licence. No Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote.
M365 Copilot (Basic) No add-on licence, but standard Copilot in those apps.
M365 Copilot (Premium) Add-on licence with the full experience in those apps.

At home: Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium subscriptions include Copilot in the Microsoft 365 apps. Only the subscription owner can use Copilot in the desktop apps.

Privacy and data settings

Personal Copilot (the updated app, from August 2026). Microsoft's privacy controls page lists these controls: Memory, Shared experiences, Chat history, Web search and Personalized advertising.

  • Memory: Settings > Personalization, then the Saved memories switch. Select Manage to delete one memory or Delete all memories. Turning the switch off does not delete memories already saved.
  • Chat history: in the Chats list, select ...More next to a chat, then Delete. To delete all of it, use the Microsoft privacy dashboard.
  • Ads: Settings > Personalization, then turn off Allow ads personalization.
  • Model training: look through Settings for any control about using your conversations to train or improve AI, and read Microsoft's privacy page for individuals. The controls page checked for this lesson did not list a training switch.

Work or school Copilot. Microsoft says prompts and responses in Copilot Chat are not used to train the underlying foundation models. Your employer's IT administrators control most settings.

Whatever the settings, do not paste your Social Insurance Number, health information, client names and addresses, or your employer's private information into any chatbot. Lesson 16 covers this in detail.

Limits

  • Like every large language model (Lesson 11), Copilot can give wrong numbers, wrong rules or made-up sources in a confident voice.
  • What your work Copilot can do depends on your employer's licence and settings. A coworker may see features you do not.
  • Copilot does not know your job site unless you tell it, it saved a memory, or (at work) it can see a file or email about it.

A prompt pattern that works

Give Microsoft Copilot five things:

  1. Who you are. "I am a first-year carpentry apprentice in BC."
  2. The facts. Measurements, quantities and the situation. Leave out private details.
  3. What you want. "Make a materials list."
  4. The format. "As a table." "In three short sentences." "Under 50 words."
  5. Show the work. "Show your math." "Give the section number and a link to the official page."

Worked example: drywall sheets

Rae, a drywall apprentice, needs sheets for one side of a wall 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no openings. The sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m (4 ft by 8 ft).

Rae types: "I am a drywall apprentice in BC. One side of a wall is 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no doors or windows. Sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m. How many sheets do I need? Show your math."

Microsoft Copilot's exact words change from day to day, so they are not printed here. Rae checks whatever it says with her own math:

  • Wall area: 4.8 × 2.4 = 11.52 m²
  • One sheet: 1.2 × 2.4 = 2.88 m²
  • Sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4

Another way: 4.8 ÷ 1.2 = 4 sheets standing side by side, each 2.4 m tall.

If Microsoft Copilot also says 4, Rae uses it. If it says something else, Rae trusts her own math and asks Microsoft Copilot to explain the difference. Before ordering, she asks her journeyperson whether to add extra for waste.

Task 3 (level 2) Materials list. Type this prompt into Microsoft Copilot:

I am building a straight wood fence 21.6 m long. Posts go every 2.4 m, with a post at each end. Each section between posts gets 2 rails, each 2.4 m long. Each post needs 2 bags of concrete mix. Make a materials list with quantities. Show your math.

Then work it out yourself. Write the correct number of posts, rails and bags. Did Microsoft Copilot get the number of posts right?

Task 4 (level 2) Explain a code clause, then check it. Type:

In plain words for a first-year apprentice, explain what WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 11.2, subsections (2) to (5), say about choosing a type of fall protection. Put the options in order.

Then open Part 11 at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-11-fall-protection and read section 11.2. Write the correct order of the four options, and one thing Microsoft Copilot got right or wrong.

Task 5 (level 2) Message to your foreman. You are an apprentice with Northgate Framing Ltd. (a fictional company). At 7:10 a.m. the lumber delivery to the Alder Street site came with 40 studs. The packing slip says 60. Ask Microsoft Copilot to write a short, polite text to your foreman, Dale, that says what happened and asks what to do. Check the message: are the time and numbers right, is there no blame, is there one clear question, and is there nothing private in it? Write the message you would send.
