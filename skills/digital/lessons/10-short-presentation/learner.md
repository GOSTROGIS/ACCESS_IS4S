# Make and give a short presentation

Steps checked in PowerPoint for Microsoft 365 on Windows 11, 2026-11-06. PowerPoint for the web and Mac have the same tabs.

## Why this matters on the job

Lead hands run toolbox talks. Apprentices explain a repair to a journeyperson. Contractors pitch a job to a client or a band office. A few clear slides and two minutes of confident talking can do more than a page of text, and the same skills work in a Teams meeting when you share your screen.

## Key ideas

### A short presentation that works

- **One idea per slide.** A heading and a few words or one picture. Put the details in your speaker notes, not on the slide.
- **Contrast.** Dark text on a light background, or light on dark. Microsoft's tips warn against designs that pull attention away from the message.
- **Your own pictures.** Use photos you took of your own work or tools, or no picture. Do not copy pictures or company logos from websites.
- **Say where facts came from.** If you state a safety rule or a spec, name the source on the last slide, such as the manufacturer's manual or a WorkSafeBC page.

### Building it

| To do this | In PowerPoint |
|---|---|
| Start with a design | **File** > **New**, choose a theme, **Create** |
| Add a slide | **Home**, select the arrow on **New Slide**, choose a layout |
| Duplicate or delete a slide | Right-click it in the thumbnail pane on the left |
| Move a slide | Drag it in the thumbnail pane |
| Add speaker notes | Select **Notes** at the bottom of the window, type in the Notes pane |
| Save | **File** > **Save**, pick the folder, type a name |

### Checking it

**Review** > **Check Accessibility** lists problems such as pictures with no alternative text (alt text). Alt text is a short description that a screen reader reads aloud. Select an item in the list to see the recommended fix. Good alt text says what the picture shows and why it is there, in a sentence or less.

### Presenting it

- **Slide Show** > **From Beginning**, or press F5.
- Press N for the next slide, P for the previous slide, Esc to stop.
- With **Use Presenter View** ticked on the **Slide Show** tab and a second screen connected, you see your notes and the next slide while the audience sees only the current slide.
- In a Teams meeting, select **Share** and choose the PowerPoint file.

### Learning a feature nobody taught you

Apps change every year. When you need something new, search the vendor's own help site, for example `slide numbers site:support.microsoft.com`, read the steps for your version, try them on a copy, and check the result.

### Worked example

Rae is a fictional millwright learner. Her topic is a dial indicator.

1. **File** > **New**, a plain theme with high contrast, **Create**.
2. Slide 1: `The dial indicator` and `Rae M., ACCESS Indigenous Skills4Success`.
3. Slide 2: `What it measures`, one photo she took of her own indicator, and notes explaining it in her own words.
4. Slide 3: `How I set it up`, three short steps.
5. Slide 4: `Source`: the maker's instruction sheet that came with her indicator.
6. **Review** > **Check Accessibility** flags the photo. She adds alt text: `Dial indicator on a magnetic base, needle touching a shaft`.
7. She presses F5 and practises out loud with a timer.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Choose a tool, part or process from your trade, for example a chalk line, a pipe wrench, a torch tip or a ladder inspection. Write your topic and where you will get your facts.

Topic: ______________________________  Source: ______________________________

**Task 2** (level 1) Make a new presentation from a theme. Add four slides: a title slide with your topic and name, two content slides, and a source slide. Save it as `presentation_[topic]_[initials]_v1` in your practice folder.

**Task 3** (level 2) Write speaker notes for slides 2 and 3: what you will say, in two or three sentences each. Keep the slides themselves to a heading and no more than about 15 words.

**Task 4** (level 2) Run **Review** > **Check Accessibility**. Fix every item it lists, or write "no issues found".

## Check yourself

1. Where do the details go, on the slide or in the notes?
2. How do you start a slide show from the first slide?
3. What is alt text for?
4. Which key ends a slide show?
5. You need a PowerPoint feature you have never used. Where do you look first?
