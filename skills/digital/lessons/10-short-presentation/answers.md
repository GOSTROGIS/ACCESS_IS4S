# Make and give a short presentation

## Task 1 (level 1)

Open answer. A good response names one tool, part or process the learner actually knows, and a source they can show: the manufacturer's manual or instruction sheet, a WorkSafeBC page, a trade textbook or their instructor. "The internet" or "a video" without a name is not enough.

## Task 2 (level 1)

Answer: a presentation with four slides (title with topic and name, two content slides, source slide), made from a theme and saved as `presentation_[topic]_[initials]_v1`.

Steps: **File** > **New**, choose a theme, **Create**. Type the title. **Home** > arrow on **New Slide**, choose a layout, three times. **File** > **Save**, choose the folder, type the name.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Points a good response covers:

- Each content slide has a heading and a few words or one picture, about 15 words or fewer.
- The Notes pane for slides 2 and 3 has two or three sentences of what the learner will say.
- The notes add detail that is not on the slide, rather than repeating it word for word.

Level 2 because the learner has to decide what goes on the slide and what goes in the notes.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Answer: the Accessibility pane shows no issues, or each issue was fixed. The most common one is a picture with missing alt text.

A good alt text describes what the picture shows and why it matters, in a sentence or less, without "a picture of". Example: `Pipe wrench with the jaw open on a 1 in black iron pipe`.

## Task 5 (level 2)

Open answer. A good response:

- names the Microsoft Support page title used (for example a page about inserting slide numbers, footers or transitions)
- confirms the page applies to PowerPoint for Microsoft 365 on Windows
- says what happened on the slides, including any step that did not match the screen and what the learner did about it

The skill being checked is finding and following official help, not the feature itself.

## Task 6 (level 1)

Observed: F5 starts the show at slide 1, N moves forward, P moves back, Esc returns to the editing view.

## Task 7 (level 2)

Points a good presentation covers:

- finishes in two minutes or less
- speaks to the group, using notes as a reminder rather than reading the slides
- explains what the tool or part is, how it is used, and one safety or quality point
- names the source on the last slide

The feedback given to a classmate names one specific thing that worked and one specific change.

## Check yourself

1. In the notes. The slide holds the heading and a few words or a picture.
2. **Slide Show** > **From Beginning**, or press F5.
3. It describes a picture so a screen reader can read it aloud to someone who cannot see it.
4. Esc.
5. The vendor's own help site, for PowerPoint support.microsoft.com, for example with a `site:support.microsoft.com` search.
