# How AI chatbots work

ACCESS Indigenous Skills4Success · Digital · Lesson plan

| Session | Total length |
|---|---|
| Digital 11 | 60 min |

## Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: type "think outside the" on the board and ask the room to finish it. Most people say "box". Explain that an AI chatbot does the same thing, one small piece at a time, very fast. Then ask: if it is only guessing the likely next word, should you trust it with a fall protection rule? Today learners find out what is inside the tool and how to check what it says.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- explain in plain words how a large language model makes an answer, and what tokens, the model, the app, the knowledge cutoff and memory are (Update and upgrade digital skills: use existing skills to learn new digital skills)
- sort job-site tasks into good and poor uses of a chatbot (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
- check an AI answer against the official source with the check-it loop (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
- say what a chatbot can know about them and why (Apply safe and responsible practices online: know what personal information you can and cannot share online)

Complexity: levels 1 and 2.

## Pre-assessment (2 min)

1. Have you used an AI chatbot? Which one, and for what?
2. When a chatbot gives you an answer, where do you think the answer comes from?

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Explain to a partner, in two or three sentences, why a chatbot can give a wrong number with total confidence.
2. A chatbot gives you a BC rule with no link. Name two things you do before you rely on it.
3. You told a chatbot your employer's name last month. When could it still know it today?

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- what a large language model is: trained on text, keeps patterns, predicts the next word
- tokens and the rule of thumb of about 75 words per 100 tokens
- the difference between the model and the app, and the tools an app can add
- why answers can sound sure and still be wrong, including made-up sources
- the knowledge cutoff, and what web search does and does not fix
- memory, and what a chatbot can know about you
- the check-it loop, using WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 11.2 as the example

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 5 + 3 = 60 min.

## Notes for the instructor

- No AI account is needed for this lesson. If you have one, a short live demo of a chatbot answering the fall protection question helps, but the check-it work is done on worksafebc.com.
- The worked example and Task 4 use the WorkSafeBC Occupational Health and Safety Regulation as published on worksafebc.com. Paraphrase the regulation in class and send learners to the page for the exact words.
- The token rule of thumb is OpenAI's for English text. Other products count tokens differently. Treat the result as an estimate.
- Lessons 12 to 15 cover Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini with the same task structure. Lesson 16 compares them and covers privacy and workplace policy.
- The older [AI literacy sequence](../../ai-literacy/learner.md) goes further into briefs, file formats and auditing AI work. Use it for learners who want more.
- Lesson 07, [Search and judge online information](../07-search-and-judge-sources/learner.md), teaches `site:` searches and reading web addresses, which learners use in the check-it loop.

## Sources

- Google. *Generative AI training and help* (Google Workspace Learning Center): large language models are trained on large amounts of text and learn to predict the next word, with the "think outside the ___" example. https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/14506784, checked 2026-11-06.
- Google. *Learn about generative AI* (Gemini Apps Help): LLMs predict words that might come next based on the prompt, LLMs do not gather information the way Search does, hallucinations. https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13954172, checked 2026-11-06.
- Google. *What is Gemini and how it works*: like all LLMs, Gemini can confidently generate inaccurate or misleading responses because LLMs work by predicting the next word. https://gemini.google/overview/, checked 2026-11-06.
- OpenAI. *Does ChatGPT tell the truth?* (OpenAI Help Center): responses come from patterns in training data, it may sound confident even when wrong, fabricated citations, knowledge cutoff, search can improve accuracy. https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313428, checked 2026-11-06.
- OpenAI. *What are tokens and how to count them?* (OpenAI Help Center): tokens can be a character or a word, 1 token is about 4 characters and 100 tokens about 75 words in English, other languages often use more tokens. https://help.openai.com/en/articles/4936856-what-are-tokens-and-how-to-count-them, checked 2026-11-06.
- Anthropic. *How do you use personal data in model training?* (Anthropic Privacy Center): models are trained on content to learn patterns and connections between words, and do not store text like a database. https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/10023555-how-do-you-use-personal-data-in-model-training, checked 2026-11-06.
- Anthropic. *Use Claude's chat search and memory to build on previous context* (Claude Help Center): memory from chats and incognito chats. https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context, checked 2026-11-06.
- OpenAI. *Memory in ChatGPT* (OpenAI Help Center): saved memories and reference chat history, controls in Settings. https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq, checked 2026-11-06.

Section 11.2(6) instruction before entering an area with a risk of falling. Section 11.2(7) a worker must use the fall protection system provided by the employer. https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-11-fall-protection, checked 2026-11-06.
