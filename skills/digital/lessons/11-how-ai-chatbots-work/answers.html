How AI chatbots work

Learn what a large language model is, why a chatbot can sound sure and still be wrong, and how to check an AI answer against the official source.

Task 3 (level 2)

Answer: about 800 tokens.

Steps: 100 tokens is about 75 words, so tokens = words × 100 ÷ 75. Multiply and divide from left to right (BEDMAS): 600 × 100 = 60 000, then 60 000 ÷ 75 = 800.

Another correct method: 600 ÷ 75 = 8 groups of 75 words, and 8 × 100 = 800 tokens. Accept "about 800". It is an estimate for English text.

Task 4 (level 2)

Answer: the chatbot is wrong. Section 11.2(7) says a worker must use the fall protection system provided by the employer.

Steps (check-it loop):

  1. Claim: a worker may choose not to use the fall protection the employer gives them.
  2. Source: OHS Regulation Part 11 on worksafebc.com.
  3. Compare: section 11.2(7) says the worker must use it. There is no "if they feel safe" exception.
  4. Decide: drop the AI answer. Write a note with the section number and date checked.

A strong answer also mentions 11.2(6): the employer must instruct the worker in the fall protection system for the area first.

Task 5 (level 2)

Why: with web search off, the answer comes from the model's training text, which stops at its knowledge cutoff. The minimum wage can change after that date, so the number may be old.

Two better ways (any two):

  • Go to the Government of British Columbia website (gov.bc.ca) and find the minimum wage page. Check the date on the page.
  • Turn on web search and open the gov.bc.ca link the chatbot gives, then read the figure on that page yourself.
  • Ask your employer, union or the BC Employment Standards Branch.

  • b. Yes. With memory on, it may have saved this from an earlier chat. You can view and delete memories in the product's settings.

Check yourself

  1. It was trained on lots of text and learned patterns. It writes an answer by predicting the next word (token), then the next, until it is done.
  2. The model is the trained engine that predicts text. The app is the website or phone app around it, which can add tools such as web search, file reading and memory.
  3. It picks likely words, and likely is not the same as true. It can also invent sources or numbers and present them smoothly.
  4. Web search lets the app look up current pages and show links. It does not guarantee the answer reads those pages correctly, so you still open the link and check.
  5. Ask, name the claim, find the official source, compare, then use it, fix it or drop it and write where you checked.
