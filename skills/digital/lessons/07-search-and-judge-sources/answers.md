# Search and judge online information

## Task 1 (level 1)

- `canada.ca`: the Government of Canada.
- `cyber.gc.ca`: the Government of Canada (the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security).
- `worksafebc.com.safety-update.net`: **not** WorkSafeBC. The domain is `safety-update.net`. The words `worksafebc.com` are only a decoy at the front, the same trick as `login.google.com.example.com`. Do not enter anything on this page.

Steps: find the part just before the first single `/` and read it from the right.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Answer: B is the best starting source. A and C are weaker evidence for a safety rule; their order cannot be established from these titles alone. Accept either order for A and C with a reason. Open and check the current official rule before applying it.

- **B** is from worksafebc.com and names the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation. It is the source itself, from the agency that enforces it in BC.
- **A** is one person's blog. It may be helpful and honest, but it repeats the rule secondhand, may be out of date and may not be about BC. Use it only to find the real rule.
- **C** is a paid ad selling products. "BC approved" is a sales claim, not a rule. Its purpose is to sell.

A good response uses at least two of the five questions (who, when, answers my question, source or copy, can I confirm it).
