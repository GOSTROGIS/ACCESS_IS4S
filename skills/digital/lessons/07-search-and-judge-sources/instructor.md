# Search and judge online information

ACCESS Indigenous Skills4Success · Digital · Lesson plan

| Session | Total length |
|---|---|
| Digital 7 | 60 min |

## Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: show two search results for the same safety topic on the projector, one from worksafebc.com and one from a look-alike address. Ask the class which one they would trust and how they can tell. Most people judge by the title and the picture. Today learners judge by the address, the date and who wrote it.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- read a web address and name who runs the site (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
- use quotes, site:, minus and filetype: to narrow a search (Use digital information: carry out digital searches to find information and content)
- rank sources by trustworthiness and check when a page was updated (Use digital information)
- keep a simple source note (Use digital information: store and organize digital information in a logical way)

Complexity: levels 1 and 2.

## Pre-assessment (2 min)

1. How do you usually decide whether a website is telling the truth?
2. What is the difference between a search engine and a website?

## Learning tasks (45 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Break a URL into parts on the board. Show the look-alike example from the Cyber Centre. Go through the table of official addresses. | 1.1 Complete Task 1. | Whiteboard, learner page | 7 min |
| 1.2 | Demonstrate each operator in the table live. Show how `site:` changes the results. | 1.2 Watch, then try one operator on their own computer. | Browser, projector | 8 min |
| 2.1 | Circulate while learners search. Check that they open the page instead of copying from the results list. | 2.1 Complete Tasks 2 and 3. | Browser | 12 min |
| 2.2 | Read the five questions aloud. Run Task 4 as a whole-class discussion after learners rank on their own. | 2.2 Complete Task 4. Share rankings. | Learner page | 8 min |
| 3.1 | Show where "Date modified" sits on a Government of Canada page. | 3.1 Complete Task 5. | Browser | 6 min |
| 3.2 | Ask pairs to compare their Task 6 results. | 3.2 Complete Task 6. | Browser | 4 min |

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Read me this address and tell me who runs it: `https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home`.
2. Write a search that finds only PDF files about lockout from WorkSafeBC.
3. A friend shares a post saying a safety rule changed last month. What two things do you check before you believe it?

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- reading a web address and finding the real domain
- look-alike addresses used by fake sites
- the search operators quotes, site:, minus, filetype:, before: and after:
- five questions for judging a source
- checking the date a page was updated
- keeping a source note

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 5 + 3 = 60 min.

## Notes for the instructor

- The old ESAF internet assessment used cnn.com, espn.com, a Yahoo directory and Ask Jeeves, and the CP Rail exercise sent learners through a company careers site page by page. Those sites and pages have changed, so this lesson keeps the skills (operators, comparing result counts, reading a careers or agency site) with current official BC and federal pages. Result counts are no longer shown in the same way, so Task 6 compares the top results instead.
- Search results differ between computers and accounts. Accept any answer that shows the right reasoning.
- For a job-search extension, have learners search for an employer's own careers page with `site:` and compare it with a job-board copy of the same posting.
- Link to AI literacy: an AI answer is another source to check with the same five questions. See the [AI literacy sequence](../../ai-literacy/learner.md).
