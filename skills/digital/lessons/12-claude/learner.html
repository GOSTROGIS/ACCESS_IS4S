Using Claude

Get started with Anthropic's Claude, find its privacy settings, and use it for a materials list, a code explanation and a message to your foreman, checking each answer.

Why this matters on the job

Claude is an AI chatbot you can use on a computer or phone to explain a document, word a message or work through a materials list. Some employers give workers Claude through a work plan. Knowing where its settings are, and what it can and cannot do, helps you use it safely and check its work before it reaches a job site.

Key ideas

Who makes it and how to get it

  • Made by Anthropic.
  • Web: claude.ai in any browser.
  • Computer app: Claude Desktop for Mac or Windows.
  • Phone app: Claude for iOS or Android.
  • Account: you sign up with an email address. You must be at least 18 and in a location Anthropic supports.
  • Plans for one person: Free, Pro, Max and Ultra. Plans for organizations: Team and Enterprise.
  • The Free plan has usage limits that reset every 5 hours and every week.
  • Price: Anthropic's pricing page shows prices in US dollars and says local pricing applies where supported. Check claude.ai/upgrade or your phone's app store for the price in Canada. You do not need a paid plan for this lesson.

Official help page: Claude Help Center, Get started with Claude, https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114491-get-started-with-claude

What it can do

Anthropic's plan page lists these for the Free plan:

  • chat on the web, the computer app and the phone app
  • search the web
  • create files and run code
  • memory across conversations
  • connect your apps and tools

You can also:

  • upload documents (such as PDF, Word .docx, CSV and plain text) and images (JPEG, PNG, GIF and WebP), up to 20 files in one chat
  • use voice mode to talk with Claude and hear it answer. Voice mode is in beta on all plans and is built to work best in the phone app.

Features depend on your plan, your device and where you live. If you do not see a feature, check the help page.

Where you may meet it at work

  • Organizations can buy Claude Team or Enterprise plans. On those plans, the organization's owners control some settings, such as whether memory is available.
  • Anthropic's plan page lists Claude in Chrome and Microsoft 365 as part of the Pro plan.
  • If your employer gives you Claude through work, use the work account for work only and follow your employer's AI policy (Lesson 16).

Privacy and data settings

  • Model improvement. On the web: select your name, then Settings > Privacy, then the switch under Help improve our AI models. The phone app uses the same path. This switch controls whether Anthropic may use your chats to train future models. If it is on, Anthropic may keep your chats in a de-identified form for up to 5 years for training.
  • Incognito chats. When you start a new chat, a ghost icon at the top right turns on incognito. It is on all plans. Anthropic says incognito chats are not used to improve Claude, even when model improvement is on.
  • Memory is on by default on Free, Pro, Max and Ultra. To see, edit or turn it off, go to Settings > Memory. In the newer settings layout, go to Settings > Your Claude and select Manage next to Your memory. Anthropic says Claude does not save sensitive topics, such as health or religion, to memory unless you turn that on.
  • Deleting a chat removes it from your chat history right away and from Anthropic's back-end storage within 30 days. Chats flagged by Anthropic's safety systems can be kept longer.

Whatever the settings, do not paste your Social Insurance Number, health information, client names and addresses, or your employer's private information into any chatbot. Lesson 16 covers this in detail.

Limits

  • Like every large language model (Lesson 11), Claude can give wrong numbers, wrong rules or made-up sources in a confident voice.
  • The Free plan's usage limit can stop you partway through a task. Start a new chat or wait for the reset.
  • Claude does not know your job site, your employer or your trade unless you tell it or memory saved it.

A prompt pattern that works

Give Claude five things:

  1. Who you are. "I am a first-year carpentry apprentice in BC."
  2. The facts. Measurements, quantities and the situation. Leave out private details.
  3. What you want. "Make a materials list."
  4. The format. "As a table." "In three short sentences." "Under 50 words."
  5. Show the work. "Show your math." "Give the section number and a link to the official page."

Worked example: drywall sheets

Rae, a drywall apprentice, needs sheets for one side of a wall 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no openings. The sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m (4 ft by 8 ft).

Rae types: "I am a drywall apprentice in BC. One side of a wall is 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no doors or windows. Sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m. How many sheets do I need? Show your math."

Claude's exact words change from day to day, so they are not printed here. Rae checks whatever it says with her own math:

  • Wall area: 4.8 × 2.4 = 11.52 m²
  • One sheet: 1.2 × 2.4 = 2.88 m²
  • Sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4

Another way: 4.8 ÷ 1.2 = 4 sheets standing side by side, each 2.4 m tall.

If Claude also says 4, Rae uses it. If it says something else, Rae trusts her own math and asks Claude to explain the difference. Before ordering, she asks her journeyperson whether to add extra for waste.

  • (a) The setting that controls whether your chats are kept or used to improve the AI: ______

Task 3 (level 2) Materials list. Type this prompt into Claude:

I am building a straight wood fence 21.6 m long. Posts go every 2.4 m, with a post at each end. Each section between posts gets 2 rails, each 2.4 m long. Each post needs 2 bags of concrete mix. Make a materials list with quantities. Show your math.

Then work it out yourself. Write the correct number of posts, rails and bags. Did Claude get the number of posts right?

Task 4 (level 2) Explain a code clause, then check it. Type:

In plain words for a first-year apprentice, explain what WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 11.2, subsections (2) to (5), say about choosing a type of fall protection. Put the options in order.

Then open Part 11 at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-11-fall-protection and read section 11.2. Write the correct order of the four options, and one thing Claude got right or wrong.

Task 5 (level 2) Message to your foreman. You are an apprentice with Northgate Framing Ltd. (a fictional company). At 7:10 a.m. the lumber delivery to the Alder Street site came with 40 studs. The packing slip says 60. Ask Claude to write a short, polite text to your foreman, Dale, that says what happened and asks what to do. Check the message: are the time and numbers right, is there no blame, is there one clear question, and is there nothing private in it? Write the message you would send.
