Using Claude

Get started with Anthropic's Claude, find its privacy settings, and use it for a materials list, a code explanation and a message to your foreman, checking each answer.

Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: tell a short story. A framer asks an AI chatbot for a fence materials list and orders straight from it. He comes up one post short because the list forgot the post at the end. Ask: whose job was it to check? Today learners get to know Claude: who makes it, where it shows up at work, how to protect their information, and how to check its work on three trades tasks.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • name who makes Claude, how to get it and which account it needs (Use online tools and platforms)
  • find and explain Claude's privacy and data settings (Apply safe and responsible practices online: protect personal information and privacy of yourself and others)
  • use Claude with a clear prompt to make a materials list, explain a regulation and write a message to a foreman (Use common digital tools to complete tasks: select appropriate digital tools based on your goals)
  • check each answer against their own math or the official source (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)

Complexity: levels 1 and 2.

Pre-assessment (2 min)

  1. Have you used Claude? On a phone, a computer or at work?
  2. What do you think happens to what you type into it?

Learning tasks (45 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Show the official help page on the projector. Go through who makes it, how to get it, accounts and plans. 1.1 Complete Task 1. Help page, projector 8 min
1.2 Walk through the privacy settings on the projector. Stress the do-not-paste list. 1.2 Complete Task 2. Claude settings or help page 6 min
2.1 Teach the five-part prompt pattern. Model the worked example and do the drywall math on the board. 2.1 Follow the worked example. Whiteboard 5 min
2.2 Circulate. Ask learners to show their own post count before they read Claude's. 2.2 Complete Task 3. Claude, calculator 8 min
2.3 Open Part 11 on worksafebc.com on the projector after learners try Task 4. 2.3 Complete Task 4. Claude, worksafebc.com 8 min
3.1 Read the Task 5 scenario aloud. Check that no real names or private details go in. 3.1 Complete Task 5. Claude 6 min
3.2 Take two or three check-it cards. Compare what different learners got from Claude. 3.2 Complete Task 6 and share. Learner page 4 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Show me, or tell me, where the Claude setting is that controls whether your chats are kept or used to improve the AI.
  2. Claude says a 21.6 m fence with posts every 2.4 m needs 9 posts. Is it right? Show your math.
  3. Name one thing you would never type into Claude at work.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • who makes Claude, how to get it, accounts, age and plans
  • what Claude can do and where it shows up at work
  • Claude's privacy and data settings, and what never to paste
  • a five-part prompt pattern
  • checking a materials list with BEDMAS
  • checking a plain-language explanation against WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 11.2
  • writing a short, clear message to a foreman and checking it

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 5 + 3 = 60 min.

Notes for the instructor

  • Check the help page and the settings menus the day before class. Menu names and plans change often. If something has moved, use what the company's own page says now.
  • Do not ask learners to buy a plan or to sign up with a work email. If learners have no account, demonstrate Claude on the projector and have learners do the checking and the check-it card.
  • Never let a learner type a real password, SIN, health detail or real client information. Tasks use the fictional Northgate Framing Ltd. and Alder Street site.
  • Assess product cards using the official page and date the learner records. Separate retention, memory and model-training controls; one switch does not necessarily control all three. Accept a documented unavailable control.
  • Answers from Claude change from day to day and from person to person. Mark the checking, not Claude's wording.
  • Lessons 12 to 15 use the same six tasks for Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini so learners can compare. Lesson 16 brings the four together and covers workplace policy and job applications.
  • Lesson 11 explains how chatbots work and the check-it loop. The older AI literacy sequence goes further with briefs, files and audits.

Evidence to check

Retain the fictional prompt, response excerpt, independent arithmetic and correction decision. Record the official help URL and access date for product and privacy findings.
