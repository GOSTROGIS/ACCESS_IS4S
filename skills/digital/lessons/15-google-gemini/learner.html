Using Google Gemini

Tell the Gemini app apart from Gemini in Gmail and Docs, find its activity settings, and use it for a materials list, a code explanation and a message to your foreman, checking each answer.

Why this matters on the job

Gemini is Google's AI assistant. It has its own app, and it also appears inside Gmail, Google Docs and other Google Workspace apps that many contractors and suppliers use. If your employer runs on Google Workspace, you may see an Ask Gemini button at work. Gemini on a personal Google Account and Gemini on a work account follow different data rules, so you need to know which one you are in.

Key ideas

Who makes it and how to get it

  • Made by Google.
  • Web: gemini.google.com. Phone: the Gemini mobile app for Android, iPhone and iPad.
  • On some devices you can use some features without signing in. You must sign in to use the Gemini app on Android and to keep your activity.
  • Account: a personal Google Account, a work Google Account with a qualifying Google Workspace edition (you must be 18 or over), or a school account if your school turned Gemini on.
  • Age: to use Gemini with a Google AI plan in most countries, you must be 13 or over, or the age that applies in your country.
  • Plans: Gemini has usage limits. Paid plans for personal accounts are called Google AI Plus, Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra. Check the price for Canada on Google's plan page in your account. You do not need a paid plan for this lesson.

Official help page: Gemini Apps Help, Use Gemini Apps, https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13275745

What it can do

Google's help pages describe these features in the Gemini app:

  • upload files: documents, spreadsheets, photos, videos and more, up to 10 files in one prompt
  • create files you can download or send to Google Drive, such as Google Docs and Sheets, PDF, Word (.docx), Excel (.xlsx) and CSV
  • generate images
  • Gemini Live: in the phone app, tap Live and talk back and forth. You can also share your camera or your screen.
  • Sources: sometimes Gemini shows a Sources button with links, which can include public websites and files you uploaded
  • Double-check: a feature that uses Google Search to find content likely similar to, or different from, what Gemini wrote

Features depend on your plan, your device and where you live. If you do not see a feature, check the help page.

Where you may meet it at work

  • Gemini app with a work account. Needs a qualifying Google Workspace edition and age 18 or over. Google says that with a work account, your chats and uploaded files are not reviewed by human reviewers or used to improve generative AI models. Your Workspace administrator controls the Keep activity setting.
  • Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive and Chat. Google calls this Google Workspace with Gemini. At work it needs an eligible Workspace plan. On a personal account it needs Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra. Look for Ask Gemini at the top right of Gmail or Docs. In Docs it can open as a side panel or a bottom bar.
  • Gemini in Workspace apps has its own usage limits, separate from the Gemini app.

Privacy and data settings

These settings are for a personal Google Account. On a work account, your administrator controls them.

  • Keep activity. On gemini.google.com, select Settings & help > Activity, or go to myactivity.google.com/product/gemini. If you are 18 or over, Keep activity is on by default. You can turn it off and delete your activity there.
  • Auto-delete. By default, Gemini activity older than 18 months is deleted automatically. You can change this on the Activity page.
  • Human review. Google's help page says that with a personal account, your chats in Gemini may be reviewed by human reviewers and used to improve Google's products and services. Do not type anything you would not want another person to read.
  • Audio and Live video. A separate setting, "Improve Google services with your audio and Gemini Live videos and screenshares", is off by default.
  • Location. Gemini may use your general location, or your device's precise location if you allow it.

Whatever the settings, do not paste your Social Insurance Number, health information, client names and addresses, or your employer's private information into any chatbot. Lesson 16 covers this in detail.

Limits

  • Google says Gemini can make mistakes and that you should not rely on its responses for professional advice.
  • Even when Gemini shows sources, Google says it can cite the wrong document or make one up. Open the source and read it.
  • Gemini does not know your job site unless you tell it, it remembers from past chats, or (in Workspace) it can see a file or email about it.

A prompt pattern that works

Give Gemini five things:

  1. Who you are. "I am a first-year carpentry apprentice in BC."
  2. The facts. Measurements, quantities and the situation. Leave out private details.
  3. What you want. "Make a materials list."
  4. The format. "As a table." "In three short sentences." "Under 50 words."
  5. Show the work. "Show your math." "Give the section number and a link to the official page."

Worked example: drywall sheets

Rae, a drywall apprentice, needs sheets for one side of a wall 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no openings. For this fictional calculation, use sheets exactly 1.2 m by 2.4 m. These are practice metric dimensions, not an exact conversion of 4 ft by 8 ft. Check actual product dimensions before ordering.

Rae types: "I am a drywall apprentice in BC. One side of a wall is 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no doors or windows. Sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m. How many sheets do I need? Show your math."

Gemini's exact words change from day to day, so they are not printed here. Rae checks whatever it says with her own math:

  • Wall area: 4.8 × 2.4 = 11.52 m²
  • One sheet: 1.2 × 2.4 = 2.88 m²
  • Sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4

Another way: 4.8 ÷ 1.2 = 4 sheets standing side by side, each 2.4 m tall.

If Gemini also says 4, Rae uses it. If it says something else, Rae trusts her own math and asks Gemini to explain the difference. Before ordering, she asks her journeyperson whether to add extra for waste.

Practice

All quantities, sites and people in the practice prompts are fictional. The fence exercise assumes no gates or waste; two bags per post is supplied practice data, not a construction specification.

Do Tasks 3, 4 and 5 in Gemini. If you do not have an account, your instructor shows Gemini on the projector and you do the checking. Never type a real client's name, your SIN or your employer's private information.

Task 3 (level 2) Materials list. Type this prompt into Gemini:

I am building a straight wood fence 21.6 m long. Posts go every 2.4 m, with a post at each end. Each section between posts gets 2 rails, each 2.4 m long. Each post needs 2 bags of concrete mix. Make a materials list with quantities. Show your math.

Then work it out yourself. Write the correct number of posts, rails and bags. Did Gemini get the number of posts right?

Task 4 (level 2) Explain a code clause, then check it. Type:

In plain words for a first-year apprentice, explain what WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 11.2, subsections (2) to (5), say about choosing a type of fall protection. Put the options in order.

Then open Part 11 at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-11-fall-protection and read section 11.2. Write the correct order of the four options, and one thing Gemini got right or wrong.

Task 5 (level 2) Message to your foreman. You are an apprentice with Northgate Framing Ltd. (a fictional company). At 7:10 a.m. the lumber delivery to the Alder Street site came with 40 studs. The packing slip says 60. Ask Gemini to write a short, polite text to your foreman, Dale, that says what happened and asks what to do. Check the message: are the time and numbers right, is there no blame, is there one clear question, and is there nothing private in it? Write the message you would send.
