# Find and measure a site on a map

Checked in Google Maps on a computer (web browser) and the City of Vancouver's new VanMap Viewer, 2026-11-06. The older public VanMap Viewer was scheduled to be retired in the third quarter of 2026, so use the new one. On a phone, Google Maps uses touch and hold instead of right-click.

## Why this matters on the job

Crews get sent to new addresses every week, and some sites have no street number yet, only a lot or a set of coordinates. Estimators measure how far a run of fence or pipe goes before they visit. Contractors check a property's zoning before they quote. Online maps make all of this faster, but they are not surveys, and they do not show what is buried underground.

## Key ideas

### Latitude and longitude

A point on Earth can be written as two numbers:

- **Latitude**: how far north or south of the equator. In BC it is a positive number, because BC is north of the equator.
- **Longitude**: how far east or west. In BC it is a negative number, because BC is west of Greenwich, England.

Google Maps writes them in decimal degrees, latitude first, with a comma between: for example `49.2610, -123.1139`. Use a period for the decimal, never a comma.

### Google Maps on a computer

- **Find coordinates**: right-click the place on the map. The latitude and longitude appear at the top of the pop-up. Click them to copy.
- **Go to coordinates**: type them in the search box.
- **Measure a straight line**: right-click the starting point, choose **Measure distance**, then click each next point. The total shows at the bottom in miles and kilometres. Measuring does not work in Lite mode.

A measured straight line is not the driving distance. Roads bend.

### VanMap (City of Vancouver)

VanMap is the City of Vancouver's map site at maps.vancouver.ca. Its **VanMap Viewer** (vanmapviewer.vancouver.ca) shows property, zoning, water and sewer information and aerial photos for addresses in Vancouver. You must agree to the VanMap Terms of Use before it opens.

The Viewer has tools for:

| Tool | Use it to |
|---|---|
| **Property Viewer Report** | See details about a property, including its zoning |
| **Map Layers** | Turn layers of information on and off |
| **Legend** | See what the colours and symbols mean |
| **Measurement** | Measure approximate lengths and areas |
| **Draw** | Mark up the map |
| **Basemap Gallery** | Switch between map and aerial views |
| **Print** | Print or save the map |

The City says its map measurements are approximate and that only a BC Land Surveyor can verify property measurements. The City's Digital Zoning Map also warns that the map is a snapshot and may not be accurate when you view it. Other BC cities and regional districts have their own map sites with similar tools.

### What no online map tells you: buried lines

Before anyone digs, even for a fence post, BC 1 Call must be contacted for a "locate". It is free. Request online at bc1c.ca or call 1-800-474-6886. Members who own buried lines respond with instructions, within 3 business days for a standard request, and you must not dig until you have every response. An online map that shows a sewer line is not a locate.

### Worked example

A foreman sends Sam the coordinates `49.2610, -123.1139` for a pickup.

1. Sam opens Google Maps and types `49.2610, -123.1139` in the search box. The pin lands at Vancouver City Hall, 453 West 12th Avenue.
2. Sam right-clicks the parking area entrance, chooses **Measure distance**, and clicks the front steps. The card at the bottom shows the straight-line distance.
3. The job is in Vancouver, so Sam opens the VanMap Viewer, agrees to the Terms of Use, searches `453 W 12th Ave`, and opens the **Property Viewer Report** to see the zoning.
