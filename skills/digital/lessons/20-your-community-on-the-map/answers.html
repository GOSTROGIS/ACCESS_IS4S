Put your community on the map

Find a place that matters to you and the training site on a map, say how far apart they are and in which direction, read a map coloured by numbers, and decide whose data a map shows and who gets to share it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Use online tools and platforms; Use digital information; Apply safe and responsible practices online

Task 1 (level 1)

Answer: two pairs of coordinates in decimal degrees, latitude first, to 4 decimal places, with a place name the learner chose.

Check: both latitudes are positive and both longitudes are negative if both places are in BC. The training site's numbers match what the instructor shows on the projector, give or take the last digit depending on where the learner clicked. No home address is written down.

Task 2 (level 1)

Answer: depends on the place chosen.

Steps a good answer shows:

  1. The bigger latitude is farther north. If the learner's place has the bigger latitude, it is north of the training site.
  2. The more negative longitude is farther west. If the learner's place has the more negative longitude, it is west.
  3. Combine them, for example "north-west". If one difference is much smaller than the other (as in the worked example, 0.0020 against 0.0159), use only the bigger one.

For a place outside BC or outside Canada, the same rules hold as long as both places are north of the equator and west of Greenwich. If not, the instructor helps the learner read the signs.

Task 3 (level 2)

Answer: a sentence with a distance, a unit and a direction, for example "My community is about 240 km north-west of the training site." (A learner's own numbers will differ.)

Why the travel distance is longer: the straight line goes over mountains, water and private land. Roads bend around hills and lakes, ferries follow set routes, and some communities are reached only by boat or plane. A good response gives both distances when a route distance is available and names at least one reason. When no driving route is available, accept the straight-line distance plus the usual travel method, as the task allows; do not require an invented travel distance.

Task 4 (level 1)

Answer: (a) Harbour, 41. (b) East Hills and Cedar Flats. (c) 10 to 19.

Task 5 (level 2)

Steps (division and multiplication left to right, BEDMAS): 12 ÷ 4,000 = 0.003, × 1,000 = 3. 27 ÷ 9,000 = 0.003, × 1,000 = 3. 7 ÷ 3,500 = 0.002, × 1,000 = 2. 4 ÷ 1,000 = 0.004, × 1,000 = 4. 18 ÷ 6,000 = 0.003, × 1,000 = 3. 41 ÷ 20,500 = 0.002, × 1,000 = 2.

Answer: Cedar Flats has the highest rate, 4 per 1,000. The map does not show that. Cedar Flats has no shading because its count is the smallest, while Harbour, tied with East Hills for the lowest rate, is solid. A map coloured by count shows where the most people are, not where something is most common.

Task 6 (level 2)

Points a good response covers (any three):

  • Ask each learner first, privately, whether they want their community, name or photo on the map. Anyone can say no without giving a reason.
  • Tell them who will see it (only the class, families at graduation, social media) and where the file will be kept.
  • Offer choices: a pin without a name, a nearby town or region instead of a community, or no pin at all.
  • Do not use program records to fill in the map. Each person gives their own information.
  • For First Nations information, respect the OCAP® principles set out by FNIGC: the people and Nation the information is about have a say in how it is collected, used and shared. Check with the program or the Nation if the map would show information about a community, not just one person's choice.
  • Delete or hand back the map file after the event if that is what people agreed.

Task 7 (level 2)

Answer: the partner repeats the sentence back with the same distance, unit and direction. The learner names one limit of the demo map, for example: it does not show where in a district the apprentices are, it says nothing about any one person, or a dark district may just have more people.

Check yourself

  1. −126.3. It is the more negative longitude.
  2. Which shade or pattern stands for which range of numbers.
  3. A count is bigger wherever more people live. Dividing by the population shows how common something is, so areas of different sizes can be compared fairly.
  4. Ownership, control, access and possession.
  5. Ask that person, and tell them who will see the map.
