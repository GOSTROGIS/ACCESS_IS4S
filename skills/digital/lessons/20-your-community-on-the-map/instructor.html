Put your community on the map

Find a place that matters to you and the training site on a map, say how far apart they are and in which direction, read a map coloured by numbers, and decide whose data a map shows and who gets to share it.

Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: "A camp job is posted. The ad gives the site's coordinates. How far is that from home, and which way?" Today each learner puts a place that matters to them on the map beside the training site, says how far and which way, reads a map coloured by numbers, and talks about whose information belongs on a map. Every learner chooses their own place. Nobody has to share where they live.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • find two places in Google Maps, read their coordinates and say which is farther north and farther west (Use online tools and platforms. Use digital information: navigate digital content)
  • measure a straight-line distance and say it in a sentence with a direction (Use online tools and platforms)
  • read a choropleth map and its legend, work out a rate per 1,000 and say what the map cannot show (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
  • decide what consent is needed before people's information goes on a map, and name the OCAP® principles as set out by FNIGC (Apply safe and responsible practices online: protect personal information and privacy of yourself and others)

Complexity: levels 1 and 2.

Pre-assessment (2 min)

  1. Which is farther north, a place at latitude 49.2 or one at 54.0?
  2. A map shows one town dark red and another pale. What do you need to know before you can say what that means?

(Answers: 54.0. The legend, and what was counted.)

Learning tasks (44 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Explain that each learner picks their own place: home community, Nation's community, a town they know or a public landmark. Say clearly that nobody shows a home address and nobody has to explain their choice. 1.1 Choose a place. Whiteboard 5 min
1.2 Show the training site in Google Maps and right-click for coordinates (Lesson 9). 1.2 Complete Task 1. Computers, Google Maps 6 min
1.3 Model the worked example on the board: compare latitudes, then longitudes, then decide if one difference is too small to mention. 1.3 Complete Task 2. Whiteboard 6 min
1.4 Demonstrate Measure distance from City Hall to BC Place, then the sentence. Ask why a ferry or road trip is longer. 1.4 Complete Task 3. Projector, Google Maps 7 min
2.1 Show the demo map. Read the legend together. Stress that the data and places are fictional. 2.1 Complete Task 4. Learner page 5 min
2.2 Work North Ridge's rate on the board: 12 ÷ 4,000 × 1,000 = 3. Remind learners of BEDMAS: division and multiplication left to right. 2.2 Complete Task 5. Whiteboard 6 min
3.1 Read the "Whose data is it?" section aloud, including FNIGC's own wording and the trademark line. Show FNIGC's page on the projector. Then read the slideshow scenario. 3.1 Complete Task 6 in pairs. Projector, fnigc.ca/ocap-training 7 min
3.2 Pair learners for the share. 3.2 Complete Task 7. None 2 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Two places: A at 50.6745, -120.3273 and B at 49.8880, -119.4960. Which way is B from A?
  2. On the demo map, Harbour is darkest. Does that mean apprentices are most common there? Explain.
  3. Name one thing you would do before putting a classmate's community on a shared map.

(Answers: south-east, because B's latitude is smaller and its longitude is less negative. No, Harbour has the biggest count but a rate tied for lowest with East Hills, 2 per 1,000, because it has the most people. Ask the classmate first and tell them who will see it.)

Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

  • choosing what to share on a map
  • reading coordinates to find direction: bigger latitude is farther north, more negative longitude is farther west in BC
  • measuring a straight-line distance and saying it in a sentence
  • choropleth maps, legends, and what they cannot show
  • counts compared with rates per 1,000
  • consent before sharing people's information, and the First Nations principles of OCAP® as set out by FNIGC

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 44 + 5 + 4 = 60 min.

Notes for the instructor

  • Never use real program or participant data in this lesson, including counts by Nation or community. If an ACCESS reach map or dashboard is shown, keep it on its fictional demo setting. The Granite Valley map and numbers are invented for this lesson.
  • Do not add facts about any Nation, territory or community to this lesson. Learners bring their own knowledge of their own place, and they choose how much to say. If a learner shares something about their community's history or territory, it is theirs to share. Do not write it into class materials.
  • Some learners may not want to show any home place, for safety or personal reasons. A public landmark or a town they have worked in is a full answer. Do not ask why.
  • OCAP®: use only the FNIGC page for definitions. FNIGC asks that any publication using the OCAP® name include the line "OCAP® is a registered trademark of the First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC)" and a link to https://fnigc.ca/ocap-training/, which the learner page does. FNIGC also notes that OCAP® is specifically First Nations, not Indigenous, principles. For Métis or Inuit learners, do not stretch OCAP® to cover them. Say that this lesson only covers what FNIGC says.
  • Post-assessment question 1 uses practice numbers only. Do not name a place for them. Only the direction matters.
  • Links: Lessons 9 and 19. Rates per 1,000 tie to the percent and ratio work in the numeracy lessons.

Evidence to check

Collect the chosen public-place sentence, fictional rate calculations and consent decisions. Do not retain home addresses or community knowledge shared orally without permission.
