Digital · Lesson 06 · Answers

Email, calendar invites and video calls

Write a clear work email with an attachment, put a meeting in your calendar, answer an invitation and join a Teams video call ready to talk.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Use online tools and platforms; Apply safe and responsible practices online; Use digital devices including computers, tablets, smart phones, and other handheld devices

Task 1 (level 1)

Sample answers. Any subject that says what the email is about, and when or who if it matters, is correct.

  • hi → Question about Monday start time, Jordan Lee
  • question → Do I need my own hard hat for orientation?
  • stuff for monday → Tools and PPE list for Monday Nov 9 job start

A good subject is short, specific and would make sense in a list of fifty emails.

Task 2 (level 2)

Points a good response covers:

  • To: the instructor's practice address. No Cc unless asked.
  • Subject names the document and week, for example Time sheet, week of Nov 2, Jordan Lee.
  • Greeting with a name, for example Hello Mr. Paul,.
  • The point in the first sentence: My time sheet for this week is attached.
  • An attachment that opens, with a clear file name.
  • Name (and signature if set up) at the end.
  • Polite, plain language. No slang, no all-capitals.

Task 3 (level 1)

Answer: Reply. The instructor asked for replies to them only, and the rest of the class does not need your answer. Reply all would fill everyone's inbox.

Task 4 (level 2)

Answer: an event titled Site safety orientation on next Monday, 7:00 a.m. to 8:30 a.m., location Riverbend Builders yard (fictional), body Bring steel-toe boots and hard hat. No attendees, so it is saved rather than sent.

Steps: Calendar > New event, type the title, set date and times, type the location, type the note in the body, Save.

Check: open the calendar on Monday's date and the event shows from 7:00 to 8:30.

Task 5 (level 1)

Any of the three is correct if the reason fits:

  • Accept: I will be there.
  • Tentative: I might be there, for example I am waiting to hear about a shift.
  • Decline: I cannot attend. A good response adds a short note or emails the organizer.

The point is that the organizer gets an answer.

Task 6 (level 2)

Observed actions, in order: background blurred on the pre-join screen, mic checked, joined, unmuted, said name, muted, raised hand, typed Ready in chat, lowered hand.

Record anything the learner needed help with. A learner with the camera off who does every other step has met the task.

Task 7 (level 2)

Observed: Share selected, one window chosen (the PDF), shared for about ten seconds, sharing stopped.

Things to close or hide before sharing at work, any of: personal email, banking or social media tabs, chat pop-ups and notifications, other people's private files, passwords or anything on the desktop that should not be seen. Sharing one window instead of the whole screen also helps.

Check yourself

  1. Everyone can see who was Cc'd. Nobody can see who was Bcc'd.
  2. Only when every person on the message needs your answer.
  3. So the organizer knows whether you are coming. An ignored invitation shows no answer.
  4. It does not guarantee that everything sensitive behind you is hidden.
  5. Raise (raise hand).
