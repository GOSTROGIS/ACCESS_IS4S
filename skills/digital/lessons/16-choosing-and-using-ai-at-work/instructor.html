Choosing and using AI at work

Compare Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini for trades tasks, keep private information out, follow your employer's AI rules, disclose AI help, and use AI honestly on a job application.

Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: tell a short fictional story. A new hire wants a cleaner schedule for the week, so she pastes the client list, with addresses and door codes, into a chatbot on her personal phone. Her lead hand finds out. Ask: what went wrong, and was the chatbot the problem? Today learners put Lessons 11 to 15 together: which assistant to use, which account, what never to paste, how to follow an employer's rules, and how to use AI honestly on a résumé.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • compare Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini and choose the right tool and account for a trades task (Use common digital tools to complete tasks: select appropriate digital tools based on your goals)
  • keep SINs, health information, client data and employer information out of AI tools (Apply safe and responsible practices online: protect personal information and privacy of yourself and others)
  • read an employer AI policy and apply it (Use online tools and platforms)
  • disclose AI help and cite the real source (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
  • tailor a résumé bullet with AI and check that every claim is true (Use online tools and platforms: use online forms for job applications)

Complexity: levels 2 and 3.

Pre-assessment (2 min)

  1. Does anyone's workplace have a rule about AI? What does it say?
  2. Would you put your SIN on a job application? Why or why not?

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Your employer approves Copilot Chat in your work account. Your friend says Gemini on your phone is better. Which do you use for a client email, and why?
  2. List three things you would take out of a document before pasting it into any chatbot.
  3. An AI-written résumé bullet says you have a forklift ticket. You do not. What do you do?

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini side by side, personal and work versions
  • choosing the approved tool and the work account for work
  • what never to paste: SIN, health information, client data, employer information, other people's details
  • what an employer AI policy covers and what to do without one
  • disclosing AI help and citing the real source
  • using AI on a job application without adding anything untrue

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 5 + 3 = 60 min.

Notes for the instructor

  • Task 5 needs an AI assistant. If learners have no account, demonstrate on the projector with the fictional truth list, then have learners do the claim-by-claim check and write their own final bullets.
  • The résumé builder link goes to the ACCESS Writing tools at skills/writing/resume-builder/. Check that it opens before class.
  • Learners bring very different digital comfort. Pair a confident learner with a newer one for Tasks 1 and 5.
  • If a learner says they already pasted private information into a chatbot, help privately: show them how to delete the chat in that product (Lessons 12 to 15) and, for work information, tell their supervisor.
  • Product facts in the table come from Lessons 12 to 15 and were checked 2026-11-06. Check the help pages again before class.
  • The older AI literacy sequence covers keeping an AI work record, which fits well after Task 4.

Evidence to check

Collect the policy-rule references and claim-by-claim résumé check. Any résumé-builder output must be labelled fictional practice and kept separate from real applications.
