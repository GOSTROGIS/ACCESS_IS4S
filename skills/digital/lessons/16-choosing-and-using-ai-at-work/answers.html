Choosing and using AI at work

Compare Claude, ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot and Gemini for trades tasks, keep private information out, follow your employer's AI rules, disclose AI help, and use AI honestly on a job application.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Use common digital tools to complete tasks; Use digital information; Apply safe and responsible practices online; Use online tools and platforms

Task 1 (level 2)

  • a. Microsoft Copilot Chat, signed in with the work account (in Teams or Outlook). It is already approved and set up by the employer, and Microsoft says Copilot Chat prompts and responses are not used to train its foundation models.
  • b. Gemini in Google Docs, with the work account. The document is already in Docs, and work-account Gemini chats are not reviewed by people or used to improve Google's AI models, according to Google.
  • c. Any of the four with voice, on a personal account. Claude voice mode, ChatGPT Voice, Copilot Voice and Gemini Live all let you talk and hear answers. Study material is not private company information, so a personal account is fine. Check anything you are unsure of against course material.
  • d. None. Do not paste the bid. A bid price is the employer's private information. Use a calculator, or ask your supervisor which approved tool to use.

A good answer always names both the tool and the account.

Task 2 (level 1)

  • a. OK. Only measurements, nothing private.
  • b. NO. The SIN is confidential.
  • c. NO. Client name, address and door code identify a person and their home.
  • d. OK. A public regulation section number.
  • e. NO. Another person's health information.
  • f. OK. Your own notes with no names.
  • g. NO. Confidential employer or owner documents.
  • h. OK. The posting is public.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • a. No. Rule 1 allows only Copilot Chat with the Northgate work account for company work.
  • b. A lead hand must check it first (rule 3).
  • c. Check it against the WorkSafeBC website before sharing it (rule 4). Use the check-it loop.
  • d. No. Rule 2: do not enter client addresses into any AI tool.

Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. Points a good response covers:

  • (a) a short, plain note, for example: "I used an AI assistant to help organize this handout. I checked every rule myself."
  • (b) the real source with enough detail to find it, for example: "Ladder rules: WorkSafeBC Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, worksafebc.com, checked 2026-11-06." Accept any WorkSafeBC page the learner actually opened, with the date they checked it.
  • The source line names WorkSafeBC, not the chatbot.

Task 5 (level 3)

Open task. A strong response:

  • gives the AI the truth list and the posting, and asks for bullets using only those facts
  • lists every claim from the AI bullets in a table and marks each true or false
  • removes anything not on the truth list. Common AI additions to catch: "paid experience", "installed wiring for clients", "journeyperson", a ticket not on the list, or a year of experience the learner does not have
  • keeps the posting's words where they are true: "electrical foundation", "WHMIS", "hand and power tools", "communication"

Example final bullets:

  • "Completed a 6-month electrical foundation program (2026), using drills, saws and fish tape safely in shop labs. WHMIS certified."
  • "Volunteered to set up lighting and power for a community event, working with the event crew."

Accept other wording if every claim is on the truth list.

Task 6 (level 2)

  • a. H. Fixing spelling does not change the facts.
  • b. N. A first-year apprentice does not hold a Red Seal. It is false and will come out in the interview or a credential check. Remove it.
  • c. H. Practising and then answering in your own words is honest.
  • d. N. The employer asked directly. Answer honestly.

Check yourself

  1. The tool your employer approved, signed in with your work account.
  2. Any four of: your SIN, health information, client names, addresses or codes, your employer's private information (bids, contracts, confidential drawings, passwords), coworkers' information, information or photos of children.
  3. Ask your supervisor before using AI for work.
  4. The real, official source (for example the WorkSafeBC regulation section), not the chatbot.
  5. It must be true, and you must be able to explain it in an interview.
