# Passwords, passkeys and phishing

ACCESS Indigenous Skills4Success · Digital · Lesson plan

| Session | Total length |
|---|---|
| Digital 8 | 60 min |

## Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: tell a short fictional story. An apprentice gets a text that his pay deposit failed and taps the link. Two weeks later his pay goes to someone else's account. Ask: what would have stopped it? Today learners build the habits that stop most account theft: strong passphrases, a second factor, and a pause before tapping.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- build a passphrase that meets Cyber Centre guidance and rate practice passwords (Apply safe and responsible practices online: know how to create a password to protect data)
- explain MFA and passkeys and respond correctly to an unexpected sign-in request (Apply safe and responsible practices online: protect data and devices from online risks and threats)
- spot phishing in email, text, voice and QR codes (Apply safe and responsible practices online: know how to avoid phishing emails)
- check a suspicious message through a separate channel and report fraud (Apply safe and responsible practices online. Use online tools and platforms)

Complexity: levels 1 and 2.

## Pre-assessment (2 min)

1. Hands up if you use the same password on more than one account. (No other details.)
2. What would make you suspicious of a text message?

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Make up a new practice passphrase on paper and show me it meets the 4-word, 15-character guidance.
2. A message from "your supervisor" asks you to buy gift cards for a client today and send the codes. What do you do?
3. What is one account you will turn on MFA for this week? (Name the type of account only.)

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- passphrases of at least 4 words and 15 characters, and passwords of at least 12 characters
- a different passphrase for each account, never shared
- password managers and their limits
- multi-factor authentication, number matching and unexpected approval requests
- passkeys
- phishing by email, text, voice and QR code, and the signs to look for
- checking through a separate channel and reporting to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 5 + 3 = 60 min.

## Notes for the instructor

- Never ask a learner to type, show or say a real password, PIN, security answer or recovery code. If a learner starts to, stop them kindly.
- If a learner thinks they have been scammed, help them privately. Point them to their bank through a number they already have, the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, and local police. Do not discuss the details in front of the group.
- Phishing examples in this lesson use the fictional Riverbend Builders and invented addresses. Do not forward real scam messages to learners' accounts.
- The [AI literacy sequence](../../ai-literacy/learner.md) covers privacy when using AI tools. The Digital chapter's Case C in [learner.md](../../learner.md) gives another suspicious-request practice.
