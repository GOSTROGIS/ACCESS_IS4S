# Using ChatGPT

Product facts on this page were checked against OpenAI's own pages (openai.com and help.openai.com) on 2026-11-06. ChatGPT changes often. Your instructor checks the help page again before class.

## Why this matters on the job

ChatGPT is an AI chatbot from OpenAI. You can use it in a browser or a phone app to explain a rule, work through a materials list or word a message. Some employers give workers ChatGPT through a business plan. Knowing its settings helps you protect your own information and your employer's, and checking its work keeps mistakes off the job site.

## Key ideas

### Who makes it and how to get it

- Made by **OpenAI**.
- **Web:** chatgpt.com. **Phone apps:** iOS and Android.
- In supported countries you can try ChatGPT **before you make an account**. Without an account, the chat is not saved. An account saves your chat history and lets you export your data.
- OpenAI recommends a personal email address for personal use and a **separate account with your work email** for work.
- **Minimum age:** 13. Anyone under 18 needs a parent's or guardian's permission.
- **Personal plans:** Free, Go, Plus and Pro. **Work plans:** Business, Enterprise and Edu.
- OpenAI says ads may appear on the Free plan in certain countries, and the Go plan may include ads.
- **Price:** check openai.com/chatgpt/pricing for the price shown to you in Canada. You do not need a paid plan for this lesson.

Official help page: OpenAI Help Center, *ChatGPT Free Tier FAQ*, https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9275245-chatgpt-free-tier-faq

### What it can do

OpenAI's help page lists these for the **Free** plan:

- search the web for up-to-date information
- upload images or files and ask about them
- analyze data
- create images
- discover and use GPTs (versions of ChatGPT set up for one job)
- store files in Library (500 MB on Free)

You can also use **ChatGPT Voice** to talk and hear spoken answers. Choose voice options in **Settings > Voice**. The Free plan has limited voice use.

Features depend on your plan, your device and where you live. If you do not see a feature, check the help page.

### Where you may meet it at work

- ChatGPT **Business**, **Enterprise** and **Edu** are workspaces run by an organization. OpenAI says that by default it does not use content from these workspaces to train its models.
- One account can belong to more than one workspace. Check which workspace you are in before you type work information.
- If your employer gives you ChatGPT through work, use the work workspace for work and follow your employer's AI policy (Lesson 16).

### Privacy and data settings

- **Model training.** On the web: open your account menu, select **Settings > Data controls**, then **Improve the model for everyone**. On the phone: open the sidebar, tap your profile icon, then **Data controls**. On personal Free, Plus and Pro accounts this is **on** by default. When it is off, new chats are not used to train OpenAI models, and they still stay in your history.
- **Temporary Chat.** A temporary chat does not appear in your history, does not create or update memories and is not used to train models. OpenAI may keep it for up to 30 days for safety.
- **Memory.** Go to **Settings > Personalization**. "Reference saved memories" covers details ChatGPT saved. "Reference chat history" lets it use past chats. Turning off saved memories also turns off chat history.
- **Your data.** Signed-in Free, Go, Plus and Pro users can export their data or delete their account from settings.

Whatever the settings, do not paste your Social Insurance Number, health information, client names and addresses, or your employer's private information into any chatbot. Lesson 16 covers this in detail.

### Limits

- OpenAI says ChatGPT can give wrong facts, make up sources and sound confident even when it is wrong. It also has a knowledge cutoff unless it uses tools such as search.
- Some tools on the Free plan have their own usage limits.
- ChatGPT does not know your job site, your employer or your trade unless you tell it or memory saved it.

### A prompt pattern that works

Give ChatGPT five things:

1. **Who you are.** "I am a first-year carpentry apprentice in BC."
2. **The facts.** Measurements, quantities and the situation. Leave out private details.
3. **What you want.** "Make a materials list."
4. **The format.** "As a table." "In three short sentences." "Under 50 words."
5. **Show the work.** "Show your math." "Give the section number and a link to the official page."

### Worked example: drywall sheets

Rae, a drywall apprentice, needs sheets for one side of a wall 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no openings. The sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m (4 ft by 8 ft).

Rae types: "I am a drywall apprentice in BC. One side of a wall is 4.8 m long and 2.4 m high, with no doors or windows. Sheets are 1.2 m by 2.4 m. How many sheets do I need? Show your math."

ChatGPT's exact words change from day to day, so they are not printed here. Rae checks whatever it says with her own math:

- Wall area: 4.8 × 2.4 = 11.52 m²
- One sheet: 1.2 × 2.4 = 2.88 m²
- Sheets: 11.52 ÷ 2.88 = 4

Another way: 4.8 ÷ 1.2 = 4 sheets standing side by side, each 2.4 m tall.

If ChatGPT also says 4, Rae uses it. If it says something else, Rae trusts her own math and asks ChatGPT to explain the difference. Before ordering, she asks her journeyperson whether to add extra for waste.
