Read a GPS fix and share a location

Convert coordinates between degrees-minutes-seconds and decimal degrees, read how accurate your phone's location is, send a meeting point a crew can find, decide when to share your live location, and read a UTM easting and northing.

Task 1 (level 1)

Answer: (a) degrees and decimal minutes (DMM), (b) decimal degrees (DD), (c) degrees, minutes, seconds (DMS).

All three are BC Place. Clues: DD has no ' or " marks and uses a minus sign. DMS has both ' and ". DMM has only ' and the minutes have decimals.

Task 2 (level 2)

Answer: 49.2608, -123.1139.

Steps:

  • Latitude: 15 ÷ 60 = 0.25 and 39 ÷ 3600 = 0.010833… Then 49 + 0.25 + 0.010833… = 49.260833… → 49.2608.
  • Longitude: 6 ÷ 60 = 0.1 and 50 ÷ 3600 = 0.013888… Then 123 + 0.1 + 0.013888… = 123.113888… → 123.1139. W means negative: −123.1139.

Typed into Google Maps, the pin lands on Vancouver City Hall.

Task 3 (level 2)

Answer: 49.2767, -123.1119.

Steps:

  • Latitude: 16 ÷ 60 = 0.266666… and 36 ÷ 3600 = 0.01. Then 49 + 0.266666… + 0.01 = 49.276666… → 49.2767.
  • Longitude: 6 ÷ 60 = 0.1 and 43 ÷ 3600 = 0.011944… Then 123 + 0.1 + 0.011944… = 123.111944… → 123.1119, negative because of W: −123.1119.

The DMS and DD versions land in the same place, BC Place. BC Place is farther north: its latitude, 49.2767, is bigger than City Hall's, 49.2608. A bigger latitude means farther north.

Task 4 (level 2)

Answer: the minus sign is missing from the longitude. Without it Google Maps reads 123.1119 as east of Greenwich instead of west, and the pin lands on the other side of the world, in northeastern China.

A good response says how to fix it: 49.2767, -123.1119, and adds the habit of checking that a BC longitude starts with a minus sign.

Task 5 (level 1)

Answer: depends on the phone and the building. A typical response: "Inside the classroom there was a wide light blue circle around the dot, so the phone was not sure where I was. By the window the circle got smaller, so the location was more certain."

A good response explains that the circle estimates uncertainty, does not guarantee a boundary, and a smaller circle indicates greater estimated certainty, and that buildings block GPS. If the dot was grey or missing, Maps could not find the phone and was showing the last known place.

Task 6 (level 2)

Answer: a text with all three parts: the coordinates copied from the pin, a landmark at the entrance (for example the main doors or a sign), and a time and day, such as "7:00 Monday".

Check: latitude first and positive, longitude second and negative, a landmark the person can see when they arrive, a time (and a day if it is not today). The partner reads the message back word for word and their pin lands at the same entrance.

Task 7 (level 2)

Answer, what the foreman could see: your name and photo, your device's recent location even when you are not using Google apps, your battery level and whether it is charging, and arrival and departure times if they set up a notification. With "until you turn this off", that includes evenings and weekends.

Two questions a good response asks (any two):

  • Is this a company rule? Is it written down in a policy?
  • Can it be a work phone instead of my personal phone?
  • Can I share only during my shift, or just send the meeting point instead?
  • Who else will see it, and how is it used?

How to stop: open Google Maps, tap your profile picture, tap Location sharing, tap the foreman's name, tap Stop.

Task 8 (level 3)

Answer: zone 10, easting about 491 700 m, northing about 5 456 400 to 5 456 500 m. The exact digits depend on where the mouse sits on the building. Any easting from about 491 600 to 491 800 and northing from about 5 456 350 to 5 456 550 is a correct reading of City Hall.

The easting is less than 500 000. Zone 10's centre line is at 123°W and has an easting of 500 000. City Hall's longitude is 123.1139°W, a little west of 123°W, so it is a little less than 500 000 m. Google Earth Pro also shows a letter after the zone number. The number is the zone.

Check yourself

  1. 3600 (60 minutes × 60 seconds).
  2. BC is west of Greenwich, England, and decimal degrees show west as negative.
  3. Google Maps is not sure where you are. You could be anywhere inside the circle.
  4. The coordinates, a landmark a person can see, and the time.
  5. In BC, grid distance in metres north of the equator. Record the zone and hemisphere too.
