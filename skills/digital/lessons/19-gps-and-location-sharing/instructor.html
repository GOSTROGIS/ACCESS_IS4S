Read a GPS fix and share a location

Convert coordinates between degrees-minutes-seconds and decimal degrees, read how accurate your phone's location is, send a meeting point a crew can find, decide when to share your live location, and read a UTM easting and northing.

Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: "The foreman's notebook says the laydown yard is at 49°16'36" north, 123°06'43" west. Your phone wants decimals. And a new crew member typed the numbers without the minus sign. Where did he end up?" Today learners convert coordinates, read how sure their phone is about its location, send a meeting point a crew can find, think through live location sharing, and see the UTM grid surveyors use. This follows Lesson 9.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • recognize DD, DMS and DMM coordinates and convert DMS to decimal degrees with BEDMAS and the correct sign (Use digital information: navigate digital content)
  • read the accuracy circle around the blue dot and explain the limits of a phone location (Use digital information: evaluate the relevance and reliability of digital information)
  • drop a pin, copy its coordinates and write a meeting-point message that is read back correctly (Use online tools and platforms: use online communication platforms)
  • decide when and how long to share a live location, and how to stop (Apply safe and responsible practices online: protect personal information and privacy of yourself and others)
  • read a UTM zone, easting and northing in Google Earth Pro (Use online tools and platforms)

Complexity: level 2, with Tasks 1 and 5 at level 1 and Task 8 at level 3.

Pre-assessment (2 min)

  1. In Google Maps, which number comes first, latitude or longitude?
  2. How many minutes are in one hour? How many seconds in one hour?

(Answers: latitude. 60 minutes and 3600 seconds. Degrees split the same way.)

Learning tasks (45 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Write City Hall in DD, DMS and DMM on the board. Explain degrees, minutes and seconds like hours, minutes and seconds. Explain N, W and the minus sign. 1.1 Complete Task 1. Whiteboard 5 min
1.2 Work the City Hall conversion on the board, divisions first, then additions, then the sign. 1.2 Complete Task 2 and check in Google Maps. Whiteboard, browser 8 min
1.3 Type the DMS version of BC Place into Google Maps on the projector. Then type the decimal version with the minus sign missing and zoom out. 1.3 Complete Tasks 3 and 4. Projector, Google Maps 8 min
2.1 Show the blue dot and accuracy circle on a phone through the projector or by walking it round. Explain GPS, Wi-Fi and cell towers. 2.1 Complete Task 5. Learners' phones 5 min
2.2 Demonstrate touch and hold to drop a pin and copy the coordinates. Read the sample meeting text aloud. 2.2 Complete Task 6 in pairs. Learners' phones 7 min
2.3 Show the Location sharing screen without turning it on. Read the list of what a sharer can see. Discuss the foreman scenario. 2.3 Complete Task 7. Projector, Google Maps Help page 6 min
3.1 In Google Earth Pro, switch Show Lat/Long to UTM. Move the mouse across City Hall and point out the zone, easting and northing. Explain the 500 000 m centre line. 3.1 Complete Task 8. Computers with Google Earth Pro 6 min

Post-assessment (4 min)

  1. Convert 49°30'00"N 122°45'00"W to decimal degrees.
  2. Your blue dot sits inside a circle as wide as a city block. Can you use it to stake a fence line? Why not?
  3. Name one thing a person you share your live location with can see besides where you are.

(Answers: 49.5, −122.75. No, the circle means you could be anywhere inside it, and a phone fix is not a survey. Any of: name, photo, battery level, recent location even when you are not using Google apps.)

Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

  • DD, DMS and DMM, and the sign rule for BC (latitude positive, longitude negative)
  • converting DMS to DD: divide first, then add (BEDMAS), then the sign
  • the blue dot, the accuracy circle and the limits of a phone location
  • dropping a pin and writing a meeting-point text that gets read back
  • live location sharing: what others see, how long, how to stop
  • UTM zones, eastings and northings in Google Earth Pro

Time check: 3 + 2 + 2 + 45 + 4 + 4 = 60 min.

Notes for the instructor

  • Before class, type both 49°16'36"N 123°06'43"W and 49.2767, -123.1119 into Google Maps on the class computers and a phone, and check both land on BC Place. Google Maps Help lists DMS as a format that works, but its example is in the eastern hemisphere.
  • Google Earth Pro must be installed for Task 8. If it is not, demonstrate on the projector and let learners read the numbers off the screen. The web and phone versions of Google Earth were not checked for a UTM display.
  • Learners do not need to turn on Location sharing in Task 7. Do not ask anyone to share their live location in class. Some learners may have personal safety reasons to keep their location private, and they do not need to explain that.
  • Do not ask learners to drop a pin on their own home. Use the training site entrance or a public landmark.
  • Which UTM zones cover all of BC was not checked for this lesson. Only zone 10 for the Vancouver area is stated.
  • The Numeracy Hub Construction Operations tab and the numeracy lessons on BEDMAS support the conversion arithmetic. Lesson 20, Put your community on the map, uses these skills next.
