Keyboard, mouse and touch screen

Sit and type in a way that protects your wrists, use the shortcuts that save the most time, type accented letters and use a touch screen.

Why this matters on the job

Trades work now includes typed daily reports, time sheets, safety forms on a tablet and messages to the office. Typing with sore wrists or hunting for keys makes a five-minute report take twenty. In BC, sprains and strains are the most common work injury, and a desk set up badly can cause them too. A few habits and a handful of shortcuts make computer work faster and easier on your body.

Key ideas

Sit so the computer fits you

WorkSafeBC's guide How to Make Your Computer Workstation Fit You gives these points for sitting at a computer:

  • Forearms level, elbows bent about 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed.
  • Wrists straight. Fingertips at the height of the middle row of keys.
  • Head upright. The top of the screen about level with your eyes, about an arm's length away.
  • Feet flat on the floor or on a firm footrest.
  • Mouse at the same height as the keyboard and close to it. Move the mouse with your whole arm, not by bending your wrist side to side.
  • Take short breaks. Pauses of 20 seconds to 2 minutes with a stretch help. If your muscles are already sore, you waited too long.

Habits to replace

These are from the ACCESS typing lesson, updated:

  • Sloppy posture. Slouching throws you off balance, so one hand works harder than the other. You get stiff, fidget and make more mistakes.
  • Resting your wrists on the desk or keyboard edge while typing. Keep wrists straight and floating while you type. A palm rest is for resting between bursts of typing, with the palm, not the wrist, on the rest.
  • Looking at your hands or the screen the whole time. You will miss lines or type them twice. Keep your eyes on the paper you are copying from, and check the screen at the end of each line or sentence.
  • Working through pain. Pain is your body asking for attention. Stop, stretch, change position, and tell your instructor or supervisor if it keeps coming back.

Shortcuts worth learning first

Hold the first key, tap the second, then let go of both.

Shortcut What it does

Ctrl + X Cut what is selected
Ctrl + V Paste
Ctrl + Shift + V Paste text only, without the old formatting

Accented letters in Word

Names and words like résumé, François and Thérèse need accents. In Word and Outlook you press a combination, let go, then type the letter.

To type Press, let go, then type the letter
é, á, í, ó, ú Ctrl + ' (apostrophe)
è, à, ù Ctrl + ` (grave accent, the key left of 1)
ê, â, ô Ctrl + Shift + ^
ä, ë, ö, ü Ctrl + Shift + :
ç Ctrl + , (comma), then c

For any other symbol, Word has a code method: type the character's code, then press Alt + X. For example, 00B0 then Alt + X gives the degree sign °, and 00BD then Alt + X gives ½. Numbers count as part of a code, so if the code comes right after a number, type a space first, press Alt + X, then delete the space.

Worked example

Ravi has to type the accented sentence below into a site report. For "résumé":

  1. Type r.
  2. Press Ctrl + ' together, let go, then type e. Word shows é.
  3. Type sum.
  4. Press Ctrl + ' again, let go, type e.

Result: résumé. If the wrong letter appears, press Ctrl + Z and try again.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Sit at your computer. Check yourself against the six points under "Sit so the computer fits you". Change one thing, such as chair height or screen distance. Write what you changed.

Task 2 (level 1) In a blank Word document, type: Bring the 25 ft tape and the chalk line. Then use only shortcuts to:

  1. select the whole line
  2. copy it
  3. paste it twice below
  4. undo the last paste
  5. save the document as shortcut-practice_[initials]_v1

Task 3 (level 1) Type this ACCESS practice passage exactly, with every accent:

Thérèse Mädchen gave François her résumé last Tuesday. She wanted him to check it for errors and make suggestions on improving the format. When François came to see Thérèse today, he returned her retyped résumé along with a small bouquet of flowers and said, "Voilà, here is your new and improved résumé."

Count the accented letters you typed. Write the number.

Task 4 (level 2) A welder's report needs this line. Type it using the Alt + X method for the symbols: Bevel: 37½° on both plates.

Task 5 (level 1) On a touch screen or touchpad, open a long web page or document. Scroll down with two fingers, zoom in on a word, then press and hold (touch screen) or two-finger tap (touchpad) to see the extra menu. Tell a partner which gesture did what.

Task 6 (level 2) Type the Task 3 passage again without looking at your hands. Time yourself for two minutes. Count the words you typed and the mistakes. Write both.
