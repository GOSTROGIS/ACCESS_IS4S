Keyboard, mouse and touch screen

Sit and type in a way that protects your wrists, use the shortcuts that save the most time, type accented letters and use a touch screen.

Session Total length
Digital 2 60 min

Opening (3 min)

Welcome learners. Hook: hold up a framing hammer and ask how a carpenter learns to swing it all day without wrecking an elbow. The answer is grip, stance and rest. A keyboard is the same: it is a tool you use for hours, and how you hold yourself decides whether it hurts. Today we set up the workstation, learn the shortcuts that save the most time, and type names with accents properly.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • adjust a computer workstation using WorkSafeBC's posture points (Apply safe and responsible practices online: recognize and minimize the effect of physical and mental stresses of being online)
  • use copy, cut, paste, undo, select all, find and save shortcuts (Use digital devices: identify and use the basic functions common to most devices)
  • type accented letters and trade symbols in Word (Use common digital tools to complete tasks)
  • use tap, scroll, zoom and press-and-hold on a touch screen or touchpad (Use digital devices)

Complexity: level 1, with Tasks 4 and 6 at level 2.

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. Show me how you would copy a sentence and put it somewhere else.
  2. Has typing or using a mouse ever made your hands, wrists, neck or back sore? (Hands up, no details needed.)

Learning tasks (45 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Walk through the six WorkSafeBC posture points. Demonstrate chair height and screen distance at one workstation. 1.1 Complete Task 1: check posture and change one thing. WorkSafeBC guide BK76, adjustable chairs 8 min
1.2 Read the four habits to replace. Ask which one learners recognize in themselves. 1.2 Name one habit to work on. Learner page 5 min
2.1 Demonstrate each shortcut in the table on the projector. Say "hold, tap, let go" each time. 2.1 Complete Task 2 using only shortcuts. Word, projector 10 min
2.2 Demonstrate Ctrl + ' then e for é, and the Alt + X code method with the space trick after a number. 2.2 Complete Tasks 3 and 4. Word 10 min
3.1 Demonstrate the four gestures on a touch screen or touchpad. If no touch devices, use touchpads only. 3.1 Complete Task 5 with a partner. Laptops with touchpads, a tablet if available 7 min
3.2 Start a two-minute timer. Remind learners: eyes on the passage, not the hands. 3.2 Complete Task 6. Record words and mistakes. Timer 5 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Show me your Task 3 passage. How many accented letters are in it?
  2. Without looking at the page, tell me the shortcut for undo and for paste text only.
  3. Your neck is sore after an hour of typing a report. Name two things on your workstation you would check.

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • WorkSafeBC's posture points for computer work and short breaks
  • four typing habits to replace
  • the most useful Windows and Word shortcuts
  • accented letters and the Alt + X symbol method in Word
  • tap, scroll, zoom and press-and-hold on touch screens and touchpads

Time check: 3 + 2 + 3 + 45 + 5 + 2 = 60 min.

Notes for the instructor

  • Typing speed is personal. Record it only for the learner's own progress. Do not compare learners or post results.
  • Learners who use voice typing, a screen reader or an adapted keyboard do the same tasks with their own method. Record the method they used.
  • Do not ask learners to describe injuries or health conditions. Point them to the WorkSafeBC guide and to their supervisor.
  • The Alt + X method works in Word. It does not work in every app. In other apps, copy the symbol from a Word document.
