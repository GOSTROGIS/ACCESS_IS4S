Reading the whole task before you calculate

Practise reading every instruction before you start, then use fractions on a cut list where the last line changes the job.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 45 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Why this matters on the job

On a job site, the instruction that changes everything is often at the bottom: "build three of these", "all lengths include the trim", "do not cut until the plans are signed off". People in the trades need to remember and follow procedures with many steps. Every step is a place where things can go wrong. Reading the whole task before you pick up a pencil or a saw is the cheapest check there is.

Key ideas

Before you calculate, read the whole task once from top to bottom. Then ask:

  1. What is the question I have to answer?
  2. What form should the answer take: a number, a length, a list, a yes or no?
  3. Is there anything at the end that changes the start?

Fraction words used on this page:

  • In 3/8, the numerator is 3 (how many parts) and the denominator is 8 (how many equal parts make the whole).
  • A proper fraction is less than one whole, such as 3/8.
  • An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator, such as 9/8 or 8/8.
  • A mixed number is a whole number and a fraction, such as 2 3/4.
  • A fraction is in lowest terms when no number except 1 divides both the top and the bottom.

Worked example

Change 19/8 to a mixed number. 19 ÷ 8 = 2 remainder 3, so 19/8 = 2 3/8. Check: 2 × 8 + 3 = 19.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Your instructor will hand out this task on its own page. Follow the instructions exactly.

Read everything on the page before you do anything else.

  1. Circle the denominator in 5/8.
  2. Circle the numerator in 3/16.
  3. Circle the proper fraction: 7/4, 3/8, 3
  4. Circle the improper fraction: 1 1/2, 9/8, 1/2
  5. Circle the mixed number: 5/16, 5, 2 3/4
  6. Circle the fraction equal to 3/4: 6/8, 4/3, 3/8
  7. Circle the fraction in lowest terms: 4/8, 6/16, 5/8
  8. Change 17/8 to a whole or mixed number: __
  9. Change 24/8 to a whole or mixed number: __
  10. Change 35/16 to a whole or mixed number: __
  11. A carpenter takes one day to complete 1/2 a set of cabinets. What fraction of the set is completed in 1/4 of a day? __
  12. A sheet of drywall is cut into quarters. Each quarter is cut into thirds. What fraction of a whole sheet is each of these small pieces? __

Now that you have read everything, do not answer questions 1 to 12. Write your name at the top of the page and hand it in.

Task 2 (level 1-2). Now answer all 12 questions from Task 1. Show your working for questions 8 to 12.

Task 3 (level 2). Read the whole cut list before you calculate anything.

Cut list: bench frame (fictional job)

Part Number Length
Legs 4 17 1/4 in
Long rails 2 36 5/8 in
Short rails 2 14 3/8 in

Note: the client has ordered 3 bench frames. Calculate net finished lengths only; ignore saw kerf, end trimming and stock-length waste for this exercise.

a) What is the total length of all the legs for the whole order? b) What is the total length of all the long rails? Of all the short rails? c) What is the total length of material for the whole order, in inches and in feet and inches? d) What total would someone get if they missed the note? How far short would their material order be?

Check yourself

  1. Why should you read the whole task before you start calculating?
  2. What is 3/4 of 1/2?
  3. Write 19/8 as a mixed number.
  4. Write 12/16 in lowest terms.
