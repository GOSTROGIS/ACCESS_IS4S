Working out the tax on a simple return

Follow a T4 slip through the main lines of a 2026 return to find federal and BC tax, then decide whether there is a refund or a balance owing.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 120 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 4 of 8. Total length: 120 minutes. Run this lesson after lesson 1 (pay stubs and the TD1) or after a session on percent.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Say: "Today we combine math with a real document. We work through the main lines of a tax return and calculate the tax ourselves, so a refund or a balance owing is never a surprise." Ask who has filed a return before, by software, at a clinic or on paper. No one needs to share their own income or tax.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say what question a tax return answers and what form the answer takes (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • find the T4 amounts that feed each line (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect T4 boxes to return lines and lines to each other (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • calculate tax by bracket, non-refundable credits, the BC tax reduction and the refund or balance owing (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check whether a result is reasonable and explain what it means (Interpret and evaluate the information)

This lesson presents facts and calculations. It is not tax advice. Learners with their own tax questions can use a free tax clinic or contact the CRA.

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. 1% of $500 = ? 2.5% of $500 = ? ($5.00, $12.50)
  2. 14% of $10,000 = ? ($1,400)

Learning tasks (90 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Explain that each rate applies only to its own bracket. Show the CRA interactive chart on progressive tax rates. Model the $70,000 worked example. 1.1 Follow the worked example. Complete Tasks 1 and 2. Learner page, projector, CRA "How tax rates and brackets work" chart 15 min
1.2 Hand out Document A. Show which T4 box goes to which line using the lines table. 1.2 Complete Task 3. Document A, lines table 10 min
2.1 Explain the CPP split and why one part is a deduction and the other a credit. Supply the 4.95 ÷ 5.95 factor and model it once. 2.1 Complete Tasks 4 and 5. Learner page, calculator 15 min
2.2 Walk through Task 6 a) together, then let learners finish b) to d). Ask: "Why do we multiply the credits by 14%, not by the bracket rate?" 2.2 Complete Tasks 6 and 7. Learner page, calculator 15 min
2.3 Point out that BC uses its own amounts and its own lowest rate, and has a tax reduction for lower incomes. 2.3 Complete Task 8. Learner page, Document B, calculator 15 min
2.4 Ask learners to compare total tax with line 43700. 2.4 Complete Task 9. Learner page 5 min
3.1 Pairs work Kim's return. One partner calculates, the other checks each line, then they swap at Task 8. 3.1 Complete Task 10 in pairs. Learner page, calculator 10 min
3.2 Introduce the CRA page. State only what the CRA states. Do not ask anyone about their status, residence or income. Point out free tax clinics and the band office. 3.2 Complete Task 11. Projector or devices, CRA "Taxes and benefits for Indigenous peoples" page 5 min

Post-assessment (15 min)

  1. Taxable income is $60,000. What is the federal tax before credits? ($58,523 × 14% = $8,193.22, plus $1,477 × 20.5% = $302.79, total $8,496.01)
  2. Federal tax before credits is $3,000 and federal credits are $3,200. What is the federal tax? ($0, because non-refundable credits cannot go below zero)
  3. Total tax is $4,276.51 and line 43700 is $4,100.00. What goes on line 48500, and when is it due? ($176.51 balance owing, due April 30, 2027)

Check answers. Discuss where each number was found and why it was the right one. Learners take turns. Make sure everyone participates.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • how tax brackets work, one slice at a time
  • following T4 boxes to the lines of a 2026 return
  • splitting CPP into a base part and an additional part
  • federal and BC non-refundable credits and the BC tax reduction
  • working out a refund or a balance owing, and the April 30 due date
  • what the CRA says about the section 87 tax exemption and free tax help
