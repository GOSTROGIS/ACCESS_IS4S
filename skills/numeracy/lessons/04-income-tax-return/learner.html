Working out the tax on a simple return

Follow a T4 slip through the main lines of a 2026 return to find federal and BC tax, then decide whether there is a refund or a balance owing.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 120 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Why this matters on the job

Every spring, workers in BC file an income tax and benefit return for the year before. The 2026 return is due by April 30, 2027, for most people. Tax software or a free tax clinic does the arithmetic for you, but knowing how the main lines connect lets you check your return, understand a refund or a balance owing, and spot a T4 slip that is wrong. Filing also matters for benefits: the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) needs a return to work out benefit and credit payments.

Key ideas

Tax brackets. Income tax is charged in slices. Each rate applies only to the part of your taxable income inside its bracket, not to all of it. For 2026:

Federal: each portion of taxable income Rate
$0 to $58,523 14%
$58,523.01 to $117,045 20.5%
$117,045.01 to $181,440 26%
over $181,440 (two more brackets) 29% and 33%
British Columbia: each portion of taxable income Rate
$0 to $50,363 5.6%
$50,363.01 to $100,728 7.7%
$100,728.01 to $115,648 10.5%
over $115,648 (four more brackets) 12.29% to 20.5%

Non-refundable tax credits. These lower your tax, not your income. Add up the credit amounts, then multiply by the lowest rate: 14% federal and 5.6% BC for 2026. If the credits are bigger than the tax, the tax is $0. You do not get the difference back.

CPP is split in two. Box 16 of your T4 holds your CPP contributions. The base part is a credit (line 30800). The additional part is a deduction from income (line 22215). For 2026, the base part is 4.95/5.95 of box 16, and the rest is the additional part. Tax software does this split for you.

Refund or balance owing. Compare the total tax with the tax your employer already took off (T4 box 22, line 43700). If more was taken off than you owe, you get a refund. If less, you have a balance owing, due by April 30.

The main lines used in this lesson

Line What it holds Where the number comes from
10100 Employment income T4 box 14
15000 Total income Add all income lines
22215 Deduction for the additional CPP contributions Additional part of T4 box 16
23600 Net income Line 15000 minus deductions
26000 Taxable income Line 23600 minus other deductions (none in this lesson)
30000 Basic personal amount $16,452 federal, $13,216 BC
30800 Base CPP contributions Base part of T4 box 16
31200 EI premiums T4 box 18
31260 Canada employment amount (federal only) $1,501, or your employment income if less
43700 Total income tax deducted T4 box 22
48400 / 48500 Refund / balance owing Line 43700 compared with total tax

Worked example: tax on two brackets

Taxable income is $70,000. Federal tax before credits:

  • First bracket: $58,523 × 14% = $8,193.22
  • Second bracket: ($70,000 − $58,523) × 20.5% = $11,477 × 0.205 = $2,352.79
  • Total: $8,193.22 + $2,352.79 = $10,546.01

Do the subtraction in the bracket first (BEDMAS), then multiply. Only $11,477 is taxed at 20.5%, not the whole $70,000.

Practice

Document A: T4 slip for 2026 (fictional)

Employer: Cedar Ridge Framing Ltd. (fictional). Employee: Morgan Lee, carpenter apprentice, lived in BC all of 2026. Morgan has no other income, no dependants and no other deductions or credits.

Box Description Amount
14 Employment income $48,000.00
16 Employee's CPP contributions $2,647.75
18 Employee's EI premiums $782.40
22 Income tax deducted $5,600.00

Document B: BC tax reduction for 2026

If net income (line 23600) is $25,570 or less, the reduction is $690. Between $25,570 and $44,952, the reduction is $690 minus 3.56% of the net income over $25,570. At $44,952 or more, the reduction is $0.

Task 1 (level 2). Taxable income is $30,000. What is the federal tax before credits? Which bracket or brackets does it use?

Task 2 (level 3). For the worked example ($70,000), work out the BC tax before credits. Show each bracket.

Task 3 (level 1). Use Document A. Copy the amounts that go on lines 10100, 31200 and 43700.

Task 4 (level 3). Split Morgan's CPP (box 16) into the base part and the additional part. Base = box 16 × 4.95 ÷ 5.95. Additional = box 16 − base. Which line does each part go on?

Task 5 (level 2). Find Morgan's total income (line 15000), net income (line 23600) and taxable income (line 26000).

Task 6 (level 3). Federal tax. Work out:

a) Federal tax on taxable income (one bracket or two?) b) Federal credit amounts: line 30000 + line 30800 + line 31200 + line 31260 c) Federal credits: the total from b) × 14% d) Federal tax: a) minus c)

Task 7 (level 2). Morgan's federal credit amounts in Task 6 b) are larger than the basic personal amount alone. Which three credits did Morgan get just from working?

Task 8 (level 3). BC tax. Work out:

a) BC tax on taxable income b) BC credit amounts: $13,216 + base CPP + EI (BC has no Canada employment amount) c) BC credits: the total from b) × 5.6% d) BC tax before the reduction: a) minus c) e) BC tax reduction (Document B) f) BC tax: d) minus e)

Task 9 (level 2). Add the federal and BC tax. Compare it with line 43700. Does Morgan get a refund or have a balance owing? How much?

Task 10 (level 3). Kim (fictional) also lived in BC all of 2026, with no other income, deductions or credits. Kim's T4: box 14 $42,000.00, box 16 $2,290.75, box 18 $684.60, box 22 $4,100.00. Work through Tasks 4 to 9 for Kim. Kim's net income is under $44,952, so use Document B.

Task 11 (level 1). Read the CRA page "Taxes and benefits for Indigenous peoples" at https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/indigenous-peoples.html. Answer from the page, in your own words:

a) Under section 87 of the Indian Act, what kind of property is exempt from tax for a person registered or entitled to be registered under the Indian Act? b) Why does the CRA say to file a return even if all your income is tax-exempt? c) Where does the page say to look for free tax help?
