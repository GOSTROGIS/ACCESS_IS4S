# Working out the tax on a simple return

## Why this matters on the job

Every spring, workers in BC file an income tax and benefit return for the year before. The 2026 return is due by April 30, 2027, for most people. Tax software or a free tax clinic does the arithmetic for you, but knowing how the main lines connect lets you check your return, understand a refund or a balance owing, and spot a T4 slip that is wrong. Filing also matters for benefits: the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) needs a return to work out benefit and credit payments.

## Key ideas

**Tax brackets.** Income tax is charged in slices. Each rate applies only to the part of your taxable income inside its bracket, not to all of it. For 2026:

| Federal: each portion of taxable income | Rate |
|---|---:|
| $0 to $58,523 | 14% |
| $58,523.01 to $117,045 | 20.5% |
| $117,045.01 to $181,440 | 26% |
| over $181,440 (two more brackets) | 29% and 33% |

| British Columbia: each portion of taxable income | Rate |
|---|---:|
| $0 to $50,363 | 5.6% |
| $50,363.01 to $100,728 | 7.7% |
| $100,728.01 to $115,648 | 10.5% |
| over $115,648 (four more brackets) | 12.29% to 20.5% |

**Non-refundable tax credits.** These lower your tax, not your income. Add up the credit amounts, then multiply by the lowest rate: 14% federal and 5.6% BC for 2026. If the credits are bigger than the tax, the tax is $0. You do not get the difference back.

**CPP is split in two.** Box 16 of your T4 holds your CPP contributions. The base part is a credit (line 30800). The additional part is a deduction from income (line 22215). For 2026, the base part is 4.95/5.95 of box 16, and the rest is the additional part. Tax software does this split for you.

**Refund or balance owing.** Compare the total tax with the tax your employer already took off (T4 box 22, line 43700). If more was taken off than you owe, you get a refund. If less, you have a balance owing, due by April 30.

### The main lines used in this lesson

| Line | What it holds | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|
| 10100 | Employment income | T4 box 14 |
| 15000 | Total income | Add all income lines |
| 22215 | Deduction for the additional CPP contributions | Additional part of T4 box 16 |
| 23600 | Net income | Line 15000 minus deductions |
| 26000 | Taxable income | Line 23600 minus other deductions (none in this lesson) |
| 30000 | Basic personal amount | $16,452 federal, $13,216 BC |
| 30800 | Base CPP contributions | Base part of T4 box 16 |
| 31200 | EI premiums | T4 box 18 |
| 31260 | Canada employment amount (federal only) | $1,501, or your employment income if less |
| 43700 | Total income tax deducted | T4 box 22 |
| 48400 / 48500 | Refund / balance owing | Line 43700 compared with total tax |

### Worked example: tax on two brackets

Taxable income is $70,000. Federal tax before credits:

- First bracket: $58,523 × 14% = $8,193.22
- Second bracket: ($70,000 − $58,523) × 20.5% = $11,477 × 0.205 = $2,352.79
- Total: $8,193.22 + $2,352.79 = $10,546.01

Do the subtraction in the bracket first (BEDMAS), then multiply. Only $11,477 is taxed at 20.5%, not the whole $70,000.

**Document B: BC tax reduction for 2026**

If net income (line 23600) is $25,570 or less, the reduction is $690. Between $25,570 and $44,952, the reduction is $690 minus 3.56% of the net income over $25,570. At $44,952 or more, the reduction is $0.
