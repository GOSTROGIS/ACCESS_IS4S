# Reading between the lines of a chart

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 33 of 34. **Total length:** 60 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator. Print or project the four practice charts on the learner page. Every chart in this lesson was written for ACCESS and is not data for any real product or crane, so do not hand out a real crane load chart in its place. Task 2 uses grade from lesson 32 and Task 6 uses cut and fill from lesson 31.

## Opening (4 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "The chart has a row for −10 °C and a row for 0 °C. It is −4 °C tonight. How much fuel does the heater need?" Take answers. Then ask: "The crane chart has a row for 16 m and a row for 20 m. The load is at 18 m. Can you do the same thing?" Say: "Today you learn to read between the rows of a chart, and when a chart says you must not." Tell learners this is session 33 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 60 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the two rows on either side of a number in a chart, and any notes on the chart (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect the position between two rows to a fraction of the change (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- work out an interpolated value, and work a chart backwards, in metric and in feet and inches (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- round in the safe direction and decide when interpolation is not allowed (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- report an estimate and say why it is an estimate (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. 6.0 + 0.6 × (−1.5) = ? (5.1, because the multiplication comes first)
2. Change 0.5 ft to inches. (6 in)

## Post-assessment (4 min)

1. Row 50 gives 300 and row 60 gives 340. What is the value at 57? (300 + 0.7 × 40 = 328)
2. You estimate a wait of 6.2 hours before stripping a form. Do you plan for 6 or 7 hours? (7, round up a wait)
3. A load chart note says to use the next larger radius. Your radius is 22 m and the rows are 20 m and 24 m. Which row do you use? (24 m)

## Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

- interpolation in four steps: the two rows, the fraction along, the change, the estimate
- working a chart backwards, and changing decimal feet to feet and inches
- rounding in the safe direction: up for fuel and waits, down for a largest size allowed
- why a steady-grade pipe gives an exact answer and most charts give an estimate
- lifting charts: follow the chart notes and the lift plan, and never estimate a rated load between rows
