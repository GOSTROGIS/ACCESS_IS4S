# Converting money with exchange rates

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 5 of 8. **Total length:** 60 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show two prices for the same tool, one in US dollars and one in Canadian dollars, with the US price lower. Ask: "Which one is cheaper?" Collect answers and leave them on the board. Ask learners to recall the location strategy for document use: decide what you need, scan the headings, find the row and column, then check.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find an exchange rate and its date in a table (Identify the mathematical information)
- choose whether to multiply or divide based on the direction of the conversion (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- convert amounts and work out exchange counter and card charges (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- judge whether an answer is the right size and compare the cost of ways to pay (Interpret and evaluate the information)

This lesson presents facts and calculations. It does not recommend a way to exchange money.

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. 50 × 1.4 = ? (70)
2. 70 ÷ 1.4 = ? (50)

## Post-assessment (10 min)

1. A part costs 40 euros. Use 1.5703. What is it in Canadian dollars? ($62.81)
2. You have $300 Canadian. A counter sells US dollars at 1.4350. How many US dollars do you get? (US$209.06)
3. A US$100 purchase converts to $139.89 Canadian on a card that charges 2.5%. What is the total? ($139.89 + $3.50 = $143.39)

Return to the opening question and answer it with the day's rate. Learners take turns explaining how they knew whether to multiply or divide.

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- reading a Bank of Canada exchange rate table and what "indicative" means
- multiplying to get Canadian dollars and dividing to get the other currency
- the gap between an exchange counter's buying and selling rates
- how a card's foreign currency conversion charge is added
- checking a conversion by reversing it
