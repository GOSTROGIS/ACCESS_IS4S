## Task 3 (level 2)

- At the counter (you buy US$, so use "we sell"): 500.00 ÷ 1.4350 = **US$348.43**
- At the Bank of Canada rate: 500.00 ÷ 1.3989 = **US$357.42**
- Difference: 357.42 − 348.43 = **US$8.99**

## Task 4 (level 2)

- At the counter (you sell US$, so use "we buy"): 120.00 × 1.3600 = **$163.20**
- At the Bank of Canada rate: 120.00 × 1.3989 = **$167.87**

## Task 5 (level 3)

- US$348.43 × 1.3600 = **$473.86** back
- Loss: $500.00 − $473.86 = **$26.14**
- As a percent: 26.14 ÷ 500 × 100 = **5.23%**

A good answer explains that the loss comes from the gap between the two counter rates.

HP 10bII+: 500, orange shift, %CHG, 473.86 = shows −5.23. The minus sign shows a loss of 5.23%.

## Task 6 (level 2)

- Canadian amount: 249.99 × 1.3989 = $349.71
- Conversion charge: 349.71 × 0.025 = 8.743, which rounds to $8.74
- Card total: $349.71 + $8.74 = **$358.45**
- With US cash from the counter: 249.99 × 1.4350 = **$358.74**
- The card costs less, by $358.74 − $358.45 = **$0.29**.

A good answer adds that the two ways cost almost the same with these rates, and that a different card charge or counter rate would change the result.

HP 10bII+: 249.99 × 1.3989 = shows 349.71, then + 2.5 % = shows 358.45.
