Converting money with exchange rates

Read an exchange rate table, convert between Canadian and other currencies, and work out what a bank, an exchange counter or a credit card adds on.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Task 1 (level 1)

1 euro was worth $1.5703 Canadian. 1 Mexican peso was worth $0.07483 Canadian.

Task 2 (level 2)

US dollars to Canadian dollars, so multiply: 249.99 × 1.3989 = 349.711, which rounds to $349.71.

Size check: the Canadian number is bigger, because one US dollar was worth more than one Canadian dollar.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • At the counter (you buy US$, so use "we sell"): 500.00 ÷ 1.4350 = US$348.43
  • At the Bank of Canada rate: 500.00 ÷ 1.3989 = US$357.42
  • Difference: 357.42 − 348.43 = US$8.99

Task 4 (level 2)

  • At the counter (you sell US$, so use "we buy"): 120.00 × 1.3600 = $163.20
  • At the Bank of Canada rate: 120.00 × 1.3989 = $167.87

Task 5 (level 3)

  • US$348.43 × 1.3600 = $473.86 back
  • Loss: $500.00 − $473.86 = $26.14
  • As a percent: 26.14 ÷ 500 × 100 = 5.23%

A good answer explains that the loss comes from the gap between the two counter rates.

Task 6 (level 2)

  • Canadian amount: 249.99 × 1.3989 = $349.71
  • Conversion charge: 349.71 × 0.025 = 8.743, which rounds to $8.74
  • Card total: $349.71 + $8.74 = $358.45
  • With US cash from the counter: 249.99 × 1.4350 = $358.74
  • The card costs less, by $358.74 − $358.45 = $0.29.

A good answer adds that the two ways cost almost the same with these rates, and that a different card charge or counter rate would change the result.

Task 7 (level 2)

(1.4350 − 1.3989) ÷ 1.3989 × 100 = 0.0361 ÷ 1.3989 × 100 = 2.58%

a) 3,500 × 0.07483 = $261.91 Canadian b) 300.00 × 0.7851 = $235.53 Canadian

Task 9 (level 2)

Answers depend on the day. A good answer:

  • copies today's US dollar rate and its date from the Bank of Canada page
  • multiplies 249.99 by that rate and rounds to the cent
  • subtracts $349.71 from the new price (or the other way round) and says whether the price went up or down
  • explains that a higher rate means a higher Canadian price
