Converting money with exchange rates

Read an exchange rate table, convert between Canadian and other currencies, and work out what a bank, an exchange counter or a credit card adds on.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Why this matters on the job

Tools, parts and safety gear are often priced in US dollars online. Crews from BC travel to job sites in Alberta, but also to Washington State or Alaska, and some workers send money to family abroad. Each time, a dollar amount has to be changed into another currency. Knowing how to convert lets you compare a US price with a Canadian one and see the extra cost a bank or card adds.

Key ideas

The Bank of Canada publishes daily exchange rates once each business day by 16:30 Eastern Time. Each rate shows how many Canadian dollars one unit of the other currency is worth. These rates are indicative only: they are averages, and banks and exchange counters use their own rates.

Two rules cover every conversion with a rate written as "Canadian dollars for 1 unit":

  • Other currency to Canadian dollars: multiply by the rate.
  • Canadian dollars to the other currency: divide by the rate.

Check the direction with common sense. One US dollar is worth more than one Canadian dollar on the date used in this lesson, so a US price turns into a bigger Canadian number.

An exchange counter has two rates. It sells you foreign money at a higher rate and buys it back from you at a lower rate. The gap is how the counter earns money.

A credit card used in another currency converts the purchase to Canadian dollars and then adds a foreign currency conversion charge, a percentage of the Canadian amount.

Worked example

On November 5, 2026, the Bank of Canada rate for the US dollar was 1.3989.

  • A part costs US$80.00. In Canadian dollars: 80.00 × 1.3989 = $111.91
  • You have $200.00 Canadian. In US dollars: 200.00 ÷ 1.3989 = US$142.97

Check: US$142.97 × 1.3989 = $200.00 (to the nearest cent). The two answers undo each other.

Practice

Document A: Bank of Canada daily exchange rates, November 5, 2026 (Canadian dollars for 1 unit)

Currency Rate
US dollar 1.3989
European euro 1.5703
Mexican peso 0.07483
New Zealand dollar 0.7851

Document B: exchange counter board (fictional, Riverbend Community Bank)

US dollars Canadian dollars for US$1
We sell (you buy US$) 1.4350
We buy (you sell US$) 1.3600

Document C: credit card terms (fictional Riverbend Classic card): foreign currency conversion charge 2.5% of the purchase in Canadian dollars. Assume the card uses the Document A rate to convert.

Task 1 (level 1). Use Document A. How many Canadian dollars was 1 euro worth on November 5, 2026? How many was 1 Mexican peso worth?

Task 2 (level 2). A welding helmet costs US$249.99 online. Use Document A to find the price in Canadian dollars, before any shipping, duty or tax.

Task 3 (level 2). Before a job in Washington State, you buy US dollars with $500.00 Canadian at the exchange counter (Document B). How many US dollars do you get? How many would $500.00 buy at the Bank of Canada rate? What is the difference in US dollars?

Task 4 (level 2). You come home with US$120.00 and sell it back to the counter. How many Canadian dollars do you get? How many would it be worth at the Bank of Canada rate?

Task 5 (level 3). Suppose you change $500.00 Canadian into US dollars at the counter (Task 3), do not spend any of it, and change it all back. How many Canadian dollars do you get back? How much did you lose, in dollars and as a percent of $500?

Task 6 (level 2). You buy the US$249.99 helmet from Task 2 with the Classic card (Document C). Find the Canadian amount, the 2.5% conversion charge and the total. Then find what the same helmet would cost in Canadian dollars if you paid with US cash bought at the counter's selling rate. Which way costs less, and by how much?

Task 7 (level 2). By what percent is the counter's selling rate higher than the Bank of Canada rate? Use (1.4350 − 1.3989) ÷ 1.3989 × 100. Work the bracket first (BEDMAS). Round to two decimal places.

Task 8 (level 2). Use Document A.

a) A hotel in Mexico costs 3,500 pesos. What is that in Canadian dollars? b) You send NZ$300.00 to a relative in New Zealand. What is that in Canadian dollars at the Bank of Canada rate, before any transfer fee?

Task 9 (level 2). Go to the Bank of Canada daily exchange rates page at https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/daily-exchange-rates/. Write down today's US dollar rate and its date. Redo Task 2 with it. Did the Canadian price go up or down since November 5, 2026, and by how much?

Check yourself

  1. One US dollar is worth $1.40 Canadian. What is US$50.00 in Canadian dollars?
  2. At the same rate, how many US dollars do you get for $140.00 Canadian?
  3. Can you always trade money at the Bank of Canada rate? Why or why not?
  4. A card charges 2.5% for conversion on a purchase of $400.00 Canadian. What is the charge?
  5. To change Canadian dollars into another currency using these tables, do you multiply or divide by the rate?
