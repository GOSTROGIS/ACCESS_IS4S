Estimate a job's time and cost

Use a past job, a unit rate or a bottom-up list to estimate a job, work a three-point time estimate with BEDMAS, and build a cost estimate with labour, material and contingency.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 28 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator with brackets and a 2027 calendar. This lesson follows the scheduling set (lessons 17 to 21): those lessons used durations as given, and this one shows where durations and costs come from. Lesson 29 uses the handrail estimate from Worked example 2 as its budget, so keep it on the board or in learners' notes. Every rate, price and company in the lesson is made up for practice. If a learner asks about real wage rates, say that rates depend on the trade, the employer and any union agreement, and that an estimator gets them from the company, not from a lesson.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Your foreman asks how long it will take you to install the decking on a deck. What do you say?" Take two or three answers. Then ask: "What if the boards show up warped?" Say: "One number hides the bad days. Today we use three numbers and a formula that weighs them, then we put a whole job's cost together line by line." Tell learners this is session 28 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say when a job needs a time or cost estimate and what form the answer takes (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • pick out the quantities, rates, crew sizes and three-point numbers from a job description (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a past job, a unit rate and a bottom-up list as three ways to reach the same estimate (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • work the simple average and the PERT average with BEDMAS, change crew-hours to days, and build a cost estimate with contingency (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check an estimate against a past job and judge whether it is reasonable (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • tell a customer the estimated cost and time and what the contingency is for (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. Work out 2 + 4 × 3 with BEDMAS. (14)
  2. A job needs 40 crew-hours. A crew of 2 works 8-hour days. How many working days? (40 ÷ 16 = 2.5, round up to 3)

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Find the PERT average for O = 2, M = 4, P = 12. ((2 + 16 + 12) ÷ 6 = 30 ÷ 6 = 5)
  2. Labour is 20 h at $40.00 an hour, material is $1,200 and consumables are $100. Add 10% contingency. What is the estimated cost? ($800 + $1,200 + $100 = $2,100, plus $210 = $2,310)
  3. A bottom-up estimate is $9,000. A past-job check gives $6,500. What should the estimator do? (Look for something missing or counted twice before using either number)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • three ways to estimate: from a past job, from a unit rate and bottom-up
  • three-point estimates and the PERT average (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6, worked with BEDMAS
  • changing crew-hours into working days, rounded up
  • a bottom-up cost estimate with labour, material, consumables and contingency
  • checking one estimate against another, and the difference between cost and price

Next session: lesson 29 checks whether a job is on budget, starting with the handrail from this lesson.
