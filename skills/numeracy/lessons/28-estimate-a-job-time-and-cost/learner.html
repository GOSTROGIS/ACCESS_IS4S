Estimate a job's time and cost

Use a past job, a unit rate or a bottom-up list to estimate a job, work a three-point time estimate with BEDMAS, and build a cost estimate with labour, material and contingency.

Why this matters on the job

Before a crew starts, somebody has to say how long the job will take and what it will cost. The owner uses the time to plan around the work. The company uses the cost to set a price and to check later whether the job made money. A lead hand who can estimate a task well is the person a foreman asks first. A guess that is too low costs the company money. A guess that is too high loses the job to another company.

This lesson comes after the five scheduling lessons (lesson 17 to lesson 21). Those lessons took the durations as given. This lesson shows where durations and costs come from.

Key ideas

Three ways to estimate.

Way How it works Example
From a past job Use what a similar job took or cost before. Quick, but only as good as the match. "The last 8-section handrail cost $6,000."
From a unit rate Multiply or divide a quantity by a rate per unit from your records. 360 m² of wall ÷ 20 m² an hour = 18 hours
Bottom-up Break the job into pieces, estimate each piece, then add them up. Slowest, but the most detailed. Labour + material + consumables + contingency

Good estimators use more than one way and compare the answers. If a bottom-up estimate and a past-job check are far apart, something is missing or wrong.

Three-point estimate. Ask for three numbers for each task:

  • O, optimistic: the best case, when everything goes well
  • M, most likely: what usually happens
  • P, pessimistic: the worst case you can realistically expect

Two ways to combine them:

  • Simple average: (O + M + P) ÷ 3
  • PERT average: (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6

PERT counts the most likely number four times, so it leans toward what usually happens. When the worst case is much further from M than the best case is, both averages come out higher than M. On most jobs that is the case: a task can run late by a lot more than it can run early.

BEDMAS in the PERT formula. In (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6, work inside the brackets first. Inside the brackets, multiply 4 × M before you add. Then divide the whole bracket by 6. On a calculator, key the brackets: ( O + 4 × M + P ) ÷ 6 =

Hours to days. A three-point estimate in crew-hours turns into working days the same way as in lesson 17: working days = crew-hours ÷ (workers × hours per day), rounded up.

Bottom-up cost. List each part of the cost:

  • Labour: hours × hourly rate
  • Material: from the take-off (lessons 13 and 14)
  • Consumables: welding wire, gas, discs, blades, fasteners
  • Equipment: rentals and machine time
  • Contingency: a percentage added for things that are likely to go wrong but cannot be named yet. Contingency = subtotal × contingency %.

Cost is not price. This lesson stops at cost. The price the customer pays also adds the company's overhead and profit, and tax where it applies. That is a separate step. Lesson 22 (Markup, margin and pricing a job) covers it.

Worked example 1: a three-point time estimate

Riverbend Decks (fictional, from lessons 19 to 21) is estimating "Install decking" on a new deck. The lead hand gives three numbers in crew-hours: O = 20 h, M = 24 h, P = 40 h. The worst case allows for a delivery of warped boards that have to be sorted.

  1. Simple average: (20 + 24 + 40) ÷ 3 = 84 ÷ 3 = 28 crew-hours.
  2. PERT: (20 + 4 × 24 + 40) ÷ 6. Multiply first inside the brackets: 4 × 24 = 96. Then add: 20 + 96 + 40 = 156. Then divide: 156 ÷ 6 = 26 crew-hours.
  3. Both are higher than the most likely 24 h, because the worst case is far from the most likely case.
  4. A crew of 2 on 8-hour days: 26 ÷ (2 × 8) = 26 ÷ 16 = 1.625. Round up: 2 working days.

Worked example 2: a bottom-up cost estimate

Coastline Steel Works (fictional) is estimating a steel handrail of 10 sections, built in the shop and installed on site. All rates and prices are made up for practice.

Line Working Cost
Shop labour 10 sections × 6 h each = 60 h, × $50.00/h $3,000.00
Install labour 2 workers × 2 days × 8 h = 32 h, × $50.00/h $1,600.00
Steel tube and plate from the take-off $1,800.00
Anchors and bolts from the take-off $200.00
Consumables wire, gas, grinding discs $250.00
Primer 2 cans $150.00
Subtotal $7,000.00
Contingency 10% $7,000.00 × 0.10 $700.00
Estimated cost $7,700.00

Check against a past job: the last handrail had 8 sections and cost $6,000. That is $6,000 ÷ 8 = $750 a section. For 10 sections: 10 × $750 = $7,500. The bottom-up estimate of $7,700 is close, so nothing big seems to be missing.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1-2). Which way of estimating is each person using: a past job, a unit rate, or bottom-up?

a) "Our crew hangs about 30 m² of board an hour, and this suite has 450 m², so about 15 hours."
b) "I listed every fitting, every hour and every rental, then added them up."
c) "We did the same washroom layout on the floor below. It took 9 days."
d) "Our records show what concrete footings cost per metre of footing, so I multiplied that by the length on this drawing."
e) "I priced the stairs, the railing and the decking one at a time, then added 10%."

Task 2 (level 2). Find the simple average and the PERT average for each task. Write the line again after each BEDMAS step.

a) Frame garage walls, in working days: O = 3, M = 4, P = 11
b) Hang drywall in a suite, in crew-hours: O = 30, M = 36, P = 54
c) Roof truss delivery, in working days: O = 8, M = 10, P = 18
d) A learner keys 3 + 4 × 4 + 11 ÷ 6 = for part a) and gets 20.83. What went wrong?

Task 3 (level 2). A painting contractor's records show that one painter rolls 20 m² of wall an hour on one coat. A tenant space has 360 m² of wall to roll.

a) How many painter-hours is one coat?
b) Two painters do the job. How many hours does each one work?
c) The painters work 8-hour days. How many working days is that?
d) Painter time costs $45.00 an hour (made up). What is the labour cost of one coat?

Task 4 (level 2-3). Inlet Mechanical (fictional) is estimating the replacement of a sump pump in a commercial building. All prices are made up for practice.

Line Details
Labour 2 pipefitters, 6 hours each, at $55.00 an hour
Pump $1,240.00
Pipe, fittings and valves $360.00
Consumables $40.00

a) Find the labour cost.
b) Find the subtotal.
c) The company adds 15% contingency on pump jobs, because old piping often has to be replaced. Find the contingency and the estimated cost.
d) The last two pump changes cost $2,400 and $2,800. Average them and compare with your estimate. Does your estimate look reasonable?

Task 5 (level 3). Riverbend Decks plans a small deck as three activities in a row. The numbers are in working days.

Activity O M P
A Dig and pour piers 1 1 4
B Frame posts, beams and joists 2 3 4
C Install decking and railings 2 2 8

a) Find the PERT average for each activity.
b) Add the PERT averages. Add the most likely numbers. Compare the two totals.
c) Round the PERT total up to whole working days.
d) The job starts on Monday, June 7, 2027. Using your answer to c), on what date does it finish? There are no statutory holidays in that part of June.
e) Why do schedules built only from "most likely" numbers often finish late?

Task 6 (level 3). Use your answer to Task 4. Write a three-sentence message to the building manager that gives the estimated cost, says what the contingency is for, and says how long the pump change should take.
