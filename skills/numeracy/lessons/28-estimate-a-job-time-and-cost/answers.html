Estimate a job's time and cost

Use a past job, a unit rate or a bottom-up list to estimate a job, work a three-point time estimate with BEDMAS, and build a cost estimate with labour, material and contingency.

  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Task 1 (level 1-2)

a) Unit rate. 450 ÷ 30 = 15 hours, from the crew's rate per square metre.
b) Bottom-up. Every piece is estimated, then added.
c) Past job. The washroom on the floor below is used as the match.
d) Unit rate. A cost per metre from records, multiplied by this job's length.
e) Bottom-up. Each part is priced, then added, then contingency goes on top.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) Simple average: (3 + 4 + 11) ÷ 3 = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 days.
PERT: (3 + 4 × 4 + 11) ÷ 6 = (3 + 16 + 11) ÷ 6 = 30 ÷ 6 = 5 days.

b) Simple average: (30 + 36 + 54) ÷ 3 = 120 ÷ 3 = 40 crew-hours.
PERT: (30 + 4 × 36 + 54) ÷ 6 = (30 + 144 + 54) ÷ 6 = 228 ÷ 6 = 38 crew-hours.

c) Simple average: (8 + 10 + 18) ÷ 3 = 36 ÷ 3 = 12 days.
PERT: (8 + 4 × 10 + 18) ÷ 6 = (8 + 40 + 18) ÷ 6 = 66 ÷ 6 = 11 days.

d) Without brackets the calculator follows BEDMAS on the line as keyed: it works 4 × 4 = 16 and 11 ÷ 6 = 1.83 first, then adds 3 + 16 + 1.83 = 20.83. Only the 11 was divided by 6. The brackets make the whole sum get divided: ( 3 + 4 × 4 + 11 ) ÷ 6 = 5.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 360 ÷ 20 = 18 painter-hours.
b) 18 ÷ 2 = 9 hours each.
c) 9 ÷ 8 = 1.125. Round up: 2 working days. The second day is a short one, but it still ties up the painters.
d) 18 × $45.00 = $810.00. Use the total painter-hours, not the hours of one painter.

Task 4 (level 2-3)

a) 2 × 6 = 12 hours. 12 × $55.00 = $660.00.
b) $660.00 + $1,240.00 + $360.00 + $40.00 = $2,300.00.
c) Contingency: $2,300.00 × 0.15 = $345.00. Estimated cost: $2,300.00 + $345.00 = $2,645.00.
d) ($2,400 + $2,800) ÷ 2 = $5,200 ÷ 2 = $2,600. The estimate of $2,645 is only $45 more than the past-job average, so it looks reasonable.

Task 5 (level 3)

a) - A: (1 + 4 × 1 + 4) ÷ 6 = (1 + 4 + 4) ÷ 6 = 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 days - B: (2 + 4 × 3 + 4) ÷ 6 = (2 + 12 + 4) ÷ 6 = 18 ÷ 6 = 3 days - C: (2 + 4 × 2 + 8) ÷ 6 = (2 + 8 + 8) ÷ 6 = 18 ÷ 6 = 3 days

b) PERT total: 1.5 + 3 + 3 = 7.5 days. Most likely total: 1 + 3 + 2 = 6 days. The PERT total is 1.5 days longer.

c) Round 7.5 up: 8 working days.

d) Mon June 7 (1), Tue June 8 (2), Wed June 9 (3), Thu June 10 (4), Fri June 11 (5), skip the weekend, Mon June 14 (6), Tue June 15 (7), Wed June 16 (8). The job finishes on Wednesday, June 16, 2027.

e) Open answer. A good answer says that "most likely" leaves out the bad days. Each task can run late by more than it can run early, and on a chain of tasks those late days add up. A schedule built only on M has no room for any of them.

Task 6 (level 3)

Open task. A good message:

  • gives the estimated cost, $2,645
  • says the contingency ($345, 15%) covers old piping that may have to be replaced once the pump is out
  • gives the time: 2 pipefitters for about 6 hours, so one working day
  • uses plain words and no more than three sentences

Example: "The pump change is estimated at $2,645. That includes $345 set aside in case the old piping needs replacing when we take the pump out. Two pipefitters should have it done in one working day."
