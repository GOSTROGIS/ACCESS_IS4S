Order of operations in trade formulas

Use BEDMAS and the calculator's brackets to work out stud counts, stair totals, perimeters and areas without mixing up the order.

  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Why this matters on the job

Trade formulas pack several steps into one line: the number of studs in a wall, the total run of a stair, the distance around a frame. If two people work the same line in a different order, they get different answers, and one of them orders the wrong material. BEDMAS is the agreed order, so a formula on a drawing means the same thing to everyone who reads it.

Key ideas

BEDMAS gives the order, from first to last:

Letter Stands for Example
B Brackets ( ) and [ ] (12 + 8) × 3: add 12 + 8 first
E Exponents: squares, powers and roots 100 − 5²: work out 5² first
D and M Division and Multiplication, left to right 24 ÷ 4 × 3: divide first, because ÷ comes first
A and S Addition and Subtraction, left to right 20 − 8 + 2: subtract first, because − comes first

D and M are one step, not two. Work them in the order they appear, left to right. The same goes for A and S.

Rewrite the line after each step. Write the whole calculation again with the part you just worked out replaced by its answer. Each line gets shorter until one number is left.

Brackets inside brackets. Work the innermost pair first, then work outward. On the calculator, every bracket is keyed with the ( and ) keys. Close every bracket you open.

Key the × before a bracket. On paper, 3(4 + 5) means 3 × (4 + 5). On the calculator, always key the × yourself.

Studs in a wall. Studs are set a fixed distance apart, centre to centre, called the spacing on centre (o.c.). There is one more stud than there are spaces:

studs = length ÷ spacing + 1

Divide first, then add 1. If length ÷ spacing is not a whole number, round it up before you add 1, so no space is wider than the spacing. This counts the regular studs only.

Stair totals. In the stairs in this lesson, the upper floor is the top step, so a flight has one tread fewer than it has risers:

total rise = number of risers × unit rise total run = (number of risers − 1) × unit run

The brackets make the subtraction happen before the multiplication.

Worked example

A wall is 4,800 mm long, with studs at 400 mm o.c. How many studs?

  1. BEDMAS divides before it adds: 4,800 ÷ 400 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13 studs.
  2. Brackets in the wrong place change the job: 4,800 ÷ (400 + 1) = 11.97, which is not a stud count.

On the TI-30Xa or the Sharp EL-531XT: 4800 ÷ 400 + 1 = shows 13.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Work out each line with BEDMAS. Rewrite the line after each step.

a) 6 + 4 × 3 b) 20 − 12 ÷ 4 c) 5 × 6 − 4 × 3 d) 18 ÷ 3 + 2 × 7 e) 8 − 3 × 2 + 1 f) 24 ÷ 4 × 3

Task 2 (level 1-2). Brackets and exponents.

a) (12 + 8) × 3 b) 2 × (450 + 300), the distance around a 450 mm by 300 mm frame c) 2 × [15 − (4 + 6)] d) 100 − 5² e) Nine holes are evenly spaced on a 1,350 mm bar, with the end holes 75 mm from the ends. The spacing is (1,350 − 2 × 75) ÷ 8. Find it.

Task 3 (level 2). Use studs = length ÷ spacing + 1 to count the regular studs.

a) A 3,600 mm wall at 400 mm o.c. b) A 6,000 mm wall at 600 mm o.c. c) A 16 ft wall at 16 in o.c. d) A 4,100 mm wall at 400 mm o.c.

Task 4 (level 2). A stair has 16 risers with a unit rise of 175 mm and a unit run of 255 mm.

a) Find the total rise. b) Find the total run. c) A learner keys 16 − 1 × 255 = and gets −239. What went wrong?

Task 5 (level 2). Work out each formula. Give the area of the ring to two decimal places.

a) A plate is split into two rectangles. Its area in mm² is 300 × 450 + 900 × 250. b) A frame is 600 mm long and 250 mm wide. The distance around it is 2 × (600 + 250). c) A ring has an outside radius of 40 mm and an inside radius of 25 mm. Its area is π × (40² − 25²).

Task 6 (level 2). A pipe run is 7 ft 9 1/2 in long. The length in millimetres is (7 × 12 + 9.5) × 25.4.

a) Write the keys you would press on your calculator, brackets included. b) Work it out. c) What answer do you get if you leave the brackets out? Why is it wrong?
