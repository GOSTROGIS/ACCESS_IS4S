Order of operations in trade formulas

Use BEDMAS and the calculator's brackets to work out stud counts, stair totals, perimeters and areas without mixing up the order.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 12 of 16. Total length: 75 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator or the ACCESS calculators page. Project the Numeracy Hub Order of Operations tab. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 03 (BEDMAS), Day 04 (brackets, squares and square roots) and Day 16 (substituting into formulas).

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Write on the board: "A crate holds 3 loose bolts and 4 bags of 5 bolts: 3 + 4 × 5." Ask two volunteers to work it out, one adding first and one multiplying first. Ask: "Which answer matches the crate?" (23, not 35.) Say: "A formula is only useful if everyone works it in the same order." Tell learners this is session 12, and it runs 75 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • pick out the operations and the brackets in a trade formula (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a formula to the job it describes, such as studs, stairs, frames and plates (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • work out a formula with BEDMAS by hand and with the calculator's brackets (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check that an answer makes sense for the job, for example a stud count that is a whole number (Interpret and evaluate the information)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. 3 + 4 × 5 = ? (23)
  2. Has your calculator ever given you an answer you did not expect? What do you think happened? (open)

Learning tasks (55 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach BEDMAS with the table. Stress that D and M are one step, worked left to right, and the same for A and S. Model "rewrite the line after each step". 1.1 Follow the table and copy one rewritten example. Learner page, Hub Order of Operations 7 min
1.2 Circulate during Task 1. Check that each learner rewrites the line. 1.2 Complete Task 1. Learner page 7 min
2.1 Teach brackets, brackets inside brackets, exponents and keying the × before a bracket. 2.1 Complete Task 2. Learner page, Textbook p. 70 10 min
3.1 Teach the stud formula with the worked example. Show the wrong-brackets result on the calculator. 3.1 Work the example on a calculator, both ways. Textbook p. 81 5 min
3.2 Circulate during Tasks 3 and 4. Ask: "Is your stud count a whole number? Should it be?" 3.2 Complete Tasks 3 and 4. Learner page 12 min
4.1 Pairs work Tasks 5 and 6. One partner keys, the other reads the keys aloud from the page and checks the display. 4.1 Complete Tasks 5 and 6 in pairs. Learner page, calculators 14 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Studs for a 4,800 mm wall at 600 mm o.c. (4,800 ÷ 600 + 1 = 9)
  2. Total run of a stair with 14 risers and a unit run of 260 mm. ((14 − 1) × 260 = 3,380 mm)
  3. The distance around a 3,600 mm by 2,400 mm deck frame, 2 × (3,600 + 2,400). (12,000 mm)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • BEDMAS, with D and M left to right and A and S left to right
  • rewriting the line after each step
  • brackets, brackets inside brackets and exponents, by hand and on the calculator
  • the stud formula and the stair totals
  • checking that an answer makes sense for the job

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W02-L01, W02-L02, W02-L03 and W05-L03.
