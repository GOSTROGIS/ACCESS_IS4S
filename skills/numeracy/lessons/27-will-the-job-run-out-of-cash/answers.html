Will the job run out of cash

Lay out the money coming in and going out of a small crew month by month, find the month the bank balance is lowest, and work out how big a line of credit the crew needs.

Task 2 (level 2-3)

a) Claims: $24,000 + $36,000 + $36,000 + $30,000 + $18,000 = $144,000. Holdback: $144,000 × 0.10 = $14,400 b) $14,400 × 1.05 = $15,120 c) August 16 to August 31 is 15 days. 55 − 15 = 40. September has 30 days, so 40 − 30 = 10. The holdback period ends on Sunday, October 10, 2027.

Task 5 (level 3)

a) May, at −$6,020. b) 3 months: April, May and June. c) $27,570 − $8,000 = $19,570 more. Cash increases over the forecast, but the month-end balance is below zero for the first three months. This cash increase is not the same as accounting profit.

Task 8 (level 3)

Open task. A good answer:

  • asks for a stated limit, for example $15,000 (Task 6)
  • names May as the month of greatest need, about $6,020 below zero
  • explains the timing: the crew and the supplier are paid before the contractor pays, claims are paid 30 days after the work, and 10% holdback is kept until October
  • says the forecast shows the account back above zero by July and about $27,570 by the end of October
  • is short, polite and uses numbers from the forecast
