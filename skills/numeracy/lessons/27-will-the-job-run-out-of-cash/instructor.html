Will the job run out of cash

Lay out the money coming in and going out of a small crew month by month, find the month the bank balance is lowest, and work out how big a line of credit the crew needs.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 27 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator and the learner page. Run this lesson after lesson 23 (progress claims and holdback). Print Document A or project it. If the group has laptops, Tasks 1 to 4 can be built in a spreadsheet, one row per item and one column per month (Digital lesson 5). Check the Builders Lien Act holdback period on BC Laws on the day, as in lesson 23.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Can a company that makes money go broke?" Take answers. Say: "Today you follow a two-person roofing crew through one season. The forecast ends with almost $20,000 more cash than it started with; that is not a calculation of accounting profit. In May the bank account is $6,000 below zero." Tell learners this is session 27 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the claims, payment terms, holdback, supplier terms and remittances in a season plan (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect the month work is done to the month the money moves (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • calculate monthly money in, money out, net cash flow and a running balance (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • find the lowest balance, size a line of credit and judge the effect of slower payment (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • ask a bank for a line of credit in writing, using the forecast (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. What is −$3,400 + $5,000? ($1,600)
  2. What is $36,000 × 0.945? ($34,020)

Learning tasks (70 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach cash flow versus profit. Model the worked example: claim to payment with holdback and GST, then the April balance. Show how a negative balance is added to. 1.1 Follow the worked example. Board, learner page 10 min
1.2 Read Document A together. Ask: "Which month is the April claim paid? The April lumber?" Explain net 30, the CRA remittance due date for a regular remitter, and the quarterly GST due date. 1.2 Read Document A and mark the month each item is paid. Learner page, CRA pages 8 min
2.1 Learners do Tasks 1 to 3. Circulate. Check that each payment is moved one month later. 2.1 Complete Tasks 1, 2 and 3. Learner page, calculator 14 min
2.2 Learners build the forecast in Task 4. Model May on the board first. Remind them that the end balance of one month starts the next. 2.2 Complete Task 4. Learner page 15 min
3.1 Discuss Tasks 5 and 6. Ask: "Why does cash rise by $19,570 over the whole forecast but the crew still needs a line of credit earlier?" 3.1 Complete Tasks 5 and 6. Learner page 8 min
3.2 Groups of three do Task 7. Each person takes two or three months, then the group checks the running balance together. 3.2 Complete Task 7 in groups. Learner page 9 min
3.3 Learners write Task 8. One or two read aloud. 3.3 Write and share Task 8. Learner page 6 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A month starts at −$2,000. Money in is $12,000 and money out is $9,500. What is the balance at the end? ($500)
  2. A $20,000 claim is paid with 10% holdback kept and 5% GST added to what is paid. How much comes in? ($20,000 × 0.945 = $18,900)
  3. The lowest balance in a forecast is −$8,300. With a $5,000 cushion and limits in steps of $5,000, what line of credit is needed? ($13,300 rounds up to $15,000)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • cash flow as money moving in the month it moves, which is not the same as profit
  • how claims, holdback, supplier terms, payroll remittances and GST move money later or earlier
  • net cash flow and the running balance, month by month
  • finding the lowest balance and sizing a line of credit
  • how slower payment from a general contractor deepens the low point
