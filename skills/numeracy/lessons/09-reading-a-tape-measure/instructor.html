Reading a tape measure to the sixteenth

Read an inch tape to the nearest 1/16 inch, write each reading in lowest terms and compare fractional lengths.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 9 of 16. Total length: 75 minutes.

Before the session: have one tape measure for each pair, scrap lumber offcuts at least 600 mm long, pencils, and the Numeracy Hub Measuring Tools tab ready to project. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 05, Day 06 and Day 07.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a tape and a 2x4 offcut. Say: "On a framing crew, every cut starts with a number you read off this tape. If each piece is a sixteenth long, a wall of studs, plates and blocks grows a little with every piece." Ask: "Where have you used a tape before: at home, on a job, in the bush, in the kitchen?" Take two or three answers. Tell learners this is session 9, and it runs 75 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the whole inch and the sixteenth spaces for a mark on a tape (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect equivalent names for the same mark, such as 12/16 and 3/4 (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • read a tape to the nearest 1/16 in, reduce to lowest terms and compare lengths with a common denominator (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check that a reading makes sense against the nearest half and quarter lines (Interpret and evaluate the information)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. Which is longer, 1/2 in or 1/4 in? (1/2 in)
  2. How many sixteenths make one inch? (16)

Learning tasks (55 min)

1.2 Teach "read an inch as equal spaces": the denominator names the space, the numerator counts spaces, count spaces not lines. Model the worked example (2 5/8 in) on a projected tape. Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A mark is ten sixteenth-inch spaces past the 48 in line. What is the reading? (48 5/8 in)
  2. Put these in order from smallest to largest: 3/4 in, 5/8 in, 11/16 in. (5/8, 11/16, 3/4)
  3. Write 6/16 in lowest terms. (3/8)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • reading a tape as equal spaces: the whole inch first, then the sixteenth spaces
  • counting spaces, not lines
  • writing readings in lowest terms and saying the whole reading out loud
  • comparing fractional lengths with a common denominator
  • letting the tape set the precision of a reading

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W02-L05 and W02-L06, and the Day 06 and Day 07 decks for equivalent fractions and marking fractional dimensions.
