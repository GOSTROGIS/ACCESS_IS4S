Roof slope, stair rise and stair run

Write a roof slope as a rise in 12 and as 1 in a number, work out the risers, unit rise and total run of a stair, and check them against the BC Building Code.

Why this matters on the job

Roofs and stairs are both about rise and run. A roof slope decides which roofing can go on it. A stair has to fit the height between floors with every step the same, inside the limits of the BC Building Code. Carpenters work these numbers out before cutting a rafter or a stringer.

Key ideas

Slope is a ratio of rise to run. Carpenters write roof slope as a rise in 12: a 4 in 12 roof rises 4 in for every 12 in it runs across. That is the ratio 4 : 12, which simplifies to 1 : 3. The BC Building Code writes the same slope as "1 in 3": 1 unit of rise for 3 units of run.

  • Rise in 12 to "1 in ?": divide 12 by the rise. 6 in 12 is 1 in 2.
  • "1 in ?" to rise in 12: divide 12 by the second number. 1 in 4 is 3 in 12.
  • Rise from run: rise = run × (rise in 12) ÷ 12.
  • In "1 in ?", a smaller second number means a steeper slope. 1 in 2.4 is steeper than 1 in 3.

What the code says about roof slope. Table 9.26.3.1 of the BC Building Code 2024 sets the minimum slope for each kind of roofing. For asphalt shingles it gives a minimum of 1 in 3 for normal application and 1 in 6 for low slope application, with no maximum.

Stair words. The total rise is the height from finished floor to finished floor. Each step up is a riser. The height of one step is the unit rise and its depth, nosing to nosing, is the unit run. In the stairs in this lesson, the upper floor is the top step, so a flight has one tread fewer than it has risers.

Stair formulas. Work in millimetres.

  • number of risers = total rise ÷ maximum rise, rounded up to a whole number
  • unit rise = total rise ÷ number of risers
  • number of treads = number of risers − 1
  • total run = number of treads × unit run

Round the number of risers up, never down: rounding down makes each riser taller than the maximum.

What the code says about stairs (BC Building Code 2024, Division B):

Risers in one flight 5 mm between adjacent treads or landings, 10 mm between the tallest and shortest the same Sentence 9.8.4.4.(1)

The code measures the rise as "the vertical nosing-to-nosing distance". Private stairs "are exterior and interior stairs that serve" single dwelling units, houses with a secondary suite including their common spaces, and garages that serve those houses. Public stairs "are all stairs not described as service stairs or private stairs." The depth of a rectangular tread "shall be not less than its run and not more than its run plus 25 mm."

Worked example

A private stair in a house has a total rise of 2,690 mm. The drawing gives a unit run of 260 mm.

  1. Risers: 2,690 ÷ 200 = 13.45. Round up to 14 risers.
  2. Unit rise: 2,690 ÷ 14 = 192.14 mm. It is between 125 mm and 200 mm.
  3. Why not 13 risers? 2,690 ÷ 13 = 206.92 mm, taller than the 200 mm maximum.
  4. Treads: 14 − 1 = 13. The unit run of 260 mm is between 255 mm and 355 mm.
  5. Total run: 13 × 260 = 3,380 mm.
  6. Flight height: 2,690 mm is less than 3.7 m (3,700 mm).

Practice

Task 1 (level 1-2). Roof slope.

a) Write 6 in 12 as "1 in ?". b) Write a slope of 1 in 4 as a rise in 12. c) A roof at 4 in 12 has a run of 4,200 mm. How much does it rise? d) Write 5 in 12 as "1 in ?".

Task 2 (level 2). Use Table 9.26.3.1.

a) Is a 3 in 12 roof steep enough for asphalt shingles in normal application? b) Is a 4 in 12 roof steep enough for asphalt shingles in normal application? c) Is a 3 in 12 roof steep enough for asphalt shingles in low slope application?

Task 3 (level 2). Rafters. The ridge is in the middle of the building, so the run is half the span. Use rafter line length = √(run² + rise²).

a) A building is 7,200 mm wide with a 5 in 12 roof. Find the run, the rise and the rafter line length. b) A building is 9,000 mm wide with an 8 in 12 roof. Find the run, the rise and the rafter line length to two decimal places.

Task 4 (level 2-3). A private stair in a house has a total rise of 2,720 mm. The treads have a unit run of 265 mm.

a) Find the number of risers and the unit rise, to two decimal places. b) Check the unit rise and the unit run against the table. c) Find the number of treads and the total run. d) What would the unit rise be with one riser fewer? Is that allowed?

Task 5 (level 2-3). A public stair has a total rise of 3,150 mm and a unit run of 300 mm.

a) Is one flight allowed for this height? b) Find the number of risers and the unit rise. c) Check the unit run, then find the total run.

Task 6 (level 2). A deck stair has 6 risers of 7 1/4 in and 5 treads with a unit run of 10 1/2 in.

a) Find the total rise and the total run in inches. b) Change the unit rise and the unit run to millimetres (1 in = 25.4 mm). c) Are they inside the private stair limits?

Task 7 (level 3). The risers of a built flight in a house measure, from the bottom: 191, 194, 189, 195, 192 and 190 mm.

a) Find the difference between each pair of neighbouring risers. b) Find the difference between the tallest and the shortest riser. c) Does the flight meet Sentence 9.8.4.4.(1)? d) Write two or three sentences to your supervisor that report what you found and where.

Check yourself

  1. Why do you round the number of risers up, not down?
  2. A flight has 15 risers. How many treads does it have, when the upper floor is the top step?
  3. Write 4 in 12 as "1 in ?".
  4. What is the maximum unit rise for a private stair in the BC Building Code 2024?
  5. A public stair has a unit rise of 185 mm. Is that allowed?
