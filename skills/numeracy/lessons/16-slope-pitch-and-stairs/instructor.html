Roof slope, stair rise and stair run

Write a roof slope as a rise in 12 and as 1 in a number, work out the risers, unit rise and total run of a stair, and check them against the BC Building Code.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 16 of 16. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator. Print or project the ACCESS plates for 9.8.4.1, 9.8.4.2, 9.8.4.4 and 9.26.3.1, and open the Numeracy Hub Carpentry tab. A tape measure lets learners measure the rise and run of a real stair in the building. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 14 (ratios, rates and proportions) and Day 23 (angles, grades and carpentry slope applications). Learners should have done lesson 15 (right triangles) before Task 3.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. If the building has a stair, take the group to it, or show a photo. Ask: "Have you ever tripped on a stair where one step was a bit taller than the rest? Why does that happen?" Take answers. Say: "The BC Building Code limits how tall each step can be and how much the steps in one flight can differ. Today you work out a stair and check it against those limits." Tell learners this is session 16, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say what a stair or roof question asks for and what form the answer takes (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • pick out the total rise, the stair type, the run and the slope from a drawing or a job description (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a slope written as a rise in 12 to the same slope written as "1 in" a number, and link stair rise and run to the right triangle (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • work out risers, unit rise, treads, total run, roof rise and rafter length (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check results against the BC Building Code 2024 limits (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • report a stair that does not meet the code (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. Simplify the ratio 4 : 12. (1 : 3)
  2. 2,690 ÷ 200 = 13.45. How many risers do you need: 13 or 14? Why? (14, because 13 would make each riser taller than 200 mm)

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A private stair has a total rise of 2,850 mm. Find the number of risers and the unit rise. (2,850 ÷ 200 = 14.25, so 15 risers of 190 mm)
  2. Write 3 in 12 as "1 in ?". (1 in 4)
  3. Is a unit rise of 185 mm allowed on a public stair? (No, the maximum is 180 mm)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • slope as a rise in 12 and as "1 in" a number
  • minimum roof slopes for asphalt shingles in Table 9.26.3.1
  • rafter run, rise and line length
  • risers, unit rise, treads and total run, with risers always rounded up
  • the stair limits in BC Building Code 2024 Tables 9.8.4.1 and 9.8.4.2, Article 9.8.3.3 and Sentence 9.8.4.4.(1)
  • reporting a stair that does not meet the code

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W04-L07, W04-L08 and W06-L09. Grades in percent and angles of slope are taught in the Day 23 deck and textbook sections 13.5 and 13.8.
