Credit card interest and minimum payments

Read a credit card information box, work out interest and minimum payments, and see what paying only the minimum costs over time.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 120 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 3 of 8. Total length: 120 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a receipt for a tool kit, real or drawn. Ask: "If this went on a credit card, what would it really cost?" Collect guesses on the board and leave them up to compare at the end. Ask learners to recall the location strategy for document use: decide what you need, scan the headings, find the row and column, then check.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find rates, fees, the grace period and the minimum payment rule in a card information box (Identify the mathematical information)
  • link a balance, a rate and a payment to the next month's balance (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • calculate monthly interest, minimum payments, cash advance costs and payment plan totals (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • judge what paying only the minimum means over time (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • explain the result to someone else in a few sentences (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

This lesson presents facts and calculations. It does not recommend a card or a way of paying.

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. What is 3% of $600? ($18)
  2. What is $240 ÷ 12? ($20)

Post-assessment (15 min)

  1. A balance is $2,000 at 24% a year. Using the classroom model, what is one month of interest? ($2,000 × 0.24 ÷ 12 = $40.00)
  2. Using the Classic card rule, what is the minimum payment on $300? (3% is $9.00, which is less than $10, so $10.00)
  3. You take a $200 cash advance and pay it back in 10 days at 22.99% a year, with a $5 fee. What does it cost? ($200 × 0.2299 ÷ 365 × 10 = $1.26, plus $5.00, total $6.26)

Review answers together. Ask where each number came from in Document A and why it was the right one. Discuss what the numbers mean for buying tools or a vehicle. Make sure everyone takes a turn.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • reading a credit card information box
  • the 21-day interest-free grace period and why cash advances have none
  • working out minimum payments and one month of interest
  • what paying only the minimum costs over time, compared with a fixed payment
  • the cost of a cash advance and of a buy now, pay later plan
