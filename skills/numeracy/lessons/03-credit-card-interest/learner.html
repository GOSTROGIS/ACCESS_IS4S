Credit card interest and minimum payments

Read a credit card information box, work out interest and minimum payments, and see what paying only the minimum costs over time.

Why this matters on the job

Many apprentices buy their first set of tools on credit. A $1,200 tool kit on a card can cost $1,200, or it can cost more than $2,400, depending only on how it is paid back. The difference is arithmetic you can do with a calculator. Knowing it before you buy helps you plan.

Key ideas

Interest rate. A credit card shows a yearly (annual) interest rate. In this lesson we use a simple classroom model for one month of interest:

monthly interest = balance × annual rate ÷ 12

Real cards work out interest on each day's balance, as the cardholder agreement explains. The model gives answers close to that and is easier to follow by hand.

Grace period. Federally regulated financial institutions must give at least 21 days without interest on new purchases, starting from the last day of the billing period (the statement date). You only get it if you pay the full balance by the due date. There is no grace period on cash advances: interest starts the day you take the cash.

Minimum payment. This is the smallest amount you must pay each month to keep the card in good standing. Paying only the minimum keeps the interest growing. Card statements from federally regulated institutions must show an estimate of how long it would take to pay the balance if you paid only the minimum.

Buy now, pay later (BNPL). The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) says that with a BNPL plan "you're financing your purchase with credit." The provider may check your credit, and missed payments can bring fees.

Worked example

A card has a balance of $800 and an annual rate of 18%. The minimum payment is $24.00.

  1. Pay the minimum: $800.00 − $24.00 = $776.00
  2. Interest for one month: $776.00 × 0.18 ÷ 12 = $11.64
  3. New balance: $776.00 + $11.64 = $787.64

After one month and a $24.00 payment, the balance went down by only $12.36. The other $11.64 of the payment covered interest.

On the HP 10bII+

The HP 10bII+ is a financial calculator. Out of the box the HP 10bII+ works in Chain mode: it does each operation in the order you key it, not in BEDMAS order. To see the mode, press RCL, then the blue shift key, then ON (marked Alg/Chain above it). CHAIN or ALGEBRAIC flashes on the screen. The keys below press = after each step, so they give the same answer in either mode.

The worked example on the HP 10bII+:

  1. 800 − 24 = shows 776.00
  2. × 18 % ÷ 12 = shows 11.64, the interest
    • 776 = shows 787.64, the new balance

The HP 10bII+ also has time value of money (TVM) keys along its top row: N (number of payments), I/YR (yearly interest rate), PV (present value), PMT (payment) and FV (future value). Enter four of them and it works out the fifth. Money you receive is positive and money you pay out is negative. Task 12 uses these keys to check the spreadsheet in Task 8. "Orange shift, %CHG" means press the orange shift key, then the key with %CHG printed on its edge.

Task 1 (level 1). Use Document A. Write down the purchase rate, the cash advance rate, the minimum payment rule and the length of the grace period for the Classic card.

Task 2 (level 2). You buy safety boots with the Classic card on October 15, 2026. The statement date is October 31, 2026. What is the earliest the payment due date can be? How much interest do you pay on the boots if you pay the full statement balance by that date?

Task 3 (level 1). What is the yearly fee for a Rewards card with one primary cardholder and one additional cardholder?

Task 4 (level 3). The Rewards card pays 1.5% back on purchases. How much would one cardholder have to spend in a year for the rewards to equal the $120 annual fee? About how much is that each month?

Task 5 (level 2). Use the Classic card's minimum payment rule. Find the minimum payment on a balance of:

a) $1,200.00 b) $250.00 c) $8.00

Task 9 (level 2). You take a $300.00 cash advance on the Classic card and pay it back 30 days later. Interest on a cash advance is charged daily from the first day. Interest = $300.00 × 0.2299 ÷ 365 × 30. Work left to right, because multiplication and division have the same rank in BEDMAS. Add the cash advance fee. What did the $300 cost you?

Task 10 (level 2). A store offers a buy now, pay later plan on a $600.00 tool set: 4 equal payments, one every 2 weeks, no interest if every payment is on time. The fictional provider charges a $10.00 fee for each missed payment.

a) How much is each payment? b) What is the total if every payment is on time? c) What is the total if one payment is missed and paid later? d) What would the tool set cost on the Classic card if you paid the full balance by the due date?

Task 11 (level 3). Write three or four sentences for a co-worker about the $1,200 tool kit. Use only your numbers from Tasks 6 to 8. Say what paying the minimum costs, what paying $100 a month costs, and where the information came from.

Task 12 (level 3, HP 10bII+). Check the $100.00 plan from Task 8 with the TVM keys. In the classroom model the payment comes off before the month's interest is added, so the calculator must be in Begin mode.

  1. Blue shift, C MEM, then 1. TVM CLR flashes: the TVM memory is cleared.
  2. 12, orange shift, P/YR. The screen shows 12.00: 12 payments a year.
  3. Orange shift, Beg/End. Repeat until BEGIN shows on the screen.
  4. 20.99 I/YR, 1200 PV, 100 +/− PMT, 0 FV.
  5. Press N.

a) What does the screen show? How many payments is that, and why is the last payment smaller than $100.00? b) Now key 12 N, then press PMT. What monthly payment clears the $1,200.00 in one year? Why is the number negative? c) Key 20.99, orange shift, NOM%, then 12, orange shift, P/YR, then orange shift, EFF%. This is the yearly rate when the month's interest is added every month. Why is it more than 20.99%?

When you finish, press orange shift, Beg/End until BEGIN goes off. The calculator keeps Begin mode until you change it.
