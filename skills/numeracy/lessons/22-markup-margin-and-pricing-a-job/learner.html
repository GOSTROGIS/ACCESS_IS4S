Markup, margin and pricing a job

Add up what a small job costs, set a price with a markup, and tell markup from margin so the job makes the money you planned.

  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Why this matters on the job

A lead hand who prices a small repair, a contractor who quotes a set of deck stairs, and a shop that sells a fabricated railing all do the same math. They add up what the job costs, then add enough on top to pay for the business and leave a profit. If the price only covers the cost, the work was done for nothing. If the math mixes up markup and margin, the job can earn less than the owner thinks.

Key ideas

Cost is what the job costs the company: material, labour and equipment. These are the direct costs of the job.

Overhead is what the business pays to stay open whether or not this job happens: the truck, insurance, the phone, the shop, the bookkeeper. Many small companies add overhead to each job as a percent of the direct cost.

Price is what the customer pays. Profit = price − cost.

Markup is the profit as a percent of the cost.

  • price = cost × (1 + markup). A 20% markup means multiply by 1.20.
  • markup % = (price − cost) ÷ cost × 100

Margin is the profit as a percent of the price.

  • price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). A 20% margin means divide by 0.80.
  • margin % = (price − cost) ÷ price × 100

The same job always has a bigger markup than margin, because the cost is smaller than the price. A 20% markup is only a 16.67% margin. Say which one you mean.

Brackets first (BEDMAS). In (price − cost) ÷ cost × 100, do the subtraction in the brackets first, then divide, then multiply.

Sales tax goes on after the price. In BC the GST is 5%. It is added on top of the price and passed on to the Canada Revenue Agency. It is not part of your profit. Lesson 23 shows how GST works on a construction invoice.

Worked example

Alder Street Renovations (a fictional company) is asked to replace a 6 m section of fence.

Item Cost
Material: posts, rails, boards, screws, concrete $640.00
Labour: 2 workers × 8 h × $45.00 an hour $720.00
Equipment: auger rental for the day $90.00
Direct cost $1,450.00

The company adds 10% for overhead and a 20% markup for profit.

  1. Overhead: $1,450.00 × 0.10 = $145.00
  2. Total cost: $1,450.00 + $145.00 = $1,595.00
  3. Price with a 20% markup: $1,595.00 × 1.20 = $1,914.00
  4. Profit: $1,914.00 − $1,595.00 = $319.00
  5. Margin: $319.00 ÷ $1,914.00 × 100 = 16.67%

The owner asked for a 20% markup and got it. The margin on the same job is 16.67%.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Find the price. Round to the cent.

a) Cost $200.00, markup 25% b) Cost $1,240.00, markup 15% c) Cost $86.40, markup 50%

Task 2 (level 2). Find the markup % and the margin % for each job. Round to two decimal places.

a) Cost $400.00, price $500.00 b) Cost $1,500.00, price $2,000.00 c) Cost $750.00, price $900.00

Task 3 (level 2). A job costs $1,200.00. The owner wants a 20% margin.

a) What price gives a 20% margin? b) What price would a 20% markup give? c) How much less does the company earn if someone uses the markup by mistake?

Task 4 (level 2-3). Alder Street Renovations is pricing a new set of deck stairs. The company adds 12% for overhead and a 15% markup. Round to the cent at each step.

Item Quantity Unit cost
2×12 pressure-treated stringer, 3.0 m 3 $38.50
2×6 pressure-treated tread board, 1.2 m 14 $9.80
Stair hardware kit 1 $46.20
Concrete mix, 30 kg bag 4 $8.95
Labour 12 h $42.00 an hour

a) Find the cost of each material line and the material total. b) Find the labour cost and the direct cost. c) Add the overhead. d) Add the markup to get the price. e) Add 5% GST. What does the customer pay?

Task 5 (level 3). A supplier lists a steel gate at $3,450.00. Contractors get 15% off the list price. The contractor installs it and charges the customer the list price for the gate.

a) What does the gate cost the contractor? b) What markup does the contractor make on the gate? c) What margin? d) A co-worker says, "We get 15% off, so we make a 15% markup." Is that right? Explain in one sentence.

Task 6 (level 3). Spot the mistake. A job costs $2,100.00. The owner says, "Price it for a 30% margin." Jordan works out $2,100.00 × 1.30.

a) What price did Jordan get? b) What margin does that price really give? c) What price gives a 30% margin?

Task 7 (level 3). A quote of $2,500.00 was built on a cost of $2,000.00. The customer asks for 20% off.

a) What is the price after 20% off? What profit is left? b) The owner will not go below a 10% markup. What is the lowest price? c) What is the largest discount, as a percent of $2,500.00, the owner can give?

Task 8 (level 3). Write two or three sentences to the owner of Alder Street Renovations about the deck stairs in Task 4. Give the price before GST, the profit in dollars and the margin as a percent.

Check yourself

  1. A part costs $80.00. What is the price with a 25% markup?
  2. Cost $48.00, price $60.00. What is the markup %? What is the margin %?
  3. Which is bigger on the same job, the markup % or the margin %?
  4. What price gives a 25% margin on a cost of $900.00?
  5. Is GST part of the contractor's profit?
