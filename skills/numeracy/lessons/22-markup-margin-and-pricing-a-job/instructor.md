# Markup, margin and pricing a job

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 22 of 34. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator. This lesson opens the Numeracy money-on-the-job set (lessons 22 to 27). It uses percent of an amount from lesson 3 and the BEDMAS rules from lesson 12. Lesson 26 shows where the labour rate in Task 4 comes from, and lesson 25 uses the markup idea again for break-even.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "A neighbour asks you to fix a fence. The material is $600. What do you charge?" Write a few answers on the board. Ask: "Did anyone count your time, the truck, the auger rental, the insurance?" Say: "Today you learn how a contractor turns a cost into a price, and why two words, markup and margin, can cost a company money if they get mixed up." Tell learners this is session 22 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- pick out the cost, overhead, markup and price on a job estimate (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect cost, profit, markup and margin for the same job (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- calculate a price from a markup or a margin, and a markup or margin from a cost and a price (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- check whether a price meets the profit the owner asked for (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- report a price, a profit and a margin to an owner (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. What is 20% of $500? ($100)
2. What is $1,450 × 1.10? ($1,595)

## Learning tasks (70 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Teach cost, overhead, price and profit with the fence job. Ask learners to sort the board items (truck insurance, posts, auger rental, phone bill, labour) into direct cost or overhead. | 1.1 Sort the items. Follow the worked example to the price. | Board, learner page | 10 min |
| 1.2 | Teach markup as a multiplier. Show that 20% markup is × 1.20. | 1.2 Complete Task 1. | Learner page, calculator | 5 min |
| 2.1 | Teach margin. Work the fence job backwards: profit ÷ price. Stress brackets first in (price − cost) ÷ cost × 100. | 2.1 Complete Task 2. Compare the markup and margin in each row. | Learner page | 10 min |
| 2.2 | Teach price from a margin: divide by (1 − margin). Spot the mistake: multiply by 1.20 when the owner asked for a 20% margin. | 2.2 Complete Task 3. | Learner page | 7 min |
| 3.1 | Circulate during Task 4. Ask: "When do you round? What is overhead a percent of?" Point out that GST goes on after the price and is not profit. | 3.1 Complete Task 4. | Learner page, calculator | 15 min |
| 3.2 | Pairs work Tasks 5 and 6. Take answers and ask each pair to say why a 15% discount is a 15% margin, not a 15% markup. | 3.2 Complete Tasks 5 and 6 in pairs. | Learner page | 10 min |
| 3.3 | Introduce Task 7 as a customer asking for a deal. Discuss what a 0% profit means for the crew. | 3.3 Complete Task 7. | Learner page | 7 min |
| 3.4 | Two learners read their Task 8 notes. The group checks that each gives the price, the profit and the margin. | 3.4 Write Task 8 and give one point of feedback. | Learner notes | 6 min |

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. A job costs $1,000. What is the price with a 30% markup? ($1,000 × 1.30 = $1,300)
2. What is the margin on that job? ($300 ÷ $1,300 × 100 = 23.08%)
3. What price gives a 30% margin on a $1,000 cost? ($1,000 ÷ 0.70 = $1,428.57)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- direct cost, overhead, price and profit
- markup as a percent of cost and margin as a percent of price
- finding a price from a markup or from a margin
- why a supplier discount is a margin, not a markup, when you sell at list
- why a discount can wipe out the profit on a job
- adding GST after the price
