Converting between fractions, decimals and millimetres

Change tape fractions to decimal inches and millimetres and back, round a calculator result to the nearest sixteenth, and move between metric units.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 11 of 16. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator or the ACCESS calculators page, and a tape with both inch and metric marks if you have one. Project the Numeracy Hub Unit Conversions tab. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 12 (decimals) and Day 15 (measurement systems and exact conversion factors).

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a tape with both scales, or show both on the screen. Say: "The plans for a job come from an architect in millimetres. The cut list is in feet and inches. Your tape reads sixteenths. Today you learn to move between all three without losing a sixteenth on the way." Tell learners this is session 11, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • pick out the unit of each length on a drawing or a cut list (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a fraction, its decimal and its length in millimetres (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • convert with the exact factor 25.4 and round a decimal inch to the nearest sixteenth (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check that a converted length is the right size (Interpret and evaluate the information)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. How many inches are in one foot? (12)
  2. Write 1/2 as a decimal. (0.5)

Learning tasks (70 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach the fraction bar as divide, and decimal inches. Show 2nd F⇄D on the TI-30Xa and a b/c on the Sharp. 1.1 Complete Task 1, by hand and with the fraction key. Textbook p. 120, calculators 8 min
1.2 Teach the exact factor: the yard is 0.9144 m, the inch is 1/36 yard, so 1 in = 25.4 mm. Build the eighths table with learners. 1.2 Fill in the eighths table on paper, then compare with the learner page. Textbook p. 167 8 min
1.3 Teach feet and inches to inches, and the brackets on the calculator for (7 × 12 + 9.5) × 25.4. 1.3 Complete Task 2. Learner page 10 min
2.1 Teach the multiply-by-16 method with the worked example. Point out that the fraction key does not round to the tape. 2.1 Work the example on a calculator. Textbook p. 168 7 min
2.2 Circulate during Tasks 3 and 4. Ask learners to say whether each answer is bigger or smaller than the starting number, and why. 2.2 Complete Tasks 3 and 4. Learner page, Hub Unit Conversions 17 min
3.1 Review metric units as powers of ten. 3.1 Complete Task 5. Learner page 7 min
3.2 Run Task 6 as a whole-group discussion. 3.2 Complete Task 6 and explain each correction. Learner page 8 min
3.3 Pairs swap pages and check one answer from each task by working it the other way (mm back to inches). 3.3 Check a partner's answers by reversing them. Learner page 5 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Change 3/4 in to millimetres. (19.05 mm)
  2. Change 600 mm to inches, to the nearest 1/16 in. (600 ÷ 25.4 × 16 = 377.95, so 378 sixteenths = 23 5/8 in)
  3. Change 6 ft 8 in to millimetres. (80 in × 25.4 = 2,032 mm)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the fraction bar as divide, and decimal inches
  • the exact factor 1 in = 25.4 mm and why it is exact
  • feet and inches to inches, then to millimetres
  • rounding a decimal inch to the nearest sixteenth
  • moving between metric units by powers of ten
  • checking that a converted number is the right size

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W04-L03, W05-L01 and W05-L02.
