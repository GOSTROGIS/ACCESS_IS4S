Converting between fractions, decimals and millimetres

Change tape fractions to decimal inches and millimetres and back, round a calculator result to the nearest sixteenth, and move between metric units.

Why this matters on the job

BC job sites use both systems. A drawing may give 1,480 mm while your tape reads feet, inches and sixteenths. A cut list may be in feet and inches, a concrete form plan in metres, and a calculator always answers in decimals. Moving between fractions, decimals and millimetres without losing accuracy is part of reading plans and placing orders.

Key ideas

A fraction bar means divide. 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375. A size written this way is a decimal inch. For a mixed number, change only the fraction and keep the whole: 2 5/8 in = 2.625 in.

One inch is exactly 25.4 mm. Canadian law defines the yard as 0.9144 m and the inch as 1/36 of a yard, so 1 in = 0.9144 m ÷ 36 = 0.0254 m = 25.4 mm. The factor is exact, not rounded.

  • Inches to millimetres: multiply by 25.4.
  • Millimetres to inches: divide by 25.4.
  • One foot is exactly 0.3048 m, and 12 in = 1 ft.

Check the size of your answer. A millimetre is much smaller than an inch, so a length in millimetres is always the bigger number. If 3/8 in comes out as 0.015 mm, you divided when you should have multiplied.

Decimal inches to the nearest sixteenth. A calculator gives an answer such as 2.83 in. Your tape shows sixteenths. To mark it:

  1. Keep the whole inches.
  2. Multiply the decimal part by 16.
  3. Round to the nearest whole number. That is the number of sixteenths.
  4. Write it over 16 and put it in lowest terms.

The fraction key does not round to the tape: 0.83 becomes 83/100, which is not a tape mark. Use the multiply-by-16 method. For finer work on a steel rule, use 32 instead of 16, and say which one you used.

Feet and inches. Change to inches first: multiply the feet by 12 and add the inches. 7 ft 9 1/2 in = 7 × 12 + 9.5 = 93.5 in.

Metric units move by powers of ten. 1 m = 1,000 mm and 1 cm = 10 mm. Going to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger, so multiply. Going to a bigger unit, the number gets smaller, so divide.

Worked example

A metric drawing shows 1,480 mm. You have an inch tape. What do you mark, to the nearest 1/16 in?

  1. Divide by 25.4 to get inches, then multiply by 16 to count sixteenths, in one line: 1,480 ÷ 25.4 × 16 = 932.28 sixteenths.
  2. Round to the nearest whole number: 932 sixteenths.
  3. 932 ÷ 16 = 58.25 in = 58 1/4 in, which is 4 ft 10 1/4 in.

Mark 58 1/4 in.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Change each fraction to a decimal, or each decimal to a fraction in lowest terms.

a) 3/4 b) 1 7/8 c) 5/16 d) 0.625 e) 0.375

Task 2 (level 1-2). Change to millimetres or metres. Do not round.

a) 12 in to mm b) 3/8 in to mm c) 5/16 in to mm d) 7 ft 9 1/2 in to mm e) 20 ft to m

Task 3 (level 2). Change each length to inches, to the nearest 1/16 in.

a) 762 mm b) 2,000 mm c) 100 mm d) 50 mm e) 1,830 mm (also write it in feet and inches)

Task 4 (level 2). A calculator gives these decimal inches. Change each one to a tape reading.

a) 3.4 in, to the nearest 1/16 in b) 2.83 in, to the nearest 1/16 in c) 0.59 in, to the nearest 1/32 in

Task 5 (level 1). Change the metric units.

a) 2.45 m to mm b) 375 mm to m c) 3.6 m to mm d) 6,096 mm to m e) 42 cm to mm

Task 6 (level 2). Spot the mistake. Explain what went wrong and give the correct answer.

a) A learner changes 3/4 in to millimetres by dividing: 0.75 ÷ 25.4 = 0.03 mm. b) A learner adds 7 ft 9 in as 7 + 9 = 16 in. c) A learner keys 0.83 into the fraction key, gets 83/100 and marks 83/100 in on the tape.
