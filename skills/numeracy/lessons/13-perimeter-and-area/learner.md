# Perimeter and area with openings and cut-outs

## Why this matters on the job

Perimeter tells a carpenter how much wall plate, rim joist, fascia or baseboard a job needs. Area tells how many sheets of drywall, sheathing or subfloor to order. On a takeoff, doors and windows come out of the wall area, and holes come out of a plate. Sheets are sold whole, so the count is always rounded up.

## Key ideas

**Perimeter** is the distance around the outside of a shape. Add the outside edges. For a rectangle:

P = 2 × (length + width)

**Area** is the flat surface inside a shape, counted in squares. For a rectangle:

A = length × width

Both lengths must be in the same unit. An area always has a small 2 on its unit: mm², m², in², ft².

**Square units change by the factor twice.** 1 m = 1,000 mm, so 1 m² = 1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000 mm². 1 ft = 12 in, so 1 ft² = 12 × 12 = 144 in². To change mm² to m², divide by 1,000,000, not by 1,000. It is often easier to change each length to metres first, then multiply.

**Break it down.** A composite shape is made of simple shapes.

1. Split the shape with dashed lines into rectangles, triangles or circles. Label them A, B, C.
2. Find any missing sizes from the overall sizes.
3. Find the area of each piece.
4. Add the pieces. Subtract any opening or cut-out.

When two methods give the same answer, you can trust it. An L shape can be two rectangles added, or one big rectangle with a corner taken away.

**Openings and cut-outs.** On a wall, subtract the doors and windows the material does not cover. On a plate, subtract the holes. For a round hole, the drawing usually gives the diameter: divide by 2 to get the radius, then use π × r².

**Counting sheets.** Divide the area by the area one sheet covers, then round up. A result of 3.1 sheets means you buy 4.

### Worked example

A wall is 6 m long and 2.4 m high. It has a door 0.9 m by 2.1 m and a window 1.5 m by 1.2 m. Drywall comes in 1.2 m by 2.4 m sheets. How many sheets?

1. Wall: 6 × 2.4 = 14.4 m²
2. Door: 0.9 × 2.1 = 1.89 m². Window: 1.5 × 1.2 = 1.8 m²
3. Net area: 14.4 − 1.89 − 1.8 = 10.71 m²
4. One sheet: 1.2 × 2.4 = 2.88 m². 10.71 ÷ 2.88 = 3.72
5. Round up: **4 sheets**

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1). Perimeter.

a) The outside walls of a house are 12,200 mm by 8,500 mm. Find the perimeter.
b) A room is 4.2 m by 3.6 m. Find the perimeter.
c) Baseboard runs around the room in part b, except across a door opening 0.9 m wide. How much baseboard is needed?

**Task 2** (level 1-2). Area. Give each answer in the units asked.

a) A base plate is 600 mm by 450 mm. Find its area in mm² and in m².
b) A sheet is 4 ft by 8 ft. Find its area in ft² and in in².
c) A floor is 3.6 m by 2.7 m. Find its area in m².

**Task 3** (level 2). Break it down.

a) An L-shaped plate fits in an 800 mm by 600 mm rectangle with a 550 mm by 400 mm corner missing. Find its area two ways: as two rectangles added (250 × 600 and 550 × 200), and as the big rectangle less the corner. Do the answers agree?
b) A plate is 400 mm by 300 mm with a hole 160 mm in diameter in the middle. Find its area to two decimal places.

**Task 4** (level 2). An exterior wall is 36 ft long and 8 ft high. Take out two windows, each 4 ft by 3 ft, and a door 3 ft by 6 ft 8 in. Sheathing comes in 4 ft by 8 ft sheets.

a) Find the net area in ft².
b) How many sheets do you order?

**Task 5** (level 2). The house in Task 1a has loadbearing outside walls. The drawings call for one bottom plate and two top plates on every outside wall. Plate stock comes in 4,880 mm lengths. Use the outside perimeter and ignore openings.

a) How much plate is needed?
b) How many lengths do you order?

**Task 6** (level 2). Open the [Shape Canvas](../../shape-canvas/index.html). Draw a wall 3,000 mm long and 2,400 mm high. Inside it, draw a door 900 mm wide and 2,100 mm high and a window 1,200 mm wide and 1,000 mm high.

a) Work out the net wall area in m² by hand.
b) Compare your answer with the area the canvas shows. If they differ, find out why.
c) How many 1.2 m by 2.4 m sheets would cover the net area?

## Check yourself

1. To change an area in mm² to m², what do you divide by?
2. A calculation gives 3.1 sheets. How many do you buy?
3. What does "break it down" mean?
4. Find the area of one 1.2 m by 2.4 m sheet.
5. Is a 12 m² floor the same as a 12 m perimeter? Explain.
