Scrap, rework and control charts

Work out scrap rate, first-pass yield and labour productivity, find what scrap and rework cost, plan how many parts to start, and read a control chart to tell normal variation from a signal.

Task 1 (level 1-2)

a) 15 ÷ 300 × 100 = 5%.
b) 6 ÷ 240 × 100 = 2.5%.
c) Passed the first time: 240 − 6 − 18 = 216. First-pass yield: 216 ÷ 240 × 100 = 90%.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) 190 ÷ (1 − 0.05) = 190 ÷ 0.95 = 200 clips.
b) 288 ÷ (1 − 0.04) = 288 ÷ 0.96 = 300 plates.
c) 150 ÷ (1 − 0.06) = 150 ÷ 0.94 = 159.57. Round up: 160 brackets.
d) 6% of 160: 160 × 0.06 = 9.6, so about 10 scrapped. Good parts: 160 × 0.94 = 150.4, so about 150 good brackets under the planning assumption. A historical scrap rate is not a guarantee: actual scrap could exceed 10, so check output and allow a buffer where needed. Starting 159 would give 159 × 0.94 = 149.46, which is short.

Task 3 (level 2-3)

a) 3 × 8 = 24 labour hours.
b) Good brackets: 216 − 12 = 204. Productivity: 204 ÷ 24 = 8.5 good brackets per labour hour.
c) 216 ÷ 24 = 9 brackets per labour hour.
d) Scrap: 12 × ($6 + $4) = 12 × $10 = $120. Rework: 12 × 0.25 = 3 h, and 3 × $48.00 = $144. Together: $120 + $144 = $264 a shift.
e) 5 × $264 = $1,320 a week.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) Centre line 2,400.0 mm, UCL 2,403.0 mm, LCL 2,397.0 mm.
b) No. Every point is between 2,397.0 mm and 2,403.0 mm.
c) Samples 9 to 16 are all above the centre line. That is 8 in a row on one side, so it is a signal, even though no point is outside the limits. The points also climb from 2,401.0 mm to 2,402.6 mm. The cut length is drifting long.
d) Any one of: the stop block has crept or come loose, the fence has moved, the blade is worn or deflecting, or the tape or gauge used to set the stop has changed.
e) Leave the saw alone. Sample 3 is inside the limits and there is no pattern yet, so it is normal variation. Moving the stop for one short reading adds variation and makes the cuts less even, not more.

Task 5 (level 3)

a)

Sample Sum Average Largest − smallest Range
1 15,000 3,000.0 3,002 − 2,997 5
2 15,005 3,001.0 3,005 − 2,997 8
3 14,995 2,999.0 3,001 − 2,996 5
4 15,000 3,000.0 3,003 − 2,997 6

Each average is the sum ÷ 5.

b) Centre line: (3,000.0 + 3,001.0 + 2,999.0 + 3,000.0) ÷ 4 = 12,000 ÷ 4 = 3,000.0 mm.
R̄: (5 + 8 + 5 + 6) ÷ 4 = 24 ÷ 4 = 6.0 mm.

c) 0.577 × 6.0 = 3.462. UCL = 3,000.0 + 3.462 = 3,003.462, so 3,003.5 mm. LCL = 3,000.0 − 3.462 = 2,996.538, so 2,996.5 mm.

d) 2.114 × 6.0 = 12.684, so the R chart upper limit is 12.7 mm.

e) Sum: 3,002 + 3,006 + 3,004 + 3,005 + 3,004 = 15,021. Average: 15,021 ÷ 5 = 3,004.2 mm. Range: 3,006 − 3,002 = 4 mm.
The average, 3,004.2 mm, is above the UCL of 3,003.5 mm, so it is a signal. The range, 4 mm, is below the R chart limit of 12.7 mm, so the spread is normal. The plates are all cutting long by about the same amount, which points to something that moved the whole setting, such as the stop.

Task 6 (level 3)

Open task. A good message:

  • says what was measured and that the average of the latest sample (3,004.2 mm) was above the upper control limit (3,003.5 mm)
  • says the spread was normal, so the whole cut length had shifted
  • says what was done: the saw was stopped, the stop or setting was checked and reset, and the plates cut since the last good sample were checked
  • is two sentences, in plain words

Example: "The latest sample on the plate saw averaged 3,004.2 mm, above the upper control limit of 3,003.5 mm, while the spread stayed normal, so the whole cut length had shifted long. I stopped the saw, reset and locked the stop, and I am checking every plate cut since the last good sample."

Check yourself 1

10 ÷ 200 × 100 = 5%.

Check yourself 2

The hours are still spent on the scrapped parts, but fewer good parts come out. Good output ÷ hours gets smaller.

Check yourself 3

Stop and look for a cause. A point outside a control limit is a signal that something in the process has changed.

Check yourself 4

Yes. Eight points in a row on one side of the centre line is a run, which is a signal, even with every point inside the limits.

Check yourself 5

No. The drawing's sizes say what the part must be. Control limits come from the process's own past data and say what the process normally does. A process can be in control and still miss the drawing.
