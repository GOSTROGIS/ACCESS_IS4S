# Site coordinates with northing, easting and azimuth

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 34 of 34. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator with sin, cos and tan⁻¹, set to degree mode. Draw a large north arrow and a compass circle marked 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° on the board. Learners should have done lesson 15 (right triangles and squaring). The Numeracy Hub Coordinate Systems tab covers the grid, angles and direction, and polar and rectangular coordinates, and makes good warm-up practice. Masking tape on the floor can stand in for the N and E axes in step 1.1.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "The surveyor's list says a corner is at 5,030 and 2,040. Which way do you walk from the control point, and how far?" Take answers, and point out that some will have walked 30 east and 40 north. Say: "Mix up north and east and the building is in the wrong place. Today you read site coordinates, write directions the way surveyors do, and check a layout before it is built." Tell learners this is session 34 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- read the northing and easting of a point from its labels (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect coordinate differences to a right triangle, and a bearing to an azimuth (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- work out distances, bearings, azimuths and new coordinates (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- check a staked layout against its coordinates and decide whether it is square (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- report a layout error with the numbers and a fix (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. A right triangle has legs of 6 m and 8 m. How long is the third side? (10 m)
2. tan⁻¹(1) = ? (45°)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. From N 300.000 E 400.000 to N 306.000 E 392.000, find the distance and the quadrant. (ΔN = +6, ΔE = −8, distance 10.000 m, NW)
2. Change N 25° W to an azimuth. (335°)
3. A point is 10.000 m from N 100.000 E 100.000 at an azimuth of 90°. Find its coordinates. (N 100.000 E 110.000)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- northing and easting, and why you always read the labels
- ΔN and ΔE and what their signs mean
- azimuths clockwise from north, bearings from north or south, and back directions
- the inverse: distance with the right triangle, then the bearing and azimuth
- the forward: a new point from a distance and an azimuth
- checking a staked layout with its diagonals and reporting an error

This lesson closes this part of the Numeracy set. Lessons 31 to 34 together cover heights, grades, charts and positions on a site.

Definitions", https://jerrymahun.com/index.php/home/open-access/32-vi-directions/101-travcomps-chap-a?showall=1, checked 2026-11-08: a bearing has a prefix N or S, an angle, and a suffix E or W, and "falls in one of four quadrants so the angle does not exceed 90°". An azimuth "is an angle to the right (clockwise) from the meridian to the line", usually from north, and "varies from 0° to 360°". A back bearing has the same angle with the quadrant reversed, and a back azimuth differs by 180°. The bearing to azimuth table: NE β, SE 180° − β, SW 180° + β, NW 360° − β.
