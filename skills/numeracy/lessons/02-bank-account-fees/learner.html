Working out the monthly cost of a bank account

Read a fee schedule, count your transactions and work out what each account would cost you in a month and a year.

Why this matters on the job

When you start work, your pay goes into a bank account by direct deposit. Bank fees come out of that pay every month. A tradesperson who buys supplies with a debit card, sends e-transfers to a co-worker for a shared ride and pays a phone bill can make 30 or more transactions a month. The account that looks cheapest is not always the cheapest for the way you bank.

Key ideas

A fee schedule is the list of what a bank charges. Read it like any table: find the row for the account, then the column for the fee.

The monthly cost of an account is:

monthly fee + (extra transactions × fee for each extra transaction) + any other fees

An extra transaction is one over the number included in the account. If 18 are included and you make 23, you have 23 − 18 = 5 extra.

Two rules from the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) are worth knowing:

  • Low-cost and no-cost accounts. Banks that signed the federal commitment offer a low-cost chequing account for no more than $4 a month, with at least 18 debit transactions. The same account has no monthly fee for people 18 or younger, students, seniors receiving the Guaranteed Income Supplement, Registered Disability Savings Plan beneficiaries and newcomers in their first year in Canada. Some banks also include Indigenous customers, Disability Tax Credit recipients, or people receiving certain social assistance. Ask the bank which groups it recognizes.
  • NSF fees. A non-sufficient funds (NSF) fee is charged when a payment bounces because there is not enough money in the account. Since March 12, 2026, federally regulated banks cannot charge more than $10 for it. They cannot charge it when the account would be overdrawn by less than $10, and they cannot charge it again if they already charged one in the last 2 business days.

Worked example

An account costs $6.00 a month and includes 20 debit transactions. Each extra transaction costs $1.00. Sam makes 26 transactions.

  • Extra transactions: 26 − 20 = 6
  • Extra fees: 6 × $1.00 = $6.00
  • Monthly cost: $6.00 + $6.00 = $12.00
  • Yearly cost: 12 × $12.00 = $144.00

Everyday Chequing $10.95 30 $1.25 your balance stays at $3,000 or more all month
Unlimited Chequing $16.95 unlimited none your balance stays at $4,000 or more all month

Notes to the fee schedule:

  1. Debit transactions are debit purchases, e-transfers sent, bill payments, pre-authorized payments and withdrawals. Deposits are free and do not count.
  2. When the monthly fee is waived, extra transaction fees still apply.
  3. Basic Chequing has no monthly fee for customers who are 18 or younger, students, seniors receiving the Guaranteed Income Supplement, Registered Disability Savings Plan beneficiaries, newcomers in their first year in Canada, or Indigenous customers.
  4. Withdrawal at another bank's ATM: $2.00 from Riverbend, plus any fee the ATM owner shows on screen. It also counts as a debit transaction.
  5. NSF fee: $10.00. Paper statement: $2.50 a month. Stop payment: $15.00.

Check yourself

  2. What is the most a low-cost account can charge as a monthly fee?
  3. A payment would overdraw your account by $8.00. Can a federally regulated bank charge you an NSF fee?
  4. Do deposits count as debit transactions at Riverbend?
