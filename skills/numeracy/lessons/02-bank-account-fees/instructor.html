Working out the monthly cost of a bank account

Read a fee schedule, count your transactions and work out what each account would cost you in a month and a year.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 2 of 8. Total length: 75 minutes.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "How many times did you tap a debit card or send an e-transfer last week?" Do not ask anyone to share their bank or their balance. Tell a short story: a first-year apprentice picks the cheapest-looking account, then pays $20 a month in extra transaction fees because the account only includes 18. Ask learners to recall the location strategy for document use: decide what you need, scan the headings, find the row and column, then check.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • decide what question the fee schedule has to answer for a given person (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • find the monthly fee, included transactions and other fees in a fee schedule (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a month of transactions to the notes on the fee schedule (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • calculate monthly and yearly account costs (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • judge which account costs least for a pattern of use, and apply the NSF fee rules (Interpret and evaluate the information)

This lesson presents facts and calculations. It does not recommend a bank or an account.

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. 7 × $1.25 = ? ($8.75)
  2. A table has rows for three accounts and a column called "Monthly fee". Where do you look for the monthly fee of the second account? (second row, "Monthly fee" column)

Learning tasks (50 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Read Document A together. Show that the notes under the table change the numbers in the table. Model the worked example on the board. 1.1 Read Document A in turns. Follow the worked example. Learner page, board 10 min
1.2 Ask learners to answer Tasks 1 and 2 alone. Ask which row of Document B does not count and why. 1.2 Complete Tasks 1 and 2. Documents A and B 5 min
2.1 Ask learners to fill in the Task 3 table, then answer Task 4. Ask "Why is the account with the lowest monthly fee not the cheapest here?" 2.1 Complete Tasks 3 and 4 with a calculator. Compare tables with a partner. Learner page, calculator 10 min
2.2 Assign Tasks 5 to 7. Point out note 2: a waived monthly fee does not waive extra transaction fees. 2.2 Complete Tasks 5, 6 and 7. Learner page, calculator 10 min
3.1 Explain the NSF rules from the FCAC news release. Work case a) of Task 8 aloud. 3.1 Complete cases b) and c) of Task 8. Learner page 5 min
3.2 Demonstrate the FCAC Account Comparison Tool on the projector. Learners without a device work in pairs. Remind learners that the tool lists what banks publish and that fees can change. 3.2 Complete Task 9. Record what the tool does not show. Projector, devices, FCAC Account Comparison Tool 10 min

Post-assessment (10 min)

  1. Basic Chequing, 25 transactions, no waiver. What is the monthly cost? ($4.00 + 7 × $1.25 = $12.75)
  2. A payment would overdraw an account by $25. What is the most a federally regulated bank can charge as an NSF fee? ($10)
  3. Name two things, other than the monthly fee, that change what an account really costs. (extra transactions, other-bank ATM fees, paper statements, NSF fees, any two)

Check answers together. Ask where each number was found in Document A and why it was the right one. Make sure everyone takes a turn.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • reading a fee schedule, including the notes under the table
  • counting transactions and working out monthly and yearly costs
  • low-cost and no-cost accounts and who can get them
  • the $10 NSF fee limit and when an NSF fee cannot be charged
  • comparing real accounts with the FCAC Account Comparison Tool
