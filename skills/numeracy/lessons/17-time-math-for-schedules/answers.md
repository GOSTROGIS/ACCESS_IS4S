# Time math for schedules

## Task 1 (level 1)

a) 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75. **7.75 h**  
b) 3 + 12 = 15. **15:15**  
c) The shift crosses midnight, so add 24 h to the end time: 06:10 + 24:00 = 30:10. Then 30:10 − 22:40 = **7 h 30 min** (7.5 h).  
d) 0.4 × 60 = **24 min**

## Task 2 (level 1-2)

a) 16:15 − 05:45 = 10 h 30 min. Take off the 30-minute lunch: **10 paid hours** a day.  
b) 10 − 8 = **2 hours** a day at time-and-a-half. None of the day is over 12 hours, so there is no double time.  
c) Regular hours: 8 × 5 = 40 h. 40 × $42.00 = $1,680.00.
Overtime hours: 2 × 5 = 10 h. Overtime rate: $42.00 × 1.5 = $63.00. 10 × $63.00 = $630.00.
Weekly overtime counts only the first 8 hours of each day, which is 40 hours, so there is no extra weekly overtime.
Total: $1,680.00 + $630.00 = **$2,310.00**

## Task 3 (level 2)

a) Working days: Jun 24 (1), Jun 25 (2), Jun 28 (3), Jun 29 (4), Jun 30 (5), skip Canada Day on Thursday July 1, Jul 2 (6), skip the weekend, Jul 5 (7), Jul 6 (8). The rough-in finishes on **Tuesday, July 6, 2027**.  
b) June 24 to June 30 is 7 days, plus July 1 to 6 is 6 days. 7 + 6 = **13 calendar days**.  
c) The next working day after July 6: **Wednesday, July 7, 2027**.

## Task 4 (level 2)

August 2 to August 13 has 2 weeks of Monday to Friday: 10 weekdays. Monday, August 2 is B.C. Day, a statutory holiday. 10 − 1 = **9 working days**.

## Task 5 (level 2)

a) 256 ÷ (4 × 8) = 256 ÷ 32 = **8 working days**  
b) 256 ÷ (5 × 8) = 256 ÷ 40 = 6.4. Round up: **7 working days**  
c) Hours each worker gives in 6 days of 10 h: 6 × 10 = 60 h. Workers needed: 256 ÷ 60 = 4.27. Round up: **5 workers**. Check: 4 workers give 4 × 60 = 240 h, which is less than 256 h. 5 workers give 300 h, which is enough.
