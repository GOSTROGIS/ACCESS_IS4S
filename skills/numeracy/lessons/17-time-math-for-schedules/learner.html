Time math for schedules

Tell working days from calendar days, add a job's working days to a start date while skipping weekends and BC statutory holidays, work out shift hours on a 24-hour clock, and turn crew-hours into working days.

Why this matters on the job

A schedule counts working days, but people live by the calendar. When a foreman says "the framing takes 8 days", that means 8 days on which the crew works. Weekends and statutory holidays do not count. If you skip that step, you tell the next trade the wrong start date. Crews are also priced and planned in hours, so you need to change hours into days and days into dates.

This is the first of five lessons on project schedules. The next lessons use these skills for Gantt charts, network diagrams, the forward and backward pass, and float.

Key ideas

Calendar days and working days. A calendar day is any day on the calendar. A working day is a day the crew works. In these lessons a working day is Monday to Friday, 8 hours, and not a statutory holiday. Some jobs work other calendars, for example 4 days of 10 hours. Always check which calendar the schedule uses.

BC statutory holidays in 2027. These come from the Province of BC Employment Standards page. Easter Monday and Boxing Day are not statutory holidays in BC.

Adding working days to a start date. The start day is working day 1. Count forward one working day at a time and skip weekends and holidays. The day you land on for the last number is the finish date. The next activity starts on the next working day.

  • Duration in working days: count only the working days.
  • Duration in calendar days: finish date − start date + 1.

The 24-hour clock. Hours run from 00:00 (midnight) to 23:59. For times after noon, add 12 to the p.m. hour: 3:15 p.m. is 15:15. Many sites, shift logs and equipment hour meters use it.

Elapsed time. Subtract the start time from the end time. If the shift crosses midnight, add 24 hours to the end time first.

Minutes and decimal hours. Divide minutes by 60. 45 min = 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 h, so 7 h 45 min = 7.75 h. To go back, multiply the decimal part by 60: 0.4 h × 60 = 24 min.

Paid hours in a shift. Paid hours = end time − start time − unpaid breaks.

The shift in the picture starts at 06:30 and ends at 17:00. Elapsed time is 17:00 − 06:30 = 10 h 30 min. The unpaid lunch is 30 min, so the worker is paid for 10 h.

Overtime in BC. Under the BC Employment Standards Act, an employee who is not under an averaging agreement gets time-and-a-half for time worked over 8 hours in a day, up to 12 hours, and double time for time over 12 hours in a day. Time over 40 hours in a week is also paid at time-and-a-half, counting only the first 8 hours worked each day. A union contract or an averaging agreement can have different rules, so check yours.

Crew-hours (person-hours). Crew-hours = number of workers × hours each one works.

  • working days = crew-hours ÷ (workers × hours per day)
  • If the answer is not a whole number, round up. A part day still ties up the crew for that day.

Worked example 1: a finish date

A crew starts a 10-working-day job on Wednesday, September 1, 2027. When does it finish?

  1. Days 1 to 3 are Wednesday September 1, Thursday September 2 and Friday September 3.
  2. Skip the weekend (September 4 and 5) and Labour Day (Monday, September 6).
  3. Days 4 to 7 are September 7, 8, 9 and 10.
  4. Skip the weekend of September 11 and 12.
  5. Days 8, 9 and 10 are September 13, 14 and 15.
  6. The job finishes on Wednesday, September 15, 2027. The next activity can start on Thursday, September 16.
  7. Calendar days: 15 − 1 + 1 = 15 calendar days for 10 working days.

Worked example 2: crew-hours to days

A drywall crew has 168 crew-hours of board to hang in a suite.

  1. With 3 hangers on 8-hour days: 168 ÷ (3 × 8) = 168 ÷ 24 = 7 working days.
  2. With the same 3 hangers on 10-hour days: 168 ÷ (3 × 10) = 168 ÷ 30 = 5.6. Round up to 6 working days.
  3. On a 10-hour day, each hanger works 2 hours over 8. Unless there is an averaging agreement, those 2 hours are paid at time-and-a-half.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Time and the 24-hour clock.

a) Write 7 h 45 min as decimal hours.
b) Write 3:15 p.m. on the 24-hour clock.
c) A night paving crew works from 22:40 to 06:10. How long is the shift?
d) Write 0.4 h in minutes.

Task 2 (level 1-2). A crane operator works from 05:45 to 16:15 with a 30-minute unpaid lunch, Monday to Friday.

a) How many paid hours does the operator work each day?
b) How many of those hours each day are overtime under the BC Employment Standards Act? Assume there is no averaging agreement.
c) The operator's regular rate is $42.00 an hour (made up for this task). What is the pay for one week of 5 shifts?

Task 3 (level 2). An electrician starts an 8-working-day rough-in on Thursday, June 24, 2027.

a) On what date does the rough-in finish?
b) How many calendar days is that?
c) On what date can the drywall crew start, if it follows right after?

Task 4 (level 2). A paving crew is booked from Monday, August 2 to Friday, August 13, 2027. How many working days is that? Explain.

Task 5 (level 2). An ironwork crew has 256 crew-hours of rebar to place.

a) How many working days does a crew of 4 need on 8-hour days?
b) How many working days does a crew of 5 need on 8-hour days?
c) The general contractor wants the rebar done in 6 working days of 10 hours. What is the smallest crew that can do it?

Task 6 (level 2-3). The engineer's note on a slab job (fictional) says: "Do not strip forms until at least 7 calendar days after the pour." The slab is poured on Thursday, September 23, 2027.

a) What is the 7th calendar day after the pour?
b) On what date can the crew strip the forms? The crew only works on working days.
c) Why does the note use calendar days and not working days?

Task 7 (level 3). Tidewater Marine Fab (fictional) works a 4-day week: Monday to Thursday, 10 hours a day, with no work on statutory holidays. A job needs 120 welder-hours and 2 welders are on it. It starts on Monday, October 4, 2027.

a) How many working days does the job need?
b) On what date does it finish?
c) Write a two-sentence text to the customer with the finish date and the reason it is later than they expected.
