# Time math for schedules

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 17 of 34. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator and a 2027 calendar (print one, or use the calendar on a phone). Print the BC statutory holiday table from the learner page or project it. This lesson starts a five-lesson scheduling set: lesson 18 (Gantt charts), 19 (network diagrams), 20 (forward and backward pass), 21 (float and the critical path), then Problem Solving lesson 10 (recover a late schedule). The same day-counting rule is used in all of them, so take time on Worked example 1.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Your foreman says the framing takes 8 days and starts on a Thursday. What day does the drywall crew show up?" Take answers and write them on the board. Most groups get different dates. Say: "The answer depends on weekends, holidays and how you count the first day. Today we make one clear rule and use it every time." Tell learners this is session 17 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- say whether a schedule question needs working days, calendar days or hours (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
- pick out the start date, duration, crew size, hours per day and holidays from a job description (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect crew-hours, hours per day, workers and working days in one formula (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- add working days to a start date, find elapsed time on a 24-hour clock, change minutes to decimal hours and change crew-hours to days (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- check that a finish date skips weekends and BC statutory holidays and that crew days are rounded up (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- tell a customer or the next trade a finish date and the reason for it (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. How many minutes are in 0.25 h? (15 min)
2. Name two statutory holidays in BC. (Any two from the table, for example Canada Day and B.C. Day)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. A 6-working-day job starts on Friday, May 21, 2027. When does it finish? (Fri May 21, Tue May 25, Wed May 26, Thu May 27, Fri May 28, Mon May 31. Victoria Day, May 24, is skipped. It finishes on Monday, May 31, 2027.)
2. A plumbing crew of 2 has 52 crew-hours of work and works 8-hour days. How many working days? (52 ÷ 16 = 3.25, round up to 4)
3. A shift runs 07:00 to 17:30 with a 30-minute unpaid lunch. How many paid hours? (10 h)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- working days and calendar days, and the BC statutory holidays for 2027
- one counting rule: the start day is day 1, skip weekends and holidays, the next activity starts on the next working day
- the 24-hour clock, elapsed time and decimal hours
- paid hours and the BC overtime rule
- crew-hours to working days, always rounded up

Next session: Gantt charts, where these dates become bars on a time scale.
