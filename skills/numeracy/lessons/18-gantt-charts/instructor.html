Read and draw a Gantt chart

Read a Gantt chart's bars, summary bars, milestones, today line and percent complete, compare the baseline with the current schedule, use a three-week look-ahead, and draw your own bars on a time scale.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 18 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a pencil, a ruler and coloured pencils or two highlighters. Print the blank Gantt grid from Task 6, or give out graph paper. If you have Microsoft Project, Excel or a free Gantt tool, a short demo at step 3.2 helps, but it is not needed. Learners should have done lesson 17 (time math): the bars in this lesson use the same rule for working days.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show the garage Gantt chart without saying anything for 30 seconds. Ask: "What is this? When does the garage get finished? What happens on May 24?" Take answers. Say: "This picture is on the wall of nearly every site office. Today you learn to read every mark on it, and then draw one." Tell learners this is session 18 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read start dates, finish dates and durations from bars on a time scale, and spot summary bars and milestones (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect the today line, percent complete and the baseline to tell what is ahead or behind (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • count working days and calendar days under a bar and draw bars from a date table (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • judge whether a late activity matters, using the critical marking and float (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • report a slip and a new finish date to an owner (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. A job starts on a Friday and lasts 3 working days with no holidays. On what day does it finish? (Tuesday)
  2. Have you seen a bar chart schedule on a job? What was on it? (Open)

Learning tasks (70 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach the parts of a Gantt chart with the garage chart: time scale, shading, bars, critical marking, summary bars, milestones. 1.1 Label the parts on their own copy. Learner page, projector 8 min
1.2 Model reading activity 9 (Pour slab): working days and calendar days. 1.2 Complete Task 1. Garage chart 8 min
1.3 Assign Task 2. Ask why the trusses sit under "Framing and roof". 1.3 Complete Task 2. Garage chart 6 min
2.1 Teach the today line and percent complete. Walk through the worked example reading of the today line. 2.1 Complete Task 3. Garage chart 9 min
2.2 Teach the baseline and the tracking Gantt chart. Ask: "Which slip moved the finish, and which did not?" 2.2 Complete Task 4. Tracking chart 10 min
2.3 Teach the three-week look-ahead and why foremen use it. Ask who must be called before each pour. 2.3 Complete Task 5. Look-ahead chart 9 min
3.1 Teach the five drawing steps. Circulate during Task 6 and check that bars skip B.C. Day and the weekends. 3.1 Complete Task 6 on the grid. Blank grid, ruler, highlighters 12 min
3.2 If software is available, show the same deck plan as a Gantt chart. Otherwise pairs swap and check each other's chart against the answer. Read two Task 7 updates aloud. 3.2 Complete Task 7 and check a partner's chart. Learner page, answers 8 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. On the garage chart, how many working days does activity 6 (trusses) take, and how many calendar days? (10 working days, May 12 to May 26 is 15 calendar days)
  2. What does a grey bar under an activity bar show on a tracking Gantt chart? (The baseline, the saved plan)
  3. A critical activity is 0% done when it should be 50% done. Does the finish date matter? (Yes, a late critical activity moves the finish date unless the crew catches up)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the parts of a Gantt chart: time scale, bars, critical marking, summary bars and milestones
  • reading working days and calendar days from a bar
  • the today line and percent complete
  • baseline and current bars on a tracking Gantt chart
  • the three-week look-ahead
  • drawing bars from a table of dates

Next session: network diagrams, which show why each bar starts where it does.
