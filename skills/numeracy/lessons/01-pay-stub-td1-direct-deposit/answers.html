Checking a pay stub and filling in a TD1

Check the numbers on a pay stub, total your claim on the TD1 and TD1BC forms, and read the banking numbers on a cheque for direct deposit.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Task 1 (level 1)

  • Pay period: October 5 to October 18, 2026
  • Regular hours: 80. Overtime hours: 4
  • Regular wage rate: $27.50 an hour
  • Gross pay: $2,365.00. Net pay: $1,833.34

All of these are read straight from Document A.

Task 2 (level 1)

Found on the stub: the employer's name and address, the hours worked, the wage rate, the overtime hours, each deduction with its purpose (CPP, EI, income tax, union dues), the gross pay and the net pay. Nothing on the BC list is missing for this pay. There is no allowance, vacation pay or time bank in this pay period, so those lines are not needed.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • Regular pay: 80 × $27.50 = $2,200.00
  • Overtime rate: 1.5 × $27.50 = $41.25 an hour
  • Overtime pay: 4 × $41.25 = $165.00
  • Gross pay: $2,200.00 + $165.00 = $2,365.00, which matches the stub.

Task 4 (level 2)

  • Total deductions: $132.71 + $38.55 + $312.40 + $48.00 = $531.66
  • Net pay: $2,365.00 − $531.66 = $1,833.34, which matches the stub.
  • Check: $1,833.34 + $531.66 = $2,365.00

Task 5 (level 2)

EI = 1.63% × $2,365.00 = 0.0163 × 2,365.00 = 38.5495, which rounds to $38.55. This matches the stub.

Task 6 (level 2)

  1. Exemption for one pay: $3,500 ÷ 26 = $134.6154 (keep the decimals)
  2. Bracket: $2,365.00 − $134.6154 = $2,230.3846
  3. CPP: 0.0595 × $2,230.3846 = 132.7079, which rounds to $132.71. This matches the stub.

A common error is to work left to right as (2,365.00 − 3,500) ÷ 26, which gives a negative number. BEDMAS does the division first.

Task 7 (level 2)

  • Line 1: $16,452
  • Line 5: $1,450
  • Line 13: $16,452 + $1,450 = $17,902

Yes, Riley needs the TD1BC, because $17,902 is more than $16,452.

  • TD1BC line 1: $13,216
  • TD1BC line 4: $1,450
  • TD1BC line 11: $13,216 + $1,450 = $14,666

Task 8 (level 2)

  • Federal line 1: $16,452
  • Federal line 8: $16,452 − $0 = $16,452
  • Federal line 13: $16,452 + $16,452 = $32,904
  • TD1BC line 1: $13,216. TD1BC line 7: $11,317
  • TD1BC line 11: $13,216 + $11,317 = $24,533

A good answer connects line 8 to line 1: the amount depends on line 1 and on the dependant's income.

Task 9 (level 2)

  • Riley, $17,902: it falls between $16,452.01 and $19,285.00, so claim code 2.
  • Dana, $32,904: it falls between $30,617.01 and $33,450.00, so claim code 7.

Task 10 (level 1)

  • Account number: 4419027
  • Not needed: 0412, the cheque number.
  • Never give: your PIN, your online banking password, or your security questions and answers. Any two of these are correct.
