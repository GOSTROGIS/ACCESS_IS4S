Is the job on budget?

Compare percent complete with percent spent, find the actual unit cost and the projected over or under, and use planned value, earned value and actual cost to say whether a job is on budget and on schedule.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Task 1 (level 1-2)

a) 1,200 × $8.00 = $9,600.
b) 480 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 40% complete.
c) $3,360 ÷ $9,600 × 100 = 35% spent.
d) Under budget so far. The job is 40% done but has used only 35% of the labour money.
e) $3,360 ÷ 480 = $7.00 a metre, which is less than the $8.00 estimate.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) 600 × $30.00 = $18,000.
b) $8,500 ÷ 250 = $34.00 a square metre.
c) Quantity left: 600 − 250 = 350 m². Cost to finish: 350 × $30.00 = $10,500. Projected total: $8,500 + $10,500 = $19,000. Over or under: $19,000 − $18,000 = $1,000 over.
d) Cost to finish: 350 × $34.00 = $11,900. Projected total: $8,500 + $11,900 = $20,400. Over or under: $20,400 − $18,000 = $2,400 over.
e) The estimate-rate forecast, $1,000 over. The cause of the higher rate (extra corners) is over, and the rest of the walls are straight runs, so the crew should get back to the estimate rate. A careful foreman also mentions that it could reach $2,400 over if the crew stays slow.

Task 3 (level 3)

a) Quantity left: 680 − 250 = 430 m². Cost to finish: 430 × $34.00 = $14,620. Projected total: $8,500 + $14,620 = $23,120. Over or under: $23,120 − $18,000 = $5,120 over.
b) The missed quantity: 80 × $30.00 = $2,400. The budget was short by this much even before the crew started.
c) The higher rate: $34.00 − $30.00 = $4.00 a square metre more. 680 × $4.00 = $2,720.
d) $2,400 + $2,720 = $5,120, which matches a).

Task 4 (level 2-3)

a) BAC = 8 × $1,500 = $12,000. PV = 5 × $1,500 = $7,500. EV = 6 × $1,500 = $9,000. AC = $8,400.
b) CV = $9,000 − $8,400 = +$600: the work done cost $600 less than its budget. SV = $9,000 − $7,500 = +$1,500: the shop has done $1,500 more work than planned, one spool ahead.
c) CPI = $9,000 ÷ $8,400 = 1.07 (1.0714 on the calculator). SPI = $9,000 ÷ $7,500 = 1.2.
d) EAC = $12,000 ÷ 1.0714… = $11,200. Keep the full calculator value of CPI. You can also work it as $12,000 × $8,400 ÷ $9,000 = $11,200. VAC = $12,000 − $11,200 = +$800.
e) Under budget and ahead of schedule. Both indexes are above 1.

Task 5 (level 3)

a)

Week CV = EV − AC SV = EV − PV CPI = EV ÷ AC SPI = EV ÷ PV
1 $0 $0 1.00 1.00
2 −$1,000 −$2,000 0.94 0.89
3 −$1,500 −$4,000 0.94 0.86
4 −$2,000 −$4,000 0.94 0.89

b) Week 2. Week 1 is exactly on plan. In week 2 both variances turn negative.

c) EAC = $40,000 ÷ ($32,000 ÷ $34,000) = $40,000 × $34,000 ÷ $32,000 = $42,500. VAC = $40,000 − $42,500 = −$2,500. If a learner divides by the rounded 0.94, they get $42,553. The difference shows why the full calculator value is used.

d) Percent complete: $32,000 ÷ $40,000 × 100 = 80%. Percent spent: $34,000 ÷ $40,000 × 100 = 85%.

e) $42,500 − $34,000 = $8,500.

Task 6 (level 3)

Open task. A good update:

  • says the garage is behind schedule (about $4,000 of work behind, SPI 0.89) and over budget ($2,000 so far, CPI 0.94)
  • gives the forecast cost at the finish, about $42,500, which is $2,500 over the $40,000 budget
  • uses plain words, with numbers the owner can check

Example: "The garage is 80% built but we have spent 85% of the budget, so we are $2,000 over budget and $4,000 of planned work behind schedule. If the costs keep running the same way, the garage will finish at about $42,500, which is $2,500 over budget."

Check yourself 1

Over budget. More of the money is spent (50%) than of the work is done (40%).

Check yourself 2

The work done so far cost more than its budget. The job is over budget.

Check yourself 3

4 × $500 = $2,000.

Check yourself 4

$10,000 ÷ 0.8 = $12,500.

Check yourself 5

The estimate was built on the wrong quantity, so the budget is too small for the real work. The extra quantity costs money at any rate, even the estimate rate.
