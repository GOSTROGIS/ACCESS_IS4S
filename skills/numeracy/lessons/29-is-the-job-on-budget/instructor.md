# Is the job on budget?

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 29 of 34. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator and their notes from lesson 28 (the handrail estimate in Worked example 2 is the budget used here). Part A (unit cost) is level 2 work and suits every learner. Part B (earned value) is level 3. If the group needs more time on Part A, finish Tasks 1 to 3 and Task 4 in class and set Tasks 5 and 6 as extension work. Every rate, budget and company in the lesson is made up for practice.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Your crew is halfway through the formwork and has spent half the money. Is the job on budget?" Most learners say yes. Then ask: "What if the take-off missed a wall?" Say: "Being busy is not the same as being on budget. Today we check a job's money two ways: by the unit cost, and by comparing what was planned, what was done and what was spent." Tell learners this is session 29 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- pick out the estimated quantity, unit rate, quantity done and cost to date, and the PV, EV and AC of a job (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect percent complete with percent spent, and earned value with planned value and actual cost (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- work out the actual unit rate, the projected over or under, CV, SV, CPI, SPI and EAC (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- decide which unit rate to use for the work left, and say whether a job is over or under budget and ahead or behind (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- write a short cost and schedule update an owner can check (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. What is 300 out of 800 as a percent? (37.5%)
2. A crew spent $2,400 to install 200 m of pipe. What did each metre cost? ($12.00)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Estimate 500 m² at $20.00. Done 200 m² for $5,000. What is the actual unit rate, and what is the projected total at that rate? ($25.00, $5,000 + 300 × $25.00 = $12,500)
2. PV is $6,000, EV is $5,000 and AC is $5,500. Is the job ahead or behind, and over or under budget? (Behind, because SV = −$1,000. Over budget, because CV = −$500.)
3. BAC is $20,000 and CPI is 1.25. What is the EAC? ($16,000)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- percent complete against percent spent
- the actual unit rate and the projected over or under, with two ways to price the work left
- how a short take-off quantity causes a loss on its own
- planned value, earned value and actual cost, and the variances and indexes they give
- the estimate at completion, and how to report it in plain words

Next session: lesson 30 looks at scrap, rework and control charts, where lost parts and lost hours show up in the numbers.
