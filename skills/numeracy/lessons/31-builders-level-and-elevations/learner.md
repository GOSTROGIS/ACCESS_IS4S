# Setting heights with a builder's level

## Why this matters on the job

Every footing, slab form, drain and fence top on a site is set to an elevation on the drawings. The heights all come from one fixed point, the benchmark. A carpenter or pipe layer reads a rod through a level, adds and subtracts, and marks the height on a stake or a form. One wrong sum can put a whole footing 100 mm too low, so the work is written down and checked before anyone pours.

## Key ideas

**Elevation** is the height of a point above a reference. In this lesson elevations are in metres to three decimals, for example 100.000. The **benchmark (BM)** is a fixed point whose elevation is known.

**Backsight (BS).** The first rod reading after you set up the level, taken on a point of known elevation (the benchmark or a turning point). A backsight is added. It is also called a plus sight.

**Height of instrument (HI).** The elevation of the line of sight through the level.

- HI = known elevation + BS

**Foresight (FS).** A rod reading on a point whose elevation you want. A foresight is subtracted. It is also called a minus sight.

- elevation of the point = HI − FS

A big rod reading means the point is low. A small rod reading means the point is high.

**Turning point (TP).** When the next point is too far away or out of sight, put the rod on a solid point that will not move, take a foresight on it, move the level, and take a backsight on the same point. The turning point carries the elevation forward.

**The arithmetic check.** In a level book, add up all the backsights and all the foresights. Then:

- sum of BS − sum of FS = last elevation − first elevation

If the two sides do not match, there is a mistake in the sums. Find it before anyone builds from the numbers.

**Rod reading for a design height.** To set a form or a stake to a design elevation:

- rod reading needed = HI − design elevation

Raise or lower the form until the rod reads that number on top of it.

**Cut and fill.** Compare the ground with the design elevation.

- ground − design is positive: the ground is too high. That is **cut** (dig it down).
- ground − design is negative: the ground is too low. That is **fill** (build it up).

**Good habits.** Keep the backsight and foresight about the same length at each setup. Write every reading in the book the moment you read it. Use BEDMAS: add the backsight first, then subtract the foresight.

### Worked example

A gravel pad for a small shop must be at design elevation 99.500. The benchmark is a nail in a power pole, elevation 100.000. The level is set up between the pole and the pad.

1. Backsight on the BM: 1.425. HI = 100.000 + 1.425 = **101.425**
2. Foresight on corner A: 1.875. Elevation A = 101.425 − 1.875 = **99.550**
3. Foresight on corner B: 2.010. Elevation B = 101.425 − 2.010 = **99.415**
4. Corner A: 99.550 − 99.500 = +0.050. **Cut 50 mm.**
5. Corner B: 99.415 − 99.500 = −0.085. **Fill 85 mm.**
6. To set a grade stake at 99.500, the rod must read 101.425 − 99.500 = **1.925** on top of the stake.

The level book for this setup:

| Point | BS | HI | FS | Elevation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BM | 1.425 | 101.425 | | 100.000 | nail in pole |
| A | | | 1.875 | 99.550 | cut 0.050 |
| B | | | 2.010 | 99.415 | fill 0.085 |

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1-2). The level is set up near a benchmark of elevation 25.000. The backsight on the BM is 1.384.

a) Find the HI.
b) Find the elevation of each point from its foresight: P1 0.912, P2 1.655, P3 2.073, P4 1.240.
c) Which point is the highest? Which is the lowest?

**Task 2** (level 2). The HI is 101.250. Find the rod reading you need on top of each:

a) a slab form, design elevation 100.150
b) the bottom of a footing trench, design elevation 99.450
c) a drain pipe invert, design elevation 98.875
d) The rod reads 1.180 on top of the slab form. Is the form too high or too low? By how much?

**Task 4** (level 2-3). A parking pad must be at design elevation 51.500. The level was set up with a backsight of 1.440 on a benchmark of 51.200. The foresights on four stakes were: stake 1 1.020, stake 2 1.385, stake 3 0.870, stake 4 1.140.

a) Find the HI.
b) Find the ground elevation at each stake.
