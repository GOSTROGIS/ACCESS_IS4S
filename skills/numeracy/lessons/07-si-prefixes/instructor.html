Metric prefixes from nano to giga

Read and convert the large and small metric prefixes found on nameplates, bills and data plans, and write their symbols correctly.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information

Session: Numeracy lesson 7 of 34. Total length: 60 minutes. This lesson extends Textbook Section 7.1 (kilo to micro) and Section 7.7 (powers of ten) to the larger and smaller prefixes used on nameplates, bills and data plans.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up or show photos of three labels: a motor or tool nameplate, a capacitor and a phone plan screen. Ask: "What does the k, the µ and the G mean?" Note the answers. Ask learners to recall what kilo, centi and milli mean from the textbook.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read a prefix and its symbol on a label (Identify the mathematical information)
  • link each prefix to its power of 10 and to the prefixes next to it (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • convert between prefixed units and work out kilowatt-hours and data use (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • spot a wrong symbol or an amount of the wrong size (Interpret and evaluate the information)

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. 2.5 × 1,000 = ? (2,500)
  2. 450 ÷ 1,000 = ? (0.45)

Learning tasks (35 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Read the key ideas aloud, taking turns. Build the prefix table on the board from tera down to pico, writing "× 1,000" between each step. Explain the capital letter rule. Model the worked example. 1.1 Read in turns. Copy the ladder. Follow the worked example. Learner page, board 8 min
1.2 Learners cover the table and fill in Tasks 1 and 2. 1.2 Complete Tasks 1 and 2, then check against the table. Learner page 5 min
2.1 Model one conversion by counting steps on the ladder. Circulate and ask "Bigger or smaller unit?" 2.1 Complete Tasks 3 and 4. Learner page, calculator 8 min
2.2 Explain kilowatt-hours as power × time. Explain that the power company in Task 5 is fictional. Mention that some devices count storage in powers of 2 and use binary prefixes. 2.2 Complete Tasks 5 and 6. Learner page, calculator 8 min
3.1 Ask learners to work Task 7 in pairs and discuss which amount looks wrong for its item. 3.1 Complete Tasks 7 and 8. Learner page 6 min

Post-assessment (10 min)

  1. Convert 0.75 kW to W. (750 W)
  2. Convert 22 nF to µF. (0.022 µF)
  3. A 750 W saw runs for 2 hours. How many kWh does it use? (1.5 kWh)

Learners give answers in turns and say whether they multiplied or divided. Make sure everyone participates.

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the prefixes from pico to tera, their symbols and their powers of 10
  • why capital and lower-case symbols mean different things
  • converting by steps of 1,000
  • kilowatt-hours on an electricity bill and gigabytes on a phone plan
  • spotting a symbol or amount that does not make sense
