# Metric prefixes from nano to giga

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 7 of 21. **Total length:** 60 minutes. This lesson extends Textbook Section 7.1 (kilo to micro) and Section 7.7 (powers of ten) to the larger and smaller prefixes used on nameplates, bills and data plans.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up or show photos of three labels: a motor or tool nameplate, a capacitor and a phone plan screen. Ask: "What does the k, the µ and the G mean?" Note the answers. Ask learners to recall what kilo, centi and milli mean from the textbook.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- read a prefix and its symbol on a label (Identify the mathematical information)
- link each prefix to its power of 10 and to the prefixes next to it (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- convert between prefixed units and work out kilowatt-hours and data use (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- spot a wrong symbol or an amount of the wrong size (Interpret and evaluate the information)

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. 2.5 × 1,000 = ? (2,500)
2. 450 ÷ 1,000 = ? (0.45)

## Post-assessment (10 min)

1. Convert 0.75 kW to W. (750 W)
2. Convert 22 nF to µF. (0.022 µF)
3. A 750 W saw runs for 2 hours. How many kWh does it use? (1.5 kWh)

Learners give answers in turns and say whether they multiplied or divided. Make sure everyone participates.

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- the prefixes from pico to tera, their symbols and their powers of 10
- why capital and lower-case symbols mean different things
- converting by steps of 1,000
- kilowatt-hours on an electricity bill and gigabytes on a phone plan
- spotting a symbol or amount that does not make sense
