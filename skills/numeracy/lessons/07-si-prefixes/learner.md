# Metric prefixes from nano to giga

## Why this matters on the job

A motor nameplate shows kilowatts, a capacitor shows microfarads, a meter reads milliamps, an electricity bill counts kilowatt-hours and a phone plan counts gigabytes. Electricians, millwrights, instrumentation techs and heavy-duty mechanics read these every day. One wrong prefix, or one wrong capital letter, changes a number by a thousand times or more.

## Key ideas

In the metric system (the SI), we put a prefix in front of a unit to make a new unit with a precise meaning:

prefix + unit of measurement = new word with a specific meaning

- kilo + metre = kilometre, meaning 1,000 metres
- milli + litre = millilitre, meaning a thousandth of a litre
- mega + watt = megawatt, meaning 1,000,000 watts

| Prefix | Symbol | Written number | Power of 10 | Trade example |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| tera | T | 1 000 000 000 000 | 10¹² | terabyte (TB) |
| giga | G | 1 000 000 000 | 10⁹ | gigahertz (GHz) |
| mega | M | 1 000 000 | 10⁶ | megawatt (MW), megapascal (MPa) |
| kilo | k | 1 000 | 10³ | kilowatt (kW), kilopascal (kPa) |
| (none) | | 1 | 10⁰ | watt (W), metre (m) |
| milli | m | 0.001 | 10⁻³ | milliampere (mA) |
| micro | µ | 0.000 001 | 10⁻⁶ | microfarad (µF), micrometre (µm) |
| nano | n | 0.000 000 001 | 10⁻⁹ | nanofarad (nF), nanometre (nm) |
| pico | p | 0.000 000 000 001 | 10⁻¹² | picofarad (pF) |

**Capital letters matter.** Prefix symbols for big multiples are capital letters, except k (kilo), h (hecto) and da (deca). Prefixes for small parts are lower-case. So m is milli and M is mega. The symbol goes right against the unit with no space: kW, not k W.

**Each step in the table is 1,000** (from pico up to tera). To convert, count the steps and multiply or divide by 1,000 for each one:

- going to a smaller unit, the number gets bigger, so multiply
- going to a bigger unit, the number gets smaller, so divide

**SI prefixes are powers of 10, even for data.** One kilobit is 1,000 bits, not 1,024. Computers sometimes count in powers of 2. For those, there are separate binary prefixes, such as kibi (Ki) and gibi (Gi).

### Worked example

A heater is rated 1,500 W and runs for 4 hours. How much energy does it use, in kilowatt-hours (kWh)?

- Energy in watt-hours: 1,500 W × 4 h = 6,000 Wh
- Kilo means 1,000, so divide: 6,000 ÷ 1,000 = 6 kWh

Check: 1,500 W is 1.5 kW, and 1.5 kW × 4 h = 6 kWh. Both ways agree.
