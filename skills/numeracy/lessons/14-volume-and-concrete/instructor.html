Volume, concrete and waste allowance

Find the volume of slabs, footings, walls and piers in cubic metres and cubic yards, add a waste allowance and work out how much to order.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 14 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator with an x³ key (TI-30Xa: 2nd x³, Sharp EL-531XT: x³) or the ACCESS calculators page. Project the Numeracy Hub Volume, Area, Layout and Measuring tab and the plate for Article 9.16.4.3. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 13 (percent and waste allowance) and Day 20 (volume of boxes and prisms).

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone been on a pour where the truck ran out, or where there was concrete left over with nowhere to put it?" Take answers. Say: "Concrete does not wait. The order has to be right before the truck leaves the plant." Tell learners this is session 14, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say what has to be ordered and in what unit (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • connect the drawing sizes, the volume, the waste allowance and the supplier's unit (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • find the volume of slabs, footings, walls and piers, convert between m³ and yd³, and add a waste allowance (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check units, check a slab against the code minimum and round the order up (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • write the order and what it includes for a crew lead (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. Which unit is a volume: m, m² or m³? (m³)
  2. What is 10% of 50? (5)

Learning tasks (70 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach volume of a box and the rule "metres first". Spot the mistake: a box with two sides in metres and one in millimetres multiplied as shown. 1.1 Complete Task 1. Learner page, Textbook p. 268 10 min
1.2 Teach the cubic yard from the exact yard: 0.9144³. Show ÷ 0.9144 x³ on both calculators. 1.2 Complete Task 2. Textbook p. 279, Hub Unit Conversions 8 min
2.1 Teach the waste allowance as one multiplier. Spot the mistake: "a 15% allowance means ordering 15% of the original quantity". Stress: apply it once, then round up. 2.1 Complete Task 3. Textbook p. 142 10 min
2.2 Model the worked example. Show the plate for Article 9.16.4.3 and read Sentence (1) aloud. 2.2 Follow the worked example and the code check. Plate 9.16.4.3 6 min
3.1 Circulate during Tasks 4 and 5. Ask: "What unit does the supplier sell in? When do you round?" 3.1 Complete Tasks 4 and 5. Learner page 18 min
3.2 Introduce Task 6 as a simulated order, not a structural design or authorization to pour. Remind learners that the note must give the number, the unit and what is included. 3.2 Complete Task 6, including the note to the crew lead. Learner page 15 min
3.3 Two or three learners read their notes aloud. The group checks that each note gives the order, the unit and one thing to check. 3.3 Read or listen to notes and give one point of feedback. Learner notes 3 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A slab is 6 m by 4 m and 100 mm thick. Find its volume. (6 × 4 × 0.1 = 2.4 m³)
  2. Change the volume in question 1 to cubic yards. (2.4 ÷ 0.9144³ = 3.14 yd³)
  3. Add a 10% waste allowance to the slab in question 1. (2.4 × 1.10 = 2.64 m³)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the volume of a box and a cylinder, with every size in metres first
  • 1 m³ = 1,000 L, and the exact cubic yard, 0.9144³ m³
  • adding a waste allowance with one multiplier, then rounding up once
  • checking a slab thickness against Sentence 9.16.4.3.(1)
  • writing an order so the crew lead knows what it includes

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W04-L05, W04-L06, W06-L03 and W06-L04.
