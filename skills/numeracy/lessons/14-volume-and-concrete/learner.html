Volume, concrete and waste allowance

Find the volume of slabs, footings, walls and piers in cubic metres and cubic yards, add a waste allowance and work out how much to order.

Why this matters on the job

Concrete is ordered by volume. If the order is short, the pour has to wait for more concrete. If it is long, the extra is paid for and wasted. A carpenter who forms a garage slab, a footing or deck piers works out the volume from the drawings, adds a waste allowance, and checks the order in cubic metres or cubic yards.

Key ideas

Volume is the space a solid takes up, counted in cubes. The small 3 shows that three lengths were multiplied: mm³, m³, ft³, yd³.

Box or slab: V = length × width × thickness. A slab, a strip footing and a straight wall are all boxes. Round pier (cylinder): V = π × r² × depth.

Change every size to metres first. A slab drawn as 7.2 m by 6 m by 100 mm is 7.2 × 6 × 0.1 m. Mixing metres and millimetres in one line gives an answer 1,000 times too big. 1 m³ = 1,000 L.

Cubic yards. A yard is exactly 0.9144 m, so a cubic yard is 0.9144 × 0.9144 × 0.9144 = 0.764 554 857 984 m³ exactly. A yard is also 3 ft, so 1 yd³ = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 ft³.

  • m³ to yd³: divide by 0.9144³ (on the calculator: ÷ 0.9144 x³).
  • yd³ to m³: multiply by 0.9144³.
  • ft³ to yd³: divide by 27.

Waste allowance. Some material is always lost on the job. A 10% waste allowance means you order what you need plus 10% of it. In one step, multiply by 1.10. For 8%, multiply by 1.08. For 15%, multiply by 1.15. Apply the allowance once, to the net quantity.

Add the waste before you round. Work out the volume with waste first, then round up once at the end to the unit the supplier sells.

The drawings set the size, and the code sets the minimum. BC Building Code 2024, Division B, Sentence 9.16.4.3.(1) says: "Concrete slabs shall not be less than 75 mm thick exclusive of concrete topping." Take the thickness from the drawings and use the code to check that it is not below the minimum.

Worked example

A garage slab is 7.2 m by 6 m and 100 mm thick. The order adds a 10% waste allowance. How much concrete, in m³ and yd³?

  1. Thickness in metres: 100 mm = 0.1 m.
  2. Volume: 7.2 × 6 × 0.1 = 4.32 m³. In cubic yards: 4.32 ÷ 0.9144³ = 5.65 yd³.
  3. With 10% waste: 4.32 × 1.10 = 4.752 m³, which is 4.752 ÷ 0.9144³ = 6.22 yd³.
  4. Check: 100 mm is not less than the 75 mm minimum in Sentence 9.16.4.3.(1).

Practice

Task 1 (level 1-2). Volume of boxes.

a) A form is 2.4 m by 1.2 m by 0.6 m. Find its volume in m³. b) A tank is 1,200 mm by 600 mm by 500 mm inside. Find its volume in m³ and in litres. c) A mixing tub is 800 mm by 500 mm by 450 mm. How many litres does it hold? How many 20 L pails fill it?

Task 2 (level 2). Convert. Give answers to two decimal places.

a) 3 yd³ to m³ b) 2 m³ to yd³ c) 54 ft³ to yd³

Task 3 (level 2). Waste allowance.

a) A job needs 85 m of pipe. Add a 10% waste allowance. The pipe is sold in 6 m lengths. How many lengths? b) A roof takes 38 sheets of sheathing. Add a 12% waste allowance. How many whole sheets? c) A floor needs 28 m² of plywood. Add a 15% waste allowance. How many m²?

Task 4 (level 2-3). A basement floor is 9.6 m by 7.2 m. The drawings call for a 75 mm slab on 100 mm of granular material.

a) How much concrete, in m³? b) How much granular material, in m³? c) Add a 5% waste allowance to the concrete. The supplier (Fraser Ready-Mix, a fictional company) sells in steps of 0.5 m³. How much do you order? d) Does the slab meet Sentence 9.16.4.3.(1)?

Task 5 (level 2-3). A deck sits on 8 round concrete piers. Each pier is 300 mm across and 1.2 m deep.

a) Find the volume of concrete in m³ and yd³, to two decimal places. b) Add a 10% waste allowance. Give the order in m³ to two decimal places.

Task 6 (level 3). A house has a strip footing 350 mm wide and 100 mm thick under a concrete foundation wall 200 mm thick and 2.4 m high. The footing and the wall each run 40 m.

a) Find the volume of the footing and of the wall in m³. b) Find the total in m³ and in yd³. c) Add an 8% waste allowance. Give the order in m³ and in yd³. d) Write two or three sentences to your crew lead that give the order, say what it includes, and name one thing to check on the drawings before the pour.

  4. Why do you add the waste before you round up?
