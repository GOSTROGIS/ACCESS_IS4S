# Volume, concrete and waste allowance

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 14 of 16. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator with an x³ key (TI-30Xa: 2nd x³, Sharp EL-531XT: x³) or the ACCESS [calculators page](../../calculators/calculators.html). Project the [Numeracy Hub Volume, Area, Layout and Measuring tab](../../hub/index.html#vlm) and the [plate for Article 9.16.4.3](../../../../programs/carpentry/bcbc-part-9/9.16/9.16.4.3.svg). This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for [Day 13](../../../../programs/carpentry/intake-142/PDF/Day-13_2026-11-18_Week-4.pdf) (percent and waste allowance) and [Day 20](../../../../programs/carpentry/intake-142/PDF/Day-20_2026-12-01_Week-6.pdf) (volume of boxes and prisms).

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone been on a pour where the truck ran out, or where there was concrete left over with nowhere to put it?" Take answers. Say: "Concrete does not wait. The order has to be right before the truck leaves the plant." Tell learners this is session 14, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- say what has to be ordered and in what unit (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
- connect the drawing sizes, the volume, the waste allowance and the supplier's unit (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- find the volume of slabs, footings, walls and piers, convert between m³ and yd³, and add a waste allowance (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- check units, check a slab against the code minimum and round the order up (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- write the order and what it includes for a crew lead (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. Which unit is a volume: m, m² or m³? (m³)
2. What is 10% of 50? (5)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. A slab is 6 m by 4 m and 100 mm thick. Find its volume. (6 × 4 × 0.1 = 2.4 m³)
2. Change the volume in question 1 to cubic yards. (2.4 ÷ 0.9144³ = 3.14 yd³)
3. Add a 10% waste allowance to the slab in question 1. (2.4 × 1.10 = 2.64 m³)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- the volume of a box and a cylinder, with every size in metres first
- 1 m³ = 1,000 L, and the exact cubic yard, 0.9144³ m³
- adding a waste allowance with one multiplier, then rounding up once
- checking a slab thickness against Sentence 9.16.4.3.(1)
- writing an order so the crew lead knows what it includes
