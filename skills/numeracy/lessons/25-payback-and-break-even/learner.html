Payback and break-even

Decide whether a tool, an upgrade or a small business pays for itself by working out the payback period, rent-or-buy days and the break-even point.

Why this matters on the job

"Should we buy it or keep renting it?" "Will new lights pay for themselves?" "How many hours do I need to bill each month to keep my truck on the road?" Crews and small contractors ask these questions all the time. Each one has the same kind of answer: how long, or how many, until the money saved or earned covers the money spent. That point is the payback or the break-even.

Key ideas

Payback period. How long until the savings pay back what you spent.

payback period = cost ÷ saving per month (or per year)

If the saving is per month, the answer is in months. If it is per year, the answer is in years.

Net saving. If the new tool also costs something to run, take that off first. Brackets first (BEDMAS):

payback period = cost ÷ (saving per month − extra running cost per month)

Rent or buy. Divide the price to buy by the rental cost per day. That is how many days of rental the price would pay for.

Break-even in units. A business has fixed costs that stay the same however much it makes (a jig, set-up time, a truck payment, insurance) and a variable cost for each unit (material and labour per piece, or wage and fuel per hour). Each unit sold at a price brings in a contribution:

contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit

Below the break-even point the business loses money. Above it, each unit adds its contribution to profit.

When the savings are not even. Add up the savings period by period in a table (a running total). The payback is where the running total reaches the cost.

Assumptions. Every payback answer rests on guesses: how often the tool is used, how long it lasts, what the rental rate will be. Write them down. If a guess is wrong, the answer is wrong.

Worked example

A fabrication crew rents a magnetic drill for $85.00 a day. A new one costs $2,550.00. The crew uses it about 3 days a month.

  1. Rental days the price would pay for: $2,550.00 ÷ $85.00 = 30 days
  2. Months to reach 30 days of use: 30 ÷ 3 = 10 months

If the drill lasts much longer than 10 months, buying pays for itself. The answer assumes the crew keeps using it 3 days a month and the rental rate stays at $85.00.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1-2). A shop replaces its old lights with LED fixtures. The job costs $3,600.00. The power bill goes down by $150.00 a month. What is the payback period in months? In years?

Task 2 (level 2). A pipe crew rents a power pipe threader for $120.00 a day. Buying one costs $4,200.00. The crew uses it about 3 days a month.

a) How many days of rental would the price pay for? b) About how many months until buying pays for itself?

Task 3 (level 2). A small shop buys a $2,400.00 air compressor so it no longer has to send work out. It saves 6 hours of labour a month at $45.00 an hour. Power and upkeep for the compressor cost $30.00 a month. What is the payback period?

Task 4 (level 2-3). A shop makes railing brackets. A welding jig and set-up cost $1,800.00. Each bracket costs $14.00 in steel and labour and sells for $26.00.

a) What is the contribution per bracket? b) How many brackets must the shop sell to break even? c) What is the profit if it sells 200 brackets?

Task 5 (level 3). Riley runs a one-truck electrical business. Fixed costs are $6,300.00 a month: truck payment, insurance, phone and shop rent. Riley charges $95.00 for each hour billed. Each billed hour costs $50.00 in wages, payroll costs and fuel.

a) How many hours a month must Riley bill to break even? b) What is the profit in a month with 160 billed hours? c) What happens in a slow month with 120 billed hours?

Task 6 (level 3). A crew lead wants a $10,000.00 trailer-mounted tool crib that saves set-up and travel time. The savings are not the same each year:

Year Saving that year Running total
1 $3,000.00
2 $4,000.00
3 $4,000.00
4 $4,000.00

a) Fill in the running total. b) In which year does the running total pass $10,000.00? c) Find the payback period to two decimal places of a year.

Task 7 (level 3). Look back at Task 2. Write two assumptions the answer depends on. For each one, say what happens to the payback if it turns out wrong.

Task 8 (level 3). Write two or three sentences to Riley in Task 5 that give the break-even hours and say what a slow month means in dollars.

Check yourself

  1. A tool costs $900.00 and saves $75.00 a month. What is the payback period?
  2. Rental is $50.00 a day and buying costs $1,500.00. How many days of rental is that?
  3. Fixed costs are $500.00, the price is $40.00 and the variable cost is $15.00 a unit. How many units to break even?
  4. In the break-even formula, what do you work out first?
  5. Why should you write down the assumptions behind a payback answer?
