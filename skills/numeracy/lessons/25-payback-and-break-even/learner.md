# Payback and break-even

## Why this matters on the job

"Should we buy it or keep renting it?" "Will new lights pay for themselves?" "How many hours do I need to bill each month to keep my truck on the road?" Crews and small contractors ask these questions all the time. Each one has the same kind of answer: how long, or how many, until the money saved or earned covers the money spent. That point is the **payback** or the **break-even**.

## Key ideas

**Payback period.** How long until the savings pay back what you spent.

payback period = cost ÷ saving per month (or per year)

If the saving is per month, the answer is in months. If it is per year, the answer is in years.

**Net saving.** If the new tool also costs something to run, take that off first. Brackets first (BEDMAS):

payback period = cost ÷ (saving per month − extra running cost per month)

**Rent or buy.** Divide the price to buy by the rental cost per day. That is how many days of rental the price would pay for.

**Break-even in units.** A business has **fixed costs** that stay the same however much it makes (a jig, set-up time, a truck payment, insurance) and a **variable cost** for each unit (material and labour per piece, or wage and fuel per hour). Each unit sold at a price brings in a **contribution**:

contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit
break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit

Below the break-even point the business loses money. Above it, each unit adds its contribution to profit.

**When the savings are not even.** Add up the savings period by period in a table (a running total). The payback is where the running total reaches the cost.

**Assumptions.** Every payback answer rests on guesses: how often the tool is used, how long it lasts, what the rental rate will be. Write them down. If a guess is wrong, the answer is wrong.

### Worked example

A fabrication crew rents a magnetic drill for $85.00 a day. A new one costs $2,550.00. The crew uses it about 3 days a month.

1. Rental days the price would pay for: $2,550.00 ÷ $85.00 = **30 days**
2. Months to reach 30 days of use: 30 ÷ 3 = **10 months**

If the drill lasts much longer than 10 months, buying pays for itself. The answer assumes the crew keeps using it 3 days a month and the rental rate stays at $85.00.
