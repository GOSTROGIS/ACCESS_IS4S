Payback and break-even

Decide whether a tool, an upgrade or a small business pays for itself by working out the payback period, rent-or-buy days and the break-even point.

Task 1 (level 1-2)

$3,600.00 ÷ $150.00 = 24 months. 24 ÷ 12 = 2 years.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) $4,200.00 ÷ $120.00 = 35 days b) 35 ÷ 3 = 11.67, so about 12 months (just under a year).

Task 3 (level 2)

Labour saved: 6 × $45.00 = $270.00 a month. Net saving: $270.00 − $30.00 = $240.00 a month. Payback: $2,400.00 ÷ $240.00 = 10 months.

BEDMAS: in $2,400.00 ÷ (6 × $45.00 − $30.00), the brackets come first.

Task 4 (level 2-3)

a) $26.00 − $14.00 = $12.00 per bracket b) $1,800.00 ÷ $12.00 = 150 brackets c) 200 × $12.00 = $2,400.00. $2,400.00 − $1,800.00 = $600.00 profit. Check: sales 200 × $26.00 = $5,200.00, costs $1,800.00 + 200 × $14.00 = $4,600.00, profit $600.00.

Task 5 (level 3)

Contribution per billed hour: $95.00 − $50.00 = $45.00.

a) $6,300.00 ÷ $45.00 = 140 hours a month b) 160 × $45.00 = $7,200.00. $7,200.00 − $6,300.00 = $900.00 profit c) 120 × $45.00 = $5,400.00. $5,400.00 − $6,300.00 = −$900.00. Riley loses $900.00 that month.

Task 6 (level 3)

a)

Year Saving that year Running total
1 $3,000.00 $3,000.00
2 $4,000.00 $7,000.00
3 $4,000.00 $11,000.00
4 $4,000.00 $15,000.00

b) In year 3 ($11,000.00 is more than $10,000.00). c) After 2 years, $10,000.00 − $7,000.00 = $3,000.00 is still to pay back. Year 3 saves $4,000.00, so it takes $3,000.00 ÷ $4,000.00 = 0.75 of year 3. Payback: 2 + 0.75 = 2.75 years, assuming the savings accrue evenly through year 3. If savings arrive only at each year-end, payback is at the end of year 3.

Task 7 (level 3)

Open task. Any two well-explained assumptions, for example:

  • The crew uses the threader 3 days a month. If it uses it less, payback takes longer. At 2 days a month it is 35 ÷ 2 = 17.5 months.
  • The rental rate stays at $120.00 a day. If the rate goes up, buying pays back sooner. If it goes down, later.
  • The threader lasts longer than the payback period and needs little repair. Repair costs or a short life make buying worth less.
  • Someone is free to pick up and return a rental. If renting also costs travel time, buying pays back sooner.

Task 8 (level 3)

Open task. A good answer gives the break-even of 140 billed hours a month, says that each hour over 140 adds $45.00 to profit, and says that a 120-hour month loses $900.00. It may suggest keeping cash aside for slow months (lesson 27).
