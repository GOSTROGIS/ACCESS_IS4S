Progress claims, holdback and GST

Work out a monthly progress claim from percent complete, take off the 10% holdback the BC Builders Lien Act requires, add GST, and find when the holdback is paid.

Why this matters on the job

On a building job, a subcontractor does not wait until the end to get paid. Each month it sends a progress claim: a bill for the work done that month. Part of every claim is kept back by law as holdback and paid weeks after the work is finished. A crew lead who can read a claim knows how much money is really coming in, and can spot a mistake before it costs the company.

Key ideas

Schedule of values. The contract price is split into lines, one for each part of the work. Each month the sub estimates how much of each line is done, as a percent.

  • value to date = line value × percent complete
  • this month's claim = total value to date − total claimed last month

Change orders. An approved change order adds to (or takes away from) the contract price. Add it to the value to date when the work is done.

Reading lesson 20 (What is in a construction contract) walks through these rules in a contract. Here you do the math. Holdback. The BC Builders Lien Act, section 4, says the person paying on a contract or subcontract must keep back a holdback equal to 10% of the work. So 10% of each claim is held back. Section 4 also says a holdback must not be kept from a worker or a material supplier.

Holdback period. When the work is finished, the payment certifier issues a certificate of completion. Under section 8 the holdback period ends 55 days after that certificate is issued. After the holdback period ends, the holdback can be paid, unless someone has filed a claim of lien. Under section 20, a lien for that work must be filed no later than 45 days after the certificate is issued.

A law that is coming. BC passed the Construction Prompt Payment Act in November 2025. When it comes into force, it will change the 55 days to 46 days. On the date of this lesson it was not yet in force. Check BC Laws before you use the 55 days on a real job.

GST on a progress claim. In BC the GST is 5%. The holdback comes off first. GST is charged on the amount being paid now, the claim less the holdback. The Canada Revenue Agency says the GST on the holdback becomes payable when the holdback is paid or when the holdback period ends, whichever comes first. Do not assume that delayed release also delays the GST: use the earlier of payment and the end of the holdback period stated above.

The claim in four lines (BEDMAS: work out each line before the next):

  1. holdback = this month's claim × 0.10
  2. net claim = this month's claim − holdback
  3. GST = net claim × 0.05
  4. amount due = net claim + GST

Worked example

Coastline Framing Ltd. (a fictional company) has a $120,000 subcontract with Northbank Builders (a fictional general contractor) to frame a small commercial building.

Line Value Done at end of March Value to date
Floor framing $30,000 100% $30,000
Wall framing $45,000 40% $18,000
Roof framing $35,000 0% $0
Sheathing $10,000 0% $0
Total $120,000 $48,000

This is the first claim, so nothing was claimed before. This month's claim is $48,000.00.

  1. Holdback: $48,000.00 × 0.10 = $4,800.00
  2. Net claim: $48,000.00 − $4,800.00 = $43,200.00
  3. GST: $43,200.00 × 0.05 = $2,160.00
  4. Amount due: $43,200.00 + $2,160.00 = $45,360.00

Northbank keeps $4,800.00 as holdback until the holdback period ends.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1). Use the Key ideas.

a) What percent does the Builders Lien Act say must be held back? b) A lumber yard sends Coastline Framing an invoice for material. Should Coastline keep a holdback from the lumber yard? Which section says so? c) How many days after the certificate of completion does the holdback period end today?

Task 2 (level 2). Claim 2 for Coastline Framing, end of April.

Line Value Done at end of April Value to date
Floor framing $30,000 100%
Wall framing $45,000 100%
Roof framing $35,000 50%
Sheathing $10,000 30%
Total $120,000

a) Fill in the value to date for each line and the total. b) Find this month's claim. (Last month's total was $48,000.) c) Find the holdback, the net claim, the GST and the amount due. d) What percent of the whole subcontract is done?

Task 3 (level 2-3). Claim 3, end of May. All four lines are 100% done. Northbank also approved change order CO-1 for an extra beam, worth $4,000. The beam is installed.

a) What is the new total value to date? b) Find this month's claim, the holdback, the net claim, the GST and the amount due. c) Add up the holdback from Claims 1, 2 and 3. Check that it is 10% of the final value of the work.

Task 4 (level 3). Spot the mistakes. Northbank's clerk sent back this version of Claim 2. Find two mistakes and write the correct amount due.

Task 5 (level 3). The payment certifier issues a certificate of completion for Coastline's subcontract on Friday, June 4, 2027.

a) What is the last day a claim of lien can be filed for this work (45 days)? b) On what date does the 55-day holdback period end? c) If no lien is filed, Coastline bills the holdback with GST. What is the amount of that invoice? d) If the Construction Prompt Payment Act were in force, the holdback period would be 46 days. On what date would it end?

Task 6 (level 3). The framing was done by the end of May. Use your answers to Tasks 3 and 5. About how many weeks after the work is done does the holdback arrive, at the earliest? Write two sentences for a new crew lead explaining why the last 10% is late.
