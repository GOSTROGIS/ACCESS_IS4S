# Progress claims, holdback and GST

## Why this matters on the job

On a building job, a subcontractor does not wait until the end to get paid. Each month it sends a **progress claim**: a bill for the work done that month. Part of every claim is kept back by law as **holdback** and paid weeks after the work is finished. A crew lead who can read a claim knows how much money is really coming in, and can spot a mistake before it costs the company.

## Key ideas

**Schedule of values.** The contract price is split into lines, one for each part of the work. Each month the sub estimates how much of each line is done, as a percent.

- value to date = line value × percent complete
- this month's claim = total value to date − total claimed last month

**Change orders.** An approved change order adds to (or takes away from) the contract price. Add it to the value to date when the work is done.

Reading lesson 20 ([What is in a construction contract](../../../reading/lessons/20-what-is-in-a-construction-contract/learner.html)) walks through these rules in a contract. Here you do the math. **Holdback.** The BC *Builders Lien Act*, section 4, says the person paying on a contract or subcontract must keep back a holdback equal to 10% of the work. So 10% of each claim is held back. Section 4 also says a holdback must not be kept from a worker or a material supplier.

**Holdback period.** When the work is finished, the payment certifier issues a **certificate of completion**. Under section 8 the holdback period ends 55 days after that certificate is issued. After the holdback period ends, the holdback can be paid, unless someone has filed a claim of lien. Under section 20, a lien for that work must be filed no later than 45 days after the certificate is issued.

**A law that is coming.** BC passed the *Construction Prompt Payment Act* in November 2025. When it comes into force, it will change the 55 days to 46 days. On the date of this lesson it was not yet in force. Check BC Laws before you use the 55 days on a real job.

**GST on a progress claim.** In BC the GST is 5%. The holdback comes off first. GST is charged on the amount being paid now, the claim less the holdback. The Canada Revenue Agency says the GST on the holdback becomes payable when the holdback is paid or when the holdback period ends, whichever comes first. So the GST on the holdback is billed when the holdback is released.

**The claim in four lines** (BEDMAS: work out each line before the next):

1. holdback = this month's claim × 0.10
2. net claim = this month's claim − holdback
3. GST = net claim × 0.05
4. amount due = net claim + GST

### Worked example

Coastline Framing Ltd. (a fictional company) has a $120,000 subcontract with Northbank Builders (a fictional general contractor) to frame a small commercial building.

| Line | Value | Done at end of March | Value to date |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Floor framing | $30,000 | 100% | $30,000 |
| Wall framing | $45,000 | 40% | $18,000 |
| Roof framing | $35,000 | 0% | $0 |
| Sheathing | $10,000 | 0% | $0 |
| **Total** | **$120,000** | | **$48,000** |

This is the first claim, so nothing was claimed before. This month's claim is $48,000.00.

1. Holdback: $48,000.00 × 0.10 = $4,800.00
2. Net claim: $48,000.00 − $4,800.00 = $43,200.00
3. GST: $43,200.00 × 0.05 = $2,160.00
4. Amount due: $43,200.00 + $2,160.00 = **$45,360.00**

Northbank keeps $4,800.00 as holdback until the holdback period ends.
