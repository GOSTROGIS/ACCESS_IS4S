Percent grade between two points on a site

Work out the grade between two elevations as a percent, as 1 in a number and in degrees, find the fall a drain or path needs, check a ramp against the BC Building Code, and change lot areas between hectares and acres.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy; Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 32 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator that has tan⁻¹ and a degree mode. Check that every calculator is in degree mode before Task 2. Print or project the code table from the learner page. Learners should have done lesson 16 (slope as "1 in" a number) and lesson 31 (elevations from a level). A sloped walkway, ramp or driveway near the building is useful for the opening.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. If there is a ramp or a sloped driveway near the building, point to it, or show a photo. Ask: "Is that ramp too steep? How would you prove it with numbers instead of guessing?" Take answers. Say: "Today you turn two heights and a distance into a grade, and check it against the BC Building Code. You will also see why a 45° bank is not a 45% grade." Tell learners this is session 32 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say whether a site question asks for a grade, a fall or an elevation (Identify the task that will require you to use numeracy)
  • pick out the two elevations and the horizontal distance from a drawing or a job description (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect a grade in percent, as "1 in" a number and in degrees, and connect it to the roof slopes in lesson 16 (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • work out grade, fall, elevations and lot areas in hectares and acres (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • check a ramp or walkway against the BC Building Code 2024 (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • report a slope that does not meet a limit (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. 0.45 ÷ 18 × 100 = ? (2.5)
  2. A roof is 1 in 4. Is 1 in 3 steeper or less steep? (Steeper, because the second number is smaller)

Learning tasks (70 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach rise, horizontal run and the grade formulas. Walk through the worked example. Show the BEDMAS order: difference first, then divide, then × 100. 1.1 Follow the worked example. Learner page 10 min
1.2 Teach % to 1 in n and back. Link to lesson 16. 1.2 Complete Task 1. Learner page, lesson 16 8 min
1.3 Check degree mode on every calculator. Model Task 2a. Ask: "Which way does the water run?" 1.3 Complete Task 2. Hub Angles and Trig 9 min
2.1 Teach rise = run × grade ÷ 100 for pipe and path falls. 2.1 Complete Task 3. Learner page 8 min
2.2 Show the code table. Read Articles 9.8.5.1 and 9.8.5.4 and Clauses 3.8.3.5.(1)(b) and 3.8.3.2.(3)(g) aloud. Stress that a larger n is gentler. Circulate during Task 4 and ask: "Which row of the table?" 2.2 Complete Task 4. Code table on the learner page 14 min
2.3 Teach that percent is not degrees. Show tan⁻¹(1) = 45°. 2.3 Complete Task 5. Learner page 6 min
3.1 Assign Task 6. Read the notes to the supervisor. 3.1 Complete Task 6, including the note. Learner page 8 min
3.2 Teach hectares and acres with the two definitions. Show a lot area from a real BC listing site on screen if one is handy, without any personal details. 3.2 Complete Task 7. Hub Unit Conversions 7 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Two points are at 20.600 and 20.150, 15 m apart. What is the grade? (0.450 ÷ 15 × 100 = 3%)
  2. Write 1 in 10 as a percent. (10%)
  3. A 6 m pipe must fall at 2%. How much does it fall? (0.12 m, or 120 mm)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • grade = rise ÷ horizontal run × 100, and the BEDMAS order
  • the same slope as a percent, as 1 in n and in degrees, and why 100% is 45°
  • the fall of a pipe or path from its grade
  • ramp limits in the BC Building Code 2024, Article 9.8.5.4 and Clause 3.8.3.5.(1)(b), and the 1 in 20 rule for accessible paths
  • lot areas in hectares and acres

Next session: lesson 33 finds values between the rows of a chart.

  • Article 9.8.5.1: Subsection 9.8.5 "applies to pedestrian ramps, except ramps in an accessible path of travel", which "shall conform to the requirements in Article 3.8.3.5." Page 9-33.
  • Article 9.8.5.4, Sentence (1): "The slope of ramps shall be not more than a) 1 in 10 for exterior ramps, b) 1 in 10 for interior ramps serving residential occupancies, c) 1 in 6 for industrial occupancies, and d) 1 in 8 for all other occupancies." Pages 9-33 and 9-34.
  • Clause 3.8.3.5.(1)(b): a ramp in an accessible path of travel shall "have a uniform slope along its length not more than 1 in 12". Page 3-271.
  • Clause 3.8.3.2.(3)(g): walking surfaces in an accessible path of travel shall "be designed as a ramp complying with this Section where the path of travel has a slope steeper than 1 in 20." Page 3-270.
  • Article 9.14.6.1, Sentence (1): "The building shall be located or the building site graded so that water will not accumulate at or near the building." Page 9-117.
