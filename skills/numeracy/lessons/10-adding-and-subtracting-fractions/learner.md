# Adding and subtracting fractions on a cut list

## Why this matters on the job

A cut list tells you how many pieces to cut and how long each one is. Before you cut, you need to know if the pieces fit on the board you have and how long the offcut will be. That means adding and subtracting lengths such as 22 3/8 in and 31 1/2 in, and allowing for the wood the saw blade turns into sawdust. Getting it right saves trips to the lumber rack and wasted boards.

## Key ideas

**Same denominator: add or subtract the numerators.** The denominator stays the same because it names the size of each part. 3/16 + 5/16 = 8/16 = 1/2.

**Different denominators: rename first.** The parts must be the same size before you add them. For tape fractions, 16 works for halves, quarters, eighths and sixteenths. 7/8 − 5/16 = 14/16 − 5/16 = 9/16. Never add the denominators: 1/2 + 1/4 is not 2/6. Rename 1/2 as 2/4, then 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4.

**Mixed numbers: add the wholes, add the fractions.** If the fractions come to one whole or more, carry the whole.

**Subtracting mixed numbers: borrow when the top fraction is too small.** In 5 1/4 − 2 7/8, you cannot take 7/8 from 2/8. Borrow 1 from the 5 and write it as 8/8: 5 2/8 becomes 4 10/8. Then 4 10/8 − 2 7/8 = 2 3/8.

**Repeated pieces: multiply.** Four pieces of 11 3/4 in is 4 × 11 3/4 = 47 in.

**Saw kerf.** Every saw cut turns a strip of wood into sawdust. The width of that strip is the kerf. If the kerf is 1/8 in and you make 3 cuts, 3 × 1/8 = 3/8 in of board is lost. Count the cuts the question describes, and use the kerf it gives.

**Check by adding back.** Add your answer to the number you took away. You should get the number you started with.

### Worked example

Three pieces are on a cut list: 12 3/4 in, 8 5/8 in and 15 1/2 in. What is the total length?

1. Add the whole numbers: 12 + 8 + 15 = 35.
2. Rename the fractions in eighths and add: 6/8 + 5/8 + 4/8 = 15/8.
3. 15/8 = 1 7/8. Carry the 1: 35 + 1 = 36.
4. Total: **36 7/8 in**

On the TI-30Xa or the Sharp EL-531XT, key: 12 a b/c 3 a b/c 4 + 8 a b/c 5 a b/c 8 + 15 a b/c 1 a b/c 2 =. The TI-30Xa shows 36⌟7⌟8 and the Sharp shows 36┌7┌8. Both mean 36 7/8.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1). Add or subtract. Give each answer in lowest terms.

a) 3/8 + 7/16 b) 5/8 + 3/4 c) 15/16 − 3/8 d) 11/16 − 1/4 e) 1/2 + 1/4

**Task 2** (level 1-2). Add or subtract. Give each answer as a mixed number in lowest terms.

a) 10 7/8 + 6 3/4
b) 12 3/16 − 5 5/8
c) 6 − 2 3/8
d) 5 1/4 − 2 7/8
e) Pieces of 18 3/8 in, 24 in and 9 1/2 in. What is the total length?

**Task 3** (level 2). Read the whole cut list before you calculate.

> **Cut list: shelf cleats** (Coastline Framing Ltd., a fictional company)
>
> Stock: one board 96 in long.
> Pieces: 30 3/8 in and 41 11/16 in.
> Make 2 cuts. The kerf is 1/8 in.

a) What is the total length of the two pieces?
b) How much board do the saw cuts use?
c) How long is the offcut? Check by adding back.

**Task 4** (level 2). Repeated pieces and centre lines.

a) Find the centre of a stud 92 5/8 in long.
b) Five blocks, each 9 3/8 in long, are cut from a 60 in board. You make 5 cuts and the kerf is 1/8 in. How long is the offcut?

**Task 5** (level 2). Spot the mistake. Explain what went wrong and give the correct answer.

a) A learner writes 1/2 + 1/4 = 2/6.
b) A learner writes 5 1/4 − 2 7/8 = 3 3/8.
c) Two pieces are cut from a board. A learner subtracts the pieces from the board length but leaves out the saw cuts, even though the cut list gives a kerf.

**Task 6** (level 2). Two pieces of trim, 6 ft 4 3/4 in and 2 ft 10 1/2 in, are laid end to end. Find the total length in feet and inches. Check by changing both to inches first.
