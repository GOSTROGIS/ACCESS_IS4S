Right triangles, the 3-4-5 check and squaring a layout

Use the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side, check a corner with 3-4-5, and square a frame or a foundation layout with its diagonals.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 15 of 34. Total length: 75 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator with x² and √ keys, or the ACCESS calculators page. For step 3.2, set out a rectangle of string or tape on the floor or a table, about 1,200 mm by 900 mm, with one corner slightly out of square, and have two tapes ready. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for Day 17 (square roots and hidden dimensions), Day 21 (Pythagorean theorem and brace lengths) and Day 22 (checking square, diagonals and layout).

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show the out-of-square rectangle on the floor. Ask: "Is this square? How would you know without a framing square?" Take answers. Say: "A tape and one rule from a right triangle can square anything from a picture frame to a foundation." Tell learners this is session 15, and it runs 75 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • name the legs and the hypotenuse of a right triangle on a drawing (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect the 3-4-5 check and the diagonals of a frame to the Pythagorean theorem (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • find a missing side with the theorem, by hand and on the calculator (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • decide if a corner or frame is square and which way to move it (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • tell a crew what to adjust (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. 5² = ? (25)
  2. In a right triangle, which side is the longest? (the side across from the right angle, the hypotenuse)

Learning tasks (55 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Teach legs, hypotenuse and a² + b² = c². Show textbook Figure 12.5, the squares on a 3-4-5 triangle. Show the calculator keys. 1.1 Complete Task 1. Textbook p. 302, calculators 8 min
1.2 Teach finding a leg. Spot the mistake: a missing leg found by adding the squares. 1.2 Complete Task 2. Learner page 8 min
2.1 Teach the 3-4-5 check and its multiples with the worked example. Spot the mistake: 3, 4 and 10 as a "scaled" check. 2.1 Complete Task 3. Textbook p. 304 9 min
3.1 Teach diagonals. Pairs measure both diagonals of the string rectangle, decide which corners to push, adjust, and measure again. 3.1 Square the string rectangle in pairs, then complete Task 4. String rectangle, tapes, Shape Canvas 14 min
3.2 Review changing a decimal inch to sixteenths for Task 5. 3.2 Complete Task 5. Learner page 5 min
3.3 Introduce Task 6 as a real layout. Circulate and read the crew notes. 3.3 Complete Task 6, including the note to the crew. Learner page, Textbook p. 319 11 min

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. Marks at 1.2 m and 1.6 m. What should the diagonal be? (2.0 m)
  2. Hypotenuse 1,000 mm, one leg 600 mm. Find the other leg. (√(1,000,000 − 360,000) = 800 mm)
  3. A frame's diagonals measure 3,605 mm and 3,598 mm. Is it square? (No, they differ by 7 mm)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • legs, hypotenuse and the Pythagorean theorem
  • adding squares to find the hypotenuse and subtracting to find a leg
  • the 3-4-5 check and its multiples
  • squaring a frame with its diagonals, and which corners to push
  • telling a crew what to adjust

Further practice: Intake 142 lecture quizzes W05-L05, W06-L06 and W06-L07.
