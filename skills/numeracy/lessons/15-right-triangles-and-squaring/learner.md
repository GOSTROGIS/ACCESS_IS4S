# Right triangles, the 3-4-5 check and squaring a layout

## Why this matters on the job

A wall, a deck or a foundation that is out of square makes every later step harder: sheets and flooring no longer line up with the edges and need tapered cuts. Carpenters check corners with the 3-4-5 method and check whole frames by measuring the diagonals. Both come from one rule about right triangles. The same rule gives the length of a brace or a rafter.

## Key ideas

**Parts of a right triangle.** A right triangle has one 90° corner. The two sides that make the corner are the **legs**. The side across from the corner is the **hypotenuse**. The hypotenuse is always the longest side.

**The Pythagorean theorem:** a² + b² = c², where a and b are the legs and c is the hypotenuse.

- To find the hypotenuse, square both legs, **add**, then take the square root: c = √(a² + b²)
- To find a leg, square the hypotenuse and the known leg, **subtract**, then take the square root: a = √(c² − b²)

If you add the squares when you are looking for a leg, your answer will be longer than the hypotenuse. That cannot happen.

**On the calculator**, the brackets make it add the squares before the square root.

**The 3-4-5 check.** 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5². If the sides of a triangle are 3, 4 and 5 units, the corner between the 3 and the 4 is square. Any multiple works, in any unit: 300-400-500 mm, 600-800-1,000 mm, 6-8-10 ft. All three numbers must use the same multiple: 3-4-10 is not a 3-4-5 check.

1. From the corner, mark 3 units along one edge.
2. From the same corner, mark 4 units along the other edge.
3. Measure between the marks. If it is exactly 5 units, the corner is square. Longer means the corner is open past 90°. Shorter means it is closed to less than 90°.

Use the largest multiple that fits on the work. A reading error of 1 mm matters less on a long diagonal than on a short one.

**Diagonals.** A diagonal cuts a rectangle into two right triangles, so d = √(length² + width²). If the opposite sides are equal and both diagonals are equal, every corner is 90°. If the diagonals differ, the two corners at the ends of the longer diagonal are less than 90°. Push those two corners toward each other and measure again until the diagonals match.

### Worked example

You mark 600 mm along one side of a wall frame corner and 800 mm along the other. The tape reads 1,006 mm between the marks. Is the corner square?

1. For a square corner: 600² + 800² = 360,000 + 640,000 = 1,000,000, and √1,000,000 = 1,000 mm.
2. The tape reads 1,006 mm, which is 6 mm too long.
3. A diagonal that is too long means the corner is open past 90°.

**Not square.** Close the corner until the marks are 1,000 mm apart.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1-2). Find the hypotenuse. Give answers to two decimal places where needed.

a) Legs 600 mm and 800 mm
b) Legs 1,500 mm and 900 mm
c) Legs 9 in and 12 in

**Task 2** (level 2). Find the missing leg. Give answers to two decimal places where needed.

a) Hypotenuse 1,300 mm, one leg 500 mm
b) Hypotenuse 2.5 m, one leg 1.8 m
c) A brace 2,000 mm long runs from the floor to a post. Its top end is 1,350 mm above the floor. How far from the post is its bottom end?

**Task 3** (level 2). The 3-4-5 check.

a) Marks at 300 mm and 400 mm. What should the diagonal be?
b) Marks at 9 ft and 12 ft. What should the diagonal be?
c) Marks at 900 mm and 1,200 mm. The diagonal reads 1,494 mm. Is the corner square, open or closed?
d) Marks at 1.2 m and 1.6 m. What should the diagonal be?
e) Do 5, 12 and 13 make a right triangle? Show the check.

**Task 4** (level 2-3). Diagonals.

a) A deck frame is 4,800 mm by 3,600 mm. What should each diagonal measure?
b) A frame measures 2,013 mm on one diagonal and 2,020 mm on the other. Is it square? Which corners do you push?
c) A door opening is 36 in by 80 in. Find the diagonal to the nearest 1/16 in.

**Task 5** (level 2-3). A right triangle has legs of 2 ft 0 in and 1 ft 3 in. Find the hypotenuse to the nearest 1/16 in, in feet and inches.

**Task 6** (level 3). A foundation layout for a shop in Prince George is 9,600 mm by 6,000 mm.

a) What 3-4-5 marks would you use at a corner, with 1,200 mm as the unit?
b) What should each diagonal measure, to the nearest millimetre?
c) The diagonals measure 11,332 mm and 11,309 mm. Is the layout square?
d) Write two sentences to the crew telling them what to adjust and how they will know it is right.
