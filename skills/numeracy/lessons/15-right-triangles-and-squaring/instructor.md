# Right triangles, the 3-4-5 check and squaring a layout

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 15 of 34. **Total length:** 75 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator with x² and √ keys, or the ACCESS [calculators page](../../calculators/calculators.html). For step 3.2, set out a rectangle of string or tape on the floor or a table, about 1,200 mm by 900 mm, with one corner slightly out of square, and have two tapes ready. This lesson follows the Intake 142 decks for [Day 17](../../../../programs/carpentry/intake-142/PDF/Day-17_2026-11-26_Week-5.pdf) (square roots and hidden dimensions), [Day 21](../../../../programs/carpentry/intake-142/PDF/Day-21_2026-12-02_Week-6.pdf) (Pythagorean theorem and brace lengths) and [Day 22](../../../../programs/carpentry/intake-142/PDF/Day-22_2026-12-03_Week-6.pdf) (checking square, diagonals and layout).

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show the out-of-square rectangle on the floor. Ask: "Is this square? How would you know without a framing square?" Take answers. Say: "A tape and one rule from a right triangle can square anything from a picture frame to a foundation." Tell learners this is session 15, and it runs 75 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name the legs and the hypotenuse of a right triangle on a drawing (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect the 3-4-5 check and the diagonals of a frame to the Pythagorean theorem (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- find a missing side with the theorem, by hand and on the calculator (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- decide if a corner or frame is square and which way to move it (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- tell a crew what to adjust (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. 5² = ? (25)
2. In a right triangle, which side is the longest? (the side across from the right angle, the hypotenuse)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. Marks at 1.2 m and 1.6 m. What should the diagonal be? (2.0 m)
2. Hypotenuse 1,000 mm, one leg 600 mm. Find the other leg. (√(1,000,000 − 360,000) = 800 mm)
3. A frame's diagonals measure 3,605 mm and 3,598 mm. Is it square? (No, they differ by 7 mm)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- legs, hypotenuse and the Pythagorean theorem
- adding squares to find the hypotenuse and subtracting to find a leg
- the 3-4-5 check and its multiples
- squaring a frame with its diagonals, and which corners to push
- telling a crew what to adjust
