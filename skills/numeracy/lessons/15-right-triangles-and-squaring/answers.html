Right triangles, the 3-4-5 check and squaring a layout

Use the Pythagorean theorem to find a missing side, check a corner with 3-4-5, and square a frame or a foundation layout with its diagonals.

Task 1 (level 1-2)

a) √(600² + 800²) = √(360,000 + 640,000) = √1,000,000 = 1,000 mm b) √(1,500² + 900²) = √(2,250,000 + 810,000) = √3,060,000 = 1,749.29 mm c) √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 in

Task 2 (level 2)

a) √(1,300² − 500²) = √(1,690,000 − 250,000) = √1,440,000 = 1,200 mm b) √(2.5² − 1.8²) = √(6.25 − 3.24) = √3.01 = 1.73 m c) The brace is the hypotenuse. √(2,000² − 1,350²) = √(4,000,000 − 1,822,500) = √2,177,500 = 1,475.64 mm. Check: it is shorter than the 2,000 mm brace.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 3-4-5 times 100: 500 mm b) 3-4-5 times 3: 15 ft c) 3-4-5 times 300, so it should be 1,500 mm. 1,494 mm is 6 mm short, so the corner is closed to less than 90°. d) 3-4-5 times 0.4: 2.0 m e) 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169, and 13² = 169. Yes, they make a right triangle.

Task 4 (level 2-3)

a) √(4,800² + 3,600²) = √36,000,000 = 6,000 mm. It is 3-4-5 times 1,200. b) No. The diagonals differ by 2,020 − 2,013 = 7 mm. Push the two corners at the ends of the 2,020 mm diagonal toward each other until both diagonals match. c) √(36² + 80²) = √(1,296 + 6,400) = √7,696 = 87.727 in. 0.727 × 16 = 11.63, which rounds to 12 sixteenths = 3/4. 87 3/4 in

Task 5 (level 2-3)

Legs in inches: 24 in and 15 in. √(576 + 225) = √801 = 28.302 in. 0.302 × 16 = 4.83, which rounds to 5. 28 5/16 in = 2 ft 4 5/16 in

Task 6 (level 3)

a) 3 × 1,200 = 3,600 mm along one side and 4 × 1,200 = 4,800 mm along the other. The marks should be 5 × 1,200 = 6,000 mm apart. b) √(9,600² + 6,000²) = √128,160,000 = 11,320.78, so 11,321 mm c) No. The diagonals differ by 11,332 − 11,309 = 23 mm. d) Open task. A good note says to push the two corners at the ends of the 11,332 mm diagonal toward each other, and that the layout is square when both diagonals read 11,321 mm and the opposite sides still measure 9,600 mm and 6,000 mm. It is short and uses the numbers.

Check yourself 3

6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10². Yes. It is 3-4-5 times 2.

Check yourself 4

Check yourself 5

3-4-5 times 2 is 6-8-10. The same multiple must be used on all three sides. 3, 4 and 10 do not fit a² + b² = c²: 9 + 16 = 25, not 100.
