What a machine or crew costs per hour

Work out the owning and operating cost of a machine per hour, add the payroll costs on top of a wage, and build a charge-out rate for a crew.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Session: Numeracy lesson 26 of 34. Total length: 90 minutes.

Before the session: learners need a calculator. Run this lesson after lesson 22 (markup and margin). Lesson 1 (pay stubs) shows CPP and EI from the worker's side, and this lesson shows the employer's side. The burden percents in the lesson are job data for a fictional company. If learners ask for the real figures, show the CRA CPP and EI rate pages and the WorkSafeBC rate search, and point out that the rates change each year and by industry.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "An apprentice earns $28 an hour. What does that hour cost the company?" Take answers. Then ask: "A loader is paid for and sits in the yard all week. Is it costing the company anything?" Say: "Today you find out what an hour of a machine and an hour of a crew really cost, and why the price on a quote is higher than the wage." Tell learners this is session 26 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the price, salvage value, life, fixed costs, fuel use, wage and burden in a cost sheet (Identify the mathematical information)
  • connect owning cost, operating cost, wage, burden and markup to one hourly rate (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
  • calculate depreciation, fixed and operating cost per hour, crew cost with burden, and a charge-out rate (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
  • judge how hours of use change the cost per hour, and compare owning with renting (Interpret and evaluate the information)
  • report hourly costs to an owner (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. What is $62,400 ÷ 6,000? ($10.40)
  2. What is $70 × 1.15? ($80.50)

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. A machine costs $50,000, has a salvage value of $15,000 and lasts 7,000 hours. What is the depreciation per hour? ($35,000 ÷ 7,000 = $5.00)
  2. Yearly fixed costs are $4,500 and the machine works 900 hours a year. What is the fixed cost per hour? ($5.00)
  3. A worker earns $40 an hour and the burden is 18%. What does an hour cost? ($47.20)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • owning cost: depreciation per hour and yearly fixed costs per hour
  • operating cost: fuel, tires or tracks, and repairs
  • why an idle machine costs more per hour
  • labour burden: vacation pay, the employer's CPP and EI, and WorkSafeBC premiums
  • a charge-out rate as cost per hour plus a markup
