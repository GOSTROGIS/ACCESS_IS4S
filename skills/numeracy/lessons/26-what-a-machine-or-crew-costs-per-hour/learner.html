What a machine or crew costs per hour

Work out the owning and operating cost of a machine per hour, add the payroll costs on top of a wage, and build a charge-out rate for a crew.

Why this matters on the job

An estimator prices work by the hour: so many hours of a loader, so many hours of a crew. A machine costs money even when it sits in the yard. A worker costs the company more than the wage on the pay stub. If the hourly cost is too low, every job loses money and nobody can see why. This lesson builds the hourly cost step by step.

Key ideas

Owning cost is what a machine costs just to own.

  • Depreciation per hour = (purchase price − salvage value) ÷ hours of life. The salvage value is what the machine is worth when it is sold or traded in.
  • Yearly fixed costs per hour = (insurance + interest on the money + licence + storage) ÷ hours used per year.

Operating cost is what a machine costs to run, per hour.

  • Fuel = litres per hour × price per litre
  • Tires or tracks = cost of a set ÷ hours they last
  • Repairs and maintenance, from the company's records

Machine cost per hour = owning cost + operating cost. The operator is costed separately, as labour.

A machine that sits costs more per hour. The yearly fixed costs are the same whether the machine works 1,200 hours or 600 hours. Fewer hours means each hour carries more of them.

Labour burden. On top of the wage, an employer pays:

  • vacation pay: in BC, at least 4% of total wages after 5 calendar days of work (Employment Standards Act, section 58)
  • CPP: the employer pays the same amount as the employee
  • EI: the employer pays 1.4 times the employee's premium
  • WorkSafeBC premiums: the company's premium rate × its assessable payroll. The rate depends on the industry (the classification unit). The premium is paid by the business.

A company adds these up as a burden percent on the wage. The CPP and EI amounts depend on the year's rates and maximums, and the WorkSafeBC rate depends on the company, so in this lesson they are given as job data.

Charge-out rate. The company adds overhead and profit on top of the hourly cost, as a markup (lesson 22).

charge-out rate = cost per hour × (1 + markup)

Worked example

Granite Ridge Excavating (a fictional company) owns a compact track loader.

Data Value
Purchase price $96,000.00
Salvage value 35% of the price
Life 6,000 hours
Hours used a year 1,000 hours
Insurance, interest and storage $7,800.00 a year
Fuel 6 L an hour at $1.85 a litre
Tracks $3,600.00 a set, lasts 1,800 hours
Repairs and maintenance $4.50 an hour

Owning cost

  1. Salvage value: $96,000.00 × 0.35 = $33,600.00
  2. Depreciation: ($96,000.00 − $33,600.00) ÷ 6,000 = $62,400.00 ÷ 6,000 = $10.40 an hour
  3. Fixed costs: $7,800.00 ÷ 1,000 = $7.80 an hour
  4. Owning cost: $10.40 + $7.80 = $18.20 an hour

Operating cost

  1. Fuel: 6 × $1.85 = $11.10 an hour
  2. Tracks: $3,600.00 ÷ 1,800 = $2.00 an hour
  3. Operating cost: $11.10 + $2.00 + $4.50 = $17.60 an hour

Machine cost per hour: $18.20 + $17.60 = $35.80 an hour, without the operator.

Crew cost per hour. A crew of a carpenter at $42.00 an hour and an apprentice at $28.00 an hour. On this payroll the burden is 15%: 4% vacation pay, 8.2% employer CPP and EI, and 2.8% WorkSafeBC.

  1. Wages: $42.00 + $28.00 = $70.00 an hour
  2. Crew cost: $70.00 × 1.15 = $80.50 an hour
  3. Charge-out with a 25% markup: $80.50 × 1.25 = $100.625, which rounds to $100.63 an hour

Practice

Task 1 (level 1-2). A mini excavator costs $58,000.00. Its salvage value is 30% of the price. It lasts 5,000 hours.

a) What is the salvage value? b) What is the depreciation per hour?

Task 2 (level 2). The mini excavator's insurance, interest and storage come to $6,000.00 a year.

a) What is the fixed cost per hour if it works 1,200 hours a year? b) What if it works only 600 hours a year? c) Explain in one sentence why the answers are different.

Task 3 (level 2). A skid steer burns 4.5 L of fuel an hour at $1.90 a litre. A set of tires costs $1,440.00 and lasts 1,200 hours. Repairs run $3.25 an hour. Find the operating cost per hour.

Task 4 (level 2-3). Back to the mini excavator in Tasks 1 and 2, working 1,200 hours a year. It burns 5 L an hour at $1.90 a litre. Tracks cost $2,400.00 and last 1,500 hours. Repairs run $3.50 an hour.

a) Find the operating cost per hour. b) Find the machine cost per hour (owning plus operating). c) For this fictional comparison, a rental yard charges $380.00 for an 8-hour day, including fuel, maintenance and all machine charges but excluding the operator. What is that per hour? d) Is owning cheaper per hour than renting when the machine works 1,200 hours a year? Name one thing that could change the answer.

Task 5 (level 2). A pipefitter apprentice earns $36.00 an hour. On this payroll the burden is 4% vacation pay, 8.2% employer CPP and EI, and 2.8% WorkSafeBC.

a) Find each burden amount per hour, to the cent. b) What is the total burden percent? c) What does one hour of this apprentice cost the company?

Task 6 (level 3). Granite Ridge prices a 6-hour job: the loader from the worked example and the two-person crew. Use $35.80 an hour for the loader and $80.50 an hour for the crew. The company adds a 25% markup to both.

a) Find the cost of the loader and of the crew for 6 hours, and the total cost. b) Find the price before GST. c) Add 5% GST. What is the total?

Task 7 (level 3). Spot the mistake. A new estimator prices a carpenter at $42.00 an hour × 1.25 markup = $52.50 an hour.

a) What did the estimator leave out? b) With the 15% burden, what does the carpenter cost the company per hour? c) What should the charge-out rate be with a 25% markup? How much was lost on each hour?

Task 8 (level 3). Write two or three sentences to the owner of Granite Ridge. Say what the loader costs per hour, what the crew costs per hour, and why the loader would cost more per hour if it worked fewer hours a year.

Check yourself

  1. A machine costs $40,000.00, is worth $10,000.00 at the end, and lasts 5,000 hours. What is the depreciation per hour?
  2. A machine burns 8 L an hour at $2.00 a litre. What is the fuel cost per hour?
  3. What does the employer pay for EI, compared with the employee?
  4. In BC, what is the lowest vacation pay after 5 calendar days of work?
  5. A worker earns $30.00 an hour and the burden is 20%. What does an hour cost the company?
