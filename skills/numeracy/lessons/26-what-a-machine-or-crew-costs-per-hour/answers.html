What a machine or crew costs per hour

Work out the owning and operating cost of a machine per hour, add the payroll costs on top of a wage, and build a charge-out rate for a crew.

  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Task 1 (level 1-2)

a) $58,000.00 × 0.30 = $17,400.00 b) ($58,000.00 − $17,400.00) ÷ 5,000 = $40,600.00 ÷ 5,000 = $8.12 an hour

Task 2 (level 2)

a) $6,000.00 ÷ 1,200 = $5.00 an hour b) $6,000.00 ÷ 600 = $10.00 an hour c) The yearly costs stay the same, so when the machine works half as many hours, each hour carries twice as much of them.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • Fuel: 4.5 × $1.90 = $8.55 an hour
  • Tires: $1,440.00 ÷ 1,200 = $1.20 an hour
  • Repairs: $3.25 an hour
  • Operating cost: $8.55 + $1.20 + $3.25 = $13.00 an hour

Task 4 (level 2-3)

a) Fuel: 5 × $1.90 = $9.50. Tracks: $2,400.00 ÷ 1,500 = $1.60. Repairs: $3.50. Operating cost: $9.50 + $1.60 + $3.50 = $14.60 an hour. b) Owning cost: $8.12 + $5.00 = $13.12. Machine cost: $13.12 + $14.60 = $27.72 an hour. c) $380.00 ÷ 8 = $47.50 an hour d) Yes. $27.72 is less than $47.50 per hour of work. A good answer names one thing that could change it, for example:

  • the machine works far fewer hours a year, so the fixed cost per hour goes up (at 600 hours it is $10.00, not $5.00)
  • a big repair, or a lower salvage value than planned
  • the crew only needs the machine a few days a year, so the rental is only paid on those days

Task 5 (level 2)

a) - Vacation pay: $36.00 × 0.04 = $1.44 - Employer CPP and EI: $36.00 × 0.082 = $2.952, which rounds to $2.95 - WorkSafeBC: $36.00 × 0.028 = $1.008, which rounds to $1.01

b) 4% + 8.2% + 2.8% = 15% c) $36.00 + $1.44 + $2.95 + $1.01 = $41.40. Check: $36.00 × 1.15 = $41.40.

Task 6 (level 3)

a) Loader: 6 × $35.80 = $214.80. Crew: 6 × $80.50 = $483.00. Total cost: $214.80 + $483.00 = $697.80. b) $697.80 × 1.25 = $872.25 c) GST: $872.25 × 0.05 = $43.6125, which rounds to $43.61. Total: $872.25 + $43.61 = $915.86.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) The labour burden: vacation pay, the employer's CPP and EI, and WorkSafeBC. The markup was put on the bare wage. b) $42.00 × 1.15 = $48.30 an hour c) $48.30 × 1.25 = $60.375, which rounds to $60.38 an hour. $60.38 − $52.50 = $7.88 lost on each hour. At $52.50 the company makes only $52.50 − $48.30 = $4.20 an hour over cost, not the 25% it planned.

Task 8 (level 3)

Open task. A good answer:

  • gives the loader cost of $35.80 an hour, without the operator
  • gives the crew cost of $80.50 an hour, including the 15% burden, and may give the $100.63 charge-out rate
  • explains that the $7,800.00 of yearly fixed costs is spread over the hours worked, so fewer hours means more cost per hour
