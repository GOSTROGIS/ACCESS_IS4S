Reading and making charts of trade wages

Use real Job Bank wage data for BC trades to read tables, make a fair bar chart, read pie and line graphs, and say what the data can and cannot show.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the mathematical information; Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information; Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question; Interpret and evaluate the information; Share the mathematical information, results and implications

Why this matters on the job

Choosing a trade, a region to work in or a job offer often comes down to numbers in a table or a chart. Job Bank, run by the Government of Canada, publishes wages for every trade in every region of BC. Charts also show up at work: in safety bulletins, production reports and union newsletters. Reading them well, and making your own fairly, helps you and your crew make decisions on good information.

Key ideas

Read the labels first. Before reading any number, find the title, the units (dollars per hour, percent, hours), the date and the source. Read any notes under the table or chart. They often change what the numbers mean.

Low, median and high wages. On Job Bank:

  • The low wage is, in most cases, the 10th percentile: 10% of workers earn that much or less.
  • The median wage is the middle value when wages are ranked from lowest to highest. Job Bank uses it as the prevailing wage.
  • The high wage is, in most cases, the 90th percentile: 90% of workers earn that much or less.

Job Bank shows wages paid to employees, not wages offered in job postings.

Three common charts.

  • A bar chart compares amounts across categories. The value axis should start at zero, so the length of each bar matches its value.
  • A line graph shows how a value changes over time.
  • A pie chart shows parts of one whole. All the slices add up to 100%, which is 360°.

Percent difference depends on the whole. "A is what percent more than B?" divides by B. "B is what percent less than A?" divides by A. The two answers are different.

Worked example

Job Bank lists the BC median wage for plumbers as $32.00 an hour and for welders and related machine operators as $35.00 an hour.

  • Difference: $35.00 − $32.00 = $3.00 an hour
  • The welder median is more than the plumber median by $3.00 ÷ $32.00 × 100 = 9.38%

Task 1 (level 1). Use Document A. What is the median wage for carpenters in BC? When were the wages updated? What is the reference period and source for the BC row?

Task 2 (level 2). List the eight BC regions in order of median wage, from highest to lowest. Which regions tie?

Task 3 (level 2). For each region, work out the range: high wage − low wage. Which region has the widest range? Which has the narrowest?

Task 4 (level 2). Make a bar chart of the median wage in each of the eight regions. Start the dollar axis at $0 and go to $35 in steps of $5. Give it a title, label both axes, and write the source and date under the chart. Use paper and a ruler, or a spreadsheet.

Task 5 (level 3). Use Document B.

a) How much higher is the median for millwrights and industrial mechanics than for carpenters, in dollars per hour? b) The millwright median is what percent more than the carpenter median? c) The carpenter median is what percent less than the millwright median? d) Why are the answers to b) and c) different?

Task 6 (level 2). Suppose a person works 40 hours a week for 50 weeks a year at the median wage. Estimate the yearly pay for a carpenter and for a millwright in BC. Write down the assumption your estimate depends on.

Task 7 (level 3). Read the reference periods and sources in Document A.

a) Which region's figure comes from a model for 2024 rather than a survey for 2023-2024? b) Which region's figure comes from Employment Insurance survey data? c) Nechako and North Coast show exactly the same three numbers. Does the table tell you why? d) Write one sentence that the table does support, and one sentence that it does not support.

Task 8 (level 3). A fictional poster shows Northeast ($30.00) and Nechako ($33.50) as bars on an axis that starts at $29.00 instead of $0.

a) On that poster, how many times taller does the Nechako bar look than the Northeast bar? (Measure each bar from $29.00.) b) How many times larger is $33.50 than $30.00, really? Round to two decimal places. c) Explain in one sentence why the poster is misleading.

Task 9 (level 2). Use Document C to plan a pie chart. Find each kind of work as a fraction of 40 hours, a percent and an angle in degrees (percent × 3.6). Check that the percents add up to 100 and the angles to 360.

Task 10 (level 2). Use Document D. Draw a line graph with the year on the horizontal axis and the hourly rate on the vertical axis. By how much does the rate go up each year? By what percent did it go up from year 1 to year 4? Can the graph tell you what the rate will be in year 5? Explain.

Check yourself

  1. On Job Bank, what does the median wage mean?
  2. A bar chart's value axis starts at $25 instead of $0. What problem can that cause?
  3. A pie chart slice is 25%. How many degrees is it?
  4. A wage goes from $30 to $33. What is the percent increase?
  5. Name two things to read on a chart before you read any of its numbers.
