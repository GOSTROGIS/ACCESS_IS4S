# Reading and making charts of trade wages

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 6 of 8. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "If you could work your trade anywhere in BC, where would you earn the most?" Collect a few guesses with reasons. Tell learners that today they will answer the question with the Government of Canada's own wage data, and then check whether the data can really answer it.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the title, units, date, source and notes of a table or chart (Identify the mathematical information)
- link rows, columns and notes to compare regions and trades (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- calculate ranges, differences, percent differences and pie chart angles, and draw a bar chart and a line graph (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- judge what the data supports and spot a misleading chart (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- share a finding in a clear chart with a title, labels and source (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. Put these in order from highest to lowest: 31.50, 33.21, 32.00, 30.00. (33.21, 32.00, 31.50, 30.00)
2. What is 4 ÷ 40 as a percent? (10%)

## Post-assessment (10 min)

1. Use Document B. What is the range of electrician wages in BC? ($47.49 − $20.00 = $27.49)
2. The electrician median is what percent more than the carpenter median? (($34.00 − $32.00) ÷ $32.00 × 100 = 6.25%)
3. Give one reason why the Cariboo median cannot be compared directly with the Kootenay median. (different reference period, 2024 compared with 2023-2024, and a model estimate compared with a survey)

Learners volunteer answers in turns and point to where on the documents the numbers came from. Make sure everyone participates.

## Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

- reading titles, units, dates, sources and notes before the numbers
- what low, median and high wages mean on Job Bank
- making a fair bar chart with an axis that starts at zero
- percent differences and why the whole matters
- planning a pie chart and drawing a line graph
- what a table can and cannot support
