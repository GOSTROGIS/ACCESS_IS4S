Reading and making charts of trade wages

Use real Job Bank wage data for BC trades to read tables, make a fair bar chart, read pie and line graphs, and say what the data can and cannot show.

Task 4 (level 2)

A good chart:

  • has a title such as "Median hourly wage for carpenters by BC region"
  • has a value axis from $0 to $35 in equal $5 steps, labelled "Dollars per hour"
  • has one bar for each of the eight regions, labelled with the region name
  • has bar heights that match Document A: 33.21, 32.00, 31.50, 33.50, 33.50, 30.00, 31.00, 32.00
  • notes the source under the chart: Job Bank, updated November 19, 2025

Because the axis starts at $0, the bars look close in height. That is a true picture: the medians are within $3.50 of each other.

Task 5 (level 3)

a) $42.00 − $32.00 = $10.00 an hour b) $10.00 ÷ $32.00 × 100 = 31.25% more c) $10.00 ÷ $42.00 × 100 = 23.81% less d) The difference is the same $10.00, but b) compares it with the carpenter wage and c) compares it with the millwright wage. A different whole gives a different percent.

HP 10bII+: b) 32, orange shift, %CHG, 42 = shows 31.25. c) 42, orange shift, %CHG, 32 = shows −23.81. The minus sign means "less".

Task 6 (level 2)

Hours in a year: 40 × 50 = 2,000.

  • Carpenter: 2,000 × $32.00 = $64,000
  • Millwright: 2,000 × $42.00 = $84,000

Assumption: the person works exactly 40 hours a week for 50 weeks, with no overtime and no time off without pay, at exactly the median wage. Real yearly pay can be higher or lower.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Cariboo Region (Small Area Estimation model, 2024). b) Northeast Region. c) No. The table does not say why. A good answer says the reason cannot be found from this table and should not be guessed. d) Supported, for example: "In 2023-2024, the median carpenter wage in the Kootenay Region was the same as the BC median, $32.00." Not supported, for example: "Carpenters earn the most in Nechako," because the regional medians are close, come from different sources and periods, and the table does not show a job's hours, overtime or cost of living.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Northeast bar: 30.00 − 29.00 = 1.00. Nechako bar: 33.50 − 29.00 = 4.50. The Nechako bar looks 4.5 times as tall. b) $33.50 ÷ $30.00 = 1.12 times (1.1167 before rounding). c) Starting the axis above zero makes a 12% difference look like a difference of 4.5 times.

Task 10 (level 2)

  • A good graph has year 1 to 4 on the horizontal axis, hourly rate on the vertical axis, four points joined in order, a title, labels and a note that the data are fictional.
  • Each year the rate goes up by $3.20 ($25.60 − $22.40, and so on).
  • From year 1 to year 4: ($32.00 − $22.40) ÷ $22.40 × 100 = 42.86%
  • The graph cannot tell you year 5. The table stops at year 4, and nothing says the pattern continues. A guess of $35.20 would be an assumption, not a fact from the data.
