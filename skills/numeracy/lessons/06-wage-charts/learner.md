# Reading and making charts of trade wages

## Why this matters on the job

Choosing a trade, a region to work in or a job offer often comes down to numbers in a table or a chart. Job Bank, run by the Government of Canada, publishes wages for every trade in every region of BC. Charts also show up at work: in safety bulletins, production reports and union newsletters. Reading them well, and making your own fairly, helps you and your crew make decisions on good information.

## Key ideas

**Read the labels first.** Before reading any number, find the title, the units (dollars per hour, percent, hours), the date and the source. Read any notes under the table or chart. They often change what the numbers mean.

**Low, median and high wages.** On Job Bank:

- The **low wage** is, in most cases, the 10th percentile: 10% of workers earn that much or less.
- The **median wage** is the middle value when wages are ranked from lowest to highest. Job Bank uses it as the prevailing wage.
- The **high wage** is, in most cases, the 90th percentile: 90% of workers earn that much or less.

Job Bank shows wages **paid** to employees, not wages offered in job postings.

**Three common charts.**

- A **bar chart** compares amounts across categories. The value axis should start at zero, so the length of each bar matches its value.
- A **line graph** shows how a value changes over time.
- A **pie chart** shows parts of one whole. All the slices add up to 100%, which is 360°.

**Percent difference depends on the whole.** "A is what percent more than B?" divides by B. "B is what percent less than A?" divides by A. The two answers are different.

### Worked example

Job Bank lists the BC median wage for plumbers as $32.00 an hour and for welders and related machine operators as $35.00 an hour.

- Difference: $35.00 − $32.00 = $3.00 an hour
- The welder median is more than the plumber median by $3.00 ÷ $32.00 × 100 = 9.38%
