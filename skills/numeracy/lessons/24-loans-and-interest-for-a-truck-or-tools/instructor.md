# Loans and interest for a truck or tools

**Session:** Numeracy lesson 24 of 34. **Total length:** 90 minutes.

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator. Bring HP 10bII+ calculators for Task 8 if the class has them. Run this lesson after lesson 3 (credit card interest), which introduces one month of interest and the TVM keys. Open the two FCAC pages listed under Sources on the projector.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Write two offers on the board for the same $25,000 truck loan: "$388 a month" and "$609 a month". Ask: "Which is the better deal?" Take a show of hands. Say: "By the end of today you will know what each one really costs, and it is not what the payment says." Tell learners this is session 24 of the Numeracy set, and it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the principal, rate, term, payment and fees in a loan offer (Identify the mathematical information)
- connect each payment to the interest and principal it pays, and the term to the total cost (Make connections between related pieces of mathematical information)
- calculate simple interest, one month of interest, compound interest and the total cost of a loan (Apply mathematical operations and tools you will need to answer the question)
- compare loan offers on total cost, not only on the payment (Interpret and evaluate the information)
- explain the cost of a loan to someone else in a few sentences (Share the mathematical information, results and implications)

This lesson presents facts and calculations. It does not recommend a lender or tell learners whether to borrow.

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. What is 8% of $1,000? ($80)
2. What is $500 × 60? ($30,000)

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. What is the simple interest on $4,000 at 7% a year for 2 years? ($4,000 × 0.07 × 2 = $560)
2. A loan balance is $18,000 at 9% a year. What is one month of interest? ($18,000 × 0.09 ÷ 12 = $135)
3. A loan of $15,000 is paid with 60 payments of $300. What is the interest paid? ($300 × 60 = $18,000, less $15,000 = $3,000)

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- simple interest on the principal
- how each loan payment pays the month's interest first and the rest comes off the balance
- compound interest when interest is added to the balance
- total paid, interest paid and the cost of borrowing
- why a longer term means a lower payment but more interest
- what a lender must disclose and the 35% legal limit FCAC describes
