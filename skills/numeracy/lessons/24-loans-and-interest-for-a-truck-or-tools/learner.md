# Loans and interest for a truck or tools

## Why this matters on the job

Many tradespeople borrow to buy a work truck, a trailer or a full set of tools. The dealer or bank shows a monthly payment, and a lower payment looks like a better deal. It often is not. The total you pay back depends on the interest rate, the length of the loan and any fees. This lesson shows you how to work out the real cost before you sign.

## Key ideas

**Principal** is the amount you borrow. **Interest** is what the lender charges for the loan. The **term** is how long you have to pay it back.

**Simple interest** is charged on the principal only:

interest = principal × yearly rate × time in years

**Interest on a loan you pay monthly.** Each month the lender charges one month of interest on what you still owe:

month's interest = balance × yearly rate ÷ 12

Your payment covers that interest first. The rest of the payment comes off the balance (the principal). Early in the loan, more of each payment goes to interest. As the balance drops, more goes to principal.

**Compound interest** is interest charged on interest. If you make no payments, each month's interest is added to the balance, and the next month's interest is charged on the bigger balance.

**Total cost of a loan.**

- total paid = monthly payment × number of payments (the last payment may differ by a few cents)
- interest paid = total paid − amount borrowed
- cost of borrowing = interest + any other charges, such as an admin fee

**Longer is not cheaper.** The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) says that the longer you take to pay off a loan, the more expensive it is. A longer term gives a lower payment but more interest in total. FCAC calls car loans of 72 months (6 years) or more long-term loans.

**What the lender must tell you.** For a personal loan from a federally regulated lender, such as a bank, FCAC says the lender must disclose the annual interest rate, the annual percentage rate (APR) if it is different, the payments, and the cost of borrowing. FCAC also says that by law lenders may not charge more than 35% interest a year.

### Worked example

Sam is buying a used work truck for $28,000.00. Sam puts $3,000.00 down and borrows the rest at 7.9% a year for 60 months. The lender's payment is $505.71 a month.

1. Loan: $28,000.00 − $3,000.00 = $25,000.00
2. First month's interest: $25,000.00 × 0.079 ÷ 12 = $164.58
3. Principal paid in month 1: $505.71 − $164.58 = $341.13
4. Balance after month 1: $25,000.00 − $341.13 = $24,658.87
5. Total paid over the loan: $505.71 × 60 = $30,342.60
6. Interest paid: $30,342.60 − $25,000.00 = **$5,342.60**
7. The truck really costs: $28,000.00 + $5,342.60 = **$33,342.60**
