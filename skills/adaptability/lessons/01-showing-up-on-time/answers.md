# Showing up and managing your time on a job site

## Task 1 (level 1)

**Answer:** 6:15 a.m.

Steps:

- Meeting at 7:15, minus 15 minutes to park and sign in = 7:00.
- 7:00 minus the 35-minute drive = 6:25.
- 6:25 minus the 10-minute buffer = 6:15.

## Task 2 (level 1)

Open answer. A good text:

- is sent before 6:00 a.m., the time Rob asked for
- says who is writing (name, and crew if Rob has several)
- says what is happening in a few words ("I'm sick with a fever")
- says it is for today's shift
- says when the writer will update Rob or expects to return

Example: "Hi Rob, it's Dale from the Unit 4 crew. I woke up with a fever and can't work today. I'll text you by 5 p.m. to say if I'll be in tomorrow."

The text does not need private medical details.

## Task 3 (level 2)

1. **A.** Urgent (20 minutes) and important (crane access and safety).
2. **B.** Important (needed to work) but not urgent yet. Book the course now so it does not become urgent.
3. **D.** Not urgent and not important at work. Answer at break.
4. **B, close to A.** Important for the apprenticeship, and becoming urgent because seats fill. Accept A with a good reason.
5. **A or C.** It is due today. Accept A (others may need the tool) or C (a quick task).
6. **C.** Feels urgent but is not important during work. Check at break.
7. **B.** Important. Plan study time before Thursday.

Accept different boxes when the learner gives a reason that fits the definitions.

## Task 4 (level 2)

a. **8:45 p.m.** The alarm is at 4:45 a.m. Count back 8 hours: 4:45 a.m. − 8 h = 8:45 p.m.

b. Any four sensible actions, for example:

- pack lunch and water
- lay out boots, vest, hard hat and glasses
- charge the phone
- check the bus schedule or the fuel gauge
- set a second alarm
- put bus pass, keys and wallet in one place

## Task 5 (level 2)

Open answer. Good agreements describe something people can see or hear, such as "Arrive by 8:55 so we start at 9:00", "One person speaks at a time" or "What is shared in circle stays in circle". Improve a vague agreement such as "Be respectful" by asking "What does that look like?"

## Check yourself

1. The start time is fixed. Counting back from it shows when each step must happen and leaves room for a buffer.
2. Who you are, what is happening, and when you will arrive, return or update the supervisor.
3. Examples: renewing a ticket before it expires, registering for technical training, studying for an exam that is a week away, booking a medical appointment.
4. Any two of these: your supervisor's name and contact, your rights and responsibilities (including reporting unsafe conditions and the right to refuse unsafe work), the workplace health and safety rules, protective equipment, first aid and how to report an injury.
