# Showing up and managing your time on a job site

All people and companies in this lesson are fictional.

## Why this matters on the job

On a BC construction site the day often starts with a toolbox talk, a crane lift or a concrete truck booked for a set time. When one person is late, the crew may wait or start short-handed. Showing up on time, and calling early when you cannot, is one of the first things a foreman notices about a new apprentice. It tells them they can count on you.

## Key ideas

### Work back from the start time

Do not plan from the time you wake up. Plan from the time you must be ready to work, and count backwards.

1. Start with the time you must be in place (for example, the toolbox talk).
2. Subtract the time to get your gear on and sign in.
3. Subtract the walk from the bus stop or parking.
4. Subtract the travel time.
5. Subtract a buffer for traffic, a late bus or a slow gate.

**Worked example.** Dale starts at Riverbend Framing Ltd. The toolbox talk is at 6:50 a.m. He needs 10 minutes to sign in and put on his boots, vest and hard hat. The walk from the bus stop is 12 minutes. The bus ride is 45 minutes. Buses leave his stop at 5:30, 5:50 and 6:10.

- Ready by 6:50, so he must reach the gate by 6:50 − 10 min = 6:40.
- Off the bus by 6:40 − 12 min = 6:28.
- On the bus by 6:28 − 45 min = 5:43.
- The 5:50 bus is too late. He takes the 5:30 bus.
- The 5:30 bus gets him off at 6:15, to the gate at 6:27 and ready at 6:37. That leaves 13 minutes of buffer before the talk.

### Tell your supervisor early

Plans change: a sick child, a flat tire, a fever. What matters is that your supervisor hears it from you, before your shift, in the way your employer asks (a call, a text or an app). A good message says who you are, what is happening, and when you expect to be back or arrive.

In BC, after 90 days with an employer, the Employment Standards Act gives employees up to 5 paid sick days and 3 unpaid sick days each calendar year. Your employer can ask for reasonably sufficient proof that you were ill or injured. For the first two health-related absences of 5 days or less in a calendar year, your employer cannot ask for a note from a doctor or nurse. Some workplaces have union agreements with other rules, so read yours.

### Know what your first day should cover

In BC, your employer must give a young or new worker a health and safety orientation before the work starts. It must include your supervisor's name and contact information, your rights and responsibilities (including reporting unsafe conditions and the right to refuse unsafe work) and the workplace health and safety rules. WorkSafeBC's checklist for employers also includes protective equipment, first aid and how to report an injury. If you are not told these things, ask.

### Urgent and important are not the same

- **Urgent** means it has to be dealt with soon.
- **Important** means it matters a lot for your work, safety or goals.

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | A: Do it now. | B: Plan a time for it. |
| **Not important** | C: Deal with it fast or pass it on. | D: Leave it for later or drop it. |

Many people spend their days in boxes A and C and never get to box B. Box B is where goals like your tickets, your technical training and your health live. Planning time for box B stops those tasks from turning into emergencies later.

### Worked example: plan B when the bus is cancelled

Marie is a first-year electrical apprentice at Coastline Electrical Contractors. Her start time is 7:00 a.m. She needs 15 minutes for coffee and to put on her gear at the site. The bus trip, door to door, takes 45 minutes. She wants a 10-minute buffer.

**Step 1. Work back from the start.**

- Ready to work at 7:00.
- At the gate by 7:00 − 15 min = 6:45.
- Leave home by 6:45 − 45 min = 6:00.
- Take off the buffer: leave home at 6:00 − 10 min = 5:50.

**Step 2. Set the alarm.** Marie needs 40 minutes to get up, eat and get dressed. 5:50 − 40 min = 5:10. Her alarm is set for 5:10.

**Step 3. The change.** At 5:40 her transit app says her bus is cancelled. The next one means she leaves home 20 minutes later, at 6:10.

- At the gate: 6:10 + 45 min = 6:55.
- Ready: 6:55 + 15 min = 7:10.
- The 10-minute buffer covers half of the 20-minute delay. She would still be 10 minutes late.

**Step 4. Look for a better option.** Her cousin can drive her. The drive takes 25 minutes, and they can leave at 6:00.

- At the gate: 6:00 + 25 min = 6:25.
- Ready: 6:25 + 15 min = 6:40, which is 20 minutes early.

**Step 5. Tell the supervisor early anyway.** At 5:45 Marie texts her foreman: "Hi Lou, it's Marie. My 5:50 bus is cancelled. My cousin is driving me and I'll be on time. If that falls through I'll take the next bus and be about 10 minutes late, and I'll text you again." The foreman knows the plan before 6:00, whatever happens.
