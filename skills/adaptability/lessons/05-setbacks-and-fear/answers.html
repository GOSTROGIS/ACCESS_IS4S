Adaptability · Lesson 05 · Lesson 6: Learning strategies that work

Setbacks, fear of failure and trying again

Use fear as a signal, turn a failed attempt into a plan, and know how exam rewrites work for BC apprentices.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Persist and persevere; Seek self-improvement; Regulate your emotions when appropriate

Task 1 (level 1)

Open answer. Good rewrites name a skill small enough to practise. Examples:

  1. "I can't yet read sixteenths quickly on a tape. I'll practise ten measurements a day."
  2. "I run out of time on tests. I'll practise timed questions."
  3. "I don't know how to save and find files yet. I'll ask for a demo and practise."
  4. "I get nervous when watched. I'll practise the task with a classmate watching first."

A rewrite that keeps a whole-person label ("I'm just slow") needs another try.

Task 2 (level 1)

  1. H. Fear leads to a safe step.
  2. H. Fear leads to preparation.
  3. U. Fear blocks a safety question.
  4. U. Fear stops progress.
  5. H. Fear leads to staying safe and reporting a hazard.

Task 3 (level 2)

a. 30 × 5 = 150 minutes a week.

b. 150 × 4 = 600 minutes. 600 ÷ 60 = 10 hours.

c. Open answer. Examples: book the rewrite date now, use a practice exam under timed conditions, ask the instructor for drawing practice, check whether she needs exam accommodations, make flash cards for formulas.

Task 4 (level 2)

Open and private. A complete plan:

  • names one clear fear
  • names a person they have talked to, or plans to talk to
  • names a buddy
  • states a specific action and a date
  • lists small daily steps, such as "Monday: write three answers to common interview questions. Tuesday: practise them aloud with my buddy."

Task 5 (level 2)

a. Contact his Apprenticeship Advisor for guidance and approval.

b. $100 (it is a certification exam).

c. Proof of upgrading.

Check yourself

  1. Healthy fear gets you to prepare or act safely. Unhealthy fear freezes you or stops you from doing what you need to.
  2. A label judges the whole person and gives you nothing to work on. A specific skill tells you what to practise.
  3. Growth mindset messages alone make little or no difference for most students. Specific practice, feedback and support matter more.
  4. Feel it, get the facts, find one or two causes you can work on, make a plan, try again.
