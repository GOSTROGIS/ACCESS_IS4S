# Re-plan your day when the job changes

All people, companies and projects in this lesson are fictional.

## Why this matters on the job

A day on site is planned around things that are booked: a crane, a lift, a concrete truck, an inspector. When one of them falls through, the whole plan can stall. Some crews stand around and wait. Good crews spend ten minutes re-planning and keep working. A new apprentice who can say "the lift is down, so here is what I can do until it is back" saves the foreman a headache and shows they can be trusted with more.

Lesson 3 showed you how to sort a change into what changed, what stayed the same and what you still do not know. This lesson goes one step further. You will rebuild the day itself, minute by minute, and check that the new plan works.

## Key ideas

### A day plan as a bar chart

A **bar chart** of the day shows time from left to right. Each task is a bar. The longer the bar, the longer the task. You can see at a glance what happens when.

Read a bar chart in three steps:

1. Read the time scale along the bottom.
2. Find the start and end of each bar.
3. Subtract to get the length: end time minus start time.

Example: a bar from 9:45 to 12:00 is 2 hours 15 minutes, or 135 minutes.

### Fixed and movable

- **Fixed** items cannot move unless someone else agrees: the toolbox talk, breaks, a booked crane or lift, an inspection, a concrete pour.
- **Movable** items can shift to another time: cleaning up, prefab work at a bench, staging material, sorting fittings.

When a change hits, plan the fixed items first. Then fill the gaps with movable work.

### Is the task ready to start?

A task is only ready when all five are in place:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Material | Is it on site and in the right place? |
| Information | Do I have the right drawing, revision and instructions? |
| Permit | Is any permit needed, such as a hot work permit, and is it issued? |
| Area | Is the area clear and safe, with no other trade in the way? |
| Tools and equipment | Are the tools, lift or crane here and working? |

If even one answer is no, the task is not ready. Do not plan it into a gap.

1. **What is fixed now?** Mark the breaks, bookings and anything you cannot move.
2. **What can not happen?** Cross out every task that needs the thing that changed.
3. **What is ready to start?** Use the five checks.
4. **What matters most?** Safety first. Then work that other people are waiting on. Then work with a deadline.
5. **Tell and confirm.** Tell your foreman what changed, what you plan to do, and what will be late. Start once they agree.

Keep every part of the old plan that still works. A re-plan is not a fresh start.

At 7:10 a.m., near the end of the toolbox talk, the foreman, Mo, says: "The scissor lift is down. The repair tech will have it running by 1:00." Here is how Rae re-plans.

**Step 1. What is fixed now?** Toolbox talk 7:00 to 7:15. Coffee break 9:30 to 9:45. Lunch 12:00 to 12:30. The lift is back at 1:00. The shift ends at 3:30.

**Step 2. What can not happen?** Everything on the lift before 1:00:

- install hangers, 7:15 to 9:30 = 135 minutes
- hang copper, 9:45 to 12:00 = 135 minutes
- part of the afternoon copper, 12:30 to 1:00 = 30 minutes

That is 135 + 135 + 30 = 300 minutes of lift work that cannot happen this morning.

**Step 3. What is ready to start?** Rae checks three movable tasks.

| Task | Material | Information | Permit | Area | Tools | Ready? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prefab copper assemblies at the bench | yes | yes | none needed | yes | yes | yes |
| Stage hangers and fittings on Level 3 | yes | yes | none needed | yes | yes | yes |
| Pressure test prep | yes | yes | none needed | no, the pipe is not hung yet | yes | no |

**Step 4. What matters most?** The hangers must go in first, because the copper hangs from them. So when the lift returns at 1:00, hangers come first.

**Step 5. Tell and confirm.** At 7:15, right after the toolbox talk, Rae finds Mo and tells him her plan:

> "Mo, it's Rae. With the lift down I'll do prefab at the bench until 11:15, then stage the hangers and fittings on Level 3 before lunch. At 1:00 I'll install hangers until 3:15, then lock the lift and clean up. The copper and the test prep will move to Wednesday. Does that work?"

Mo agrees. Here is the new plan. The short blue block from 7:15 to 7:30 is time to talk to Mo and gather what she needs for the bench.

| Lock lift and clean up, 3:15 to 3:30 | 15 |
| **Total** | **510** |

The shift from 7:00 to 3:30 is 8 hours 30 minutes = 510 minutes. The new plan fills it exactly, with no gaps and no overlaps.

**What moves to Wednesday?** The old plan had 390 minutes of lift work: 135 + 135 + 120. The new plan has 135. So 390 − 135 = 255 minutes of copper hanging moves to Wednesday, plus the 60-minute test prep. That is 255 + 60 = 315 minutes, or 5 hours 15 minutes. Rae said so when she told Mo her plan, so he can plan Wednesday.
