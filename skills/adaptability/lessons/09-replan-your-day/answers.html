Re-plan your day when the job changes

Read a day plan as a bar chart, find what a change breaks, and build a new plan that keeps everything that still works.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Plan and prioritize; Persist and persevere; Demonstrate responsibility

Task 1 (level 1)

a. 135 minutes. 7:15 to 9:30 is 2 hours 15 minutes. 2 × 60 + 15 = 135.

b. 2:30 p.m.

c. 390 minutes. Install hangers 135 + hang copper 135 + hang copper 120 = 390. The afternoon copper bar runs 12:30 to 2:30, which is 120 minutes. Test prep does not need the lift.

Task 2 (level 1)

  1. F
  2. F (booked with the city)
  3. M
  4. F
  5. F (booked crane)
  6. M
  7. M

Accept a different answer with a good reason, for example "the blocking is needed before the 2:00 inspection, so it is fixed today."

Task 3 (level 2)

a. 450 minutes.

  • 7:15 to 9:30 = 135 minutes
  • 9:45 to 12:00 = 135 minutes
  • 12:30 to 3:30 = 180 minutes
  • 135 + 135 + 180 = 450

b. 7.5 hours. 450 ÷ 60 = 7.5.

c. The chart runs from 7:00 to 3:30. At one square for 15 minutes, that is 34 squares (510 ÷ 15 = 34). Check: toolbox talk 1 square, first framing bar 9 squares, break 1, second framing bar 9, lunch 2, afternoon framing 12. 1 + 9 + 1 + 9 + 2 + 12 = 34.

Task 4 (level 2)

Only C is ready now.

  • A fails the area check. Drywallers are in the area until 10:00. It becomes ready at 10:00 if the area is clear.
  • B fails the permit check. No hot work permit, no welding.
  • C passes all five checks.
  • D fails the tools and equipment check. The gauge is at the shop.

Task 5 (level 2)

a. 10:15 a.m. 8:00 + 2 h 15 min = 10:15.

b. 12:45 p.m. 10:15 + 2 h 30 min = 12:45.

c. Open answer. Reasonable plans include:

  • stagger lunch so a few workers eat at a time while the pour continues
  • the pour crew eats right after the pour, 12:45 to 1:15
  • tell the crew at the morning re-plan, not at noon, so people can bring food and plan

The plan must keep enough people on the concrete at all times.

Task 6 (level 2)

Open answer. A good text names who is writing, what changed, the new plan, what will be late and one question.

Example: "Hi Dana, it's Kai. Room 204 isn't drywalled yet, so I can't pull wire there this morning. I'll make up the device boxes for the third floor until it's ready. The pull in 204 will be about 3 hours behind. Do you want me on 204 this afternoon or on the third floor?"

Task 7 (level 3)

a. One good plan:

Time Task
7:15 to 9:30 Bolt up the 40 connections on Level 2
9:30 to 9:45 Break
9:45 to 11:00 Shake out the next load of steel by piece mark
11:00 to 12:00 Set beams with the crane, Level 3
12:00 to 12:30 Lunch
12:30 to 3:30 Set beams with the crane, Level 3

Accept other plans that keep the break and lunch, use only ready work before 11:00 and use the crane from 11:00.

b. With the plan above:

  • crane time planned: 7:15 to 9:30 (135) + 9:45 to 12:00 (135) = 270 minutes
  • crane time in the new plan: 11:00 to 12:00 (60) + 12:30 to 3:30 (180) = 240 minutes
  • beam setting that moves: 270 − 240 = 30 minutes

The bolt-up of today's beams also moves to another day, because the afternoon is now used for setting.

c. Example: "Crane's down till 11, so we're bolting up Level 2 and shaking out the next load till then. We'll set beams from 11 through the afternoon and run about half an hour short on setting, with today's bolt-up moving to tomorrow. Okay?"

Task 8 (level 3)

a. Protect what can be finished before the rain at 1:00. Pave complete sections in an order that leaves no half-done area, starting with the part the client needs most (for example, the entrance and drive lanes). Reason: from 7:30 to 1:00 is 330 minutes. At one truck every 20 minutes that is 17 loads (7:30, 7:50, ... 12:50). At one every 40 minutes it is 9 loads (7:30, 8:10, ... 12:50). So the crew can place only about half of the planned asphalt.

b. Any two sensible tasks, for example: prepare the next section (sweep, check grades), finish and roll edges, set up or adjust traffic control, check and clean equipment.

c. Tell the client this morning, as soon as the crew knows, not at 1:00. Say how much will be paved today, which part, and when the rest will be done.

Task 9 (level 2)

Any three of these:

  • Hanging copper from a ladder changes the work method to get around the broken lift. It may not be safe or allowed, and the foreman did not approve it.
  • There is no coffee break, and "stage parts" runs through lunch.
  • The pressure test is planned for piping that is not hung yet, so it is not ready to start.
  • The lift is back at 1:00, but the plan never installs the hangers.
  • Jesse did not tell the foreman or confirm the plan.

Task 10 (level 3)

Open answer. A good response marks what was fixed, crosses out what could not happen, uses only ready tasks, puts safety and work others wait on first, and says who was told. The bar chart should add up to the time available.

Check yourself

  1. Subtract the start time from the end time.
  2. Fixed: toolbox talk, breaks, a booked crane, lift or inspection, a concrete pour. Movable: clean-up, prefab at a bench, staging material, sorting fittings.
  3. Material, information, permit, area, tools and equipment.
  4. Work that other people are waiting on, then work with a deadline.
  5. So the foreman can plan around it, warn others and change the next day's plan.
