Re-plan your day when the job changes

Read a day plan as a bar chart, find what a change breaks, and build a new plan that keeps everything that still works.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Plan and prioritize; Persist and persevere; Demonstrate responsibility

All people, companies and projects in this lesson are fictional.

Why this matters on the job

A day on site is planned around things that are booked: a crane, a lift, a concrete truck, an inspector. When one of them falls through, the whole plan can stall. Some crews stand around and wait. Good crews spend ten minutes re-planning and keep working. A new apprentice who can say "the lift is down, so here is what I can do until it is back" saves the foreman a headache and shows they can be trusted with more.

Lesson 3 showed you how to sort a change into what changed, what stayed the same and what you still do not know. This lesson goes one step further. You will rebuild the day itself, minute by minute, and check that the new plan works.

Key ideas

A day plan as a bar chart

A bar chart of the day shows time from left to right. Each task is a bar. The longer the bar, the longer the task. You can see at a glance what happens when.

Read a bar chart in three steps:

  1. Read the time scale along the bottom.
  2. Find the start and end of each bar.
  3. Subtract to get the length: end time minus start time.

Example: a bar from 9:45 to 12:00 is 2 hours 15 minutes, or 135 minutes.

Fixed and movable

  • Fixed items cannot move unless someone else agrees: the toolbox talk, breaks, a booked crane or lift, an inspection, a concrete pour.
  • Movable items can shift to another time: cleaning up, prefab work at a bench, staging material, sorting fittings.

When a change hits, plan the fixed items first. Then fill the gaps with movable work.

Is the task ready to start?

A task is only ready when all five are in place:

Check Question
Material Is it on site and in the right place?
Information Do I have the right drawing, revision and instructions?
Permit Is any permit needed, such as a hot work permit, and is it issued?
Area Is the area clear and safe, with no other trade in the way?
Tools and equipment Are the tools, lift or crane here and working?

If even one answer is no, the task is not ready. Do not plan it into a gap.

Five questions to re-plan your day

  1. What is fixed now? Mark the breaks, bookings and anything you cannot move.
  2. What can not happen? Cross out every task that needs the thing that changed.
  3. What is ready to start? Use the five checks.
  4. What matters most? Safety first. Then work that other people are waiting on. Then work with a deadline.
  5. Tell and confirm. Tell your foreman what changed, what you plan to do, and what will be late. Start once they agree.

Keep every part of the old plan that still works. A re-plan is not a fresh start.

Worked example

Rae is a first-year steamfitter/pipefitter apprentice with Fraser Bend Piping Ltd. On Tuesday she is hanging copper pipe on Level 3 of a new clinic. All overhead work on Level 3 needs the scissor lift. This is her plan for the day.

At 7:10 a.m., near the end of the toolbox talk, the foreman, Mo, says: "The scissor lift is down. The repair tech will have it running by 1:00." Here is how Rae re-plans.

Step 1. What is fixed now? Toolbox talk 7:00 to 7:15. Coffee break 9:30 to 9:45. Lunch 12:00 to 12:30. The lift is back at 1:00. The shift ends at 3:30.

Step 2. What can not happen? Everything on the lift before 1:00:

  • install hangers, 7:15 to 9:30 = 135 minutes
  • hang copper, 9:45 to 12:00 = 135 minutes
  • part of the afternoon copper, 12:30 to 1:00 = 30 minutes

That is 135 + 135 + 30 = 300 minutes of lift work that cannot happen this morning.

Step 3. What is ready to start? Rae checks three movable tasks.

Task Material Information Permit Area Tools Ready?
Prefab copper assemblies at the bench yes yes none needed yes yes yes
Stage hangers and fittings on Level 3 yes yes none needed yes yes yes
Pressure test prep yes yes none needed no, the pipe is not hung yet yes no

Step 4. What matters most? The hangers must go in first, because the copper hangs from them. So when the lift returns at 1:00, hangers come first.

Step 5. Tell and confirm. At 7:15, right after the toolbox talk, Rae finds Mo and tells him her plan:

"Mo, it's Rae. With the lift down I'll do prefab at the bench until 11:15, then stage the hangers and fittings on Level 3 before lunch. At 1:00 I'll install hangers until 3:15, then lock the lift and clean up. The copper and the test prep will move to Wednesday. Does that work?"

Mo agrees. Here is the new plan. The short blue block from 7:15 to 7:30 is time to talk to Mo and gather what she needs for the bench.

Check the new plan. Add up the minutes from 7:00 to 3:30:

Block Minutes
Toolbox talk, 7:00 to 7:15 15
Talk to Mo and gather material, 7:15 to 7:30 15
Prefab, 7:30 to 9:30 120
Coffee, 9:30 to 9:45 15
Prefab, 9:45 to 11:15 90
Stage parts, 11:15 to 12:00 45
Lunch, 12:00 to 12:30 30
Set up, 12:30 to 1:00 30
Install hangers, 1:00 to 3:15 135
Lock lift and clean up, 3:15 to 3:30 15
Total 510

The shift from 7:00 to 3:30 is 8 hours 30 minutes = 510 minutes. The new plan fills it exactly, with no gaps and no overlaps.

What moves to Wednesday? The old plan had 390 minutes of lift work: 135 + 135 + 120. The new plan has 135. So 390 − 135 = 255 minutes of copper hanging moves to Wednesday, plus the 60-minute test prep. That is 255 + 60 = 315 minutes, or 5 hours 15 minutes. Rae said so when she told Mo her plan, so he can plan Wednesday.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Use Rae's plan before the change.

a. How long is the first "Install hangers" bar, in minutes?

b. What time does test prep start?

c. How many minutes of lift work were in the old plan?

Task 2 (level 1) Mark each item F (fixed) or M (movable) for a framing crew.

  1. Toolbox talk at 7:00
  2. City inspection booked for 2:00
  3. Sweeping the deck
  4. Lunch at 12:00
  5. Crane lift of roof trusses booked for 10:00
  6. Cutting blocking at the saw station
  7. Sorting hangers and nails for tomorrow

Task 3 (level 2) Riverbend Framing Ltd. plans this day: toolbox talk 7:00 to 7:15, frame walls 7:15 to 9:30, break 9:30 to 9:45, frame walls 9:45 to 12:00, lunch 12:00 to 12:30, frame walls 12:30 to 3:30.

a. How many minutes of wall framing are planned?

b. Write that as hours in decimal form.

c. Draw the day as a bar chart on grid paper. Use one square for 15 minutes.

Task 4 (level 2) Rae has 30 minutes free. Use the five checks. Which task is ready to start now? For each task that is not ready, name the check that fails.

Task Facts
A. Install firestop sleeves on Level 2 Sleeves on site. Drawing on hand. Drywallers are working in that area until 10:00.
B. Weld pipe supports in the mechanical room Supports and welder on site. The hot work permit has not been issued yet.
C. Prefab two valve assemblies at the bench Valves, pipe and fittings at the bench. Drawing on hand. Tools at the bench.
D. Pressure test the Level 1 piping Piping is complete. The test gauge is still at the shop.

Task 5 (level 2) A concrete pour is booked to start at 8:00 and take 2 hours 30 minutes. The batch plant calls: the first truck will be 2 hours 15 minutes late.

a. What time does the pour start now?

b. If the pour runs without a stop, what time does it finish?

c. The crew's lunch is 12:00 to 12:30. Concrete cannot wait. What should the foreman decide about lunch? Give one reasonable plan.

Task 6 (level 2) Write the text you would send your foreman when a change stops your work. Use this case: you are an electrical apprentice, and the drywall crew has not finished the room where you were to pull wire this morning. Include what changed, what you will do instead, what will be late, and one question.

Task 7 (level 3) Sky is an ironworker apprentice with Northshore Steel Erectors Ltd. This was the plan for Thursday:

Time Task
7:00 to 7:15 Toolbox talk
7:15 to 9:30 Set beams with the crane, Level 3
9:30 to 9:45 Break
9:45 to 12:00 Set beams with the crane, Level 3
12:00 to 12:30 Lunch
12:30 to 3:30 Bolt up the beams set in the morning

At 7:05 the crane operator reports a fault. The crane will be back at 11:00. Ready work without the crane: 40 connections on Level 2 still need bolting from yesterday, and the next load of steel needs to be shaken out (sorted and laid out by piece mark) on the ground.

a. Write a new plan from 7:15 to 3:30 that keeps the break and lunch.

b. How many minutes of crane time were planned? How many are in your new plan? How many minutes of beam setting move to another day?

c. Write the two-sentence radio call to the foreman.

Task 8 (level 3) A paving crew with Valley Line Paving Ltd. has two problems on the same morning:

  • The job spec says no paving in rain. Rain is forecast from 1:00 p.m.
  • One truck driver is sick, so asphalt will arrive every 40 minutes instead of every 20 minutes.

The plan was to pave the whole parking lot today, starting at 7:30.

a. Which part of the plan should the crew protect first, and why?

b. Name two things the crew could do while waiting between trucks.

c. What should the foreman tell the client, and when?

Task 9 (level 2) Jesse wrote this new plan after the lift broke down. Find three problems.

Time Task
7:15 to 9:30 Hang copper on Level 3 from a ladder
9:30 to 11:00 Prefab at the bench
11:00 to 12:30 Stage parts on Level 3
12:30 to 3:30 Pressure test the Level 3 piping

Jesse did not call the foreman.

Task 10 (level 3) Think of a day at work, school or home when a plan fell apart. Use the five questions to write the plan you wish you had made. Draw it as a bar chart. You choose how much to share.

Check yourself

  1. How do you find the length of a bar on a day chart?
  2. Give two fixed items and two movable items on a job site.
  3. Name the five checks that tell you a task is ready to start.
  4. After safety, what kind of work should go first in a re-plan?
  5. Why do you tell your foreman what will be late, not just what you will do?
