Adaptability · Lesson 12 · Instructor

Set and adjust goals across your apprenticeship

Turn your trade's hours and levels into dated goals, track your progress, and adjust the plan when a layoff, injury or family need gets in the way.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Set or adjust your goals and expectations; Plan and prioritize; Persist and persevere

Adaptability lesson 12 of 14. Total time: 90 minutes.

Opening

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Share one bump in your own apprenticeship: a layoff, a level you had to wait for, a move. Say how long it set you back and what you did. Ask: "If you were laid off for two months next winter, what would you do with the time?" Explain that this is the last Adaptability lesson, lesson 12 of 14, that it builds on lesson 7 (the apprenticeship path), and that it runs 90 minutes. Learners need a computer or phone for Task 10.

Objective

Time: included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • read a SkilledTradesBC program profile and turn its hours and levels into a dated plan (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)
  • track progress as a percent, hours to go and weeks to go (Plan and prioritize)
  • write short, mid and long-term goals that can be checked (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)
  • adjust the plan after a layoff or a change of sponsor, using SkilledTradesBC rules (Persist and persevere)

Pre-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.2)

  1. "Who reports your work-based training hours?"
  2. "You have 1,000 hours of 6,000. What fraction of the way are you?"

Notes for the instructor

  • Program profiles change. Before you teach, open the current Carpenter profile on skilledtradesbc.ca and check that the hours and levels match the learner page. If they have changed, update the numbers on the board.
  • Leah's 38-hour average, her savings and the January and June hours are fictional. Real hours depend on the employer and the season.
  • Learners may ask about EI amounts or how long EI lasts. Refer them to Service Canada and to ACCESS staff. Do not estimate amounts.

Post-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 4.1)

  1. "You have 4,000 of 6,360 hours. How many to go? At 40 hours a week, how many weeks?" (2,360 hours, 59 weeks)
  2. "You lose your sponsor in a non-compulsory trade. Do your hours keep counting?" (No, not until you have a new sponsor.)
  3. "Read one of your Task 10 goals. How will you know it is done?"

Summary

Time: 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • reading a program profile: hours, levels and pass marks
  • progress as a percent, hours to go and weeks to go
  • goals you can check
  • what to do when a layoff, injury or change of sponsor gets in the way
  • using a layoff for school, study or tickets

Sources

  • Carpenter program profile, implementation date April 1, 2024: 6,360 work-based training hours, Levels 1 to 4 of 210 hours each (assuming 30 hours in school per week), minimum 70% in each level and on the Interprovincial Red Seal exam, program generally takes 4 years: SkilledTradesBC, Carpenter Program Profile, https://skilledtradesbc.ca/media/2811/download, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Loss or change of a sponsor: in an STC (compulsory) trade the apprentice cannot continue to work in that trade as an apprentice until they have a new sponsor. In a non-STC trade the apprentice may keep working but will not receive credit for work-based training hours until they have a new sponsor: SkilledTradesBC, Apprenticeship Agreement Policy, https://skilledtradesbc.ca/media/2948/download, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Only sponsors can report hours, sponsors are recommended to report every 3 to 6 months, and apply for EI while attending technical training: SkilledTradesBC, Start an Apprenticeship, https://skilledtradesbc.ca/start-an-apprenticeship, checked 2026-11-08.
  • If you change employers, confirm your hours have been reported in the Portal and update your information with your next employer: SkilledTradesBC, Your apprenticeship journey (brochure), https://skilledtradesbc.ca/media/2372/download, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Construction Electrician is one of BC's seven Skilled Trades Certification (compulsory) trades, and Carpenter is not: SkilledTradesBC, Are You Impacted by Skilled Trades Certification?, https://skilledtradesbc.ca/are-you-impacted, as cited in Adaptability lesson 7 (checked 2026-11-06).
  • Canada Apprentice Loan for apprentices in Red Seal trades: SkilledTradesBC, Canada Apprentice Loan, https://skilledtradesbc.ca/canada-apprentice-loans, as cited in Adaptability lesson 7 (checked 2026-11-06).
  • Adaptability components "Set or adjust your goals and expectations", "Plan and prioritize" and "Persist and persevere": Government of Canada, Skill components and proficiency levels, https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training/initiatives/skills-success/learning-steps.html, checked 2026-11-08.
