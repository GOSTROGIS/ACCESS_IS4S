Adaptability · Lesson 12 · Answers

Set and adjust goals across your apprenticeship

Turn your trade's hours and levels into dated goals, track your progress, and adjust the plan when a layoff, injury or family need gets in the way.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Set or adjust your goals and expectations; Plan and prioritize; Persist and persevere

Task 1 (level 1)

a. 6,360 hours.

b. 4 levels.

c. 840 hours. 4 × 210 = 840.

d. 28 weeks. 840 ÷ 30 = 28. Or 4 levels × 7 weeks = 28.

Task 2 (level 2)

40%. 2,544 ÷ 6,360 × 100 = 0.4 × 100 = 40.

Task 3 (level 2)

a. 3,260 hours. 6,360 − 3,100 = 3,260.

b. 82 weeks. 3,260 ÷ 40 = 81.5. Round up to 82, because 81 weeks would leave her 20 hours short.

Task 4 (level 2)

a. 14 weeks. $3,150 ÷ $225 = 14.

b. No. In 12 weeks he saves 12 × $225 = $2,700, which is $450 short. He needs $3,150 ÷ 12 = $262.50 a week. That is $262.50 − $225 = $37.50 more a week.

Task 5 (level 2)

Open answer. Each goal must name what, by when, help, and how you will know. Examples:

  1. "Pass the fractions and metric quiz with 80% or more by March 15. I'll practise 20 minutes a day with the Numeracy Hub and ask my instructor about the ones I miss. I'll know from the quiz mark."
  2. "Send applications to 6 contractors in my trade by the end of this month. Program staff will check my résumé. I'll know when 6 are sent and logged."
  3. "Get 70% or more on my Level 1 exam in June. I'll use practice questions three times a week with a study partner. I'll know from my exam result."

Task 6 (level 3)

a. 147 weeks. 139 + 8 = 147.

b. No. Carpenter is a non-compulsory (non-STC) trade. She may keep working, but hours worked without a sponsor do not count toward her apprenticeship until she has a sponsor again. Framing for a neighbour with no sponsor does not count.

c. Any three, for example: check her hours in the Portal, apply for EI, ask about a Level 2 seat at a later date, study with practice questions, get a ticket she needs (such as first aid or fall protection), look for a new or returning sponsor, talk to an Apprenticeship Advisor.

Task 7 (level 2)

a. No. In a compulsory trade, he cannot keep working in that trade as an apprentice until he has a new sponsor.

b. Check that all his hours from the closed employer were reported. Make sure his new sponsor and registration are updated in the Portal (the new sponsor can request the change, and he approves it).

c. A SkilledTradesBC Apprenticeship Advisor.

Task 8 (level 3)

a. Plan A: 7 × 30 = 210 hours. Plan B: 7 × 45 = 315 hours.

b. Plan A, by 315 − 210 = 105 hours.

c. Open answer. For example: a June seat is the only one open, she has a family need in January, or she wants to stay with a crew through the winter for experience.

Task 9 (level 2)

  • Employment Insurance (EI) for the technical training period. SkilledTradesBC tells apprentices to apply.
  • Canada Apprentice Loan: an interest-free loan for apprentices registered in a Red Seal trade, up to $4,000 per period of technical training (lesson 7).

Task 10 (level 3)

Open answer. A good plan uses the real numbers from the learner's program profile, has three goals that can be checked, and has a specific "what if" line, such as "If I am laid off, I will check my Portal hours that week, apply for EI and ask for the next open seat in my next level."

Check yourself

  1. In the SkilledTradesBC Portal.
  2. Hours to go ÷ hours worked in a week. Round up to a whole week.
  3. You can keep working, but your hours do not count until you have a new sponsor.
  4. You cannot keep working in that trade as an apprentice until you have a new sponsor.
  5. That all your work-based training hours have been reported in the Portal.
