Adaptability · Lesson 12 · Learner

Set and adjust goals across your apprenticeship

Turn your trade's hours and levels into dated goals, track your progress, and adjust the plan when a layoff, injury or family need gets in the way.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Set or adjust your goals and expectations; Plan and prioritize; Persist and persevere

All people and companies in this lesson are fictional. The SkilledTradesBC program facts are real.

Why this matters on the job

An apprenticeship takes years. Along the way there are layoffs in the slow season, injuries, family needs, a move, a change of employer. Apprentices who finish are not the ones who never hit a bump. They are the ones who know exactly where they stand, in hours and levels, and who adjust the plan instead of dropping it.

Lesson 7 mapped the steps from sponsor to Red Seal. This lesson turns those steps into numbers and dates for you.

Key ideas

Know your program's numbers

Every trade has a SkilledTradesBC program profile. It lists the levels of technical training, the hours in each level, and the work-based training hours you need. For example, the Carpenter program profile (in effect from April 1, 2024) lists:

Requirement Carpenter
Work-based training hours 6,360 hours
Technical training 4 levels, 210 hours each, based on 30 hours a week in school
Pass mark 70% in each level of technical training, and 70% on the Interprovincial Red Seal exam
Usual length about 4 years

At 30 hours a week, one level of 210 hours takes 210 ÷ 30 = 7 weeks.

Look up your own trade's profile on skilledtradesbc.ca. The numbers are different for every trade.

Track your hours

Your sponsor reports your work-based training hours in the SkilledTradesBC Portal. SkilledTradesBC recommends that sponsors report them every 3 to 6 months. Only sponsors can report hours, but you can sign in and check them. Check often.

To track progress:

  • Hours done as a percent: hours done ÷ hours required × 100
  • Hours to go: hours required − hours done
  • Weeks to go: hours to go ÷ hours you work in a week

Goals you can check

A goal you can check names four things: what, by when, who or what will help, and how you will know it is done (lesson 7). "Work hard" cannot be checked. "Reach 3,400 hours by next September, shown in the Portal" can.

When something gets in the way

Plans change. What matters is what you do next.

  • Laid off or changing employers. Before you leave, check that your hours have been reported in the Portal. When you start with a new employer, make sure the new sponsor and your registration are updated in the Portal.
  • Lost your sponsor in a non-compulsory trade (such as Carpenter). You can keep working, but your hours do not count toward your apprenticeship until you have a new sponsor.
  • Lost your sponsor in a compulsory trade (such as Construction Electrician). You cannot keep working in that trade as an apprentice until you have a new sponsor.
  • Going to technical training. Apply for Employment Insurance (EI) for the training period. Red Seal trade apprentices can apply for the Canada Apprentice Loan (lesson 7).
  • Not sure what to do. Contact a SkilledTradesBC Apprenticeship Advisor.

A layoff can also be a chance: a time to take a level of technical training, study for an exam or get a ticket you need.

Worked example

Leah is a carpentry apprentice with Sumas Ridge Homes Ltd. She finished Level 1. The Portal shows 1,900 work-based training hours. She averages 38 hours a week on the job.

Step 1. Where am I? 1,900 ÷ 6,360 × 100 = 29.9%. She is just under one third of the way through her hours.

Step 2. How far to go? 6,360 − 1,900 = 4,460 hours.

Step 3. How many working weeks? 4,460 ÷ 38 = 117.4. She rounds up to 118 working weeks, because 117 weeks would leave her short.

Step 4. Add school. Levels 2, 3 and 4 take 7 weeks each: 3 × 7 = 21 weeks. Her plan is 118 + 21 = 139 weeks, about 2.7 years (139 ÷ 52 = 2.67).

Step 5. Set goals. Leah writes three goals:

Goal By when Help How I'll know
Short: save $2,800 to cover rent during Level 2 in 16 weeks $175 a week into savings savings account shows $2,800
Mid: pass Level 2 and reach 3,400 hours in 12 months sponsor, training provider, study group Level 2 mark and Portal hours
Long: Red Seal carpenter in about 3 years sponsor, Apprenticeship Advisor certificate received

Check the savings goal: 16 × $175 = $2,800.

Step 6. Adjust when something changes. In November, Sumas Ridge lays Leah off for the slow season. The layoff lasts 10 weeks. She:

  • checks the Portal before her last day, and sees all her hours are there
  • applies for EI
  • asks the training provider if there is a Level 2 seat this winter
  • studies for Level 2 using practice questions (lesson 6)

Her plan moves by 10 weeks: 139 + 10 = 149 weeks.

If she gets a winter seat in Level 2, she uses 7 of the layoff weeks for school instead of losing them. Then her finish moves by only 10 − 7 = 3 weeks: 139 + 3 = 142 weeks.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Use the Carpenter program facts.

a. How many work-based training hours does a carpenter need?

b. How many levels of technical training are there?

c. How many hours of technical training is that in total?

d. How many weeks of school is that, at 30 hours a week?

Task 2 (level 2) Jordan is a carpentry apprentice with 2,544 hours in the Portal. What percent of the 6,360 hours is that?

Task 3 (level 2) Mia is a carpentry apprentice with 3,100 hours. She works 40 hours a week.

a. How many hours does she have to go?

b. How many working weeks is that? Round up to a whole week.

Task 4 (level 2) Ben wants to save $3,150 before his next level of technical training. He can save $225 a week.

a. How many weeks will it take?

b. His training starts in 12 weeks. Will he make it? If not, how much more a week would he need to save?

Task 5 (level 2) Rewrite each goal so it can be checked. Name what, by when, help, and how you will know.

  1. "Get better at math."
  2. "Find a sponsor soon."
  3. "Do well at school."

Task 6 (level 3) Use Leah's plan. This time her layoff lasts 8 weeks, and she cannot get a Level 2 seat during it.

a. How many weeks is her new plan?

b. During the layoff she does some framing for a neighbour, without a sponsor. Do those hours count toward her apprenticeship? Explain using the rule for her trade.

c. List three useful things she could do during the 8 weeks.

Task 7 (level 2) Andre is a construction electrician apprentice. His employer closes and he loses his sponsor. Construction Electrician is a compulsory trade in BC.

a. Can he keep working as an electrical apprentice for another contractor tomorrow, before a new sponsor is registered?

b. What two things should he check or do in the Portal?

c. Who can he contact for advice?

Task 8 (level 3) Leah can take Level 2 in January (Plan A) or in June (Plan B).

  • In January, work is slow. She would average 30 hours a week if she stayed on the job.
  • In June, work is busy. She would average 45 hours a week if she stayed on the job.

Level 2 takes 7 weeks either way.

a. How many work-based hours would she miss with each plan?

b. Which plan costs her fewer hours? By how many?

c. Give one reason someone might still choose the other plan.

Task 9 (level 2) Name two money supports to apply for before a level of technical training, and say who each one is for.

Task 10 (level 3) Look up your own trade's program profile on skilledtradesbc.ca, or use the Carpenter facts. Write a three-year plan with:

  • one short, one mid and one long goal, each one you can check
  • the hours or levels each goal needs
  • one "what if" line: what you will do if you are laid off, injured or needed at home for a month

You choose how much to share.

Check yourself

  1. Where can you check your work-based training hours?
  2. How do you work out the weeks you have to go?
  3. What happens to your hours if you lose your sponsor in a non-compulsory trade?
  4. What happens if you lose your sponsor in a compulsory trade?
  5. What should you check before your last day with an employer?
