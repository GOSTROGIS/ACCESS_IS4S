# Keep learning as an apprentice in BC

## Task 1 (level 1)

1. Find your trade.
2. Find a sponsor.
3. Register as an apprentice in the Portal.
4. Register for technical training.
5. Pass the Standard Level Exams.
6. Pass the certification exam.
7. Get a Recommendation for Certification.
8. Receive a Certificate of Qualification.

Steps 4 and 5 repeat for each level of technical training.

## Task 2 (level 2)

a. The **second digit** (the 2 in 7**2**310). It is **TEER 2**.

b. An apprenticeship of 2 to 5 years, or a college program of 2 to 3 years.

c. Open answer. Reasons include: more training and skill are needed, certification is required, the work carries more responsibility or safety risk, fewer people can do it.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Open answer. Wages, outlook and hours change, so mark the method, not the numbers. A good table:

- has every row filled in
- uses current figures from WorkBC and SkilledTradesBC, not from memory or an old handout
- names the page for each fact
- gets the NOC code and TEER right for the chosen trade

Some facts for checking common choices:

- Construction Electrician: NOC 72200, TEER 2, a Red Seal trade, and a compulsory trade in BC.
- Carpenter: NOC 72310, TEER 2. Not one of BC's seven compulsory trades.
- Welder: NOC 72106, TEER 2. Not one of BC's seven compulsory trades.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**Answer:** $16,000.

Steps: up to $4,000 per period of technical training × 4 levels = $16,000. This is the most that could be borrowed. It is a loan, so it is paid back.

## Task 5 (level 3)

Open answer. A good plan:

- has three goals that build on each other toward a trade
- gives each goal a date
- names real help (a person, an organization or a tool)
- says how the learner will know it is done, in a way someone else could check ("hours show in the Portal", not "I feel ready")
- fits the steps from Task 1, for example a sponsor before registration
