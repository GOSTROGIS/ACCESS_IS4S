# Keep learning as an apprentice in BC

All people in this lesson are fictional.

## Why this matters on the job

A Red Seal or Certificate of Qualification is not handed out for time served. You earn it by learning on the job, in the classroom and at the exam table over several years. Apprentices who know the steps, track their hours and plan their training finish. Those who leave it to chance often stall at Level 1 or Level 2. Knowing the path also helps you choose a trade that fits you.

## Key ideas

### How jobs are grouped in Canada

Canada's National Occupational Classification (NOC 2021) gives every occupation a five-digit code. The **second digit** is the TEER category (Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities).

| TEER | What the job usually needs |
|---|---|
| 0 | Management experience |
| 1 | A university degree |
| 2 | A college program of 2 to 3 years, or an apprenticeship of 2 to 5 years, or supervisory or major safety duties |
| 3 | A college program or apprenticeship of less than 2 years, or more than 6 months of on-the-job training |
| 4 | Secondary school, or several weeks of on-the-job training |
| 5 | A short work demonstration and no formal education |

Examples: 72310 Carpenters, 72200 Electricians (except industrial and power system) and 72106 Welders and related machine operators are all TEER 2.

WorkBC's 2025 Labour Market Outlook forecasts about 228,400 job openings in TEER 2 and 181,300 in TEER 3 in BC from 2025 to 2035. Every occupation on its High Opportunity Occupations list is in TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3, which means some training after high school.

### The BC apprenticeship path

SkilledTradesBC describes an apprenticeship as about 80 percent work-based training with a sponsor and about 20 percent in-class technical training. Across all trades, the average time to finish is 4 years.

1. **Find your trade.** SkilledTradesBC has a "find your trade" quiz.
2. **Find a sponsor.** A sponsor is often your employer. They make sure you are trained by a certified journeyperson and they report your hours.
3. **Register as an apprentice** in the SkilledTradesBC Portal. Your sponsor confirms it.
4. **Register for technical training** at a SkilledTradesBC-designated training provider. Classes fill up quickly, so register early. Many apprentices go once a year.
5. **Pass the Standard Level Exams** that come with each level of technical training.
6. **Pass the certification exam** (70 percent or higher) after all levels are done.
7. **Get a Recommendation for Certification** from your sponsor.
8. **Receive your credential:** a Certificate of Qualification for BC, with a Red Seal Endorsement in Red Seal trades so you can work across Canada.

### Compulsory trades

In BC, seven trades fall under Skilled Trades Certification (compulsory trades): Construction Electrician, Industrial Electrician, Powerline Technician, Steamfitter/Pipefitter, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Mechanic, Gasfitter Class A and Class B, and Sheet Metal Worker. To work in these trades you must be a registered apprentice, a trade qualifier or a certified journeyperson. Employers must keep a ratio of no more than two apprentices to one journeyperson. Mobile Crane Operator and Tower Crane Operator become compulsory trades on July 5, 2027.

### Paying for technical training

- The **Canada Apprentice Loan** gives interest-free loans of up to $4,000 per period of technical training to apprentices registered in a Red Seal trade.
- SkilledTradesBC advises apprentices to apply for **Employment Insurance (EI)** for their technical training period.

### A learning plan you can check

The old ACCESS Individual Enhancement Plan tracked short, mid and long-term goals. A good plan for each goal names:

- what you will do
- by when
- who or what will help
- how you will know it is done

**Worked example.** Tasha wants to be a construction electrician.

| Goal | By when | Help | How I'll know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short: apply to 5 electrical contractors for an apprentice job | End of next month | Program staff, online job boards | 5 applications sent |
| Mid: registered apprentice with 1,000 hours logged | In 12 months | Sponsor, SkilledTradesBC Portal | Hours show in the Portal |
| Long: Red Seal Endorsement | In 5 years | Sponsor, training provider, study group | Certificate received |

### Worked example: how many weeks to the next level?

Tasha is now a registered construction electrician apprentice. Her sponsor wants her to have 1,500 work-based training hours before she goes to Level 2. This is her sponsor's plan, not a SkilledTradesBC rule. The Portal shows 1,200 hours. She works 38 hours a week.

**Step 1. Hours to go.** 1,500 − 1,200 = 300 hours.

**Step 2. Weeks to go.** 300 ÷ 38 = 7.89. Round up to **8 weeks**, because after 7 weeks she would have only 7 × 38 = 266 more hours, 34 short.

**Step 3. Check.** After 8 weeks: 1,200 + 8 × 38 = 1,200 + 304 = 1,504 hours. That is past 1,500.

**Step 4. Act now.** Classes fill quickly, so Tasha registers for Level 2 technical training today, for a class that starts after week 8. She also writes a goal she can check: "1,500 hours in the Portal in 8 weeks."

## Beyond the Red Seal: the route to a BCIT degree

A Red Seal does not have to be the end of your learning. It can also open the door to a degree. BCIT's Bachelor of Technology in Construction Management (BTech CM) has an entry route for certified tradespeople, called Option D. When it was checked in November 2026, BCIT listed these requirements for Option D:

- a Red Seal in an approved trade
- Math 11 at 60%, or BCIT's BLDT 1011 (Applied Construction Math) at 60%
- BCIT's Construction Operations Associate Certificate, with a GPA of at least 67%
- at least one year of construction work on top of your apprenticeship hours
- English Studies 12 at 73%
- a pre-entry assessment with the program head before you apply

Requirements can change. Confirm them with the program at cmgt@bcit.ca before you build a plan around them.
