Working for yourself in the trades

Know what changes when you work for yourself in a trade in BC, from business licences and GST to setting money aside for tax and CPP and getting WorkSafeBC coverage, and plan the first steps in order.

Task 1 (level 1)

a. Employee. The boss controls her hours, supplies the welder and truck, and she cannot send someone else.

b. Self-employed. He owns his equipment, controls his hours, can hire a helper and can make a profit or take a loss.

c. The facts point to employee. The boss controls the hours, supplies all the tools and pays by the hour. Calling someone a contractor does not decide it. The CRA looks at the whole working relationship.

Task 2 (level 1)

a. The city or town. b. BC Registries. c. The CRA. d. WorkSafeBC. e. WorkSafeBC.

Task 3 (level 2)

a. No. 7,200 + 8,100 + 6,900 + 6,700 = $28,900. No quarter is over $30,000 and the four quarters together are not over $30,000. This person is a small supplier. They may still register by choice.

b. Yes, right away. The second quarter alone is $31,500, which is over $30,000 in a single quarter. This person stops being a small supplier immediately, must charge GST on the job that put them over, and must register.

Task 4 (level 2)

  1. Subtotal: $1,850 + $460 = $2,310
  2. GST: 5% of $2,310 = 0.05 × $2,310 = $115.50
  3. Total: $2,310 + $115.50 = $2,425.50

Task 5 (level 2)

25% of $2,310 = 0.25 × $2,310 = $577.50

Set aside: $115.50 + $577.50 = $693.00

Task 6 (level 3)

CPP = ($41,200 − $3,500) × 11.9% = $37,700 × 0.119 = $4,486.30

Brackets first: subtract the $3,500 before you multiply. $41,200 is below $74,600, so no second contribution applies.

Task 7 (level 2)

a. Yes. If you hire workers in BC, you must register with WorkSafeBC.

b. No. A sole proprietor is not automatically covered. She would be covered only if she had bought Personal Coverage.

c. If a GC hires a registered sub who is not paying WorkSafeBC premiums, the GC can be held liable for those premiums. A clearance letter before and after the job shows Dana's account is active and in good standing for the whole contract.

Task 8 (level 3)

Open task. A good list has at least six steps in a sensible order and names where each is done. For example:

  1. Talk to an accountant about structure, tax and how much to set aside (accountant).
  2. Choose a structure and a name (own decision, with advice).
  3. Get the name approved and register, if not using your own legal name (BC Registries).
  4. Check permits and licences for your trade and city (BizPaL, local government).
  5. Get a business licence in each city you will work in, or an inter-municipal licence where one covers your area (local government).
  6. Open a separate bank account and set up a way to track receipts and invoices.
  7. Decide on WorkSafeBC Personal Coverage, and register if you will hire (WorkSafeBC).
  8. Get the insurance your contracts ask for (insurance broker).
  9. Track sales by quarter and register for GST before you go over $30,000, or by choice (CRA).

Check yourself

  1. Any two: who controls how and when the work is done, who supplies the tools and equipment, whether you can hire a helper or send someone else, whether you can make a profit or take a loss.
  2. No.
  3. $30,000 in taxable sales, in a single quarter or over four calendar quarters in a row.
  4. Both the employee and the employer share. For 2026 that is 11.9% of net self-employment earnings between $3,500 and $74,600.
  5. No. A sole proprietor can buy Personal Coverage.
