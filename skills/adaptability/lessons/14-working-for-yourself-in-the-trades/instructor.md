# Working for yourself in the trades

Adaptability lesson 14 of 14. Total time: 90 minutes.

## Opening

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Hands up if you know someone in the trades who works for themselves. What do they like about it? What do they complain about?" Write the answers in two columns. Point out that most of the complaints are about money and paperwork: taxes, invoices, insurance. Explain that this is the last Adaptability lesson, lesson 14 of 14, that it looks further down the road than lesson 12, and that it runs 90 minutes. Learners need a calculator.

## Objective

**Time:** included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- tell an employee from a self-employed person using the CRA's questions (Demonstrate responsibility)
- list the set-up steps for a one-person trade business in BC and where each one is done (Plan and prioritize)
- apply the GST small supplier test, add GST to an invoice and set money aside from each payment (Demonstrate responsibility)
- work out self-employed CPP and explain WorkSafeBC coverage for a sole proprietor (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)

## Pre-assessment

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.2)

1. "What is taken off an employee's pay cheque before they get it?"
2. "What is 5% of $800?" ($40)

### Notes for the instructor

- This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. The case is fictional.
- Rates and limits change every year: check the CPP rate and earnings limits, the GST small supplier limit and the BC Registries fee on the government pages in the Sources before you teach. If they have changed, update the board.
- Do not give tax advice. The 25% set-aside is Dana's own plan with her accountant, written for the example. If a learner asks how much they should set aside, send them to an accountant or the CRA.
- Business licence fees and rules differ in every city. Do not quote a fee. Send learners to their local government and to BizPaL.
- A business licence may not be the only permit a trade business needs. BizPaL lists suggested local, provincial and federal permits and licences for a business type and place. Have learners run it for their own trade.
- Some learners may have been paid "as contractors" when they were really employees. If a learner raises it, point them to the CRA page on employee or self-employed and to ACCESS staff. Do not judge their situation in class.
- Liability insurance is not covered as a legal rule in this lesson. In the case, the GC asks for it because many contracts do.

## Post-assessment

**Time:** 4 minutes (step 4.1)

1. "Your sales were $7,000, $8,000, $7,500 and $8,000 in the last four quarters. Do you have to register for GST?" (Yes. $30,500 is over $30,000 across four quarters.)
2. "Add 5% GST to a $1,200 job." ($60 GST, $1,260 total)
3. "You hire your first helper. What must you do with WorkSafeBC?" (Register.)

## Summary

**Time:** 2 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- the CRA's questions for employee or self-employed
- business structure, name registration and business licences
- the GST $30,000 small supplier test and the 5% GST on an invoice
- setting money aside, and paying both shares of CPP
- WorkSafeBC Personal Coverage, registering when you hire, and clearance letters
