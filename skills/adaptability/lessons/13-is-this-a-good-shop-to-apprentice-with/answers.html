Is this a good shop to apprentice with?

Use a five-part checklist to judge whether a shop will support you through your apprenticeship, ask the right questions at an interview, and know what to watch for in your first 90 days.

  • Components: Set or adjust your goals and expectations; Persist and persevere; Seek self-improvement

a. 2 (apprenticeship support). b. 3 (gear and facilities). c. 5 (path forward). d. 4 (commitment). e. 1 (crew culture). f. 2 (apprenticeship support). g. 5 (path forward). h. 3 (gear and facilities).

Task 2 (level 2)

a. Worker. Appropriate footwear including safety footwear.

b. Employer. It is PPE the Regulation requires that is not on the worker's list.

c. Worker. Clothing for protection against the weather.

d. Worker. Safety headgear.

e. Employer. At no cost to the worker.

Task 3 (level 2)

Area Score Why
1. Crew culture 2 "Speak up" is said, but there is no set way to report and nothing is written.
2. Apprenticeship support 3 Written first-week checklist, named journeyperson, hours on a schedule.
3. Gear and facilities 3 A range of sizes stocked and a lockable washroom on every site.
4. Commitment 2 A short policy exists, but nobody knows who to go to.
5. Path forward 2 Raises only if you ask, varied work depends on who you are with.
Total 12 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 12
Average 2.4 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4
Band Foundational 2.4 is in the 1.8 to 2.4 band.

Accept a score one level off in an area if the reason fits the description. Recalculate the average from the learner's own scores.

Task 4 (level 2)

Yes, it is a red flag. It is about area 4 (the shop's commitment: a way to report that works) and area 1 (whether you can speak up safely). The red flag rule says to treat the whole shop as a risk whatever the average says, and to deal with that area first. A good answer adds a next step: find out the shop's written reporting procedure, and keep notes of what was said.

Task 5 (level 2)

Open task. Examples:

  • Area 1: "If an apprentice makes a mistake, how does the crew handle it?"
  • Area 2: "Who would I be working with day to day, and how often are hours reported?"
  • Area 3: "Do you stock PPE in different sizes, or do I order my own?"
  • Area 4: "Who would I talk to if I had a problem with someone on the crew?"
  • Area 5: "What kind of work do first-year apprentices do here? How do pay raises work?"

A good answer has three questions, each about a different area, polite and specific.

Task 6 (level 3)

Open task. A good answer names two specific signs and two steps for each. For example:

  • Sign: my hours have not shown up in the Portal after three months. First: ask my journeyperson or the office. Next: a SkilledTradesBC Apprenticeship Advisor.
  • Sign: I have only swept and carried material for six weeks. First: ask my journeyperson for one new task a week. Next: talk to the owner, using my program outline.
  • Sign: jokes on the crew are aimed at me or someone else and keep going. First: report it through the shop's written procedure. Next: ACCESS staff, and WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line if the employer does not deal with it.

Task 7 (level 2)

b. 64% of 50 = 0.64 × 50 = 32 apprentices.

c. 20% of 50 = 0.20 × 50 = 10 apprentices.

Check: 8 + 32 + 10 = 50.

Task 8 (level 3)

a. $1.50 × 40 = $60 more a week.

b. $1.50 × 2,000 = $3,000 more a year.

c. Open answer. A strong answer weighs the $3,000 against what Jesse risks at Stonefield: a score of 1.4, a red flag, no named journeyperson, hours reported "when we get around to it", and mostly sweeping and grinding. Alder Creek scores 2.8, with hours on a schedule and real trade work, which is what helps apprentices finish. Many learners will pick Alder Creek. A learner who picks Stonefield should say how they would deal with the red flag and get their hours reported.

Check yourself

  1. Crew culture and speaking up, support for your apprenticeship, gear and facilities that fit, the shop's commitment, a path forward.
  2. Any two PPE items the Regulation requires other than weather clothing, general purpose work gloves, appropriate footwear and safety headgear. For example, a fall-protection harness, a respirator, hearing protection or eye protection required for the job.
  3. Add the five scores and divide by 5.
  4. If any area has a safety or harassment problem, treat the whole shop as a risk, whatever the average says.
  5. Your sponsor.
