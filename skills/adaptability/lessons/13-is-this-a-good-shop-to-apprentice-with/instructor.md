# Is this a good shop to apprentice with?

Adaptability lesson 13 of 14. Total time: 90 minutes.

## Opening

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Ask: "If you had two job offers next week, what would you look at besides the wage?" Write answers on the board. Then share the Statistics Canada finding: at the end of the expected program time, only 16 out of every 100 apprentices had their certificate. Ask what they think pushes the other 84 off track. Explain that this is Adaptability lesson 13 of 14, that it builds on lessons 7 (the apprenticeship path) and 12 (goals across your apprenticeship), and that it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective

**Time:** included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- name the five areas that help apprentices stay with a shop, and what BC rules already require in some of them (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)
- score a shop from a description, work out the average and read the band (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)
- spot a red flag and decide what to do about it (Persist and persevere)
- write interview questions and a first-90-days watch list for their own job search (Seek self-improvement)

## Pre-assessment

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.2)

1. "Who supplies your hard hat and safety boots on a BC job: you or the employer?"
2. "Name one thing an employer could do that would make you want to stay."

### Notes for the instructor

- The five areas and four levels are adapted from the Apprentice Retention Self-Check, the instructor's own employer self-assessment workbook. The workbook is written for employers. This lesson turns it around so the learner does the checking.
- Some learners have been harassed, mocked or pushed out of a job. Do not ask anyone to share their own story. Keep the examples fictional. If a learner says they are being harassed now, talk with them privately after class and connect them with ACCESS staff. WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line is 1.888.621.7233.
- The scores in Task 3 have one model answer, but accept a score one level off if the learner's reason fits the description.
- Washrooms and changing areas are in the checklist as signs of a supportive shop. This lesson does not state a legal rule about them. If learners ask, say the OHS Regulation has requirements for washroom facilities and point them to worksafebc.com.
- A high average is not a promise. The red flag rule matters more than the number. Make that point when you summarize.
- Wages in Task 8 are made up. Do not quote real BC apprentice wage rates from memory.

## Post-assessment

**Time:** 4 minutes (step 4.1)

1. "A shop scores 3, 2, 2, 3, 2. What is the average and band?" (12 ÷ 5 = 2.4, Foundational)
2. "Your employer says you have to buy your own respirator for the spray booth. Is that right under section 8.2?" (No. The employer supplies required PPE other than weather clothing, general gloves, footwear and safety headgear, at no cost.)
3. "Name one question you would ask at an interview about area 2."

## Summary

**Time:** 2 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- why the shop you work for matters to finishing your apprenticeship
- the five areas: crew culture, apprenticeship support, gear and facilities, the shop's commitment, a path forward
- what section 8.2, WorkSafeBC's bullying and harassment duties and SkilledTradesBC's sponsor duties already require
- scoring a shop, the average and the bands, and the red flag rule
- interview questions and a watch list for the first 90 days
