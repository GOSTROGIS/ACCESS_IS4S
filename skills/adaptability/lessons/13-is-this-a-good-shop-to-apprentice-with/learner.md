# Is this a good shop to apprentice with?

All people and companies in this lesson are fictional. The facts about apprenticeship completion, PPE, sponsors and bullying policies are real.

## Why this matters on the job

Statistics Canada followed apprentices across Canada who registered from 2008 on. At the end of the time their program was supposed to take, only **16%** had their certificate. **64%** were still in their program and **20%** had left it. Apprentices who worked in a job closely related to their trade were also more likely to earn their certificate within one and a half times the expected program time than those who did not (38% compared with 32%).

Where you work is a big part of whether you finish. A shop that gives you a journeyperson, real trade work, gear that fits and a safe place to speak up keeps you moving. A shop that does not can stall you for years. You cannot control everything about a shop, but you can learn to read one before you sign on, and you can spot the warning signs early.

## Key ideas

### Five things that keep apprentices in a shop

This checklist looks at a shop from **your** side. It has five areas.

| Area | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1. Crew culture and speaking up** | How the crew treats people. Whether you can report a problem or admit a mistake without being punished for it. |
| **2. Support for your apprenticeship** | A plan for your first week, a named journeyperson who supervises you, and hours reported to SkilledTradesBC on a schedule. |
| **3. Gear and facilities that fit** | PPE and tools that fit you. A safe, private washroom and a place to change. |
| **4. The shop's commitment** | A written bullying and harassment policy, a way to report that works even when the problem is your own boss, and a named person who answers for how apprentices are treated. |
| **5. A path forward** | Real trade tasks across your program, not just clean-up. A clear pay step as you progress. Time off for technical training. |

### What BC rules already say

More on these in Collaboration lesson 08 ([Respectful workplaces](../../../collaboration/lessons/08-respectful-workplaces/learner.html)), lesson 07 ([Apprenticeship path](../07-apprenticeship-path/learner.html)) and Reading lesson 22 ([Employee, apprentice or contractor?](../../../reading/lessons/22-employee-apprentice-or-contractor/learner.html)).

Some of the checklist is backed by rules a shop must follow:

- **PPE.** Under BC's Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, section 8.2, a worker supplies clothing for the weather, general purpose work gloves, appropriate footwear including safety footwear, and safety headgear. The employer supplies **all other** PPE the Regulation requires, at no cost to the worker.
- **Bullying and harassment.** WorkSafeBC says every employer, of any size, must have a policy statement that bullying and harassment is not acceptable, procedures for workers to report it (including when the employer or a supervisor is the one doing it), and procedures for dealing with complaints. Workers must be told about the policy and trained on it.
- **Your sponsor.** SkilledTradesBC says a sponsor must make sure you have proper equipment, know which certified journeyperson supervises your work, and get work-based training across the full scope of your trade. Only your sponsor can report your hours, ideally every 3 to 6 months. Under the Apprenticeship Agreement Policy, the sponsor must release you, as far as reasonably possible, to attend technical training.

### Four levels for each area

Score each area from 1 to 4.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reactive | Nothing is in place. Things only happen after a problem blows up. |
| 2 | Foundational | Some good things happen, but nothing is written down. It depends on who is around that day. |
| 3 | Established | It is written down, most of the crew follows it, and someone is named to make it happen. |
| 4 | Leading | The shop checks how it is working, fixes gaps and could show another shop how it does it. |

**Average score** = total of the five scores ÷ 5.

| Average | Band | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 to 1.7 | Reactive | Big risk. Your apprenticeship is left to chance. |
| 1.8 to 2.4 | Foundational | Mixed. Good people, but it depends on who you work with. |
| 2.5 to 3.2 | Established | Solid. Most of what you need is in place. |
| 3.3 to 4.0 | Leading | Strong. The shop works at keeping apprentices. |

**Red flag rule.** If any area has a **safety or harassment** problem, such as "nobody reports anything here" or "the foreman's jokes are part of the job", treat the whole shop as a risk, whatever the average says. Make that area the first thing you deal with or ask about.

## Worked example: two job offers

Jesse finished a pre-apprenticeship program and has two offers as a first-year apprentice.

**Alder Creek Mechanical.** At the interview the owner showed Jesse a one-page first-week plan and named the journeyperson Jesse would work with. Hours go into the Portal every three months. There is a written respect policy and the office manager takes complaints if someone does not want to go to their foreman. Gloves only come in one size, but the owner said they would order Jesse's size. A posted wage sheet shows pay going up at each level, and apprentices rotate through rough-in, service and finishing.

| Area | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Crew culture | 3 | A known way to report, and apprentices said mistakes are treated as learning |
| 2. Apprenticeship support | 3 | Written first-week plan, named journeyperson, hours reported on a schedule |
| 3. Gear and facilities | 2 | Gloves ordered on request, not stocked |
| 4. Commitment | 3 | Written policy, a named person outside the foreman |
| 5. Path forward | 3 | Posted wage steps, rotation through the trade |

Total: 3 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 14. Average: 14 ÷ 5 = **2.8**. Band: **Established (solid)**.

**Stonefield Fabrication.** The foreman said, "You'll learn by watching." Nobody could say who would supervise Jesse. When Jesse asked about hours, the foreman said, "We do that when we get around to it." There is no written policy. "If you can't take a joke, this isn't the place for you." Apprentices mostly sweep and grind. A friendly journeyperson sometimes shows the new people things.

| Area | Score |
|---|---|
| 1. Crew culture | 1 |
| 2. Apprenticeship support | 2 |
| 3. Gear and facilities | 1 |
| 4. Commitment | 1 |
| 5. Path forward | 2 |

Total: 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 7. Average: 7 ÷ 5 = **1.4**. Band: **Reactive (big risk)**. The "take a joke" line is also a **red flag** for area 1.
