Adaptability · Lesson 02 · Instructor

Owning your work and your mistakes

Report mistakes early, focus on what you control, and answer criticism without blaming or caving in.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Demonstrate responsibility; Seek self-improvement

Adaptability lesson 2 of 14. Total time: 75 minutes.

Opening

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Share a time you made a mistake at work and what happened when you reported it, or when you did not. Keep it short and honest. Ask: "Who would you rather work beside: someone who never admits a mistake, or someone who tells you right away?" Explain that this is Adaptability lesson 2 of 14, and that the session is 75 minutes.

Objective

Time: included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • tell owning a mistake apart from excuses and blame (Demonstrate responsibility)
  • state the BC duty to report unsafe conditions and acts (Demonstrate responsibility)
  • report a mistake in four parts, including one change for next time (Demonstrate responsibility, Seek self-improvement)
  • answer criticism with the A B C D tools (Seek self-improvement)

Pre-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.2)

  1. "What does it mean to be accountable at work?"
  2. "Who are we accountable to, and what for?"

These are the brainstorm questions from the original ACCESS lesson. Write answers on the whiteboard in two columns: "to whom" and "for what".

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening story, objectives, session length. Listen and respond to the opening question. 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment brainstorm. Call out answers. Whiteboard 5 min
2.1 Build on the brainstorm. From the ACCESS lesson, add the areas we are responsible for: our actions and choices, our time, our attitude, how we treat others, keeping our word. Point out that accountability does not mean taking blame for others' choices. Add to the list. Whiteboard 8 min
2.2 Introduce Task 1. Ask learners to say what makes a statement an excuse. Complete Task 1 alone, then check with a partner. Learner page 7 min
2.3 Draw the three columns. Note from the ACCESS lesson that the word "control" can feel loaded for some people, and "what is mine to do" works as well. Complete Task 2. Share one item that was hard to place. Learner page 10 min
3.1 Explain OHS Regulation section 3.10 and the protection against being penalized. Read Marcus's call aloud. Ask: "What made this a good report?" Name the four parts in Marcus's call. Write Task 3. Learner page 13 min
3.2 Explain the ripple effect from the ACCESS lesson: an action is a drop in a pond, and each ring is a person or group it reaches. Remind learners they can use the example decision. Complete Task 4. Volunteers share one ring. Paper, markers 10 min
3.3 Model one A B C D reply with a volunteer. Explain that the tools feel stiff at first, like learning a new language, and smooth out with practice (from the ACCESS lesson). Role-play Task 5 in pairs. Write one reply. Learner page 9 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer. 5 min
4.2 Summarize. Name one tool they will try this week. 3 min

Total: 5 + 5 + 8 + 7 + 10 + 13 + 10 + 9 + 5 + 3 = 75 minutes.

The learner page also has the control, influence and no-control circles, and a worked example of a strong and a weak report about six studs cut short. Use the worked example as the model before Task 3. These fit inside the existing step times.

Notes for the instructor

  • Some learners have been blamed unfairly in school, work or the justice system. Keep the role plays about work statements, not personal history.
  • In step 3.3, stop any role play that turns into real insults. The boundary tool is for real situations, and learners should not have to sit through name-calling to practise it.
  • If a learner describes ongoing humiliation or intimidation at a job, refer them to lesson 4 (bullying and harassment) and to program support staff.

Post-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 4.1)

  1. "You see a guardrail missing on the second floor. What must you do?"
  2. "Give the four parts of a mistake report for this: you cut a door header 50 mm short."
  3. "Your foreman says 'This is wrong' and walks off. Which A B C D tool would you use, and what would you say?"

Summary

Time: 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • what accountability is, and what it is not
  • the BC duty to report unsafe conditions and acts
  • control, influence and no control
  • reporting a mistake in four parts
  • the A B C D tools for answering criticism
