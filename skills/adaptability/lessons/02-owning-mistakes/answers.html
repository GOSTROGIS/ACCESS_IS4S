Owning your work and your mistakes

Report mistakes early, focus on what you control, and answer criticism without blaming or caving in.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Demonstrate responsibility; Seek self-improvement

Task 1 (level 1)

  1. E. Blames the drawing.
  2. O. Names the error and the next step.
  3. E, as worded. It blames others. An owning version: "I didn't see the pour moved. Where should I check the schedule each day?"
  4. O. Admits being late and asks for the safety information.
  5. E. Points at others instead of fixing their own tools.

Task 2 (level 1)

  • Control: what time you get up, the questions you ask in class, whether you study tonight, whether you report a frayed extension cord.
  • Influence: your foreman's mood (you can affect it a little by how you work and speak), what a co-worker says about you (you can affect it by your actions).
  • No control: the bus being late, the date of your Level 1 exam.

Accept "influence" for the exam date if the learner explains they could ask to write at another sitting. Accept "no control" for a co-worker's words with a reason.

Task 3 (level 2)

Open answer. A good report covers all four parts and is made right away, not tomorrow morning.

Example: "Hi, it's Sam. In Suite 204 the panel directory has circuits 7 and 9 labelled the wrong way round. I've confirmed it with the tester and I haven't changed anything else. Can I fix the labels now, before the 9:00 inspection, or do you want to check it first? Next time I'll test each circuit before I write the directory."

Check that the reply:

  • states the facts without blaming anyone
  • says what Sam already did
  • asks for a decision or help
  • names one specific change

Task 4 (level 2)

Open answer. For the example decision, a good ripple includes:

  • Ring 1 (you): more money this week, but missed learning, possible gap before an exam, risk to program attendance rules.
  • Ring 2 (people closest): family may benefit from the pay or miss time with you.
  • Ring 3 (others): a classmate loses a study partner, the instructor needs to catch you up, the employer gets help for a shift.

Look for both good and bad effects and at least one person in each ring.

Task 5 (level 2)

Open answer. Suggested tools and sample replies:

  1. Acknowledge, then Boundaries. "You're right, I left it open. I'll lock it every night. I do care about this job, so please talk to me about it directly."
  2. Clarify. "Which part looks sloppy to you? Show me and I'll fix it."
  3. Disagree. "I disagree. I've been on time every day this month. If something came up today, let's talk about it."
  4. Boundaries. "Can we talk over here? I'm happy to hear what was wrong with the cut, but not with name-calling."

A good reply is calm, short, honest, and does not attack back.

Check yourself

  1. Report it to a supervisor or the employer as soon as possible.
  2. What happened, what I have done, what I need, what I will do next time.
  3. Any two of: your actions, your words, when you leave home, how you answer criticism, whether you report hazards, whether you study.
  4. Clarify.
