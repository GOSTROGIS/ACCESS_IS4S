# Keep going after rejected work

Adaptability lesson 11 of 14. Total time: 90 minutes.

## Opening

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Share a time your own work was failed by an inspector, a test or a foreman, and what you did next. Keep it honest and short. Ask: "What is the first thing you feel when someone rejects your work? What is the first thing you want to say?" Explain that this is Adaptability lesson 11 of 14, that it pulls together lessons 2 (owning mistakes), 3 (responding to change) and 4 (staying calm), and that it runs 90 minutes.

## Objective

**Time:** included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

- stay calm and respond in a useful way when work is rejected (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)
- read a notice of deficiency, find the real cause and check work against BC Building Code stair limits (Persist and persevere)
- plan a fix and a re-inspection, and decide when to keep trying, change the approach or ask for help (Persist and persevere)
- add a check to their own routine so the same error does not happen again (Seek self-improvement)

## Pre-assessment

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 1.2)

1. "Two stair risers are 190 mm and 196 mm. What is the difference?"
2. "An inspector fails your work. Who do you tell first?"

### Notes for the instructor

- The stair rules come from BC Building Code 2024, Part 9. Numeracy lesson 16 teaches the stair math in more depth. If learners have done it, go faster through step 2.2.
- The City of Fraser Bend and its 2-business-day booking rule are fictional. Real municipalities set their own booking rules. Tell learners to check with the authority having jurisdiction.
- Some learners carry heavy experiences of being judged or rejected. Keep the focus on work, not on worth. Task 10 is optional to share.

## Post-assessment

**Time:** 5 minutes (step 4.1)

1. "The top riser of a private stair is 204 mm. Does it pass Table 9.8.4.1?" (No. The limit is 200 mm.)
2. "Your fix has failed twice the same way. What should you do?" (Change the approach or ask for help.)
3. "Name one step you would take before calling for a re-inspection."

## Summary

**Time:** 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- a rejection is about the work, not the person
- the six steps after rejected work
- the BC private stair limits: 200 mm maximum rise, 5 mm between adjacent risers, 10 mm across a flight
- laying out from finished floor to finished floor
- planning a fix and a re-inspection, and adding a check to your routine
