## Task 3 (level 2)

**Yes, it meets the 10 mm rule.** Tallest 189 mm, shortest 182 mm. 189 − 182 = 7 mm. 7 is not more than 10.

Check the adjacent pairs too: 3, 1, 2, 1, 4 and 2 mm. All are 5 mm or less, so the flight also meets the 5 mm rule.

## Task 4 (level 2)

## Task 5 (level 3)

a.

- 13 risers: 2745 ÷ 13 = 211.15..., about **211.2 mm**
- 14 risers: 2745 ÷ 14 = 196.07..., about **196.1 mm**
- 15 risers: 2745 ÷ 15 = **183.0 mm**

b. **14 and 15 risers.** 13 risers gives 211.2 mm, which is over 200 mm.

## Task 7 (level 2)

Open answer. A good report has all four parts. Example:

"Hi, it's Fatima. The inspector failed the Suite 3 rough-in. Two boxes in the kitchen have no support. I've marked both boxes with tape and left the rest as is. I need about 30 minutes and four box brackets to fix them, and the re-inspection booked after that. Next time I'll check every box for support before I call the rough-in done."

## Task 8 (level 3)

Open answer for the table. A good plan includes:

| What | Who | When | How long | How I will check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measure finished floors and total rise | Tom | Tue a.m. | 1 h | Tape, finished floor to finished floor |
| Lay out and cut new stringers | Tom and journeyperson | Tue p.m. | 3 h | Check the layout against 2673 ÷ 14 |
| Install stringers and treads | Tom and journeyperson | Wed | 6 h | Level and square |
| Measure all 14 risers | Tom | Wed p.m. | 30 min | Every riser 190 or 191 mm, pairs within 5 mm |
| Book the re-inspection | Foreman | Wed p.m. | 10 min | Booking number written down |

Earliest re-inspection: **Friday.** Booking on Wednesday, 2 business days ahead, gives Thursday as day 1 and Friday as day 2.

## Task 9 (level 3)

a. **Change the approach, or ask for help.** The same fix (tightening reachable joints) failed twice in almost the same way. A third try of the same thing will probably fail too.

b. Any three, for example:

- test the line in sections to find which part loses pressure
- check joints he could not reach, and the test cap and gauge connection
- listen for a hiss, or use leak detection solution on joints
- check whether a valve in the line is letting by
- ask a journeyperson to look with him

c. Tell the foreman **now, after the second fail**, before trying again: what failed twice, what he has checked, and what help or time he needs.
