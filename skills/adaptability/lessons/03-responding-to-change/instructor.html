Responding to change on a project

Read a change notice, sort what changed from what stayed the same, and rebuild your plan for the day.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Persist and persevere; Set or adjust your goals and expectations; Plan and prioritize

Adaptability lesson 3 of 14. Total time: 75 minutes.

Opening

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Tell a short story about a day on a job when the plan fell apart, and what the crew did next. Ask: "What is the most common change you have seen on a job?" Explain that this is Adaptability lesson 3 of 14, and that the session is 75 minutes.

Objective

Time: included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • sort a written change into what changed, what stayed the same and what is unknown (Persist and persevere)
  • rebuild a day's plan around a change, keeping the work that still fits (Plan and prioritize)
  • decide when to keep trying, change the approach, or stop and report (Persist and persevere)
  • name their own steady anchors (Set or adjust your goals and expectations)

Pre-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.2)

  1. "When your plan for the day changes suddenly, what do you usually do first?"
  2. "What is one change you saw coming last month, and one you did not?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening story, objectives, session length. Listen. Answer the opening question. 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Answer aloud. 5 min
2.1 Warm-up from the ACCESS lesson: write CHANGE in the middle of the flip chart. Ask how work, tools and daily life have changed since their grandparents' and parents' time. Record answers around the word. Call out changes. Flip chart 7 min
2.2 Teach expected and unexpected change and the five steps. Show where the revision letter sits on a real drawing title block if one is available. Name one expected and one unexpected change on a site. Learner page, a sample drawing 8 min
2.3 Read Site memo 14 aloud. Model sorting item 1 into the three lists. Complete Task 1 in pairs. Learner page 10 min
3.1 Walk through Task 2 a and b on the board. Let pairs build their own plan for c. Ask two pairs to show different plans. Stress that more than one plan can work. Complete Task 2. Learner page, whiteboard 13 min
3.2 Go through the four questions in "Keep trying, or change the approach?" Complete Task 3. Compare answers in a group of three. Learner page 9 min
3.3 Introduce steady anchors and the ACCESS "roots and wings" idea. Keep sharing optional. Learners who prefer not to write about their own life can write about a fictional worker. Complete Tasks 4 and 5. Volunteers share one item. Learner page 10 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer. 5 min
4.2 Summarize. Name the first step they will take next time a plan changes. 3 min

Total: 5 + 5 + 7 + 8 + 10 + 13 + 9 + 10 + 5 + 3 = 75 minutes.

The learner page also has a figure of the five steps and a worked example that sorts a plumbing crew's memo and rebuilds its day. It uses a different memo from the tasks, so it does not give away the Task 1 and Task 2 answers. Walk through it before learners read Site memo 14. These fit inside the existing step times.

Notes for the instructor

  • Task 5 asks about a turning point. Some learners' turning points involve loss, care, residential school impacts in their families, or the justice system. Make sharing fully optional and do not ask follow-up questions in front of the group.
  • The old ACCESS lesson's theory box on overstimulation and "destimulation tactics" closely follows Alvin Toffler's book Future Shock (1970). This lesson keeps only the idea of steady anchors, credited to Toffler, in new wording.

Post-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 4.1)

  1. "A text says the concrete truck moved from 8:00 to 10:30. What changed, what stays the same, and what would you ask?"
  2. "You've tried to start the generator three times the same way and it won't go. Keep trying, change the approach, or stop and report?"
  3. "Name one of your steady anchors, if you are willing to share."

Summary

Time: 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • expected and unexpected change on a project
  • the five steps: notice, sort, ask, adjust, check
  • rebuilding a day plan after a change notice
  • when to keep trying, change the approach, or stop and report
  • steady anchors in our own lives
