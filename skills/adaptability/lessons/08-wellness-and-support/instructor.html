Adaptability · Lesson 08 · Instructor

Looking after yourself with wellness and support in BC

Look after your sleep, energy and boundaries, map your supports, and know which BC help line to call and when.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Regulate your emotions when appropriate; Seek self-improvement; Demonstrate responsibility

Adaptability lesson 8 of 8. Total time: 90 minutes.

Opening

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Share how you look after yourself through hard stretches of work, such as a long project, shift work or time away at camp. Keep it practical. Ask: "What helps you get through a tough week?" Explain that this is the last of eight Adaptability lessons, and that the session is 90 minutes.

Before the session, check whether an Elder or ACCESS cultural support person can join for step 2.3. If one can, give them that time and follow their lead.

Objective

Time: included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • describe the sleep and activity basics that support fitness for work (Demonstrate responsibility)
  • describe the FNHA First Nations Perspective on Health and Wellness and use it to reflect on their own wellness (Seek self-improvement)
  • say no to a request while keeping the relationship (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)
  • map their own support network and backup plans (Seek self-improvement)
  • choose the right BC support line for a situation (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)

Pre-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.2)

From the ACCESS self-care and boundaries lesson:

  1. "Write down why self-care is important."
  2. "Write down why boundaries are important."

Keep the papers. Learners compare them with their answers at the post-assessment.

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening story, objectives, session length. Answer the opening question. 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Write answers. Paper, pens 5 min
2.1 Ping-pong activity adapted from the ACCESS lesson. A volunteer puts on mittens and a heavy sweater. Toss ping-pong balls to them one at a time, naming a responsibility for each ("rent", "class", "work", "kids", "a friend in trouble"). Keep going until balls drop. Then ask the group how the volunteer could cope: put some balls down, ask someone else to hold a few, take the mittens off (rest). Debrief: what are the mittens in real life? Watch or take part. Discuss what the balls and the mittens stand for. 24 ping-pong balls, mittens, sweater 10 min
2.2 Teach the sleep and activity basics and WorkSafeBC's fitness-for-work rule. Go through the stress signs. Complete Task 5. Learner page 10 min
2.3 Present the FNHA First Nations Perspective on Health and Wellness, using FNHA's poster or web page. Say clearly that this is FNHA's model, that FNHA calls it a starting point for discussion, and that each Nation has its own teachings. If an Elder or cultural support person is present, hand over to them. Complete Task 1 privately. Volunteers share one small action, if they wish. FNHA web page or poster 15 min
3.1 Teach boundaries and the three steps for saying no, from the ACCESS lesson. Model one. Discuss the ACCESS self-esteem question: "Can money buy self-esteem?" Write five situations where it is hard to say no (the original ACCESS exercise), then complete Task 3. Deliver one "no" to the class. Learner page 15 min
3.2 Explain the three kinds of support and why backup plans matter for keeping a job. Complete Task 4 privately. Learner page 10 min
3.3 Go through the BC supports table line by line. Point out the difference between 9-1-1, 9-8-8 and 310-6789. Suggest learners save two numbers in their phones now. Complete Task 2. Save numbers if they wish. Learner page, phones 12 min
4.1 Post-assessment. Answer, then compare with the pre-assessment papers. Pre-assessment papers 5 min
4.2 Summarize. Name one thing they will do for themselves this week. 3 min

Notes for the instructor

  • Cultural content. The old ACCESS "Walk in Balance" lesson had a "Resources & the Medicine Wheel" lecture with no notes and no source. Medicine wheel teachings are not shared by all Nations and belong to the Nations and knowledge keepers who hold them. This lesson uses the FNHA model, which is published by a BC First Nations health organization and credited to it, and leaves room for an Elder or knowledge keeper to share their own Nation's teachings. Do not teach a general "Indigenous" version of balance teachings.
  • Mental health safety. This lesson is not counselling. If a learner says they are thinking about suicide, stay with them, take it seriously, and connect them to 9-8-8 or 1-800-784-2433 and to program support staff right away. If someone is in immediate danger, call 9-1-1. Know your organization's procedure before teaching this lesson.
  • Privacy. Tasks 1 and 4 are private. Do not collect them.
  • Check the numbers. Phone numbers and services change. Check the supports table against helpstartshere.gov.bc.ca before each delivery.
  • The 2014 article "Weaving Soft Skills Development into Everyday Employment Services in BC" by Valerie G. Ward and Dorothy I. Riddle identified social supports as a soft skill that many BC job seekers need help with. Only that topic is used here.

Post-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 4.1)

  1. "Now, why is self-care important? Why are boundaries important?" Compare with the pre-assessment.
  2. "What is the difference between 9-1-1, 9-8-8 and 310-6789?"
  3. "Say no to this: a friend asks you to skip class to help them move."

Summary

Time: 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • sleep, activity and being fit for work
  • the FNHA First Nations Perspective on Health and Wellness
  • boundaries and saying no
  • building a support network and backup plans
  • which BC support line to use, and when

Keep practising these skills, and notice them in others, so they get stronger.
