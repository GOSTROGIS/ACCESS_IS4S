Staying calm under pressure and handling anger

Notice anger early, cool down in ways that work, and say what you need without blowing up or bottling it up.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Regulate your emotions when appropriate; Demonstrate responsibility

Adaptability lesson 4 of 8. Total time: 90 minutes.

Opening

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.1)

Welcome learners. Tell a short story about a time pressure got to you or someone on your crew, and what happened next. Keep it light enough that nobody feels singled out. Ask: "What gets people most wound up on a job site?" Explain that this is Adaptability lesson 4 of 8, and that the session is 90 minutes.

Objective

Time: included in step 1.1

By the end of the session learners will be able to:

  • name their own early signs of anger (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)
  • recognize eight anger styles and the effect of each (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)
  • choose a cool-down action supported by current research (Regulate your emotions when appropriate)
  • turn an outburst into an assertive statement (Regulate your emotions when appropriate, Demonstrate responsibility)
  • tell reasonable direction from bullying and harassment, and know where to report it (Demonstrate responsibility)

Pre-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 1.2)

  1. "What do you usually do when you get angry at work or in class?"
  2. "Does 'blowing off steam' make anger go away? Why do you think so?"

Note the answers to question 2. Come back to them in step 2.3.

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening story, objectives, session length. Answer the opening question. 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Answer aloud. 5 min
2.1 Explain that anger is normal and often signals that something matters. Brainstorm body signs as a group. Complete Task 1. Whiteboard 10 min
2.2 Teach the eight anger styles from the ACCESS lesson. Ask which styles hurt the person, which hurt others, and which get results. Complete Task 2 in pairs. Discuss the effect of each style on the crew. Learner page 15 min
2.3 Return to pre-assessment question 2. Explain the 2024 review. Point out that the old ACCESS quiz counted jogging and throwing darts as better ways to handle anger, and that newer evidence shows calming activities work better. Offer an optional one-minute slow-breathing practice. Learners may sit it out. Discuss. Try the breathing practice if they choose. Learner page 10 min
3.1 Model the "When, I, I need" statement. Do the first outburst in Task 3 together. Complete Task 3. Read one aloud to a partner. Learner page 15 min
3.2 Explain that cool-down plans are personal and no one strategy fits everyone. Remind learners that the plan is private. Complete Task 4. Learner page 10 min
3.3 Teach what to do when anger comes at you. Read the WorkSafeBC description of bullying and harassment and the reporting steps. Complete Task 5 in small groups. Share answers. Learner page 12 min
4.1 Ask the post-assessment questions. Answer. 5 min
4.2 Summarize. Name one thing from the cool-down list they will try. 3 min

Total: 5 + 5 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 12 + 5 + 3 = 90 minutes.

Notes for the instructor

  • This lesson teaches a skill. It is not anger-management therapy. If a learner describes violence at home, threats or fear for their safety, speak with them privately and connect them with program support staff and the supports listed in lesson 8. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
  • Do not make breathing, eyes-closed or mindfulness practice compulsory. Some learners find them uncomfortable, and the Adaptability chapter does not require them.
  • Keep role plays about the fictional site scenarios. Stop any role play that becomes a real conflict between learners.
  • The original ACCESS emotional intelligence lessons copied text from HelpGuide.org, and the scenarios had no stated source. This lesson uses only the topics, with new scenarios.

Post-assessment

Time: 5 minutes (step 4.1)

  1. "Your partner drops your level and it breaks. You feel your face get hot. What do you do in the next two minutes?"
  2. "Say an assertive statement for this: a co-worker keeps parking in front of the gate."
  3. "A journeyperson keeps mocking your accent in front of the crew. What can you do?"

Summary

Time: 3 minutes (step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • anger as a normal feeling, and its early body signs
  • eight anger styles and their effects
  • calming activities that reduce anger, according to current research
  • assertive statements: when, I, I need
  • what to do when anger comes at you, and how to report bullying and harassment
