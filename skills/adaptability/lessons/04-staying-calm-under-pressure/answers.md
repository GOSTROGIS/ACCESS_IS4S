# Staying calm under pressure and handling anger

## Task 1 (level 1)

Open and personal. Any three body signs are correct, for example a hot face, fast heartbeat, clenched jaw or fists, tight shoulders, shallow breathing, a louder voice, shaking hands or racing thoughts. The circled sign is the learner's own choice.

## Task 2 (level 1)

1. Exploding.
2. Withholding.
3. Body (anger showing up as physical symptoms). Accept "holding it in" as well.
4. Turning it on yourself.
5. Assertive.
6. Redirecting.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Open answer. Sample statements:

1. "When my tape goes missing, I lose time looking for it. I need people to ask before they borrow it."
2. "When the schedule changes and I don't hear about it, I end up in the wrong place. I need changes sent to the crew by text."
3. "When I'm left to finish the job alone, I fall behind. I need one more person on this for the next hour."

Check that each statement:

- describes what happened without insults or "you always"
- says how it affected the speaker
- asks for something specific and reasonable

## Task 4 (level 2)

Open and private. A complete plan fills in all four lines with something the learner can really do on a site. Calming actions from the 2024 review (slow breathing, relaxing muscles, stepping away) are good choices. If a learner writes "go hit something", explain that this did not reduce anger in the research and suggest a calming option instead.

## Task 5 (level 2)

1. **Reasonable direction.** Fixing a wrong cut is part of the job. Ask what went wrong and fix it.
2. **Possible bullying and harassment.** It is repeated and meant to humiliate. Report it to the employer, following the employer's procedure.
3. **Reasonable direction.** It is a work decision caused by the crane being down.
4. **Possible bullying and harassment.** Repeated name-calling in front of others is meant to humiliate. Report it to the employer. If the employer does not take reasonable steps, call the WorkSafeBC Prevention Information Line.
5. **Reasonable direction.** Discipline for repeated lateness without calling is part of managing workers. Own it (lesson 2) and call ahead next time.

## Check yourself

1. The earlier you notice, the more choices you have before you say or do something you regret, or before someone gets hurt.
2. Activities that calm the body down.
3. When [what happened], I [how it affected me]. I need [what I am asking for].
4. Your employer, using the employer's reporting procedure.
