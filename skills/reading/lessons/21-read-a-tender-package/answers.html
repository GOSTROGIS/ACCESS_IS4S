Read a tender package

Find your way through the drawings, specifications and addenda in a bid package, and use the order of precedence to decide which document wins when they disagree.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to read; Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) 2:00 p.m. on March 12. Late bids are not opened. (Document A, item 1) b) March 5. Questions go to the architect in writing. (Document A, item 2) c) Only the architect, by a written addendum. No other answer, written or spoken, changes the documents. (Document A, item 3)

Task 2 (level 1)

a) 06 10 00 Rough Carpentry b) 05 50 00 Metal Fabrications c) 09 91 00 Painting

Task 3 (level 2)

a) Drawing. Location in the room is "where". b) Specifications. Material thickness and type is "which material". c) Specifications. Number of coats is "how the work is done". d) Drawing. Shape is "what it looks like", shown on a plan or detail. e) Specifications. Colour is a product requirement. (Then check the addenda.)

Some learners may say the plywood thickness can also appear in a drawing note. That is true, and Task 6 shows what to do when the two disagree.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) At 1:50, 1 mm on the drawing stands for 50 mm in real life. 62 × 50 = 3100 mm

b) Build to 3000 mm, the written dimension. Document A, item 5 e: "Written dimensions govern. Do not scale the drawings." The 100 mm difference may come from the sheet being printed a little too large. If the written dimensions around it do not add up, ask a question in writing.

Task 5 (level 2)

650 mm. Both sheets are dated February 10, so the larger-scale drawing governs (Document A, item 5 c). Detail 2 on A-301 is at 1:10, which is a larger scale than the 1:50 plan on A-101.

Task 6 (level 2)

19 mm, from the specification. In Document A, item 5, specifications (b) rank above drawings (c). So spec section 06 10 00 wins over the note in Detail 4.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) 1000 mm. Addendum 1 (February 24) changed door D2 from 900 mm to 1000 mm. Addenda rank first in Document A, item 5 a.

b) The bid form must list every addendum received (Document A, item 4). Leaving out Addendum 2 shows the estimator may not have priced the change of paint colour. The owner may reject the bid, or the company may be held to the full documents and have to do work it did not price. Either way, it can cost the job or cost money.

c) No. Document A, item 3: only a written addendum from the architect changes the documents, and no other answer, written or spoken, counts. A site visitor's comment changes nothing. Price Slate Blue, as Addendum 2 says.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Both instructions are in the specifications. The order of precedence ranks specs above drawings, but here two spec sections disagree with each other. Both have the same date. Nothing in Document A says which spec section wins.

b) A sample question:

To: Alder & Moss Architects Job: Tool library fit-out, Unit 4 Date: March 2 Question: Section 05 50 00 says the fabricator shop primes the steel guard with one coat of grey primer. Section 09 91 00 says the painter primes all bare steel on site before finish coats. Please confirm who primes the guard, and whether shop primer must also be applied, so both trades price it once.

Points a good question covers: - names the job and the date - names both spec sections and says exactly what each one says - asks one clear question - sent in writing before the March 5 deadline

Check yourself 1

Drawings show what and where: the location, shape and size of the work. Specifications are written requirements: which materials and products, what quality and how the work is done.

Check yourself 2

A formal written change to the bid documents, issued before the bid closes. Once issued, it is part of the documents and must be included in the price.

Check yourself 3

The 1:10 detail, because it is the larger scale. Check the order of precedence in your own package to be sure.

Check yourself 4

A written dimension is what the designer intended. A printed sheet can shrink, stretch or be printed at the wrong size, so a scaled length can be wrong. Most packages say written dimensions govern.

Check yourself 5

No. Only a written addendum changes the bid documents. If a call tells you something important, ask for it in writing.
