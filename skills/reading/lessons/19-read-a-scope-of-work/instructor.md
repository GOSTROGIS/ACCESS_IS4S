# Read a scope of work

Session 19 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 60 minutes

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone on a job been asked to do 'one more quick thing'? Did you get paid for it?" Take two or three stories. Tell a short one: a steel crew installs a handrail. The superintendent asks them to "just add" a second rail on a ramp. It takes a day and a half and more steel. When the invoice goes in, the general contractor says, "That was never in your price." Nobody wrote anything down. Today learners practise reading a scope of work so they know what is in and what is out before they say yes.

This is session 19 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 60 minutes.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the work included, exclusions, assumptions and acceptance criteria in a scope of work (Identify the information)
- connect a site request to the section of the scope that answers it (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- decide whether a request is in scope, out of scope or an assumption problem, and say what to do next (Understand and apply the information)
- spot a gap in a scope that could cause an argument later (Evaluate the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (4 min)

1. "What do you think 'by others' means on a job document?"
2. "If the boss on site asks you to do extra work, is that enough to start?"

## Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

- work included, exclusions, assumptions and acceptance criteria
- reading the included list as a checklist: if it is not on the list, it is not in the price
- "by others" and "by owner" in exclusions
- telling the supervisor when an assumption breaks
- scope creep, and asking for a written change order before extra work starts

Total time: 5 + 3 + 4 + 38 + 6 + 4 = 60 minutes.

### Notes for the instructor

- Message (c) is the hardest to sort. It is not extra work, so it is not "out of scope". It breaks the first assumption in section 4. The crew should tell its own supervisor, because a lost install day may change the schedule or the price.
- Message (d) is out of scope twice over: removing the old railing is excluded ("By Kestrel Point"), and section 2.7 only covers Fraser Bend's own scrap.
- Some learners will say "just do the small stuff to keep the customer happy". That is a real choice a company can make, but the decision belongs to the person who signs change orders, not to the crew.
