Read a scope of work

Find what the crew must do, what is left out and when the work counts as done, and spot extra work that was never in the scope.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

Before a crew starts, the company agrees on a scope of work: a list of exactly what it will do for the price. On site, people ask for "one more quick thing" all the time. If the crew says yes to work that was never in the scope, the company pays for the hours and the material, and the job runs late. Knowing what is in and what is out lets you answer the request politely and send it to the right person.

Key ideas

1. The four parts of a scope. Most scopes of work have: - Work included: what the crew will do. Often a numbered list. - Exclusions: what the crew will not do, often marked "by others" or "by owner". - Assumptions: things the price depends on, such as "the stair is finished before we install". - Acceptance criteria: how everyone will know the work is done. Think of it as a "done when" list.

2. Read the included list as a checklist. Each numbered item is one piece of the job. If a piece of work is not on the list, it is not in the price.

3. Exclusions tell you who does it. "Finish paint by others" means another trade paints. "Permit by owner" means the owner gets the permit. Your crew does not.

4. Assumptions can break. If an assumption turns out to be false (the stair is not ready, there is no power), tell your supervisor the same day. The price or the schedule may have to change.

5. "Done" means every acceptance item is met. Finishing the install is not the same as the work being accepted. Check each item on the "done when" list.

6. Scope creep. Small extra requests on site add up. This is called scope creep. Do not refuse rudely, and do not just do it. Point to the scope, say who decides, and ask for a written change order. If something in the scope is unclear, ask a question in writing: see Communication lesson 12. For how to say no politely, see Communication lesson 04.

Worked example

Question: Does Fraser Bend have to paint the new handrail black?

  1. Look in Work included (section 2). Item 2.4 says "Shop prime all steel with one coat of grey primer." Item 2.6 says touch up the primer. Nothing about a finish colour.
  2. Look in Exclusions (section 3). It says "Finish paint. By others (painting contractor)."
  3. No. Fraser Bend primes the steel grey. The painting contractor does the finish paint.

Practice documents

All documents below are for fictional companies.

Document A: Scope of work

Fraser Bend Metalworks Ltd. (fictional company) · Subcontract SC-14 · Scope of work For: Kestrel Point Builders Ltd. (fictional company), general contractor Job: Tool library fit-out, Unit 4 (fictional building) Date: March 2

1. Purpose Kestrel Point needs a handrail on the new mezzanine stair and a guard along the mezzanine edge so the tool library can open.

2. Work included 2.1 Prepare shop drawings of the handrail and guard. Send them to Kestrel Point for review before fabrication starts. 2.2 Fabricate two steel stair handrails: one 4.2 m long and one 3.6 m long. 2.3 Fabricate one steel guard, 6.0 m long, for the mezzanine edge. 2.4 Shop prime all steel with one coat of grey primer. 2.5 Deliver to site. Install and anchor the handrails and guard. 2.6 Touch up the primer on site after installation. 2.7 Remove our own cut-offs, scrap and packaging from site at the end of each day.

3. Exclusions - Finish paint. By others (painting contractor). - Building permit. By owner. - Removing the existing wood railing. By Kestrel Point. - Handrails at the exterior ramp. - Structural design of the stair and mezzanine. By owner's engineer.

4. Assumptions - The stair and mezzanine are complete and ready for anchors on install day. - 120 V power is available within 15 m of the work. - Access is through the rear loading door. - Work is done on day shift, Monday to Friday.

5. Acceptance criteria (done when) - Installed as shown on the reviewed shop drawings. - All anchors in place and tight. - No sharp edges or weld spatter. - Primer touched up. - Kestrel Point's site superintendent has walked the work and signed the checklist.

6. Changes Any work not listed in section 2 needs a written change order signed by Kestrel Point's project manager before the work starts. A verbal request does not count.

Document B: Texts and requests on site during install week

a) Site superintendent: Can you guys put a handrail on the outside ramp while you're here? Quick job. b) Site superintendent: Painter starts Friday. Make sure your primer touch-ups are done Thursday. c) Kestrel Point lead hand: Heads up, the stair treads aren't set yet. You can't anchor to it until Monday. d) Site superintendent: Can you throw the old wood railing in your bin when you leave? e) Owner's rep: Can you paint it black instead of grey? It would look better. f) Site superintendent: Please sweep up your cut-offs before you go tonight.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Use Document A. a) Who is doing the work, and who hired them? b) What is the total length of steel rail and guard Fraser Bend will make, in metres?

Task 2 (level 1) Name two things in the Exclusions, and who does each one.

Task 3 (level 2) Read each message in Document B. Sort it into one of three groups, and give the section of Document A that tells you. - In scope: the crew should do it. - Out of scope: it needs a written change order first, or it belongs to someone else. - Assumption problem: something the price depends on is not true. Tell the supervisor.

Task 4 (level 2) The crew finishes installing on Wednesday. The anchors are tight, the edges are clean and the primer is touched up. The site superintendent is away until Friday. Is the work done and accepted? Explain using section 5.

Task 5 (level 3) Answer message (a) from the site superintendent. Be polite. Name the section of the scope, say what is needed before your crew can do it, and who has to sign.

Task 6 (level 3) Read Document A as a whole. Find one thing it does not say that could cause an argument later. Say why.

Check yourself

  1. What does "by others" mean in an exclusion?
  2. What is an acceptance criterion?
  3. A request for extra work arrives by text. What does it need before you start?
  4. Why read the assumptions, not just the work included?
