Orthographic projection: read the views of an object

Read the front, top and right side views of an object drawn in third-angle projection, find hidden features, and match a set of views to the object it shows.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) The top view. b) The right side view. c) Third-angle projection. The symbol shows the circles on the left and the narrow end of the cone pointing at the circles. The layout agrees: top view above, right side view to the right.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) Width. The left and right edges of the two views line up. b) Height. The top and bottom edges of the two views line up. c) Depth. The mitre line carries depth from the top view to the side view.

Task 3 (level 1)

a) Hidden lines (two thin dashed lines). b) The groove is in the back face. From the front you cannot see it, because the front of the block is in the way. c) The top view. Looking down, you see the notch in the back edge as object lines.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) The front view and the right side view. The front view shows the step as an L shape. The side view shows the step as a solid line across the view, 40 mm below the top. b) The top view. c) On the right. In the right side view the front of the object is on the left, next to the front view, so the back is on the right. The dashed line near the right edge is the groove.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) 100 mm long. The steel is 10 mm thick. b) 18 mm apart. They are the two sides of the Ø18 hole, 9 mm each side of the centre line. c) A hole looks like a circle only when you look straight down its length. In the front view you look straight through the hole. In the top and side views you look across it, so you see its two sides as hidden lines.

Task 6 (level 2)

  • A matches views 2. The front view has a hidden line across it (the low step at the back cannot be seen from the front). The right side view is an L, high at the front.
  • B matches views 3. The front view is a U shape. The top view has two lines running front to back for the slot.
  • C matches views 1. The front view has a notch at the top right. The top view shows the notch at the front right corner.

Points a good response covers: gives all three matches and names a feature in at least one view for each.

Task 7 (level 2)

a) First-angle projection. The top view below the front view is the first-angle layout. b) The cone is on the left and the circles are on the right. The narrow end of the cone points away from the circles. c) The fabricator would read the views on the wrong sides. The top view would be read as a bottom view, and the side view as the opposite side. Holes, notches or brackets could be built on the wrong side, making a mirror image of the part. Always check the symbol before you read the views.

Task 8 (level 2)

a) The top view. You see the branch opening as two circles. b) The bore (inside) of the run and the branch. You cannot see the inside from outside, so it is drawn with hidden lines. c) 6 mm. (60 − 48) ÷ 2 = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 mm.

Task 9 (level 3)

a) Width 6 × 25 = 150 mm. Depth 4 × 25 = 100 mm. Height 4 × 25 = 100 mm. b) A notch (rabbet) along the bottom of the front face, the full width of the block. The line across the front view is the top edge of the notch, 2 squares (50 mm) up. In the top view the notch is under the top surface, so its back edge shows as a hidden line 2 squares (50 mm) back from the front. c) The right side view sits to the right of the front view, with its top and bottom lined up with the front view. It is 4 squares wide (depth) and 4 squares high. The notch is at the bottom left (the front of the block is on the left), 2 squares wide and 2 squares high.

Answer figure. The right side view is an L shape, 4 squares by 4 squares, with a 2 by 2 notch cut from the bottom left corner.

Task 10 (level 3)

No, the sketch is wrong. In third-angle projection the front of the object faces the front view. In the right side view the front of the object is on the left, next to the front view. The upright leg is at the back of the bracket, so it must be on the right side of the view, as in Figure 3.

What could happen: a fitter working from the sketch could weld the upright leg to the wrong edge of the base. The hole would then be on the wrong side. The bracket would be a mirror image and might not bolt up. Check a sketch against the front view and top view before it goes to the shop.

Task 11 (level 3)

Sample answer (other wording is fine if it gives the same cuts):

  1. Start with the piece 150 mm wide, 90 mm deep, 100 mm high. Mark the front face.
  2. Step: at the right end, cut away a block 50 mm wide and 40 mm down from the top. Cut all the way through from the front face to the back face.
  3. Groove: on the back face, measure 40 mm from the left end and mark a groove 20 mm wide (from 40 mm to 60 mm). Cut it 20 mm deep into the back face, from top to bottom.
  4. Check: from the front you should see the step but not the groove.

Points a good response covers: names the face for each cut, gives the size of each cut, gives the location from a named end, and says the step goes right through and the groove runs the full height.

Check yourself 1

The top view goes directly above the front view. The right side view goes directly to the right of the front view.

Check yourself 2

Width and height.

Check yourself 3

Thin short dashes. It is an edge that is there but cannot be seen from that side.

Check yourself 4

The front edge of the object.

Check yourself 5

Look at the projection symbol. If the narrow end of the cone points at the circles, it is third-angle. If it points away, it is first-angle. Also check the layout: a top view above the front view is third-angle, below is first-angle.
