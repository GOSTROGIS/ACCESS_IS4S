# Orthographic projection: read the views of an object

Session 11 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a simple object, such as a wood block with a notch or a short piece of steel angle. Ask three learners to describe it: one looking from the front, one from above, one from the side. Each sees a different shape. Ask: "Which one is right?" All of them. A drawing puts all three views on one sheet, in fixed places, so a reader can build the shape back in their head. Tell a short story: a fitter reads a supplier drawing as if the side view were on the other side, and welds a lug to the wrong face. The drawing was right. The reading was wrong.

This is session 11 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes. It starts the drawing series (lessons 11 to 18). Lesson 5 covered the words on a floor plan. This lesson covers how views work.

Before the session: bring two or three small objects (a notched block, a piece of angle with a hole, a pipe tee or elbow), grid paper, pencils and erasers. A clear plastic box helps show the "glass box" idea.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name the front, top and right side views and find them in a third-angle layout (Identify the information)
- follow one feature, such as a hole or a notch, from view to view using projection lines (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- use the views and given sizes to answer a job question and sketch a missing view (Understand and apply the information)
- spot a view placed on the wrong side, and check the projection symbol before trusting a drawing (Evaluate the document(s))

## Post-assessment (9 min)

1. Draw three empty boxes on the board in an L. "Label them for third-angle projection."
2. Show the bracket from Figure 3 and point at the top view. "Why are there dashed lines here?"
3. "You get a drawing with the top view below the front view. What do you check before you build anything?"

## Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

- front, top and right side views, and the two sizes each view shows
- the third-angle layout used in Canada and the USA
- how views line up, projection lines and the 45° mitre line
- object lines, hidden lines and centre lines
- first-angle projection and the projection symbol
- following one feature through all three views
- sketching a missing view on grid paper

Next session (lesson 12) covers pictorial drawings: isometric, oblique and perspective.
