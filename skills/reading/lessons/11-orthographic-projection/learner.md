# Orthographic projection: read the views of an object

## Why this matters on the job

A shop drawing for a steel bracket, a pipe spool or a stair part rarely shows a picture of the object. It shows flat views: the object seen straight on from the front, from above and from the side. A welder, pipefitter or carpenter has to put those views together in their head to see the real shape. If you read a view from the wrong side, a hole ends up on the wrong leg or a notch is cut on the wrong face. This lesson is the first in a set of drawing lessons. [Reading lesson 5](../05-read-a-floor-plan/learner.html) showed the words on a floor plan. These lessons show how the lines work.

## Key ideas

**1. A view is the object seen straight on.** In a **front view** you look straight at the front. In a **top view** you look straight down. In a **right side view** you look straight at the right end. Each view shows only two of the three sizes:

- The front view shows **width** and **height**.
- The top view shows **width** and **depth**.
- The right side view shows **depth** and **height**.

Drawing an object as a set of flat views is called **orthographic projection** (or multiview drawing). Most objects need only three views. A flat plate may need only one or two.

**2. Third-angle projection is used in Canada and the USA.** The views always sit in the same places:

- The **top view** goes directly **above** the front view.
- The **right side view** goes directly to the **right** of the front view.

A simple way to remember it: each view sits on the side it was looked at from.

**3. The views line up.** The front and top views share the same width, so their left and right edges line up. The front and right side views share the same height, so their top and bottom edges line up. The top and right side views share the same depth. Thin lines that carry a point from one view to the next are called **projection lines**. A **45° mitre line** turns depth in the top view into depth in the side view.

**4. In the top view and the side view, the front of the object faces the front view.** In the top view the front edge is at the bottom, next to the front view. In the right side view the front edge is on the left, next to the front view.

**5. Three kinds of line do most of the work.**

- An **object line** (thick, solid) is an edge you can see.
- A **hidden line** (thin, short dashes) is an edge you cannot see from that side, because it is behind or inside the object.
- A **centre line** (thin, long and short dashes) marks the centre of a hole, a pipe or a round part.

**6. First-angle projection puts the views on the other side.** Much of Europe and Asia uses **first-angle projection**. The views look the same, but the top view goes **below** the front view and the right side view goes to the **left**. A drawing should show a **projection symbol** near the title block. It is a cone drawn in two views. In the third-angle symbol the narrow end of the cone points at the circles. In the first-angle symbol it points away from them.

**7. Read one feature at a time.** Pick a line in one view. Follow the projection lines to find the same feature in the other two views. One view alone can fool you. A line in the front view could be a step, a groove or the edge of a hole. The other views tell you which.

*Figure 3. Steel angle bracket with one Ø18 hole, shown as a pictorial and in three views. The front view shows the hole as a circle with centre lines. The top view and the right side view show it as two hidden lines with a centre line.*

**Question:** Where does the hole show in each view, and why?

1. **Front view.** You look straight at the face with the hole. You see a circle. Centre lines cross at its centre.
2. **Top view.** You look down on the top edge of the upright leg. That edge is the strip 10 mm deep at the back of the view. The hole runs through the leg, under that strip, so you cannot see it. It shows as **two hidden lines** 18 mm apart, one on each side of a centre line.
3. **Right side view.** You look at the end of the angle. The upright leg is on the **right** of this view, because it is at the back of the object and the front of the object faces the front view. The hole is inside the leg, so it shows as **two hidden lines** across the leg.
4. Each hidden line is half the hole size from the centre: 18 ÷ 2 = 9 mm. So the lines are at 45 − 9 = **36 mm** and 45 + 9 = **54 mm** above the bottom.

**Answer:** a circle in the front view, and two hidden lines (with a centre line) in the top view and the right side view. The hidden lines in the side view are 36 mm and 54 mm above the bottom.

a) Which view is directly above the front view?
b) Which view is directly to the right of the front view?

**Task 2** (level 1)
Use Figure 1. Name the size (width, height or depth) that each pair of views shares.
a) Front view and top view
b) Front view and right side view
c) Top view and right side view

**Task 3** (level 1)
Use Figure 1.
a) What kind of line shows the groove in the front view?
b) Why is it drawn that way?
c) In which view can you see the groove as object lines?

**Task 4** (level 2)
The notched block in Figure 1 is 150 mm wide, 90 mm deep and 100 mm high. A carpenter wants to check the step at the top right before cutting.
a) In which two views can you see the height of the step?
b) In which view can you see the width of the groove as object lines?
c) The groove is at the back. In the right side view, is it on the left or the right? Explain using Key idea 4.

**Task 5** (level 2)
Use Figure 3 and Worked example 1. An ironworker is checking the bracket before welding.
a) How long is the bracket, and how thick is the steel?
b) In the top view, how far apart are the two hidden lines?
c) Why does the hole show as a circle in only one of the three views?

**Task 6** (level 2)
Use Figure 5. Match each block (A, B and C) to its set of views (1, 2 or 3). For each match, name one line or shape that helped you decide.

**Task 7** (level 2)

a) Which projection method was used?
b) What should the projection symbol look like?
c) What could go wrong if a fabricator read it as third-angle?

**Task 8** (level 2)
Use Figure 4. A plumber is checking a tee on a drawing.
a) In which view do you look straight into the branch?
b) What do the dashed lines in the front view show?
c) A reducing tee has a run with a 60 mm OD and a 48 mm bore. How thick is the wall?

**Task 10** (level 3)
