Reading · Lesson 10 · Answers

Read on a screen and check the source

Find information fast on a phone, and decide whether a web page, post or search summary can be trusted before you act on it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Evaluate the document(s); Reflect on the document(s)

Answers for Tasks 1, 2 and 5 were checked against the live pages on 2026-11-06. If a page has changed, use what the current official page says.

Task 1 (level 1)

A workplace label provides: 1. the product identifier (the name) 2. safe handling information 3. a reference to the SDS

Steps: open the page, open find on page, type "workplace label", and step through the matches until you reach the list.

Task 2 (level 1)

Section 8 is "Exposure controls/Personal protection".

Steps: scroll to "What information is on the SDS?" and read the table. If the table is cut off, zoom out, turn the phone sideways, or use find on page for "8".

Task 4 (level 2)

The address is worksafebc-update.example, not worksafebc.com. It adds an extra word to the real name, and it has a different ending. Both are common tricks. It also asks for your password from a link.

What to do: do not log in. Close the page. If you need something from WorkSafeBC, type worksafebc.com yourself or use a bookmark you made earlier. Tell your supervisor if you think a crew is being targeted.

Task 5 (level 2)

The page says the PDF does not include all updates made after it was published, so you must also check and download any applicable revisions, bulletins or other updates on the page.

Why it matters: a rule you read in the PDF may have been changed by a revision. Building to an old rule can fail an inspection.

Task 6 (level 3)

The summary is not right for BC. It does not match WorkSafeBC, and it says nothing about who wrote it.

A good message to the foreman: "That summary isn't from an official source. WorkSafeBC's WHMIS 2015 page says that in BC, SDSs must be checked every three years. Ours are four years old, so they're past due for a check. I can find the page for you."

Points a good response covers: says the summary is wrong, gives the correct rule (every three years), names WorkSafeBC as the source, and draws the right conclusion for a four-year-old SDS. Accept any respectful wording.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Result 1 is written for BC workers and employers who need to know the rules. Result 2 is written for people who might buy a binder kit.

b) Result 1 informs: it states the rule and the reason ("to ensure they include the most current information"). Result 2 uses fear and urgency: capital letters, "Expert Warns!", "Don't get fined!", and a price.

c) A page that gains from your choice, such as a sale, has a reason to shape what you believe. The official page has no product to sell. That does not make every official page perfect, but it tells you where to start, and it means you should check result 2's claims before acting on them.

Check yourself 1

Use the find on page tool in your browser's menu (in Safari on iPhone it is called Search Document) and type the word.

Check yourself 2

Any two of: worksafebc.com, gov.bc.ca, bclaws.gov.bc.ca. Also accept canada.ca for federal rules.

Check yourself 3

Any three of: who published it (the web address), the date or version, whether it names its evidence or source, whether it is trying to sell something, whether the same thing appears on an official page.

Check yourself 4

Treat it as a lead only. Go to the original, official page and read the actual words before you act.

Check yourself 5

Do not enter it. Close the page and type the official address yourself, or ask your supervisor.
