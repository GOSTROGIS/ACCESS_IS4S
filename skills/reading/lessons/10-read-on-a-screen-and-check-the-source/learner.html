Read on a screen and check the source

Find information fast on a phone, and decide whether a web page, post or search summary can be trusted before you act on it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Evaluate the document(s); Reflect on the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

On a BC job site, the SDS, the building code, a WorkSafeBC rule or a tool manual is often read on a phone. Searching online is fast, but anyone can post anything. A forum post, an ad or a quick search summary can be wrong, out of date, or from another province or country. Before you act on something you read online, check who wrote it and whether it is current.

Key ideas

1. Make the screen work for you. - Pinch to zoom in on small print. Turn the phone sideways for wide tables. - Use find on page to jump to a word instead of scrolling. - Chrome on Android: tap More (top right), then Find in page. - Chrome on iPhone: tap More, then Find in Page… - Safari on iPhone: tap the Page Menu button, then the Search Document button. - Phone menus change with updates. If yours looks different, check the help page for your browser.

2. Check who published it. Look at the web address. Official BC and federal sources include worksafebc.com, gov.bc.ca, bclaws.gov.bc.ca and canada.ca. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security advises checking that the address matches the organization, with no typos and no different ending such as .net or .org. Check for contact information, a physical address and an "About us" page.

3. Check the date and version. Look for a date, a version or a "last updated" note. A rule from 2010 may not be the rule today. Some official documents warn you themselves. For example, the BC Building Code page says its PDF does not include every later update.

4. Check the evidence. Does it name its source? Can you find the same thing on an official page? Be careful with headlines and claims that make you feel strongly or seem too good to be true.

5. Check the purpose. Is the page trying to inform you, or sell you something? A page that sells SDS binders has a reason to say your SDSs are out of date.

6. Summaries are not sources. A search summary, a social media post or an AI answer can be wrong. Use it only as a lead. Go to the original, official page and read the actual words.

7. Never log in from a link you were sent. If a page asks for your password or payment, type the official address yourself instead of tapping the link.

Worked example

Question: How often must SDSs be checked in BC?

  1. A search result from a site called "safetyanswers-bc.example" says every 5 years. The address is not an official one, and it sells binders. Treat it as a lead only.
  2. Go to worksafebc.com and open the WHMIS 2015 page. Use find on page to search for "three years".
  3. It says: "In British Columbia, SDSs must be checked every three years to ensure they include the most current information."
  4. Answer: every three years, from WorkSafeBC.

Document B: Search summary (fictional)

Practice

For Tasks 1, 2 and 5 you need a phone, tablet or computer with internet.

Task 1 (level 1) On your phone, open the WorkSafeBC WHMIS 2015 page: https://www.worksafebc.com/en/health-safety/hazards-exposures/whmis/whmis-2015 Use find on page to search for "workplace label". What three pieces of information does a workplace label provide?

Task 2 (level 1) On your phone, open the CCOHS SDS page: https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/whmis_ghs/sds.html Find the table of SDS sections. Zoom in or turn your phone sideways. What is the heading of Section 8?

Task 6 (level 3) Your foreman reads Document B out loud and says, "Good, ours are only four years old, so we're fine." Is the summary right? Using an official source, write what you would say to the foreman. Name your source.

Task 7 (level 3) Compare result 1 and result 2 in Document A. a) Who is each one written for? b) How does each one try to get you to act? c) Why does that matter when you decide what to believe?

Check yourself
