Find a rule in the BC Building Code

Use the edition, the index and the numbering system of the BC Building Code 2024 to find the exact rule that applies, and read it word by word.

Answers were checked against the BC Building Code 2024 PDF, web version revision 2, on 2026-11-06. If a later revision changes these articles, the current Code governs.

9.30.2.1.(3) - 9 is the Part - 9.30. is the Section (Flooring) - 9.30.2. is the Subsection (Panel-Type Underlay) - 9.30.2.1. is the Article (Required Underlay) - (3) is the Sentence

Task 2 (level 2)

a) March 8, 2024. It applies to projects with building permits applied for after that date. b) The page says to check and download any applicable revisions, bulletins or other updates on the page, because the PDF does not include all updates made after it was published.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 9.30.6. (the index lists "ceramic tile, 9.23.15.5., 9.30.6." under Flooring). b) 9.29.10.1. for installation, and 9.29.10. for ceramic tile as an interior finish.

Steps: the index is alphabetical. Look for the main word ("Flooring", "Ceramic tile"), then read the indented lines under it. Learners who pick 9.23.15.5. found a real entry, but it is about subflooring, not the tile itself.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) Yes. Sentence (3) says panel-type underlay shall be provided under ceramic tile applied with adhesive. b) Sentence 9.30.2.1.(3), or "9.30.2.1., Sentence (3)".

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Not less than 200 mm. b) A minimum. "Not less than" means at least. An offset of 250 mm meets the rule. An offset of 150 mm does not.

Task 6 (level 2)

Ceramic tile shall be set in a mortar bed, or applied to a sound smooth base with a suitable adhesive.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) 3.7 m. The vertical height of any flight of stairs shall not exceed 3.7 m. b) Not more than 100 mm. Handrails and constructions below handrails, including handrail supports and stair stringers, shall not project more than 100 mm into the required width of a stair or ramp. c) In Article 1.4.1.2. of Division A (Volume 1). Article 9.2.1.1. says so, and Part 9 repeats it.

For the opening story: neither carpenter was exactly right. The limit is 100 mm. Four inches is about 102 mm (4 × 25.4 = 101.6), so even 4 inches is slightly over the limit, and 6 inches is well over.

Task 8 (level 3)

Points a good response covers:

  • The handout was written for an older edition of the Code. The current edition is the BC Building Code 2024, in effect since March 8, 2024.
  • Code numbers and rules can change between editions. An old number may now point to a different rule, or the rule may have changed.
  • Look it up in the current Code, and check the revisions page.
  • Which edition applies to a project depends on when its building permit was applied for, so on a real job ask the supervisor or the building official which edition the permit is under.

A learner who says "the old handout is wrong" has gone too far. The point is that it has not been checked against the current edition, not that it is known to be wrong.
