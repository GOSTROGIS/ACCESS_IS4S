Topographic maps and site plans

Read the scale, contour lines, spot elevations, grid and legend on a topographic map and a site plan, and use them to find heights, distances, slope and which way water will run.

Figures 1 and 2 are fictional, so every answer below was read from the figures as drawn. The values were checked against the script that drew them.

Task 1 (level 1)

a) 1:50 000 b) 20 m c) 1 km d) 300 m and 400 m

Steps: the scale, contour interval and grid size are all in the notes under the map. The elevations are written on the heavy lines.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) 411 m (near the top right) and 254 m (near the bottom left). b) 157 m. 411 − 254 = 157.

Task 3 (level 2)

South. Any two clues: - The V shapes point north where the contour lines cross the creek. A V points upstream, so upstream is north and the water flows south. - The contour numbers get smaller going south (400 in the north, 300 further south), and water runs downhill. - The lowest spot elevation (254) is in the south, and the highest (411) is in the north.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) About 0.2 km (200 m): two tenths of a grid square. b) About 0.4 km (400 m): four tenths of a grid square. c) The north is steeper. Both pairs of lines are 20 m apart in height (the contour interval). In the north the ground rises 20 m in about 200 m. In the south it rises 20 m in about 400 m. Lines close together mean steep.

Accept estimates close to these. The point is that the northern lines are about half as far apart as the southern ones.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) 534613. - Easting: the grid line to the left is 53. The bridge is 4 tenths east of it: 534. - Northing: the grid line below is 61. The bridge is 3 tenths north of it: 613.

b) The cabin. 523 is 3 tenths east of line 52, and 627 is 7 tenths north of line 62.

Task 6 (level 2)

Between 300 m and 320 m. The heavy 300 line is south of the bridge, and the next line north (320) is north of it. Look closely at the creek: the V of the 300 line reaches up the creek to just below the bridge, and the bridge is still below the V of the 320 line.

Task 7 (level 2)

a) 1.4 km. The bridge is at easting 53.4 and the west edge is at easting 52.0. 53.4 − 52.0 = 1.4 km. b) 2.8 cm. - 1.4 km = 1400 m = 140 000 cm. - Map distance = real distance ÷ scale number: 140 000 ÷ 50 000 = 2.8 cm. - Check: 2 cm is 1 km, so 1.4 km is 1.4 × 2 = 2.8 cm.

c) 3.5 km. - Real distance = map distance × scale number: 7 × 50 000 = 350 000 cm. - 350 000 cm ÷ 100 = 3500 m, and 3500 m ÷ 1000 = 3.5 km. - Check: 2 cm is 1 km, so 7 cm is 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 km.

Task 8 (level 1)

a) The north-west corner, 101.50. b) The south-east corner, 99.00. c) 0.5 m (in the legend: "existing contour, 0.5 m interval"). d) The south side.

Task 9 (level 2)

a) 2.50 m. 101.50 − 99.00 = 2.50. b) Toward the south, a little to the east, toward Alder Lane and the south-east corner. The ground drops from the 101.0 line down to the 99.5 line, and water runs straight across the lines, downhill. c) 100.50 m. The west lot line is 25.0 m long, so its middle is 12.5 m from each end. The 100.5 contour meets the west lot line exactly there. (Check: the west line falls 2.00 m, from 101.50 to 99.50. Halfway down is 101.50 − 1.00 = 100.50.) d) 10.0 m by 12.0 m, and 6.0 m from the south lot line.

Task 10 (level 3)

8%. 1. Fall along the west lot line: 101.50 − 99.50 = 2.00 m. 2. Distance: 25.0 m (the written dimension). 3. Percent grade = (2.00 ÷ 25.0) × 100. Brackets first (BEDMAS): 2.00 ÷ 25.0 = 0.08. Then 0.08 × 100 = 8%.

Task 11 (level 3)

a) 1:50 000. - At 1:25 000: 10 km = 1 000 000 cm ÷ 25 000 = 40 cm wide. 14 km = 1 400 000 cm ÷ 25 000 = 56 cm tall. Too big for 20 cm × 28 cm. - At 1:50 000: 1 000 000 ÷ 50 000 = 20 cm wide. 1 400 000 ÷ 50 000 = 28 cm tall. Fits exactly. - At 1:250 000: 1 000 000 ÷ 250 000 = 4 cm wide. 1 400 000 ÷ 250 000 = 5.6 cm tall. It fits, but it is tiny. - So 1:50 000 is the biggest scale that fits.

b) No at 1:100. Yes at 1:200. - At 1:100: 20 m = 2000 cm ÷ 100 = 20 cm wide, and 25 m = 2500 cm ÷ 100 = 25 cm tall. Too big for 12 cm × 15 cm. - At 1:200: 2000 ÷ 200 = 10 cm wide, and 2500 ÷ 200 = 12.5 cm tall. It fits.

Task 12 (level 3)

a) Points a good answer covers: - The map is not wrong. It is old. It shows the land as it was when its data was collected. NRCan tells users to check the map data and publication date in the bottom left corner. - Things built after that date, such as new roads, clearings and buildings, may be missing. Things that were removed may still be shown. - Check: the newest map or a current online map, the company's own directions to the site, and ask the foreman or someone who has driven the road. Never use a map to decide where it is safe to dig. Use BC 1 Call (Digital lesson 9).

b) The note says "Do not scale this drawing." Use the written dimensions: the lot is 20.0 m wide, the house is 5.0 m from the west lot line and 10.0 m wide. 20.0 − 5.0 − 10.0 = 5.0 m from the house to the east lot line.
