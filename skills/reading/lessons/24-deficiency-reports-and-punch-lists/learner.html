Deficiency reports and punch lists

Read a deficiency report, a deficiency log and a punch list, find who has to fix what, where and by when, and know what you may and may not do until the work is closed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

Near the end of every job, and often in the middle, someone walks the building and writes down what is wrong or not finished. Each item becomes a deficiency report, and the list of them becomes a log. If an item has your trade's name on it, the report tells you exactly what to fix, where it is and when it is due. Fixing it right the first time saves a second trip. Covering it up before it is checked can mean tearing out finished work. Being able to read these documents quickly is part of being trusted on a crew.

Key ideas

1. A deficiency is work that is missing, damaged, or not built the way the drawings and specifications show. A blocked drain, a dented corner, a missing piece of blocking and the wrong door handle are all deficiencies.

2. A deficiency report answers who, what, where and when. Look for these parts:

Part What it tells you
Number Each report has its own number, such as DR-021. Use it when you talk about the item.
Date found and found by When it was seen, and who to ask about it
Location Level, room number and name, and often a grid line, as in Reading lesson 17
Reference The drawing, detail or specification section that shows how it should be
Description What is wrong, in words
Photo A photo number, so you can see it before you go
Trade responsible Whose crew fixes it
Target date When it must be fixed
Status Where the item is in the process

3. Status words. Every company uses its own words. In this lesson: - Open: not fixed yet. - Ready for re-inspection: the trade says it is fixed. Nobody has checked yet. - Closed: checked and accepted, with a date and initials. - Reopened: it was checked and it was still not right, so it is open again.

4. A non-conformance report (NCR) is used for a bigger problem: one that cannot be fixed just by redoing the work the way the drawings show. Something built in the wrong place, or the wrong material, needs a decision from the designer before anyone touches it.

5. A punch list is the list of small items still to finish or fix, room by room, before the owner takes over the building. It is written on a walk-through near the end of the job.

6. Reading a log. A deficiency log is a table, so read it the way Reading lesson 4 taught: title and date first, then the column headings, then your row. To find what is late, compare each target date with today's date.

Worked example

Question: Use Document B. What is wrong, where, who fixes it, and by when?

  1. Number and date: DR-021, found 2026-11-03 by T. Morin, the superintendent.
  2. Where: Level 1, Room 104 (Staff room), east wall at grid C-3.
  3. What: the blocking for the upper cabinets is missing. Detail 4 on sheet A-501 shows it.
  4. Who and when: Framing, by 2026-11-05.
  5. What to look at before going: photo P-021, plan A-201 and detail 4 on A-501.
  6. Why it is urgent: the wall is still open. Once it is boarded, adding blocking means cutting the drywall.

Practice documents

All documents in this lesson are fictional. They were written for this lesson.

Document A: The company procedure

Ridgeline Builders Ltd. (fictional company) · Quality procedure QP-03: Deficiencies and the punch list

Words used in this procedure - Deficiency: work that is missing, damaged, or not built the way the drawings and specifications show. - Deficiency report (DR): the form used to record one deficiency. Each DR has its own number. - Non-conformance report (NCR): used instead of a DR when the problem cannot be fixed just by redoing the work the way the drawings show, for example work built in the wrong place or with a material that is not the one specified. The superintendent sends every NCR to the consultant. Nobody fixes, moves or covers NCR work until the consultant answers in writing. - Punch list: the list of small items still to finish or fix in each room before the owner takes over.

Steps 1. Anyone who finds a deficiency tells the superintendent the same day. 2. The superintendent writes a DR within one working day. It gives the location (level, room, grid), the drawing or specification reference, what is wrong, a photo, the trade responsible and a target date. The DR goes on the deficiency log as Open. 3. The superintendent gives a copy of the DR to the foreman of the trade responsible. 4. The trade fixes the work by the target date. If the date cannot be met, the foreman tells the superintendent before the date, and a new date is written on the log. 5. When the work is fixed, the foreman signs the DR, and the log status changes to Ready for re-inspection. Do not cover the work with drywall, ceiling tile, paint or concrete until it has been re-inspected. 6. The superintendent re-inspects. If the work is right, the DR is Closed, with the date and initials. If it is not right, the DR is Reopened, with a note saying why. 7. Two weeks before handover, the superintendent and the consultant walk every room and write the punch list. Any DR that is still open goes on the punch list.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Use Document B. a) What is the DR number, and what date was the deficiency found? b) Which room, and which wall? c) Which trade must fix it? d) What is the target date?

Task 2 (level 1) Use Document C. a) Which DRs are Closed? b) Which DR is Ready for re-inspection? c) How many DRs are Open or Reopened? List them.

Task 3 (level 2) Use Document C. The log was printed on 2026-11-10. a) Which Open or Reopened DRs are past their target date on 2026-11-10? b) Which Open DR is due next? c) Which trade has more than one DR on the log? Name the DRs.

Task 4 (level 2) Use Documents A and C. The ceiling crew says DR-019 is fixed and wants to start dropping tiles into the corridor grid this afternoon. a) What does step 5 of the procedure say? b) Who has to do what before the tiles go in?

Task 6 (level 2) Use Documents A and C. Look at DR-025. a) Why is this an NCR and not a normal DR? Use the words of the procedure. b) A framer offers to move the cabinet to the right spot today. What does the procedure say?

Task 7 (level 3) Use Documents A, C and E. Look at DR-024. a) What does "Reopened" tell you happened, and which step of the procedure is that? b) DR-024 also shows up in Document E. Task 8 (level 3) Use Documents A and D. a) Find five things wrong with Document D. Use step 2 of the procedure. b) Rewrite the description so a drywaller who has never seen the site could find and fix the problem. Make up the missing details for a room in Unit 4.

Task 9 (level 2) Use Document E. a) How many items in Room 104 are for the painter? b) You are the tile setter. Which item is yours? c) Which item was already on the deficiency log?
