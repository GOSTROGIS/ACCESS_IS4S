Deficiency reports and punch lists

Read a deficiency report, a deficiency log and a punch list, find who has to fix what, where and by when, and know what you may and may not do until the work is closed.

  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Session 24 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 75 minutes

Before the session: print Documents A to E for each learner, or project them. If you have a real deficiency report, punch list or inspection form from a job, with names and addresses blacked out, bring it so learners can compare it with Ridgeline's. This lesson builds on Reading lesson 4 (Find it in a table, log or form) and on the grid lines in lesson 17.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone had to go back and redo work after someone walked the job with a clipboard? How did you find out what was wrong?" Take two or three stories. Then tell a short one: a framing crew gets a note that says "blocking missing, staff room". They go to the wrong wall, the board crew closes the right wall that afternoon, and the cabinet installer finds no blocking a week later. The drywall has to be cut open. Ask: "What would the note have needed to say?" Today is about reading those notes, the log they go on, and the punch list at the end of the job.

This is session 24 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 75 minutes.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the location, trade, target date and status on a deficiency report and a log (Identify the information)
  • connect a report to its drawing reference, its log entry, the procedure step and the punch list (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • decide what a crew may and may not do with an item, using the procedure and today's date (Understand and apply the information)
  • judge whether a deficiency report gives enough information to act on (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. "What do you think a 'punch list' is?"
  2. "If a note says 'fix wall upstairs', what would you need to ask before you could fix it?"

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • what a deficiency is
  • the parts of a deficiency report: number, date, location, reference, description, photo, trade, target date and status
  • status words: Open, Ready for re-inspection, Closed and Reopened
  • reading a deficiency log against today's date
  • the difference between a deficiency report and a non-conformance report in one company's procedure
  • the punch list and how open items end up on it
  • what makes a report clear enough to act on

Total time: 5 + 3 + 4 + 52 + 6 + 5 = 75 minutes.

Notes for the instructor

  • Every company is different. The procedure, forms, status words and time limits in this lesson belong to a fictional company. Real employers, general contractors and contracts use their own forms and steps. Tell learners to ask for their employer's procedure on a new job, and to follow it.
  • The worker's job. On most sites a crew member does not write the formal report. Their job is to tell the supervisor right away, to read the reports that come back to their trade, and to avoid covering work that has not been closed. Task 8 makes this point through step 1 and step 2 of Document A.
  • NCR decisions. Learners do not need to decide what an NCR should say. The point of Task 6 is that some problems are not the crew's to fix until someone with authority answers in writing.
