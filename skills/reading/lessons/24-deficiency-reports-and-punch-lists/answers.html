Deficiency reports and punch lists

Read a deficiency report, a deficiency log and a punch list, find who has to fix what, where and by when, and know what you may and may not do until the work is closed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) DR-021, found 2026-11-03. b) Room 104 (Staff room) on Level 1, the east wall at grid C-3. c) Framing (Northside Framing Ltd.). d) 2026-11-05.

Steps: the number and date are in the top table. Location, trade and target date each have their own label.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) DR-018 and DR-021. b) DR-019. c) Four: DR-020, DR-022 and DR-023 are Open, and DR-024 is Reopened.

DR-025 is not counted as Open. It is an NCR waiting for the consultant, and the procedure treats it differently.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) DR-020 (target 2026-11-06) and DR-024 (target 2026-11-09). Both dates are before 2026-11-10. b) DR-022, the floor drain, due 2026-11-12. DR-023 is due one day later, 2026-11-13. c) Framing: DR-021 and DR-025. Every other trade has one DR on the log.

Steps: scan the Status column for Open and Reopened. For each one, compare the Target date with 2026-11-10. A date earlier than today is past due.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) Step 5 says do not cover the work with drywall, ceiling tile, paint or concrete until it has been re-inspected. b) Ready for re-inspection means the trade says it is fixed but nobody has checked. The superintendent must re-inspect the grid (step 6) and close DR-019 before the tiles go in. The ceiling foreman should ask the superintendent to come and check it, and the crew should work somewhere else until then.

Task 5 (level 2)

Order: C, E, G, D, B, A, F

Someone finds it and tells the superintendent (C). The superintendent writes the DR and logs it (E), then gives a copy to the foreman (G). The trade fixes the work (D), and the foreman signs (B). The superintendent re-inspects (A) and closes it (F).

Task 6 (level 2)

a) The cabinet was built in the wrong place (300 mm left of the location on A-201). The procedure says an NCR is used when the problem "cannot be fixed just by redoing the work the way the drawings show, for example work built in the wrong place". A decision is needed first, so it goes to the consultant. b) No. The procedure says "Nobody fixes, moves or covers NCR work until the consultant answers in writing." The framer should wait for the written answer. The consultant may accept it where it is, or may want it somewhere else again.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) The painter marked it fixed, the superintendent re-inspected it and it was still not right (one corner was still bare), so it went back to open with a note. That is step 6. b) Item 104-2, "Paint the wall behind the fridge space (DR-024)". Step 7 says any DR still open at the walk-through goes on the punch list. c) 2026-11-30. 2026-11-16 plus 14 days. November has 30 days, so 16 + 14 = 30.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Any five of: - no date found - "Found by: Kyle" has no last name or job title, so nobody knows who to ask - "upstairs" is not a location: no level, no room number or name, no wall, no grid line. Unit 4 is all on Level 1, so "upstairs" makes no sense on this job. - no drawing or specification reference - the description says nothing: what is wrong with the wall? - no photo - the trade has a question mark, so nobody is sure whose crew fixes it - "ASAP" is not a date - it was written by a worker. Step 2 says the superintendent writes the DR. Step 1 says the worker should tell the superintendent.

b) A good rewrite names the level, room number and name, the wall or grid line, what is wrong, how big the problem is, and how it should look when fixed. For example:

Level 1, Room 102 (Office), south wall at grid A-2, beside door 102. The drywall has a hole about the size of a fist, 300 mm above the floor, and the joint above it is cracked for about 600 mm. Patch, tape, mud and sand to match the wall around it, ready for paint. See photo P-026.

Any made-up details are fine if they are specific enough for a stranger to walk to the spot and know what to do.

Task 9 (level 2)

a) Two: 104-1 and 104-2. b) 104-4, clean the grout haze off the backsplash. c) 104-2, which names DR-024.

Check yourself 1

Work that is missing, damaged, or not built the way the drawings and specifications show.

Check yourself 2

Any five of: the report number, the date found, who found it, the location (level, room, grid), the drawing or specification reference, what is wrong, a photo, the trade responsible, the target date and the status.

Check yourself 3

The trade says the work is fixed, but nobody has checked it yet. No, do not cover it. Wait until it is re-inspected and closed.

Check yourself 4

A DR is for work that can be fixed by redoing it the way the drawings show. An NCR is for a problem that cannot, such as work in the wrong place or the wrong material. An NCR goes to the consultant, and nobody fixes, moves or covers the work until the consultant answers in writing.

Check yourself 5

On a walk-through two weeks before handover, room by room. It lists the small items still to finish or fix, plus any DR that is still open.
