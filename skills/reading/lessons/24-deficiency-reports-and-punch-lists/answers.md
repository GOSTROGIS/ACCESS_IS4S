## Task 8 (level 3)

a) Any five of:
- **no date found**
- **"Found by: Kyle"** has no last name or job title, so nobody knows who to ask
- **"upstairs" is not a location**: no level, no room number or name, no wall, no grid line. Unit 4 is all on Level 1, so "upstairs" makes no sense on this job.
- **no drawing or specification reference**
- **the description says nothing**: what is wrong with the wall?
- **no photo**
- **the trade has a question mark**, so nobody is sure whose crew fixes it
- **"ASAP" is not a date**
- it was written by a worker. Step 2 says the **superintendent** writes the DR. Step 1 says the worker should **tell** the superintendent.

b) A good rewrite names the level, room number and name, the wall or grid line, what is wrong, how big the problem is, and how it should look when fixed. For example:

> Level 1, Room 102 (Office), south wall at grid A-2, beside door 102. The drywall has a hole about the size of a fist, 300 mm above the floor, and the joint above it is cracked for about 600 mm. Patch, tape, mud and sand to match the wall around it, ready for paint. See photo P-026.

Any made-up details are fine if they are specific enough for a stranger to walk to the spot and know what to do.

The trade says the work is fixed, but **nobody has checked it yet**. **No, do not cover it.** Wait until it is re-inspected and closed.

## Check yourself 4

A **DR** is for work that can be fixed by redoing it the way the drawings show. An **NCR** is for a problem that cannot, such as work in the wrong place or the wrong material. An NCR goes to the consultant, and nobody fixes, moves or covers the work until the consultant answers in writing.

## Check yourself 5

On a walk-through **two weeks before handover**, room by room. It lists the small items still to finish or fix, plus **any DR that is still open**.
