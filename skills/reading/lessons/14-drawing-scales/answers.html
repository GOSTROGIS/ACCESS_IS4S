Drawing scales and the scale rule

Read imperial architectural scales, engineer's scales and metric ratio scales, use a scale rule, change a measured drawing length into a real size and back, and know when a drawing must not be scaled.

Task 1 (level 1)

a) In the title block, or under the title of a view (such as a detail). b) Not to scale. The drawing is not drawn to any scale, so it must not be measured. c) Yes. For example, a floor plan at 1/4" = 1'-0" with a stair detail at 1 1/2" = 1'-0" on the same sheet.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) Every 1/4 inch on the paper stands for 1 foot in real life. b) 1/4" = 1'-0" (twice as big as 1/8"). c) 1:50 (twice as big as 1:100).

Task 3 (level 1)

a) Near the right end, not at the very end. b) To the left. c) It is one foot cut into inches. You read the inches there, after reading the whole feet on the other end of the line.

Task 4 (level 2)

22'-6"

Steps: at 1/4" = 1'-0", multiply by 4. 5 5/8 × 4 = 22 1/2 feet. 1/2 foot = 6 inches.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) 11'-6". At 1/2" = 1'-0", multiply by 2. 5 3/4 × 2 = 11 1/2 feet. b) 3'-0". At 3/4" = 1'-0", multiply by 4/3. 2 1/4 × 4/3 = 3 feet. c) 2'-6". At 3" = 1'-0", multiply by 1/3 (divide by 3). 7 1/2 ÷ 3 = 2 1/2 feet.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) 57.5 mm. 1150 ÷ 20 = 57.5. b) 28 mm. 140 ÷ 5 = 28. c) 42 mm. 4200 ÷ 100 = 42.

Task 7 (level 2)

a) 68 feet. At 1" = 20', multiply inches by 20. 3.4 × 20 = 68. b) 8500 mm = 8.5 m. 85 × 100 = 8500.

Task 8 (level 2)

a) 3000 mm. A half-size copy of a 1:50 drawing is really at 1:100. 30 × 100 = 3000 mm. The helper's answer is off by 1500 mm. b) The written dimension for that wall. If there is no dimension, the scale bar on the sheet, which shrank with the copy and still reads correctly.

Task 9 (level 3)

a) The detail is not to scale. The lengths on the paper do not match the real sizes, so laying a ruler on it means nothing. b) The written dimensions: landing 1200 mm, opening 3000 mm. c) Check the same sizes on the plan and other details. If they still do not agree, tell the foreman and ask the designer in writing (an RFI) before building.

Task 10 (level 3)

a) About 3150 mm (3.15 m). 63 × 50 = 3150. b) Yes, but only as a rough guide. No dimension is given, so scaling is the only way to get a location from this sheet. The answer is approximate. Check that the print is full size (use the scale bar if there is one). c) Confirm the location before cutting: check the reflected ceiling plan or electrical drawings for a dimension, and ask the foreman or designer. Mark it and get it checked before cutting a hole that is hard to patch.

Task 11 (level 3)

a) No. 4800 ÷ 20 = 240 mm and 3600 ÷ 20 = 180 mm. That is bigger than 150 mm by 100 mm. b) Yes. 4800 ÷ 50 = 96 mm and 3600 ÷ 50 = 72 mm. That fits in 150 mm by 100 mm. c) Yes. 16 × 1/4 = 4" and 12 × 1/4 = 3". That fits in 6" by 4".
