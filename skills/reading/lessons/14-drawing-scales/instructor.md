# Drawing scales and the scale rule

Session 14 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a full-size sheet and a half-size copy of the same drawing. Ask: "Both say SCALE 1:50. Can they both be right?" Let learners argue it out. Tell a short story: a crew lays out a footing from a reduced copy, measuring with a ruler and the written scale. Every footing comes out half size. The written dimensions were on the drawing the whole time.

This is session 14 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes. It follows lesson 13 (lines and dimensions).

Before the session: bring architect's scale rules and metric scale rules (one per pair if possible), 12-inch rulers, a calculator per pair, and one full-size and one reduced copy of any drawing with a scale bar. If you have no scale rules, Figures 1 and 2 show how they read, and a plain ruler with the multiplier table in Key idea 3 works.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find the scale of a drawing or view, and recognize NTS (Identify the information)
- connect a length on the paper to a mark on a scale rule, and to the written dimension (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- convert a measured drawing length to a real size and back, in imperial and metric scales (Understand and apply the information)
- decide when a drawing must not be scaled, and when a scaled length is only a rough guide (Evaluate the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. "What does 1:100 mean?"
2. Hand out a scale rule. "Find the 1/4 scale. Where is the zero?"

1. "At 1/8" = 1'-0", a wall measures 2 1/2". How long is it?" (20'-0")

3. "Give two reasons not to measure a drawing when a dimension is written."

## Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

- what a scale is and where to find it
- imperial architectural scales and the multiplier for each
- reading an architect's scale rule
- engineer's scales for site plans
- metric ratio scales and the metric scale rule
- converting paper lengths to real sizes and back
- scale bars, NTS, and why written dimensions win
