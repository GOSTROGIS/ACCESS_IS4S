What is in a construction contract

Find the parties, the work, the price, payments, changes, warranty and holdback in a short subcontract, and learn what a builders lien protects in BC.

Session 20 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 75 minutes

Before the session: learners need a calculator and a calendar (a phone calendar is fine). Open the Builders Lien Act on BC Laws so you can show sections 2, 4, 8, 20 and 42 on the screen: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/97045_01. Check the top of the page for the date the Act is current to, and check whether the Construction Prompt Payment Act has come into force (Key idea 6). If it has, the holdback period in section 8 may have changed: update Key idea 4, Task 5 and the answers before you teach.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone finished a job and waited months for the last part of the money?" Take a story or two. Explain that in BC part of that wait is the law: the person paying keeps back a holdback until the job is done and the lien deadline has passed. Today learners read a short subcontract, find its main parts and do the holdback math.

This is session 20 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 75 minutes.

Be clear with learners: this lesson teaches reading. It is not legal advice. Lien deadlines are strict. For a real unpaid bill, they should talk to a lawyer.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the parties, work, price, schedule, payment, changes, holdback and warranty in a subcontract (Identify the information)
  • connect a site event to the contract section that controls it (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • work out holdback and payment amounts, and the holdback and lien dates from a certificate of completion (Understand and apply the information)
  • judge whether a clause works under BC law and whose interests a contract protects (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. "What is the difference between a contract and a handshake deal?"
  2. "Have you heard the word 'holdback' or 'lien'? What do you think it means?"

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the nine parts of a construction contract
  • getting changes in writing before the work starts
  • the 10% holdback under section 4 of the BC Builders Lien Act, and that it is not kept from a worker's wages
  • the holdback period of 55 days after a certificate of completion
  • builders liens: who has them, the 45-day filing deadline, and that lien rights cannot be signed away
  • checking BC Laws for the current version of the Act

Total time: 5 + 3 + 4 + 52 + 6 + 5 = 75 minutes.

Notes for the instructor

  • Section 10 of Document A is written to fail. Section 42 (2) of the Act makes an agreement that the Act does not apply, or that its remedies are not available, void. Learners should see that a clause can be in a signed contract and still have no effect, the same idea as Reading lesson 09.
  • Counting days. "55 days after June 10" lands on August 4, and "45 days after June 10" lands on July 25. Learners who count June 10 as day 1 will be one day early. In real life, do not leave a lien to the last day.
  • How the 10% is measured. Section 4 bases the holdback on the greater of the value of the work actually done and the amount paid. In this lesson the two are the same, so the math stays simple.
  • Employee or contractor. If learners ask whether the framer in Task 7 is really an employee, point ahead to Reading lesson 22.

  • Builders Lien Act, SBC 1997, c. 45, BC Laws, https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/97045_01, checked 2026-11-08. Sections used:
  • s. 1 (1): "worker" is an individual engaged for wages, and "completed" means substantially completed.
  • s. 2 (1): a contractor, subcontractor or worker who performs work or supplies material has a lien for the unpaid price on the owner's interest, the improvement, the land and the material delivered.
  • s. 4 (1): a holdback of 10%. s. 4 (6): no holdback from a worker or material supplier.
  • s. 7 (3): a certificate of completion is issued on the request of a contractor or subcontractor.
  • s. 8 (1) and (4): the holdback period expires 55 days after the certificate of completion, and payment after that discharges liens unless one has been filed.
  • s. 17: no claim of lien under $200.
  • s. 20 (1): a claim of lien may be filed no later than 45 days after the certificate of completion.
  • s. 42 (2): an agreement that the Act is not to apply, or that its remedies are not available, is void.
  • Construction Prompt Payment Act, SBC 2025, c. 24, assented to November 27, 2025, BC Laws, https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/25024, checked 2026-11-08.
