# Read a workplace policy or agreement

## Why this matters on the job

On your first day with a new employer you may get an employee handbook and a stack of forms to sign. The handbook tells you how to report an absence, when time sheets are due and what happens with your own tools. An agreement you sign can affect your pay. In BC, the Employment Standards Act sets minimum standards. An agreement cannot take them away, even if you sign it. Reading before you sign protects you.

## Key ideas

**1. Use the index and the definitions first.** A handbook is a reference book. Find your topic in the index or contents. Then read the definitions section: a word like "employee" or "supervisor" may have a special meaning in this document.

**2. Must, should and may.**
- **Must** (or *shall*): required. No choice.
- **Should**: expected. Check what the document says about exceptions.
- **May**: allowed, not required. "The supervisor *may* ask for a note" means they can, but do not have to.

Many handbooks define these words. Use the handbook's own definitions if it has them.

**3. Watch who does what.** Underline the person in each rule: "*you* must call *your supervisor*". A message to the wrong person may not count.

**4. Look for limits and conditions.** *Only if, unless, including, except, in a row, before.*

**5. Check an agreement against the law.** In BC, section 4 of the Employment Standards Act says its requirements are minimum requirements, and an agreement to waive any of them has no effect. The Province also says it is illegal to make employees pay for business expenses, such as stolen or damaged company property, even if they agree to it. If something in a handbook or agreement looks like it takes away a minimum standard, ask before you sign. The BC Employment Standards website is https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards

**6. Ask who wrote it and why.** A handbook is written by the employer, for employees, to set rules and protect the business. That is normal. Knowing who wrote it helps you read it fairly and notice what it leaves out.

### Worked example

**Question:** Do part-time workers have a probation period?

1. Index: "Probation ... 5". Go to page 5.
2. Read section 3 closely: "All new employees, including part-time employees, are on probation for the first 3 months."
3. "Including part-time employees" answers it: **yes, 3 months.**

## Practice documents

All documents below are for a fictional employer.

**Document A: Employee handbook (excerpt)**

> **Kestrel Point Builders Ltd. (fictional company) · Employee Handbook · Edition 4, January 2026**
>
> **Index**
> Absence, reporting ........ 7
> Cell phones ............... 12
> Definitions ............... 3
> Overtime .................. 9
> Probation ................. 5
> Time sheets ............... 8
> Tools, company ............ 13
> Tools, personal ........... 13
>
> **Section 1. Definitions (page 3)**
> "Employee" means anyone on Kestrel Point's payroll, including apprentices and part-time employees.
> "Supervisor" means the foreman or site superintendent you report to.
> "Must" means required. "Should" means expected unless there is a good reason. "May" means allowed but not required.
>
> **Section 3. Probation (page 5)**
> All new employees, including part-time employees, are on probation for the first 3 months. Your supervisor will review your work before the probation ends.
>
> **Section 4. Reporting an absence (page 7)**
> If you will be absent or late, you must call or text your supervisor before your shift starts. A message left with a co-worker does not count. If you are absent because of illness for more than 2 shifts in a row, your supervisor may ask for a note.
>
> **Section 5. Time sheets (page 8)**
> You must submit your time sheet in the company app by 10:00 a.m. Monday for the week before. If you miss the deadline, tell the office right away.
>
> **Section 6. Overtime (page 9)**
> Employees who want extra hours may agree in writing to be paid their regular rate for those hours instead of overtime rates.
>
> **Section 8. Cell phones (page 12)**
> Personal phone use should be kept to breaks. You must not use a phone while operating equipment or while working on a ladder or scaffold.
>
> **Section 9. Tools (page 13)**
> Kestrel Point supplies power tools. You must supply your own hand tools from the basic list for your trade. You may use your own power tools only if your supervisor has inspected them and you have signed the Personal Tool Form (KP-30).

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1)
Use the index in Document A. On what page would you find:
a) the rules for using your own power tools?
b) what to do if you will be late?
c) when time sheets are due?

**Task 2** (level 1)
Use the definitions in Document A. Label each rule as *must*, *should* or *may*, and say what it means for you.
a) "Personal phone use should be kept to breaks."
b) "Your supervisor may ask for a note."
c) "You must not use a phone while operating equipment."

**Task 3** (level 2)
Is an apprentice an "employee" under this handbook? Point to the words that show it.

**Task 4** (level 2)
You wake up sick at 5:30 a.m. Your shift starts at 7:00. You text a co-worker on your crew and ask him to tell the foreman. Have you followed section 4? What should you have done?

**Task 5** (level 2)
You want to bring your own cordless circular saw to work. What two things must happen first?

**Task 6** (level 3)
Read section 6, Overtime. Then read Key idea 5. Is there a problem with section 6? Explain.

**Task 7** (level 3)
You are handed Document B on your first day and told to sign it. Read it closely.
a) What does it allow the employer to do?
b) How does that compare with what the Province of BC says about business expenses?
c) What could you do before you sign?

**Task 8** (level 3)
Think about Document A as a whole.
a) Who wrote it, and who is it written for?
b) What does it want the reader to do?
c) Name one thing a new worker might want to know that this excerpt does not say.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between "must" and "may"?
2. Why read the definitions section before the rules?
3. Can you sign away a minimum standard under BC's Employment Standards Act?
4. Name one question to ask yourself about who wrote a document.
