Read a workplace policy or agreement

Use the index, definitions and must, should and may to understand an employee handbook, and check an agreement against BC employment standards before you sign.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s); Reflect on the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) Page 13 ("Tools, personal") b) Page 7 ("Absence, reporting") c) Page 8 ("Time sheets")

Task 2 (level 1)

a) Should. You are expected to keep personal phone use to breaks, unless there is a good reason, such as an urgent family call. b) May. Your supervisor is allowed to ask for a note but does not have to. c) Must (not). This is required. You are never allowed to use a phone while operating equipment.

Task 3 (level 2)

Yes. Section 1 says "'Employee' means anyone on Kestrel Point's payroll, including apprentices and part-time employees."

Task 4 (level 2)

No. Section 4 says you must call or text your supervisor, and "a message left with a co-worker does not count." You should have called or texted the foreman yourself before 7:00 a.m.

Task 5 (level 2)

  1. Your supervisor must inspect the saw.
  2. You must sign the Personal Tool Form (KP-30).

Both are needed. The word "only if" in section 9 sets the conditions.

Task 6 (level 3)

Yes, there is a problem. Section 6 lets employees agree in writing to be paid their regular rate for extra hours instead of overtime rates. Section 4 of the BC Employment Standards Act says its requirements are minimum requirements and an agreement to waive them has no effect. The Province's own interpretation guide uses almost this exact case: an agreement to work extra hours at the regular rate has no effect, and the employer must still pay the overtime rates the Act requires.

Points a good response covers: names the clause, names the Act's rule that minimums cannot be waived, and says that signing would not make it valid. Learners do not need to know the overtime rates themselves for this task.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) It allows the employer to deduct from your pay the cost of a company tool that is lost, stolen or damaged while signed out to you.

b) The Province of BC says it is illegal to make employees pay for business expenses, even if they agree to it, and lists stolen or damaged company property as an example. So the deduction clause goes against what the Province says, and signing it would not make such deductions allowed.

c) Any of these: - Do not sign on the spot. Ask for a copy to read. - Ask the person what the clause means and point out the Province's rule politely. - Look up "Deductions" on the BC Employment Standards website, or contact Employment Standards. - If you are in a union, ask your union representative. - You can still agree to take good care of tools. That part of the agreement is reasonable.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) It was written by Kestrel Point Builders Ltd. (the employer), for its employees, including apprentices and part-time workers. b) It wants the reader to follow the company's rules for absences, time sheets, phones and tools, and in places to agree to terms that favour the company. c) Any sensible answer, for example: how much the pay is and when payday is, what the overtime rates are, how to report a safety concern or refuse unsafe work, how to report harassment, how holidays and vacation work, or who to contact with a question about the handbook.

Check yourself 1

Must means it is required. May means it is allowed but not required.

Check yourself 2

Because words like "employee" or "supervisor" may have a special meaning in that document, and the meaning changes who a rule applies to.

Check yourself 3

No. Section 4 says an agreement to waive the Act's minimum requirements has no effect.

Check yourself 4

Any one of: Who wrote it? Who is it written for? What does the writer want me to do? Whose interests does it protect? What does it leave out?
