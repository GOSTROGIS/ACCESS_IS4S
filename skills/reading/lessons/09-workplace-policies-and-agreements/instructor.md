# Read a workplace policy or agreement

Session 9 of 10 in the Reading lessons · 75 minutes

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "On your first day at a job, how many papers did you sign? Did you read them all?" Most learners will say no. Tell a short story: a new worker signs a stack of forms on day one. Three months later, a drill goes missing from the gang box and money comes off his cheque. He says, "I never agreed to that." The form says he did. Today learners practise reading the handbook and the forms before they sign.

This is session 9 of 10 in the Reading lessons. It runs 75 minutes.

Be clear with learners: this lesson teaches reading. It is not legal advice. For a real question about their rights, send them to BC Employment Standards or, for union members, their union.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find a rule in a handbook using the index and definitions (Identify the information)
- apply must, should and may, and the conditions in a rule, to a real situation (Understand and apply the information)
- check a handbook rule or agreement against BC employment standards (Evaluate the document(s))
- say who wrote a document, for whom and why (Reflect on the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. "What is the difference between 'you must' and 'you may'?"
2. "If you sign something at work, does that always make it legal?"

## Post-assessment (6 min)

1. "Using Document A, what time are time sheets due, and what must you do if you miss it?"
2. "Read this sentence: 'Your supervisor may approve a shift swap.' Must the supervisor approve it?"
3. "Name one place a BC worker can go with a question about whether a workplace rule meets the Employment Standards Act."

## Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

- using an index and a definitions section
- must, should and may
- who does what, and limit words such as including and in a row
- the BC Employment Standards Act sets minimum standards that an agreement cannot take away
- business expenses such as damaged or stolen company property cannot be charged to workers
- asking who wrote a document, for whom and why

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 52 + 6 + 4 = 75 minutes.
