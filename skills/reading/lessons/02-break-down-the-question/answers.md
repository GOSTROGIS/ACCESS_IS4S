# Break down the question first

## Task 1 (level 1)

| Question | Question word | Kind of answer |
|---|---|---|
| a) Who keeps the training records? | Who | a person or role |
| b) When should you do the pre-use check? | When | a time |
| c) How many bars must show before you start work? | How many | a number |
| d) Where is the daily log card kept? | Where | a place |
| e) Why do you think the platform must be fully lowered before you check the fluid? | Why | a reason |

The answers, if learners go on to find them: a) the shop supervisor. b) at the start of every shift, before you raise the platform. c) at least 2. d) in the holder on the base. e) The procedure does not give the reason. Accept a sensible reason, such as "the level only reads correctly when the platform is down", and point out that this is the learner's own reasoning, not a fact from the document.

## Task 2 (level 1)

a) ~~What should~~ **you do** ~~if a~~ **tire** ~~on the lift has a~~ **deep cut** ~~in it~~?
b) **Which fluid** ~~can you use to~~ **top up** ~~the~~ **hydraulic tank**?

Accept small differences. The test is whether the key words alone still carry the question.

## Task 3 (level 2)

a) Given: the gate does not latch by itself. Requested: the tag. Answer: **DO NOT USE** (item 1). Also tell the lead hand.

b) Given: the daily log card. Requested: three things to write. Answer: **your full name (printed), your signature, and the date** (item 7).

c) Given: an item fails. Requested: a person or role. Answer: **the lead hand** (last paragraph).

## Task 4 (level 2)

The words **"trained on this model"** and **"training records"** match "signed off". The answer is **the shop supervisor**, because the shop supervisor keeps the training records. The procedure says the lead hand does not. A learner who answers "the lead hand" has missed the limit word "not".

## Task 5 (level 2)

a) **No.** Item 4 says "Charge it in the charging bay only." Wait for the bay or ask the lead hand.
b) **No.** Item 5 says "Both must stop all movement." One working is not enough. Tag the lift and tell the lead hand.
c) **Yes.** Item 4 says do not start "if fewer than 2 bars show". Two bars is not fewer than 2, so you can start. This is a close-reading point: "fewer than 2" means 0 or 1.

## Task 6 (level 1)

The question asks **how many were going to St Ives**. A good answer is **one**: the speaker. The speaker *met* everyone else on the way, so the riddle does not say the others were going to St Ives at all. People who start multiplying the sevens have answered a different question: how many were met. Accept other answers if the learner can point to the words that support them. The discussion matters more than the number. The lesson is to read the question to the end before working.

## Task 7 (level 2)

a) The question asks **when** (a time). The answer gives a **where** (a place). A matching answer: "At the start of this shift, before I raised the platform."
b) The question asks **why** (a reason). The answer gives a **who**. A matching answer: "Because the entry gate did not latch by itself" (or another failed item).
c) The question asks **how many** (a number). The answer gives a person. The procedure does not give a number. A matching answer: "I don't know. The shop supervisor keeps the training records, so I'll ask them."

## Check yourself 1

A frequency, such as every shift, daily, weekly or every 30 days.

## Check yourself 2

The given is the information the question already tells you. The requested is the information the question asks you to find.

## Check yourself 3

Think of words that mean the same thing, such as check, look at, examine or test, and scan for those.

## Check yourself 4

Any three of: only, except, not, before, after, first, last, at least, no more than, both, either, fewer than.
