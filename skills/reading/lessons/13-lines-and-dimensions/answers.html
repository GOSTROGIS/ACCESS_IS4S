The alphabet of lines and reading dimensions

Name each kind of line on a drawing and what it means, read feet-and-inch and millimetre dimensions, and add up chain and baseline dimension strings to find a missing size.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) The object line, the cutting-plane line and the short break line. b) The hidden line. c) The phantom line.

Task 2 (level 1)

  1. Object line (the left end of the strap)
  2. Hidden line (the side of a hole, seen in the top view)
  3. Centre line (through the left hole)
  4. Dimension line (the 1200 dimension)
  5. Extension line (carrying the left end down to the dimensions)
  6. Leader (carrying the note Ø14 2 HOLES)
  7. Cutting-plane line (A-A)
  8. Section lines (hatching in SECTION A-A)
  9. Long break line (the zigzag in the front view)
  10. Phantom line (the gate post)

Note: the top view uses short break lines (thick and wavy) at the break.

Task 3 (level 1)

a) 12'-6" b) 0'-9" c) 3'-4 1/2" d) 2400 (no unit, because the drawing note says all dimensions are in millimetres)

Task 4 (level 2)

a) 1200 mm. The written dimension gives the full length. The break lines only mean part of the strap was left out of the drawing. b) 1120 mm. 1200 − 40 − 40 = 1120. Each hole centre is 40 mm from its end. The two holes are the same distance from each end, as the note and the drawing show. c) There are two holes, each 14 mm in diameter.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Toward the right (toward the right end of the strap). The arrows on the cutting-plane line point to the right, and they show the viewing direction. b) The cutting plane goes through the middle of the hole. There is no steel where the hole is, so there is nothing to hatch. Only the cut steel above and below the hole is hatched. c) The gate post is not part of the strap. A phantom line shows a part next to the one being drawn, so the reader knows where the strap goes without thinking the post is part of this drawing.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) 12'-0". 1'-0" + 5'-0" + 6'-0" = 12'-0". b) The baseline dimension to the third bolt is 12'-0". It matches. c) 6'-0". 18'-0" − 12'-0" = 6'-0".

Task 7 (level 2)

a) X = 1800 mm. 7200 − (900 + 1200 + 900 + 2400) = 7200 − 5400 = 1800. b) 3900 mm. 900 + 1200 + 1800 = 3900. c) 1200 mm.

Task 8 (level 2)

Yes, it adds up.

  1. Feet: 2 + 3 + 5 + 4 = 14 feet.
  2. Inches: 3 1/2 + 0 + 10 1/2 + 8 = 22 inches.
  3. 22 inches = 1 foot 10 inches. Carry 1 foot.
  4. Total: 15'-10". This matches the overall dimension.

Task 9 (level 3)

No. 1500 + 2750 + 3000 + 1250 = 8500. The overall says 8600. The string is 100 mm short of the overall.

What to do: do not set forms from a guess. Tell the foreman and send a written question (an RFI) to the engineer or designer asking which size is correct. Check other drawings (the plan, the structural drawings and the latest revision) for the same dimensions. Keep the written answer.

Points a good response covers: shows the sum, gives the 100 mm difference, says not to guess, and says to ask in writing.

Task 10 (level 3)

a) 12 mm. There are 4 chain dimensions to the last bolt (1'-0", 5'-0", 6'-0", 6'-0"). Each mark is 3 mm long, so the errors add up: 4 × 3 = 12 mm. b) Up to 3 mm. Each bolt is measured from the end on its own, so the errors do not add up. c) Measuring from the baseline (one end) is better. The bolts must line up with fixed holes in a base plate. With chain measuring, small errors add up along the wall. With baseline measuring, each bolt is only as far off as one measurement.

Task 11 (level 3)

8'-0" = 8 × 12 = 96 inches. 96 × 25.4 = 2438.4 mm.

No, the co-worker is wrong. 2400 mm is 38.4 mm shorter than 8'-0". Before anything is ordered, say that the two drawings do not give the same size, and ask the foreman or designer in writing which size is correct. Do not mix metric and imperial sizes without converting.
