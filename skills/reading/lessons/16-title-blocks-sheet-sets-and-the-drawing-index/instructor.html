Title blocks, sheet sets and the drawing index

Read the title block on a real sheet, find any sheet in a full drawing set with the drawing list, and check the issue, the revision and the date before you build.

Session 16 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

Before the session: project or print the figures on the learner page. Each figure links to a full-size image, so learners on phones can zoom in. If you have a paper drawing set from any job, bring it: learners should handle a real title block and a real drawing list. This lesson builds on Reading lesson 05 (Read a floor plan and its notes) and on the drawing-reading lessons 11 to 15. Lesson 17 goes on to symbols, legends, grids and schedules, and lesson 18 is a capstone that uses the London Square set from start to finish.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hold up a thick drawing set, or show Figure 1 on the screen. Ask: "If the foreman says 'grab A-401', how do you know what that is?" Let a few people guess. Then show that A-401 is a section in London Square and an elevation in the leləm̓ set. Tell a short story: an electrician on a school job roughs in a classroom from the tender set. The construction set moved three outlets. The tender set had no revision clouds yet, because it was not revised. The crew read an old issue, and the boxes came out. Today is about finding the right sheet and proving it is current.

This is session 16 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the sheet number, title, scale, issue and date in a real title block (Identify the information)
  • use a drawing list to find the sheet for a trade question, in three differently numbered sets (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • read revision clouds, deltas and issue blocks, and write any drawing date as year-month-day (Understand and apply the information)
  • spot when a drawing list, issue block or scale note cannot be trusted as it stands, and say what to do (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. Show Figure 11 (the whole leləm̓ A-201 sheet) for 15 seconds. "Point to where you would look first to find out what this sheet is."
  2. "What do the letters A, S, M and E stand for on a drawing set?"
  3. "Write today's date in numbers only." Collect a few answers. Learners will write it several ways. Come back to this in Key idea 6.

Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

  • a drawing set as a book of sheets, split by discipline letter
  • why sheet numbers differ from office to office, and why the drawing list comes first
  • every part of a title block, on a 2022 metric sheet, an imperial teaching sheet and a 1999 scanned sheet
  • issue blocks, revision clouds and deltas
  • writing dates as year-month-day
  • general notes, including "do not scale"
  • what to do when a drawing list or title block does not add up

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 62 + 9 + 6 = 90 minutes.

Notes for the instructor

  • The sets are real. The site owner holds the rights to all three drawing sets and directed that they be published. Treat them with respect. The leləm̓ Community Centre is a Musqueam-associated project. Use the name as printed and do not make jokes about the project or the people on it.
  • London Square dates. Its issue lines read "Feb 10 14", "Jan 19 14" and "Dec 16 13", and the plumbing sheets give the same issues as 14.02.10, 14.01.18 and 13.12.14 (year.month.day). But every London Square plot stamp reads 2/22/2012. A sheet cannot be plotted before it is issued, so something in the teaching set does not add up. This lesson does not ask learners to settle it. If a strong learner notices, praise them: it is exactly the kind of check this lesson teaches.
  • leləm̓ A-201 scale. The title block says "As indicated", the Graphic Scale box is empty, and no view title with a scale appears under the plan (we searched the sheet text and looked at it). The leləm̓ E-300 power plan of the same floor does say 1 : 100. Task 11 uses this on purpose.
  • Fire Hall scan. The Fire Hall PDF was made from paper prints. Its pages are slightly different sizes, so a scale taken off the scan is not reliable. The graphic scale bar in the title block shrinks and stretches with the print, which is why scanned and copied drawings often carry one.
  • Fire Hall drawing list. "M09 Detail" is also typed over the top of the general notes heading on the same sheet (Figure 10). That supports the answer to Task 9: sheets were added after the list was laid out.
