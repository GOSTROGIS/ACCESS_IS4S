# Find it in a table, log or form

## Why this matters on the job

Parts lists, bin labels, maintenance logs, time sheets, material requests and inspection forms are made of lists and tables. You do not read them from top to bottom like a story. You go straight to the row and column you need. A wrong row means the wrong bolt, a missed oil change or a time sheet that does not get paid.

## Key ideas

**1. A simple list** is a group of items under one label. All the items have something in common. Lists are put in an order: by number, by letter, by date, or by the order you do things. Knowing the order helps you find things fast.

**2. A table** puts lists side by side. The first column usually names the thing. The other columns tell you more about it. Read it in this order:
1. **Title**: what is this table about, and for what date or job?
2. **Column headings** across the top, and **row labels** down the side.
3. **Notes and symbols**: an asterisk (*), a dash (–) or a footnote can change the meaning of a cell.
4. Find your row. Keep a finger or a ruler on it while you move across.

**3. Where a row meets a column.** Some tables need two pieces of information to find a third. "How many hours did compressor 2 run on Wednesday?" needs the row (Wednesday) and the column (compressor 2, hours run today). The answer is in the cell where they meet.

**4. A form** is a table you fill in. Before you write, check:
- Who fills in which part? Some parts say "Office use only".
- Which fields are required? They are often marked with an asterisk (*).
- What format is asked for? For example, a date written as YYYY-MM-DD.
- Who signs? Never sign for someone else.

### Worked example

**Question:** What is the box quantity for 1/2" flat washers?

1. Title: Fastener bin list. Good.
2. Headings: Bin, Item, Size, Material, Box quantity.
3. Scan the **Item** column for "Flat washer". Two rows. Check the **Size** column: B07 is 1/2".
4. Move across row B07 to **Box quantity**: **100**.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1)
Use Document A.
a) In what order are the rows listed?
b) Which bin holds 1/2" × 3" hex bolts?
c) How many bolts are in one box from bin B02?

**Task 2** (level 1)
Use Document A. You are fixing a railing outdoors. Which bins hold a matching 3/8" stainless bolt, nut and lock washer?

**Task 3** (level 2)
Use Document B.
a) How many hours did compressor C2 run on Wednesday?
b) The cell for C1 hours since last oil change on Wednesday is blank. What number belongs there? Show how you worked it out.
c) What does the dash in the C2 oil column on Thursday mean?

**Task 4** (level 2)
Use Document B.
a) On which day did C1 go past the oil change limit?
b) Name two things in Friday's row for C1 that need action.
c) On which day was oil added to C2? How do you know?
d) Which compressor ran more hours this week, and by how many?

## Check yourself

1. Name the three things to read in a table before you look for your answer.
2. In a table where you need a row and a column, where is the answer?
3. What does an asterisk (*) next to a form field often mean?
4. A form has a box for your lead hand's signature. Your lead hand is not here. What do you do?
