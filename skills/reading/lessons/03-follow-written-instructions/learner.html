Reading · Lesson 03 · Learner

Follow written instructions step by step

Read a set of instructions all the way through, find the order, the numbers and the conditions, and carry them out without skipping a step.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information

Why this matters on the job

A plumber gluing ABS drain pipe, a carpenter staining a rail, a millwright changing a filter: each job comes with written steps. Every step you skip or do out of order is a place where the job can fail. A joint that is pressure tested too soon can leak inside a finished wall. Reading instructions well is a safety skill and a money skill.

Key ideas

1. Read all the steps before you start. You may find a warning at the end that changes step 1, or a tool you need to get first.

2. Find the order. Numbers and letters show order. So do these words: first, next, then, after, before, while, until, finally, last. Watch for "but first", which moves a step earlier.

3. Find the numbers. Times, temperatures, sizes and amounts. Write them down or circle them. Check the unit: seconds or minutes, mm or inches.

4. Find the conditions. "If…", "when…", "only if…", "unless…". A condition tells you when a step applies, or when to stop.

5. Find the warnings. "Do not", "never", "discard". These are steps too.

6. One numbered step can hold two actions. "Hold the joint for 30 seconds and wipe off the extra" is two actions. Do both.

7. Keep your place. Tick each step as you finish it.

Worked example

"Apply a thin coat of cement to the fitting, then a heavier coat to the pipe. Assemble quickly while the cement is still wet."

  • Order words: then. Fitting first, pipe second.
  • Two actions in the first sentence: thin coat on fitting, heavier coat on pipe.
  • Condition: while the cement is still wet. If it has dried, you are too late.

Practice documents

Document A: Read everything first

Read all of these steps before you do anything. 1. Write your name at the top of this page. 2. Circle the word "bolt" in step 4. 3. Write today's date under your name. 4. Count the bolts in the box on your table and write the number here: ____ 5. Draw a small square in the margin. 6. Now that you have read all the steps, do only step 1. Then put your pencil down.

Document B: Practice label for a solvent cement

This practice label is adapted from an older ACCESS handout. It is for a fictional product. Real products differ, so always follow the label on the can you are using.

Coastline ABS Cement, Medium Bodied, Black (fictional product) General directions for making a solvent cement joint - Look at the cement before using it. If it has turned to jelly, throw it out. Do not use primer or cleaner to thin the cement. - Cut the pipe square and remove all dirt and burrs. - Check the dry fit. The pipe should go easily into the fitting about 1/4 of the way. - Apply a thin coat of cement inside the fitting. Do not let it puddle. - Apply a heavier coat of cement to the pipe. Leave no bare spots. - Assemble quickly while the cement is still wet. Push the pipe into the fitting with a 1/4 turn until it bottoms out. - Hold the pipe and fitting together for 30 seconds. Wipe off the extra cement at the joint. - Allow 15 minutes before handling the joint. - Allow 24 to 48 hours before applying pressure to the joint. - Stir the cement often while using it. Use an applicator about 1/2 the diameter of the pipe. Keep the can tightly closed when not in use.

Document C: Toolbox card

Kestrel Point Builders Ltd. (fictional company) · Toolbox card TC-04: Staining the new deck rails Stain the rails only when all of these are true: - the wood is dry to the touch - the air temperature is at least 10 °C now, and the forecast keeps it at or above 10 °C until the stain is dry to the touch (see the drying time on the can) - no rain is forecast for the next 24 hours

If any one of these is not true, do other work and tell the foreman.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Follow Document A exactly.

Task 2 (level 1) These steps from Document B are mixed up. Put the letters in the correct order.

A. Hold the pipe and fitting together for 30 seconds. B. Cut the pipe square and remove burrs. C. Apply a heavier coat to the pipe. D. Look at the cement and throw it out if it has turned to jelly. E. Push the pipe in with a 1/4 turn until it bottoms out. F. Check the dry fit. G. Apply a thin coat inside the fitting. H. Wait 24 to 48 hours before applying pressure.

Order: ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___ ___

Task 3 (level 2) Use Document B. a) How long do you hold the joint together? b) How long before you can handle the joint? c) How long before you can apply pressure? d) How far should the pipe go into the fitting on a dry fit? e) You are joining 3-inch pipe. About what size applicator should you use?

Task 4 (level 2) A co-worker describes how he made a joint. Find three things he did that go against Document B.

"The cement was getting thick so I thinned it with a bit of primer. I put cement on the pipe and the fitting, then went and grabbed a coffee while it set up a bit. Then I pushed them together. I pressure tested the line an hour later."

Task 5 (level 2) Use Document C. The can of stain says "dry to the touch in 4 hours". a) It is 9:00 a.m. The rails are dry. It is 14 °C now. The forecast says 12 °C at 1:00 p.m. and 6 °C overnight. Rain is forecast to start in 30 hours. Can you stain the rails this morning? Explain. b) Same day, but rain is now forecast to start in 12 hours. Can you stain? What do you do instead?

Task 6 (level 2) Turn this paragraph into numbered steps, in the right order.

Before you leave the area at the end of the day, close every can of cement and primer tightly. Put the cans back in the flammables cabinet, but first wipe any cement off the outside of each can with a dry rag. Used rags go in the metal rag bin with the lid closed, not in the regular garbage. Last, write on the job board which joints are less than 24 hours old so that nobody pressure tests them in the morning.

Check yourself

  1. Why should you read all the steps before starting?
  2. Name four words that show the order of steps.
  3. What does "but first" do to the order of steps?
  4. In Document C, how many conditions must be true before you stain, and what do you do if one is not?
