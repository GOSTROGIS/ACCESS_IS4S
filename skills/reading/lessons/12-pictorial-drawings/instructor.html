Pictorial drawings: isometric, oblique and perspective

Tell isometric, oblique and perspective drawings apart, know which lines you can measure on each, read a single-line piping isometric, and sketch an object on isometric grid paper.

  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Session 12 of 18 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show a photo or a real view down a straight road or a long hallway. Ask: "What happens to the edges of the road as they go away from you?" They seem to meet. Then show a piping isometric or a flat-pack furniture instruction sheet. Ask: "Do the edges meet here?" No, they stay parallel. Tell learners that drawings use different kinds of pictures for different jobs, and that some can be measured and some cannot.

This is session 12 of 18 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes. It builds on lesson 11 (orthographic views).

Before the session: print isometric grid paper (or use the grid in Figure 6) and square grid paper. Bring a 30/60 set square if you have one, and a real piping isometric or spool sheet if your trade partners can share one.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell isometric, cavalier oblique, cabinet oblique, one-point perspective and two-point perspective drawings apart (Identify the information)
  • connect a pictorial drawing to the orthographic views it came from, and follow a pipe route through an isometric (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • read lengths from a piping isometric and sketch an object on isometric grid paper (Understand and apply the information)
  • judge which lines on a pictorial can be measured, and when a drawing must not be scaled (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. Show Figure 1 with the labels covered. "Which of these three pictures looks the most stretched? Why?"
  2. "Have you ever used a drawing where the pipe was shown as one line? What did it show you?"

Post-assessment (9 min)

  1. "Draw the three isometric axes and label the angles."
  2. "A sketch shows a bolt hole as a perfect circle on the front of a part. Is it isometric or oblique? How do you know?"
  3. "A pipe isometric says NOT TO SCALE. Where do you get the length of a run?"

Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

  • isometric drawings: 30° axes, 120° apart, full size along the axes
  • isometric and non-isometric lines, and circles as ellipses
  • cavalier and cabinet oblique, with true-shape front faces
  • one-point and two-point perspective, vanishing points and the horizon line
  • reading a single-line piping isometric
  • sketching on isometric grid paper
  • which pictorial suits which job, and why you only measure along the axes

Next session (lesson 13) covers the alphabet of lines and how to read dimensions.

Government of Canada, "Skill components and proficiency levels", https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/training/initiatives/skills-success/learning-steps.html, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Pictorial views are perspective, isometric and oblique. In isometric the plumb lines are vertical and the horizontal lines are at 30° to a line parallel to the bottom of the page, there is no vanishing point, and lengths are exact only when parallel to an axis. In oblique the front face is drawn to exact scale and the oblique lines go back at 30° to 45°. New Brunswick Community College and Camosun College, "Views and Projections", Technically Drawn (open textbook, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, adapted from BC Trades Access Common Core Competency D-3), https://pressbooks.atlanticoer-relatlantique.ca/lined/chapter/d3-21/, checked 2026-11-08.
  • The three isometric axes are 120° apart and circles appear as ellipses on all three isometric planes. Isometric drawings are drawn at full scale along the axes. Engineering LibreTexts, Illinois Institute of Technology, "3.3: Isometric projections", https://eng.libretexts.org/Courses/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology/Introduction_to_Engineering_Drawing_and_Design/03%3A_Module_C_-__Auxiliary_views_section_views_isometric_and_oblique_projections/3.03%3A_Isometric_projections, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Lines parallel to the isometric axes are isometric lines and can be measured. Lines not parallel to the axes are non-isometric lines and cannot be measured. Goodheart-Willcox, "Introduction to Isometric Drawings" (AutoCAD and Its Applications Basics, supplemental material), https://www.g-wlearning.com/cad/0619/ch03/data/supmat03a.pdf, checked 2026-11-08.
  • In oblique drawings the front face is true shape and size and depth is projected at an angle, commonly 45°. Cavalier projection draws depth at true length. Cabinet projection draws depth at half length and is common in the furniture industry. A circle parallel to the front plane stays a circle. Engineering LibreTexts, Illinois Institute of Technology, "3.4: Oblique projections", https://eng.libretexts.org/Courses/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology/Introduction_to_Engineering_Drawing_and_Design/03%3A_Module_C_-__Auxiliary_views_section_views_isometric_and_oblique_projections/3.04%3A_Oblique_projections, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Perspective is the most realistic drawing and is used by architects where realism is required. Isometric drawings are the most used type of drawing in the piping industry. Richard Gentry, Sacramento City College, "1.2: Types drawings", Blueprint Reading and Technical Sketching (Workforce LibreTexts), https://workforce.libretexts.org/Courses/Sacramento_City_College/Blueprint_Reading_and_Technical_Sketching_(Gentry)/01%3A_Introduction_to_Blueprints_and_Technical_Sketches/1.02%3A_Types_drawings, checked 2026-11-08.
  • In one-point and two-point perspective the vanishing points sit on the horizon line at eye level. One-point uses one vanishing point and two-point uses two. Northern Michigan University, Art and Design Foundations, "Linear Perspective", http://artnet.nmu.edu/foundations/doku.php?id=linear_perspective, and Illinois State Board of Education and CAERT, "Perspective Projection Methods", https://www.isbe.net/CTEDocuments/TEE-600112.pdf, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Piping isometric drawings are not scale drawings, so they are dimensioned. Pipes are shown as single lines, and symbols show fittings, valves and welds. ScienceDirect Topics, "Piping Isometric" (book excerpts), https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/piping-isometric, and S. V. Lele, "Piping Isometric Drawings", http://www.svlele.com/piping/isoinfo.htm, checked 2026-11-08. The north arrow on a piping isometric usually points up and to the right: "Piping Isometrics" (Pipe Drafting and Design, chapter 13), https://kh.aquaenergyexpo.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Piping-Isometrics.pdf, checked 2026-11-08.
  • The pipe support block, the sloped block, the shed, ISO-101 and the step block, and all their sizes, are fictional and were drawn for this lesson by a script. The isometric and oblique drawings use the standard axis angles. The perspective drawing was computed with a pinhole camera model, so its vanishing points are exact. ISO-101 is drawn not to scale on purpose, like a real piping isometric.
