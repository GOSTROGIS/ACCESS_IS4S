# Pictorial drawings: isometric, oblique and perspective

## Task 1 (level 1)

a) The **cavalier oblique** and the **cabinet oblique** drawings.
b) In oblique, the front face is drawn true shape, like a front view. The hole is on the front face, so it stays a circle.
c) An **ellipse.**

## Task 2 (level 1)

a) **30°.**
b) **120°.**
c) The **height** axis.

## Task 3 (level 1)

a) **HL = horizon line** (the viewer's eye level). **VP = vanishing point.**
b) The one-point drawing has **one** VP. The two-point drawing has **two** VPs.
c) They **stay vertical.** Only the horizontal edges run to the vanishing points.

## Task 4 (level 2)

a) **60 mm** (full depth).
b) **30 mm** (half depth). 60 ÷ 2 = 30.
c) **Cabinet oblique.** The depth line is 600 ÷ 2 = **300 mm** long at full size.

## Task 5 (level 2)

a) **Yes.** It is parallel to the width axis, so it is an isometric line drawn at full size.
b) **Yes.** It is vertical, parallel to the height axis.
c) **No.** It is not parallel to any axis, so it is a non-isometric line. It is drawn about 79 mm long, but the real edge is about 67 mm (Worked example 1).

## Task 6 (level 2)

1. **Up 1200 mm**
2. **East 2400 mm** (through the gate valve to the tee)
3. **East 1500 mm**
4. **Down 600 mm**
5. **North 1800 mm** to the end flange

Steps: north is up and to the right (north arrow), so lines going down and to the right are east. Vertical lines are up or down.

## Task 7 (level 2)

a) **1 tee** and **1 valve** (a gate valve).
b) 7500 + 900 = **8400 mm = 8.4 m.**
c) The lengths of the lines on the paper do not match the real lengths. Use only the written dimensions.

## Task 8 (level 2)

a) **Oblique.** Put the face with the holes at the front. The holes stay true circles and the sketch is quick.
b) **Perspective.** It looks the most like what the eye sees.
c) **Isometric.** Piping isometrics show the whole route in one picture, with every fitting and written dimensions.
d) **Isometric** (on isometric grid paper). All three sizes are drawn at full length along the axes, so nothing looks stretched. (Cabinet oblique is also a good answer if the learner explains that half depth stops the stretched look.)

## Task 9 (level 3)

From the red dot (front left bottom corner):

1. Draw the width: 8 steps along the line going down and to the right.
2. Draw the depth: 5 steps along the line going up and to the right.
3. Draw the height: 6 steps straight up.
4. Complete the box, then cut away the step: 3 steps in from the right end and 3 steps down from the top, all the way through the depth.

## Task 11 (level 3)

In a perspective drawing, things farther away are drawn smaller, and the edges slope toward the vanishing points. The same real length is drawn at a different size in different places, so the framer cannot measure the door or header from it.

The framer needs an **orthographic drawing**: a dimensioned **elevation** of the wall with the door (and the floor plan). It should show the rough opening width and height, the header size and the height of the header from the floor, with written dimensions.

Points a good response covers: says sizes change with distance in perspective, names an orthographic view (elevation or plan), and lists at least two sizes the framer needs.
