## Task 2 (level 1)

The signal word is **Warning**. A product with **Danger** would be **more** hazardous. Danger is used for more severe hazards.

## Task 3 (level 2)

a) **Section 8.** Nitrile gloves.
b) **Section 4.** Wash with soap and water.
c) **Section 7.** In a cool, well-ventilated area away from heat and ignition sources, such as a flammables cabinet, and away from strong oxidizers.
d) **Section 5.** Dry chemical, carbon dioxide or foam. Not a direct water jet.
e) **Section 1.** Removing grease and oil from metal parts.
f) **Section 16.** 2024-02-12.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Any three of:
- the **type** of gloves (nitrile) and eye protection (safety glasses with side shields, or goggles)
- the **24-hour emergency phone number**
- first aid for skin, breathing it in and swallowing it, not only the eye
- which extinguishers to use and not to use
- that it must be kept away from **strong oxidizers**
- the recommended use and the restrictions on use
- the hazard class and category
- the date of the latest revision

## Task 5 (level 2)

A good workplace label shows:

- **Product identifier:** ShopClean Degreaser 40 (exactly as on the SDS)
- **Safe handling information:** for example, "Flammable. Keep away from heat, sparks and flames. Wear nitrile gloves and safety glasses. Keep the bottle closed." The flame and exclamation mark pictograms may be used.
- **Reference to the SDS:** for example, "See the SDS in the shop SDS binder."

The crew will use the bottle all week, so a full workplace label is needed. A bottle with only "degreaser" written on it is not enough.

## Task 6 (level 3)

Next check due: **2027-02-12** (2024-02-12 plus three years). On 2026-11-06 it is **not overdue**. It is due in a little over three months, so it is a good time to ask the supplier whether there is a newer version.

## Task 7 (level 3)

**Do not use the new product until you have the right SDS.** The product identifier on the label must match the SDS. "ShopClean 40 Citrus" from a different supplier is a different product as far as WHMIS is concerned, even if it looks the same. Its hazards, gloves or first aid could be different. Tell your supervisor and ask for the SDS that matches the new jug. In BC, the employer must not let a hazardous product be used unless the WHMIS requirements for labels, product identifiers and SDSs are met.

Points a good response covers: the names do not match, the supplier is different, the binder SDS cannot be assumed to apply, and the next step is to get the right SDS through the supervisor.

## Task 8 (level 3)

**No, it is probably not good enough.** WorkSafeBC's OHS Regulation says the employer must make the SDS **readily available at the workplace** to workers who may be exposed to the product. A binder locked away during your shift is not readily available to you.

What to do: raise it politely with the foreman. Ask for a copy you can reach during the night shift, for example a second binder in the shop or a device with the SDSs on it. If it is not fixed, take it to the joint health and safety committee or the worker health and safety representative.
