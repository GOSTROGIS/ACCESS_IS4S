Read a WHMIS label and a safety data sheet

Find the hazard, the protection and the emergency steps on a WHMIS supplier label and its 16-section safety data sheet, and know when a workplace label is needed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to read; Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

Degreasers, solvent cement, spray paint, cutting fluid, concrete additives and welding gas all come with a WHMIS label and a safety data sheet (SDS). In BC, your employer must make the SDS readily available to workers who may be exposed to the product. The label tells you the main hazards in seconds. The SDS tells you the details: which gloves, how to store it, what to do if it splashes in your eye. Reading them well protects you and your crew.

This lesson is reading practice. It does not replace the WHMIS education and training your employer must give you for the products at your workplace.

Key ideas

1. WHMIS is the Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System. A hazardous product at work has a label and a safety data sheet. Canada updated the WHMIS supplier rules in December 2022. The transition period ended on December 14, 2025, so labels and SDSs should now meet the updated rules.

2. A supplier label comes on the product from the supplier. It must be in English and French. It has these parts:

Label part What it tells you
Product identifier the name of the product, which must match the SDS
Initial supplier identifier the name, address and phone number of the Canadian maker or importer
Pictogram(s) a symbol inside a red diamond border that shows the type of hazard
Signal word Danger for more severe hazards, Warning for less severe ones
Hazard statement(s) standard sentences that say what the hazard is
Precautionary statement(s) standard sentences that say how to protect yourself, store the product and respond to an emergency, including first aid
Supplemental label information other information, such as an instruction to read the SDS

3. A workplace label is needed when a product is made at the workplace, when it is poured or pumped into another container (decanted), or when the supplier label is lost or cannot be read. A workplace label gives: - the product identifier (the name, matching the SDS) - safe handling information - a reference to the SDS

4. The SDS has 16 sections, always in the same order. That makes it easy to scan.

Section Heading
1 Identification
2 Hazard identification
3 Composition/Information on ingredients
4 First-aid measures
5 Fire-fighting measures
6 Accidental release measures
7 Handling and storage
8 Exposure controls/Personal protection
9 Physical and chemical properties
10 Stability and reactivity
11 Toxicological information
12 Ecological information
13 Disposal considerations
14 Transport information
15 Regulatory information
16 Other information (includes the date of the latest revision)

For most jobs, read Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 first.

5. In BC, SDSs are checked every three years. WorkSafeBC says SDSs must be checked every three years to make sure they have the most current information. Look at the revision date in Section 16.

Worked example

Question: What should you do if ShopClean Degreaser 40 splashes in your eye?

  1. Purpose: first aid for the eye.
  2. On the label, scan the precautionary statements for "IF IN EYES". It says to rinse cautiously with water for several minutes, remove contact lenses if present and easy to do, and continue rinsing.
  3. For more detail, go to SDS Section 4, First-aid measures, and read "Eye contact".

Practice documents

All documents below are for a fictional product from a fictional supplier. Only the English part of the label is shown.

Document A: Supplier label

ShopClean Degreaser 40

[Pictogram: flame] [Pictogram: exclamation mark]

WARNING

Flammable liquid and vapour. Causes serious eye irritation.

Keep away from heat, hot surfaces, sparks, open flames and other ignition sources. No smoking. Keep container tightly closed. Wear protective gloves and eye protection. IF IN EYES: Rinse cautiously with water for several minutes. Remove contact lenses, if present and easy to do. Continue rinsing. If eye irritation persists: Get medical attention. Store in a well-ventilated place. Keep cool.

Read the safety data sheet before use.

Tidewater Chemical Supply Ltd., 1200 Harbour Road, Port Kestrel, BC · 604-555-0142

Document B: Safety data sheet (excerpt)

SAFETY DATA SHEET · ShopClean Degreaser 40

Section 1. Identification Product identifier: ShopClean Degreaser 40 Recommended use: removing grease and oil from metal parts Restrictions on use: do not use on hot surfaces or near open flames Supplier: Tidewater Chemical Supply Ltd., 1200 Harbour Road, Port Kestrel, BC · 604-555-0142 Emergency telephone: 604-555-0199 (24 hours)

Section 2. Hazard identification Classification: Flammable liquids, Category 3. Eye irritation, Category 2A. Pictograms: flame, exclamation mark. Signal word: Warning. Hazard statements: Flammable liquid and vapour. Causes serious eye irritation.

Section 4. First-aid measures Eye contact: Rinse cautiously with water for several minutes. Remove contact lenses, if present and easy to do. Continue rinsing. If eye irritation persists, get medical attention. Skin contact: Wash with soap and water. Inhalation: Move the person to fresh air. Ingestion: Rinse mouth. Call a poison centre or doctor.

Section 5. Fire-fighting measures Suitable extinguishing media: dry chemical, carbon dioxide, foam. Unsuitable extinguishing media: direct water jet.

Section 7. Handling and storage Keep the container closed when not in use. Store in a cool, well-ventilated area away from heat and ignition sources, such as a flammables cabinet. Keep away from strong oxidizers.

Section 8. Exposure controls/Personal protection Engineering controls: use with good general ventilation. Eye protection: safety glasses with side shields, or goggles. Hand protection: nitrile gloves.

Section 16. Other information Date of latest revision: 2024-02-12

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Find each of the seven supplier label parts on Document A. Write what each one says.

Task 2 (level 1) What is the signal word on Document A? Would a product with the signal word "Danger" be more or less hazardous?

Task 3 (level 2) Which SDS section would you go to for each question? Then answer it from Document B. a) What kind of gloves should you wear? b) What should you do if it splashes on your skin? c) Where should the product be stored? d) What kind of fire extinguisher can be used on it? e) What is the product meant to be used for? f) When was the SDS last revised?

Task 4 (level 2) Name three things the SDS tells you that the label does not.

Task 5 (level 2) You pour some degreaser into a spray bottle. The crew will use it all week. Write the workplace label for the spray bottle.

Task 6 (level 3) Today is 2026-11-06. In BC, SDSs are checked every three years. Using Section 16 of Document B, when is this SDS next due to be checked? Is it overdue?

Task 7 (level 3) A new jug arrives. Its label says "ShopClean 40 Citrus" and gives a different supplier. The SDS in the binder is for "ShopClean Degreaser 40" from Tidewater. A co-worker says, "Same stuff, just use the binder SDS." What do you do, and why?

Task 8 (level 3) You work the night shift. Your foreman says the SDS binder is kept in the site office, which is locked after 4:00 p.m. Is that good enough? What would you do?

Check yourself

  1. What are the two signal words, and which one is for the more severe hazard?
  2. Name the three things a workplace label must show.
  3. Which SDS section tells you about personal protective equipment?
  4. Which SDS section has the date of the latest revision?
  5. Name one time when a workplace label is needed.
