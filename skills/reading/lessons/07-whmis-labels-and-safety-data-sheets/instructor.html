Read a WHMIS label and a safety data sheet

Find the hazard, the protection and the emergency steps on a WHMIS supplier label and its 16-section safety data sheet, and know when a workplace label is needed.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to read; Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Session 7 of 10 in the Reading lessons · 75 minutes

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Put a real product from the shop on the table, such as a can of spray paint or a jug of cleaner, label facing the class. Ask: "Without opening the SDS, what can you tell me about the danger from this label alone?" Take answers. Then ask: "Where is the SDS for it in this building?" Many learners will not know. Today is about getting the safety information out of a label and an SDS quickly and correctly.

This is session 7 of 10 in the Reading lessons. It runs 75 minutes.

Tell learners clearly: this is a reading lesson. It is not WHMIS certification, and it does not replace the WHMIS education and training their employer must provide for the products at their workplace.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say what they need to find before they open a label or SDS (Identify the task that requires you to read)
  • find the seven parts of a supplier label and the right SDS section for a question (Identify the information)
  • connect label statements to the matching SDS sections (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • write a workplace label and decide when one is needed (Understand and apply the information)
  • judge whether an SDS matches the product and is current (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. "What does SDS stand for, and how many sections does one have?"
  2. "What is the difference between 'Danger' and 'Warning' on a label?"

Post-assessment (6 min)

  1. Hand out a real SDS for a product used in the trade (from the supplier's website or the shop binder). "Find the gloves the SDS recommends. Which section did you use?"
  2. "You see a bucket of liquid with no label at all. What do you do?"
  3. "Name three SDS sections you would read before using a new product."

Summary (4 min)

During this session we covered:

  • what WHMIS is, and that the updated supplier rules now apply
  • the seven parts of a supplier label
  • when a workplace label is needed, and what it shows
  • the 16 sections of an SDS and which ones to read first
  • checking that the SDS matches the product and is current
  • the employer's duty to make SDSs readily available

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 52 + 6 + 4 = 75 minutes.
