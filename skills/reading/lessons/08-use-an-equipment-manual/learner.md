# Use an equipment manual

## Why this matters on the job

Every grinder, saw, pump and compressor comes with a manual. Millwrights, fabricators and service techs use manuals to set up equipment, fix faults and order parts. A manual is not read like a book. You jump to the page you need using the contents page, the index and the tables. The safety warnings at the front apply to every page after them.

## Key ideas

**1. Contents page or index?**
- The **contents page** at the front lists the sections in order. Use it when you know the general topic, such as "maintenance".
- The **index** at the back lists small topics in alphabetical order, with page numbers. Use it when you are looking for one thing, such as "brushes", that does not have its own section.

**2. Read the safety warnings first.** They are at the front, and they apply to the whole manual. Words like *always*, *never*, *do not* and *only* matter.

**3. The specifications table** lists the numbers for the tool: voltage, current, speed, size, weight. The same numbers are usually on the **nameplate** (the rating label on the tool). Check that they match.

**4. The troubleshooting table** has three columns: the problem, the possible cause and what to do. Find your problem in the first column, then read across. A problem can have more than one possible cause. Try the simple, safe fixes first.

**5. The parts list** connects a reference number on a drawing to a part number you can order. Order by the **part number**, not the reference number.

**6. A manual can disagree with what you see online.** The manual for your model and edition is written for that tool. If a video, a forum or a co-worker says something different, follow the manual and ask your supervisor.

### Worked example

**Question:** What size disc does the AG-125 take?

1. It is a number about the tool, so go to the specifications table.
2. The contents page says Specifications is on page 4.
3. Read across the row "Disc diameter": **125 mm**.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1)
Use the contents page.
a) On what page does fitting a disc start?
b) On what page is the troubleshooting table?

**Task 2** (level 1)
Use the specifications table and the nameplate.
a) What is the no-load speed?
b) What is the rated input current?
c) Do the nameplate and the specifications table agree on voltage, current and speed?

**Task 3** (level 2)
You find three 125 mm discs in the shop. Which ones can you use on this grinder? Which warning tells you?
a) A disc marked "Max 8 500 r/min"
b) A disc marked "Max 13 300 r/min"
c) A disc marked "Max 11 000 r/min"

**Task 4** (level 2)
You are grinding at the far end of a yard, 30 m from the outlet, on a long, thin extension cord. The grinder keeps slowing down and the body is getting hot. Use the troubleshooting table: what is the likely cause, and what do you do? Which other section does the table send you to?

**Task 5** (level 2)
The wheel guard on your grinder is cracked and the clamp nut is lost. What part numbers do you order? Can you keep using the grinder until the parts arrive?

**Task 6** (level 2)
Your crew uses this grinder 6 hours a day, 5 days a week. About how many weeks of use is it until the 100-hour brush check? Show your work.

**Task 7** (level 3)
You want to read everything the manual says about carbon brushes. Would you use the contents page or the index? Which pages would you read, and why might the contents page alone miss some of them?

**Task 8** (level 3)
A co-worker shows you a video where someone takes the guard off a grinder "to get into tight corners". He says it is fine because the person in the video is a pro. What does the manual say? What do you do?

## Check yourself

1. When is the index better than the contents page?
2. What are the three columns of a troubleshooting table?
3. When you order a part, do you use the reference number or the part number?
4. Where in a manual do you find the warnings that apply to the whole tool?
5. A disc's maximum speed must be equal to or greater than what?
