## Task 4 (level 2)

**No.** Key idea 2: the facts decide, not the label, and calling a worker a contractor does not decide it even if the worker agrees. Key idea 5: the Province of BC lists "doesn't have statutory deductions taken from earnings" as something that does not, on its own, make a worker a contractor. Ray not taking deductions is a choice Ray is making. It does not change what the work really is.

## Task 5 (level 3)

Any three, with a reason:
- **Apprenticeship hours.** Ray is not Jordan's sponsor, so Ray does not submit work-based training hours to SkilledTradesBC. A later sponsor can only credit past hours with enough proof, and cash work with no paperwork gives Jordan none. "I'll sign something later" is a promise, not proof.
- **No deductions.** No CPP, EI or income tax is taken off and sent to the CRA for Jordan. The tax is still owed, so Jordan may face a bill at tax time with nothing set aside.
- **Proof if something goes wrong.** With no paperwork it is hard to prove where Jordan worked, how many hours, and what he is owed, if he is hurt or not paid.
- **Employment standards.** If Jordan really were an independent contractor, the *Employment Standards Act* would not protect him. If he is really an employee, he is protected, but he may have to prove it with no records.
- **Probation, orientation and steady work.** Document A gives a probation period, a safety orientation and set hours. Document B promises none of these.

## Task 6 (level 3)

Any two:
- **The CRA:** Jordan (or Ray) can ask for a ruling on whether the work is employment or self-employment, in My Account or with form CPT1, by June 29 of the year after the work.
- **BC Employment Standards:** for questions about whether the *Employment Standards Act* applies, and about pay. Website: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards
- **SkilledTradesBC:** for questions about registering as an apprentice, sponsors and getting hours counted. Website: https://skilledtradesbc.ca
