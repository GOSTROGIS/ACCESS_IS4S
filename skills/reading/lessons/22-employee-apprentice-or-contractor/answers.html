Employee, apprentice or contractor

Read a job offer and judge whether it makes you an employee or a self-employed contractor, and what that means for deductions, WorkSafeBC coverage and your apprenticeship hours.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s); Reflect on the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) $24.00 an hour b) 40 hours a week, Monday to Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. c) 3 months d) Granite Ridge supplies all power tools (and the work truck and fittings). Jordan supplies basic hand tools from the list.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) Document A: $24 × 40 = $960 b) Document B: $30 × 40 = $1,200 c) $1,200 − $960 = $240 more each week, before any deductions.

Task 3 (level 2)

Question What Document B says Points to
Control "I'll tell you where and when each day." Ray decides. Employee
Tools and equipment "Use my tools and my van." Ray supplies them. Employee
Helpers Nothing says Jordan can hire or send someone else. The offer is to Jordan. Employee
Financial risk Jordan pays no costs of his own. Employee
Investment and management Jordan has no business, truck or tools in this. Employee
Chance of profit Paid by the hour. No way to make a profit or a loss. Employee

It looks like employee work. Every one of the six questions points to employee, even though Ray calls it contractor work. Only the CRA, Employment Standards or a court can make the final decision, but the facts do not match the label.

Task 4 (level 2)

No. Key idea 2: the facts decide, not the label, and calling a worker a contractor does not decide it even if the worker agrees. Key idea 5: the Province of BC lists "doesn't have statutory deductions taken from earnings" as something that does not, on its own, make a worker a contractor. Ray not taking deductions is a choice Ray is making. It does not change what the work really is.

Task 5 (level 3)

Any three, with a reason: - Apprenticeship hours. Ray is not Jordan's sponsor, so Ray does not submit work-based training hours to SkilledTradesBC. A later sponsor can only credit past hours with enough proof, and cash work with no paperwork gives Jordan none. "I'll sign something later" is a promise, not proof. - No deductions. No CPP, EI or income tax is taken off and sent to the CRA for Jordan. The tax is still owed, so Jordan may face a bill at tax time with nothing set aside. - Proof if something goes wrong. With no paperwork it is hard to prove where Jordan worked, how many hours, and what he is owed, if he is hurt or not paid. - Employment standards. If Jordan really were an independent contractor, the Employment Standards Act would not protect him. If he is really an employee, he is protected, but he may have to prove it with no records. - Probation, orientation and steady work. Document A gives a probation period, a safety orientation and set hours. Document B promises none of these.

Task 6 (level 3)

Any two: - The CRA: Jordan (or Ray) can ask for a ruling on whether the work is employment or self-employment, in My Account or with form CPT1, by June 29 of the year after the work. - BC Employment Standards: for questions about whether the Employment Standards Act applies, and about pay. Website: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards - SkilledTradesBC: for questions about registering as an apprentice, sponsors and getting hours counted. Website: https://skilledtradesbc.ca

Task 7 (level 3)

a) - Document A was written by Granite Ridge Mechanical (Dana K., Operations Manager). It wants Jordan to accept the job on its terms and sign by May 15. It sets out pay, hours, tools and the apprenticeship clearly. - Document B was written by Ray. He wants Jordan on site Monday, paid by the hour, with no paperwork. "More money in your pocket" is a selling point that leaves out what Jordan gives up.

b) Any sensible answer, for example: overtime pay, vacation, what happens to hours on rain days or slow weeks, how pay rises as an apprentice moves through levels, who pays for school time, how to report a safety concern, or what is on the hand-tool list and what it costs.
