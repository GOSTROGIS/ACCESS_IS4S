# Employee, apprentice or contractor

## Why this matters on the job

Some offers come on letterhead. Some come by text: "cash, no paperwork, you're a contractor". The words in an offer change a lot: who takes off tax, whether BC's employment standards protect you, how WorkSafeBC treats you, and whether your hours count toward your apprenticeship. Reading the offer closely before you start protects your pay and your path to a ticket.

This lesson teaches reading. It is not legal or tax advice. The places to ask are at the end of the Key ideas.

## Key ideas

**1. Two kinds of working relationship.**
- An **employee** works in someone else's business. The employer directs the work and pays wages.
- A **self-employed person** (an independent contractor) runs their own business and sells a service to a customer.

**2. The label does not decide it. The facts do.** The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) says the facts of the whole working relationship decide your status. The Province of BC says that calling a worker an independent contractor, even if the worker agrees, does not decide it. A good question to ask is: **whose business is it?**

**3. The CRA's six questions.** The CRA looks at:

| Question | Points to employee | Points to self-employed |
|---|---|---|
| **Control:** who decides what work you do, when and how? | The payer decides | You decide and can turn work down |
| **Tools and equipment:** who supplies them and pays to repair and insure them? | The payer supplies most of them | You supply them and have a big investment in them |
| **Helpers:** can you hire someone or send someone in your place? | You must do the work yourself | You can hire and pay a helper |
| **Financial risk:** do you pay costs that are not paid back? | No ongoing costs of your own | You pay fixed costs even when there is no work |
| **Investment and management:** do you run a business? | No business of your own | You have money in the business and manage it |
| **Chance of profit:** can you make a profit or a loss? | Paid by the hour, no profit or loss | Paid a flat price for a job, and you carry the costs |

No single answer decides it. The CRA looks at each one, then at all of them together.

**4. Why it matters.**
- **Employee:** the employer takes Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions, Employment Insurance (EI) premiums and income tax off your pay and sends them to the CRA, with its own share of CPP and EI. BC's *Employment Standards Act* protects you. WorkSafeBC says BC workers are covered automatically, and an employer who hires workers must register.
- **Self-employed:** nobody takes deductions off what you are paid. You run a business and look after its tax. The *Employment Standards Act* does not apply to independent contractors. A self-employed sole proprietor is not automatically covered by WorkSafeBC, but can buy optional Personal Coverage.
- **Apprentice:** you register with SkilledTradesBC, and your employer is your **sponsor**. Sponsors submit your work-based training hours to SkilledTradesBC. A new sponsor can credit hours from an earlier job, but must collect enough proof first. Cash work with no paperwork leaves you with no proof.

**5. Things that do not prove you are a contractor.** The Province of BC lists things that, on their own, do not make a worker an independent contractor. You may still be an employee even if you agree to be called a contractor, charge GST, send invoices instead of time cards, have no deductions taken off, work without much supervision, drive your own car, bring your own tools, or are paid by the piece.

**6. Where to ask.**
- **CRA:** either you or the payer can ask the CRA for a ruling on whether you are an employee or self-employed. You can ask in My Account or with form CPT1. Ask by June 29 of the year after the work.
- **BC Employment Standards:** https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards
- **SkilledTradesBC**, for questions about your apprenticeship and hours: https://skilledtradesbc.ca

### Worked example

**Question:** A framer brings her own hammer, tool belt and nail gun to work. Does that make her a contractor?

1. Key idea 3, tools: supplying tools can point to self-employed, but the CRA also notes that many tradespeople, such as mechanics, must supply their own tools even when they are full-time employees.
2. Key idea 5: "provides their own tools" is on the Province's list of things that do not decide it on their own.
3. **No, not on its own.** Look at the other questions too: who controls her work, who pays her, and whose business it is.

## Practice documents

**Document A: An offer letter**

> **Granite Ridge Mechanical Ltd. (fictional company)**
> Dear Jordan,
> We are pleased to offer you a position as a **first-year plumbing apprentice**.
> - **Pay:** $24.00 an hour, paid every second Friday by direct deposit.
> - **Hours:** 40 hours a week, Monday to Friday, 7:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.
> - **Probation:** the first 3 months.
> - **Tools:** Granite Ridge supplies all power tools, the work truck and fittings. You supply the basic hand tools on the attached list.
> - **Deductions:** CPP, EI and income tax are deducted from each pay.
> - **Apprenticeship:** Granite Ridge will be your sponsor. We will help you register with SkilledTradesBC and we submit your work-based training hours each month.
> - **Safety:** Granite Ridge is registered with WorkSafeBC. You will attend our site safety orientation on your first day.
> Please sign and return this letter by May 15.
> Signed: Dana K., Operations Manager

**Document B: A text message**

> **Ray (Ray's Renos, fictional):** Hey Jordan, got lots of work for you. $30 an hour cash, no paperwork. You're a contractor so no deductions, more money in your pocket. I'll tell you where and when each day. Use my tools and my van. Start Monday 7 am. Don't worry about your apprentice hours, I'll sign something later.
