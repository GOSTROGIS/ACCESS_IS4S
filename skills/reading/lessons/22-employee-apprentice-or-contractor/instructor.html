Employee, apprentice or contractor

Read a job offer and judge whether it makes you an employee or a self-employed contractor, and what that means for deductions, WorkSafeBC coverage and your apprenticeship hours.

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Read Document B out loud as if it just came in on your phone. Ask: "Thirty an hour cash. Who would take it?" Many hands will go up. Ask: "What do you think you might be giving up?" Collect a few ideas without judging. Tell learners that today they will read two offers side by side and work out what each one really is.

This is session 22 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 75 minutes.

Be clear with learners: this lesson teaches reading. It is not legal or tax advice. For a real question, they can ask the CRA, BC Employment Standards or SkilledTradesBC (Key idea 6). Some learners may have done cash work. Keep the tone practical, not blaming.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find the pay, hours, probation, tools, deductions and sponsor terms in a job offer (Identify the information)
  • apply the CRA's six questions to an offer to judge employee or self-employed (Understand and apply the information)
  • judge whether a label such as "contractor" fits the facts, and what an offer leaves out (Evaluate the document(s))
  • say who wrote an offer and what the writer wants (Reflect on the document(s))

Pre-assessment (4 min)

  1. "What is the difference between an employee and a contractor?"
  2. "Who reports an apprentice's hours to SkilledTradesBC?"

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • employee and self-employed: whose business is it?
  • the facts decide, not the label
  • the CRA's six questions: control, tools, helpers, financial risk, investment and management, chance of profit
  • deductions, employment standards and WorkSafeBC for employees and for the self-employed
  • sponsors submit apprentice hours to SkilledTradesBC, and cash work leaves no proof
  • where to ask: the CRA, BC Employment Standards and SkilledTradesBC

Total time: 5 + 3 + 4 + 52 + 6 + 5 = 75 minutes.

Notes for the instructor

  • Do not say Jordan "is" an employee under Document B. Only the CRA (for CPP and EI), Employment Standards or a court can decide. Learners can say the facts point to employee work, and why.
  • WorkSafeBC. WorkSafeBC says BC workers are covered automatically, so a worker who is really an employee is not left with no coverage just because the boss did not register. The real problem is proof: with no paperwork it is hard to show where, when and for whom you worked.
  • The $240 a week. Learners may see the gross pay difference as the end of the story. Point out that Document A's pay comes with deductions taken off and sent to the CRA for the worker, and with hours reported toward a ticket. Do not quote tax rates in this lesson.
  • Related lessons. Reading lesson 09 (agreements cannot take away employment standards), Numeracy lesson 01 (the pay stub and deductions) and Adaptability lesson 07 (the apprenticeship path).
