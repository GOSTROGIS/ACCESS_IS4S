# Capstone: read a real drawing set

Session 18 of 18 in the Reading lessons · 120 minutes

**Before the session:** learners need a calculator. Project the figures or print them, one set per pair. Each figure links to a full-size image for zooming on a phone. This is the last lesson of the Reading drawing series. It uses skills from Reading lesson 05 (read a floor plan and its notes), the drawing lessons 11 to 15 (views, pictorial drawings, lines and dimensions, scales, sections and details), lesson 16 (title blocks and sheet sets) and lesson 17 (symbols, legends, grids and schedules). The math comes from Numeracy lesson 11 (fractions, decimals and metric) and Numeracy lesson 13 (perimeter and area). All tasks use one real set: London Square.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Hand each pair the figures in a stack, out of order, like a real set that came off the printer. Say: "The foreman wants a report on the Level 4 washrooms before lunch. Where do you start?" Let pairs try for one minute. Most start with the plan. Point them back to the drawing list. Today they work a real job, from the drawing list to the RFI.

This is session 18 of 18 in the Reading lessons. It runs 120 minutes.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find every sheet a job needs from the drawing list, and read each scale and issue (Identify the information)
- follow section marks, callouts, wall tags, door tags and fixture tags across at least five sheets (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- add written dimensions, convert a scale length, compute an area in square feet and square metres, and check floor heights from elevations (Understand and apply the information)
- find where two sheets do not agree and write a clear RFI (Evaluate the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. "Name three sheets you would want before you fit out a washroom."
2. "What does 1/8" = 1'-0" mean?"
3. "What is an RFI, and when do you send one?"

## Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

- starting every job at the drawing list
- reading scales and issues in title blocks
- grid dimensions, scale lengths and bay areas in imperial and metric
- following section marks and callouts from plan to section to wall section to wall type
- following door and fixture tags to schedules and plumbing sheets
- checking one trade's sheets against another's
- writing an RFI when two sheets do not agree

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 92 + 9 + 6 = 120 minutes.

### Notes for the instructor

- **The two problems in Part E are real.** On P-6 the left plan is titled "5 Level 3" but shows units 401 to 411 and a floor elevation of 276.17', and the right plan is titled "6 Level 4" but shows units 301 to 311 and 264.17'. Building Section 1 on A-401 gives Level 4 at 276'-2" and Level 3 at 264'-2". So the view titles on P-6 are swapped. Wall section 07 on A-602 is titled "@ 137th St." while every other London Square sheet uses 828 Kingston Blvd. London Square is a teaching set made from a real project, so the street name may be a leftover. Learners should treat both as real questions for the design team, which is what a crew would do.
- **"403" means two things on A-241:** Unit 403 (an office unit) and room 403 (the women's washroom, with door 403). Watch for learners who read the wrong one. It is a good teaching moment.
- **Plumbing legend.** The London Square plumbing legend on P-1 (lesson 17, Figure 15) lists WC and WB but no urinal. P-6 tags the urinals U-1. If a learner notices, praise it and add it to their RFI.
- **Door 402 "2 x A".** The schedule gives a 5'-0" wide opening with door type "2 x A". Learners can reason that it is a pair of type A leaves. The door types legend does not say so in words, so accept "probably a pair of type A doors, confirm with the architect".
- **The bay in Task 4 is grid to grid.** It is not the finished floor area. Columns, walls and the core take some of it. That is fine for a first price, but say so.
- **Wall thickness in Task 6.** The W2 layers add to 13" (3 1/2 + 1 + 1 1/2 + 1/2 + 6 + 1/2). The poly vapour barrier is too thin to count. Real walls vary, so a framer checks the details.
