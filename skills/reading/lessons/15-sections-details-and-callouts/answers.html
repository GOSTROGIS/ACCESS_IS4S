Sections, details and callouts

Read cutting-plane lines and section views, name common material hatch patterns, and follow section marks and detail callouts from one sheet to another.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 1)

a) Which way you look at the cut surface. Here they point up, toward the back of the bushing. b) SECTION A-A. c) The steel that the cutting plane passes through (the cut surface).

Task 2 (level 1)

a) A section is cut here. It is drawn as section 1 on sheet A-301. Look the way the triangle points. b) The circled area is drawn bigger as detail 3 on sheet A-501. c) The wall the triangle points at is drawn as elevation 2 on sheet A-201.

Task 3 (level 1)

a) Wood framing cut across the grain (for example a sill plate or a stud). b) Concrete. c) Batt insulation. d) Rigid insulation.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) It shows the outside and the inside in one view. The left half shows the outside shape. The right half shows the bore, the wall thickness and a bolt hole. b) The bushing is round and the same on both sides, so half of it is enough to show the inside. The other half can show the outside. c) Only one small feature (the drilled hole at the end) needs to be shown inside. A broken-out section opens just that area. The rest of the shaft stays as an outside view, and no cutting-plane line is needed.

Task 5 (level 2)

  • A: Concrete (dots and small triangles): footing, foundation wall and slab.
  • B: Earth (short strokes in clusters).
  • C: Gravel (small open circles) under the slab.
  • D: Rigid insulation (crosshatch) under the slab and on the inside of the foundation wall.
  • E: Wood framing cut across the grain (box with a diagonal cross): the sill plate.
  • F: Batt insulation (looping line) in the stud cavity.

Points a good response covers: names all six and links each one to its pattern in the legend. A strong answer adds that the drawing set's own legend has the final say.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) 20 mm. (100 − 60) ÷ 2 = 40 ÷ 2 = 20. b) Inner edge: 40 − 5 = 35 mm from the centre. Outer edge: 40 + 5 = 45 mm from the centre. (Ø10 means a radius of 5.)

Task 7 (level 2)

a) Sheet A-301, section 1 (the wall section), found from the bubble 1 over A-301 on A-101. b) Sheet A-501, detail 3 (the sill detail), found from the bubble 3 over A-501 on A-301. c) The wall section is at 1:20. The sill detail is at 1:5.

Task 8 (level 2)

a) 5 mm. 100 ÷ 20 = 5. b) 20 mm. 100 ÷ 5 = 20. c) 4 times bigger. 20 ÷ 5 = 4.

Task 9 (level 3)

What could have happened: the detail may have been renumbered or moved to another sheet in a revision, the callout may have a typing error, or your copy of A-501 may be an old revision.

What to do: do not guess and do not build that part from the wall section alone. Check the sheet index and the revision of each sheet. Look at the other details on A-501 and nearby sheets for a sill detail. If you still cannot find it, tell your foreman and send a written question (RFI) to the designer. Keep the answer with your drawings.

Task 10 (level 3)

45° lines are used for brick on many architectural drawings, but they are also the general-purpose section lining, and many offices use close 45° lines for steel. Patterns vary between offices, so the lines alone do not prove it is brick.

Places to look: the general notes or symbols sheet at the front of the set, the wall type or assembly schedule, the specifications, a note or leader on the wall section itself, or the plans and elevations (which may label the wall as brick veneer or concrete).

If you still cannot tell: ask the foreman, and send a written question to the designer before ordering or building.

Task 11 (level 3)

Sample answer:

  1. On the floor plan (A-101), find the section mark on the outside wall where you are working.
  2. Read the bubble: the top number (1) is the section, and the bottom is the sheet number (A-301).
  3. Go to sheet A-301 and find the title with the same bubble, 1 over A-301. That is the wall section.
  4. On the wall section, find the circle around the sill and its bubble, 3 over A-501.
  5. Go to sheet A-501 and find the title 3 over A-501. That is the sill detail, drawn at 1:5.
  6. Check that the detail matches the place you are working, and use its written dimensions.

Points a good response covers: uses all four words correctly, goes plan to section to detail, and says to match the title bubble on the destination sheet.

Check yourself 1

The arrows on the cutting-plane line (or the triangle on the section mark bubble).

Check yourself 2

Holes and empty spaces (anything the cut does not pass through).

Check yourself 3

The top number is the section or detail number. The bottom is the sheet where it is drawn.

Check yourself 4

Hatch patterns are not the same in every office. The legend for that drawing set tells you what each pattern means.

Check yourself 5

So that small parts and connections are big enough to read and dimension.
