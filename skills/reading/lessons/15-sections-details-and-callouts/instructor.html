Sections, details and callouts

Read cutting-plane lines and section views, name common material hatch patterns, and follow section marks and detail callouts from one sheet to another.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Session 15 of 26 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Cut a piece of foam, a carrot or a short length of pipe insulation in half in front of the group, and hold up the cut face. Ask: "What can you see now that you could not see before?" That is a section. Then show a page from a real drawing set with a small circle and a bubble on it. Ask: "Where does this send you?" Tell learners that by the end of the session they will be able to go from a floor plan to the exact detail they need in under a minute.

This is session 15 of 26 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes. It is the last lesson of the first half of the drawing series. Lessons 16 to 18 use real construction drawing sets.

Before the session: if you can, bring a short real drawing set (three or four sheets: a plan, a sections sheet and a details sheet) or a printout of one, so learners can follow a real callout after the worked example. Bring coloured pencils for marking hatch patterns.

Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find a cutting-plane line, a section mark and a detail callout, and name common material hatch patterns (Identify the information)
  • follow a section mark or detail callout from one sheet to the matching title on another sheet (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
  • use a section view and its scale to answer size questions, such as wall thickness or drawn size (Understand and apply the information)
  • spot a broken reference or an unclear hatch pattern, and decide what to check or ask before building (Evaluate the document(s))

Pre-assessment (5 min)

  1. "Have you seen a circle with a line through it and numbers on a drawing? What did you think it meant?"
  2. Show Figure 2 with the names covered. "Which of these patterns do you already know?"

Post-assessment (9 min)

  1. Draw a bubble reading 5 over S-201 on the board. "What does it tell you, and where do you go?"
  2. "In a section view, which parts are hatched and which are left blank?"
  3. "A detail is at 1:5 and the plan is at 1:50. How many times bigger is the detail?" (10 times)

Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

  • cutting-plane lines, viewing arrows and section views
  • section lines and what is left blank
  • full, half and broken-out sections
  • common material hatch patterns, and why the legend wins
  • section marks, detail callouts, elevation markers and drawing titles
  • following references from plan to section to detail
  • why details are drawn at a bigger scale

This lesson ends the first half of the drawing series. Next session (Reading lesson 16) uses real construction drawing sets.

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 62 + 9 + 6 = 90 minutes.

  • Cutting-plane lines are heavy lines with arrows that show the direction of view. Section lining marks the surface exposed by the cut, and different section lining identifies different materials. A full section passes entirely through the object. A half section removes one quarter of the object, shows both inside and outside, is often used for symmetrical objects, and usually leaves hidden lines off the unsectioned half. A broken-out section is bounded by a freehand break line and needs no cutting-plane line. Ric Costin, "1.7: Sectional Views", Basic Blueprint Reading (Workforce LibreTexts, CC BY 4.0), https://workforce.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Manufacturing/Basic_Blueprint_Reading_(Costin)/01:_Chapters/1.07:_Sectional_Views, checked 2026-11-08.
  • Section lines are thin, continuous diagonal lines for surfaces on the cutting plane. Short break lines bound broken-out sections. Engineering LibreTexts, Illinois Institute of Technology, "3.2: Section Views", https://eng.libretexts.org/Courses/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology/Introduction_to_Engineering_Drawing_and_Design/03:Module_C-__Auxiliary_views_section_views_isometric_and_oblique_projections/3.02:_Section_Views, checked 2026-11-08.
