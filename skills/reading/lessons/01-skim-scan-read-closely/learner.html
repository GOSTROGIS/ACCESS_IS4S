Reading · Lesson 01 · Learner

Skim, scan and read closely

Decide why you are reading, then choose the fastest way to find what you need in a workplace safety document.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the task that requires you to read; Identify the information

Why this matters on the job

On a BC job site you are handed safety bulletins, toolbox talk sheets, site rules and incident guides. Most of the time you do not need every word. You need one answer, and you need it right: who to tell, how long you have, what to bring. Knowing why you are reading tells you how to read.

Key ideas

1. Start with your purpose. Finish this sentence before you read: "I am reading this so that I can…". For example: "…find out who I report a near miss to."

2. Choose a way of reading.

Way of reading What you do Use it when
Skim Read the title, headings, bold words and the first sentence under each heading. Move fast. You want the general idea, or you want to know where things are.
Scan Hold one key word or number in your head. Run your eyes down the page until you see it. Stop there. You know what you are looking for: a name, a number, a time, a word.
Read closely Read every word of the sentence or two around what you found. Watch for small words that change the meaning: must, only, not, within, before, unless. You have found the right place and the details matter.

3. Scan, locate, decide. This is the ACCESS routine. Scan for your key word. Locate the sentence that holds it. Decide whether that sentence answers your question, or whether you need to keep looking.

Worked example

Question: Who does a supervisor tell first after an incident?

  1. Purpose: I need a person or role ("who").
  2. Skim the guide below. The heading 3. Report first looks right.
  3. Scan under that heading for a role. You find "the site superintendent".
  4. Read closely: "Tell the site superintendent right away, by phone or in person. Do not wait to finish your notes."
  5. Decide: The answer is the site superintendent, right away, by phone or in person.

You did not read the whole guide. You read about 25 words closely.

Practice document

Kestrel Point Builders Ltd. (fictional company) Incident Investigation Guide for Site Supervisors Version 3 · Issued March 2026 · Replaces Version 2

1. Purpose of this guide This guide helps site supervisors investigate incidents on Kestrel Point job sites. It explains what to do, in what order, and what records to keep. The goal of every investigation is to find out why an incident happened and to stop it from happening again. An investigation is not about finding someone to blame.

2. What counts as an incident For this guide, an incident is any event at work that injured a worker so that the worker needed medical treatment. It is also any event that did not hurt anyone, or caused only a minor injury, but could have caused a serious injury. We call that a near miss. Treat near misses as seriously as injuries.

3. Report first Tell the site superintendent right away, by phone or in person. Do not wait to finish your notes. If a worker is hurt, first aid comes before everything else.

4. Preliminary investigation Start the preliminary investigation right away. WorkSafeBC requires the employer to complete the preliminary investigation and its report within 48 hours of the incident. Use Kestrel Point form KP-12. Fix any unsafe condition you find before work starts again in that area.

5. Full investigation The full investigation looks for the underlying causes. WorkSafeBC requires the full investigation report to be submitted within 30 days of the incident. The safety coordinator submits it. You give the safety coordinator your notes, sketches and photos.

6. Mapping the scene Before anything is moved, sketch the area. Note, measure and mark: - where people, equipment and materials were - anything broken, bent or out of place - marks on the ground, such as tire tracks or drag marks - spills, wet spots or loose material on walking surfaces - lighting, noise and weather at the time - guards, rails and other safety devices, and whether they were in place

7. Talking to witnesses Talk to each witness alone, as soon as you can. Ask what they saw, not what they think caused it. Write down their words. Thank them.

8. Corrective actions For each cause you find, write one action that will fix it, the person responsible and a date.

9. Records Keep a copy of every form, sketch and photo in the project's safety binder. The safety coordinator keeps the electronic copies.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Skim the guide for one minute only. Then cover it and answer. a) How many numbered headings does the guide have? b) Who is the guide written for? c) Which version is it?

Task 2 (level 1) Scan for the number of hours the employer has to complete the preliminary investigation report. What key word or number did you scan for?

Task 3 (level 1) Scan for the section on mapping the scene. List three things a supervisor must note, measure and mark.

Task 4 (level 2) Read closely. A labourer trips on an extension cord at the top of a stairwell. She grabs the rail and is not hurt. Does this count as an incident under this guide? Point to the words that decide it.

Task 5 (level 2) For each purpose below, say whether you would skim, scan or read closely first. Give a reason for one of them. a) You want to know what the guide covers before a safety meeting. b) You need the form number for the preliminary investigation. c) You need to know exactly who submits the full investigation report and what you hand over.

Task 6 (level 2) A co-worker says: "We have a week to write up the preliminary report, and the supervisor sends the full report to WorkSafeBC." Use the guide to correct both parts of this statement.

Check yourself

  1. What sentence should you finish before you start reading?
  2. You need one phone number from a two-page notice. Do you skim, scan or read closely first?
  3. Name three small words that can change the meaning of a sentence and that tell you to read closely.
  4. What are the three steps of the ACCESS routine?
