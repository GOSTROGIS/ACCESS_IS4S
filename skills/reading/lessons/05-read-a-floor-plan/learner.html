Read a floor plan and its notes

Use the title block, legend, schedules, notes and detail references on a construction drawing to find what to build and where.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

Carpenters, tilesetters, plumbers and electricians all work from the same set of drawings. The drawing shows the shape of the job, but most of the answers are in the words around it: the title block, the legend, the schedules and the notes. Missing a revision or a note can mean tearing out finished work.

Key ideas

1. Kinds of views. - A plan (floor plan) looks straight down on the building, as if the roof were lifted off. - An elevation looks straight at a wall from the side. - A section shows what you would see if you sliced through a wall or floor. - A detail is a close-up, drawn bigger, of one small part.

2. The title block sits in a corner of every sheet. It names the project, the drawing, the drawing number, the scale and the date. It lists the revisions: each change to the drawing, with a number and a date. Always check that you have the latest revision.

3. The legend explains the symbols and abbreviations used on that set of drawings. Abbreviations are not the same on every job, so read the legend for each set.

4. Schedules are tables on the drawing. A room finish schedule says what goes on each floor, base, wall and ceiling. A door schedule gives the size and type of each door. The plan shows a door number. The schedule tells you about that door.

5. Notes are rules written on the drawing. A general note applies to the whole drawing unless it says otherwise. Notes often say "TYP" (typical), meaning the same thing applies in all similar places.

6. Detail references look like (1/A5.1): detail number 1, found on drawing A5.1. Go to that drawing for the close-up.

7. Scale. "Scale 1:50" means 1 mm on the drawing stands for 50 mm in real life. A wall 2250 mm long is drawn 2250 ÷ 50 = 45 mm long. Paper prints can shrink or stretch, so most drawings tell you to use the written dimensions, not a ruler. A ruler is only for a rough check.

Worked example

Question: What size is door D1?

  1. Find D1 on the plan. It is in the wall between room 104 and the corridor.
  2. The legend says D1 is a door number: see the door schedule.
  3. In the door schedule, row D1 says 915 × 2135.
  4. The legend says dimensions are in millimetres, so the door is 915 mm wide and 2135 mm high.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Use the title block. a) What is the drawing number and the scale? b) What is the latest revision, and what was its date? c) What changed in that revision?

Task 2 (level 1) Use the plan and the legend. a) How many toilets (WC) are there in room 104? In room 105? b) How many hand sinks (LAV) are there in total? c) Which room is the corridor? d) Which wall of room 105 is on the north side?

Task 3 (level 2) a) How high does the wall tile go on most walls in room 105? b) How high does it go behind a hand sink? c) Where do you look for a close-up of the wall at a hand sink?

Task 4 (level 2) a) On a full-size print of drawing A2.1 (scale 1:50), the north wall of room 104 measures 45 mm with a ruler. How long is the wall in real life? b) A helper uses a ruler on a reduced photocopy and gets a different length. Which number do you trust, and which note tells you so?

Task 5 (level 2) The east wall of room 105 has no fixtures on it. It is 3000 mm long. Using the room finish schedule, how many square metres of wall tile go on that wall? (Ignore the cove base.)

Task 6 (level 2) A supplier sends a quote for door D2 at 865 mm wide. Is that correct? Which part of the drawing tells you?

Task 7 (level 2) What does P&MG mean, and where does note 4 say it applies?

Task 8 (level 3) Read general note 2 and detail 1 on A5.1 together. Is there a problem? If so, explain it and say what you should do before you set the tile and mirror.

Check yourself

  1. What is the difference between a plan and an elevation?
  2. Where on a drawing do you check that you have the latest revision?
  3. A plan shows "(3/A6.2)". What does it mean?
  4. At a scale of 1:50, how many millimetres in real life does 1 mm on the drawing stand for?
  5. What should you do when two parts of a drawing set do not agree?
