# Read a floor plan and its notes

Session 5 of 10 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has anyone worked from drawings on a site? What was the hardest part?" Most learners say the symbols or the size of the set. Tell a short story: a tile crew finishes two washrooms to the height on the plan. A week later the foreman finds the revision that lowered the tile behind the sinks. The tile comes off. The drawing was right. The crew read the old revision. Today is about reading the words on a drawing, not just the lines.

This is session 5 of 10 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find information in a title block, legend, schedule and general notes (Identify the information)
- connect a mark on the plan to a schedule, a note or a detail on another sheet (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- use scale and written dimensions to answer a job question (Understand and apply the information)
- spot when two parts of a drawing set do not agree, and know what to do (Evaluate the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. Show the plan for 15 seconds. "What kind of building space is this, and how do you know?"
2. "What does 'scale 1:50' mean?"

## Post-assessment (9 min)

1. "Your foreman says, 'Tile both washrooms to 1800.' Using the drawing, what would you check before you start?"
2. "A plan shows a door marked D3. Where do you look to find out its size?"
3. "Name two places on this drawing set where you found information that is not shown by lines."

## Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

- plans, elevations, sections and details
- the title block and checking the latest revision
- legends, abbreviations and why they change from job to job
- room finish and door schedules
- general notes, TYP and detail references
- drawing scale and why written dimensions come first
- what to do when parts of a drawing set do not agree

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 62 + 9 + 6 = 90 minutes.
