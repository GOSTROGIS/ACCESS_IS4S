a) **2250 mm.** 45 × 50 = 2250. This matches the written dimension on the plan.
b) **Trust the written dimension, 2250 mm.** General note 1 says "Do not scale this drawing. Use the written dimensions." A reduced photocopy is no longer at 1:50, so ruler measurements on it are wrong. Note 3 also says to check dimensions on site before ordering.

## Task 5 (level 2)

**5.4 m²**

Steps:
1. Tile height from the schedule: 1800 mm = 1.8 m.
2. Wall length: 3000 mm = 3.0 m.
3. Area = length × height = 3.0 × 1.8 = 5.4 m².

Note 2's exception only applies behind a LAV. The east wall has no fixtures, so the full 1800 mm height applies.

## Task 7 (level 2)

**P&MG means patch and make good**: repair the damaged finishes and refinish them to match. Note 4 says it applies to **all finishes affected by the work, including the corridor walls at the new doors**.

## Task 8 (level 3)

**Yes, there is a conflict.**

- Note 2 says the mirror goes **above** the tile, and the tile behind each LAV stops at 1200 mm AFF.
- Detail 1 on A5.1 puts the **bottom of the mirror at 1050 mm AFF**. That is 150 mm (1200 − 1050) below the top of the tile, so the mirror would sit over the tile, not above it.

What to do: **do not guess.** Stop before setting the tile and mirror at that wall. Tell your supervisor and ask the consultant in writing which height is correct. On many jobs this written question is called an RFI (request for information). Note 5 is about drawings and specifications not agreeing. Two parts of the drawings not agreeing calls for the same careful step: ask in writing and keep the answer.

Points a good response covers: names both sources, gives both numbers, explains the overlap, and gives a safe action that involves asking in writing.
