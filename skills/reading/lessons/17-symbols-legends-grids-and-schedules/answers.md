# Symbols, legends, grids and schedules

Every answer below was read from the real sheets shown in the figures.

## Task 1 (level 1)

a) **Door tag:** a number in a rounded (oval) shape. **Wall tag:** a mark in a rectangle. **Window tag:** a mark in a hexagon (six sides).
b) A **revision tag**: it marks a change, and the number is the revision.
c) **True north and project north** ("true/project NORTH").
d) **R = risers and T = treads**: 20 risers at 7 1/2" each and 19 treads at 11" each. The legend itself does not spell out R and T, so this comes from what you learned about stairs in Numeracy lesson 16. Confirm on the stair sheets if it matters.

## Task 5 (level 2)

a) Floor **rubber sheet**, base **wood**, wall **GWB \***, ceiling **exposed NLT**.
b) The star means **"Gypsum wall board to be abuse resistant."** So GWB is **gypsum wall board**.
c) **"Tile up to 7'-0" high."** 7'-0" = 84 inches. 84 × 25.4 = **2133.6 mm, about 2134 mm**.

Note: this is an imperial height on a metric set. A tilesetter should confirm it with the interior elevations of the kitchen.

## Task 7 (level 2)

a) A circle around a device means **ceiling mounted**. A square around a device means **floor mounted**.
b) **OC = over counter.** So it is a ground fault (GFI) duplex receptacle mounted over the counter.
c) With a 3: **3-way switch.** With a D: **dimmer switch.**
d) **Occupancy sensor, ceiling mounted, line voltage. VS means it runs in vacancy sensor mode.**

## Task 11 (level 3)

a) In the abbreviation list (Figure 14), **FD = floor drain**. In a hexagon on a duct (Figure 13), **FD = fire damper**. Both are on the same sheet, M0.01.
b) Electrical (Figure 9): **WP = weather proof.** Structural (Figure 18): **WP. = work point.**
c) A good rule: **"Read the abbreviation list and legend of the trade and sheet you are working from, and look at what the symbol is attached to. If it could mean two things, ask before you build."**

Points a good answer covers: both meanings for each pair, the idea that context matters, and asking when unsure.

## Task 12 (level 3)

a) London Square (Figure 2): **"A.F.F. Finished Face".** leləm̓ electrical (Figure 9): **"A.F.F. Above Finished Floor".** leləm̓ mechanical (Figure 14): **"AFF Above Finished Floor".**
b) **The London Square entry looks wrong.** Its own symbols legend (Figure 1) uses A.F.F. as a height in the ceiling tag ("1'-0" A.F.F."), which only makes sense as "above finished floor". The same list already has F.F. for "Finished Face", so the A.F.F. line looks like a copy error.
c) Read it as 18" above the finished floor only after you confirm it. Tell your supervisor and ask the architect in writing (an RFI) to confirm the meaning and correct the legend. Keep the written answer.

## Task 13 (level 3)

a) **110C Gym. Storage: rubber sheet. 110 Gym: maple hardwood sport floor.**
b) The two floors are different materials and may not be the same height, so the door needs a transition or threshold, and the door bottom must clear both floors. The carpenter and the flooring installer need to agree on the finished floor heights before the door is set. If the drawings do not show the transition, ask.
c) **A-201 floor plan** (door tag 119 and room tags 110 and 110C), **A-081 door schedule** (mark 119 gives the from and to rooms) and **A-090 room finish schedule** (room numbers 110 and 110C). The **door mark 119** and the **room numbers** link them.
