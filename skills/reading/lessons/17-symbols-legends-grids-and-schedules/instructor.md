# Symbols, legends, grids and schedules

Session 17 of 18 in the Reading lessons · 90 minutes

**Before the session:** project the figures on the learner page or print them. Each figure links to a full-size image, so learners can zoom in on a phone. Have a calculator on hand for Task 3 and Task 5c. If you can, bring one legend sheet from a local job so learners see that every office draws its own. This lesson follows lesson 16 (title blocks and sheet sets) and uses the same real drawing sets. Lesson 18, the capstone, uses every skill from lessons 05 and 11 to 17.

## Opening (5 min)

Welcome learners. Show Figure 3 with the red letters covered. Ask: "What do you recognize on this plan?" Most learners spot doors and walls. Few know what the split circles mean. Then show Figure 1. Say: "Every office makes its own dictionary for its drawings. Today you learn to use the dictionary, not to memorize it." Tell a short story: an apprentice on a commercial job sees "FD" on a duct drawing and goes looking for a floor drain. It was a fire damper. The same two letters meant two things on the same sheet.

This is session 17 of 18 in the Reading lessons. It runs 90 minutes.

## Objective (3 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- find grid lines, markers, tags and symbols on a real plan and say what each one is (Identify the information)
- follow a tag or marker from the plan to the right schedule or sheet (Make connections between different parts of the document(s))
- use a legend and an abbreviation list to read electrical, plumbing, HVAC and structural symbols, and do simple math with grid and schedule values (Understand and apply the information)
- notice when a legend or an abbreviation does not make sense, and say what to do (Evaluate the document(s))

## Pre-assessment (5 min)

1. Show Figure 7 for 20 seconds. "Which trade uses this sheet? How can you tell?"
2. "What do you think a grid line is for?"
3. "What does TYP mean on a drawing?" (Lesson 05 taught it.)

## Summary (6 min)

During this session we covered:

- legends and abbreviation lists, and that each set has its own
- grid lines, grid bubbles and the north arrow
- section, wall section, elevation and detail markers, and how to read view over sheet
- room, door, wall, window and equipment tags, and the schedules they point to
- door, room finish, footing and panel schedules
- electrical, plumbing, HVAC, fire protection and structural symbols on real sheets
- why the same letters can mean different things, and what to do when a legend looks wrong

Total time: 5 + 3 + 5 + 62 + 9 + 6 = 90 minutes.

### Notes for the instructor

- **The red letters on Figure 3** were added for this lesson. Everything else in every figure is as printed.
- **A.F.F. in London Square.** The London Square abbreviation legend lists "A.F.F. Finished Face". Its own symbols legend uses A.F.F. as a height ("1'-0" A.F.F." in the ceiling tag), and the leləm̓ electrical and mechanical lists both say "Above Finished Floor". Task 12 is built on this. The same London Square legend also shows "<" for centerline and ">" for plate, which look like lost symbols, and lists "F D." for floor drain while its plumbing legend (Figure 15) writes FD.
- **2A:24 style labels.** The leləm̓ E-001 legend has a "panel and circuit numbering system" for panel names, but it does not explain the short "2A:24" labels on the plans. The panel schedule on E-901 confirms them: panel 2A, circuit 24 is "REC. KITCHEN FRIDGE", matching the FR receptacle. Learners do not need the numbering system diagram for these tasks.
- **Stair tag.** The London Square legend does not spell out R and T. In Numeracy lesson 16, learners met risers and treads. Accept "risers" and "treads" and point out that the legend does not say so.
- **GFI spelling.** E-001 spells it "INTERUPTER". Point it out only if learners ask.
