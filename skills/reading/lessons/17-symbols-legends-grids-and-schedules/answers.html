Symbols, legends, grids and schedules

Use grid lines, markers, tags, schedules and each trade's legend to look up what a symbol on a real sheet means, then follow it to the right table.

Every answer below was read from the real sheets shown in the figures.

Task 1 (level 1)

a) Door tag: a number in a rounded (oval) shape. Wall tag: a mark in a rectangle. Window tag: a mark in a hexagon (six sides). b) A revision tag: it marks a change, and the number is the revision. c) True north and project north ("true/project NORTH"). d) R = risers and T = treads: 20 risers at 7 1/2" each and 19 treads at 11" each. The legend itself does not spell out R and T, so this comes from what you learned about stairs in Numeracy lesson 16. Confirm on the stair sheets if it matters.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 1850 + 15800 + 1850 = 19500. Yes, it matches the overall dimension of 19500. b) 15.8 m (15800 mm ÷ 1000). c) 99520 − 98520 = 1000. The floor marked 99520 is 1000 mm (1 m) higher than the gym floor.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) From 110C Gym. Storage to 110 Gym. b) 1828 x 2450 (width x height, mm), 44 thick. c) Maple veneer, clear finish, type D. The description is "Flush - Double". d) Three doors: 118, 119 and 120. (Door 115 goes from 110A Gym. Storage, a different room.)

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Floor rubber sheet, base wood, wall GWB *, ceiling exposed NLT. b) The star means "Gypsum wall board to be abuse resistant." So GWB is gypsum wall board. c) "Tile up to 7'-0" high." 7'-0" = 84 inches. 84 × 25.4 = 2133.6 mm, about 2134 mm.

Note: this is an imperial height on a metric set. A tilesetter should confirm it with the interior elevations of the kitchen.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) Circuit 9: REC. KITCHEN 114 MICROWAVE, 20 A breaker. b) Circuit 24: REC. KITCHEN FRIDGE, 15 A breaker. c) The oven needs two circuit spaces, 16 and 18, on one 40 A breaker ("KCH. KITCHEN IND. OVEN"). The schedule shows a single 40 A breaker cell across rows 16 and 18. d) 2nd floor elec (the electrical room on the second floor). 120/208 Wye, 3 phase, 4 wire.

Task 7 (level 2)

a) A circle around a device means ceiling mounted. A square around a device means floor mounted. b) OC = over counter. So it is a ground fault (GFI) duplex receptacle mounted over the counter. c) With a 3: 3-way switch. With a D: dimmer switch. d) Occupancy sensor, ceiling mounted, line voltage. VS means it runs in vacancy sensor mode.

Task 8 (level 2)

a) A plumbing fixture or equipment tag ("WC-X"). b) FCO = floor cleanout. Domestic cold water is the line marked DCW. c) Supply air. The duct is 600 x 300, the clear inside size, and the first number (600) is the side you see in that view. d) Type S-1 (a supply outlet), size 200ø (200 round), flow 100 L/s. e) FHC = fire hose cabinet. FEX = fire extinguisher.

Task 9 (level 2)

a) The mark sits in a hexagon, for example F1 in a six-sided shape. The legend says "Footing type. See plan & schedule." b) A pad footing 1700 by 1700, 350 deep, reinforced with 20M bars at 300 each way, at the bottom. (DP. = deep, FTG. = footing, R/W = reinforced with, E.W. = each way, BOT. = bottom. The leləm̓ set is metric, so the sizes are in millimetres.) c) TYP-C-309/S302 (typical detail C-309 on sheet S302), from note 2.

Task 10 (level 2)

a) WC = water closet (toilet). WB = wash basin. b) FD = floor drain.

Task 11 (level 3)

a) In the abbreviation list (Figure 14), FD = floor drain. In a hexagon on a duct (Figure 13), FD = fire damper. Both are on the same sheet, M0.01. b) Electrical (Figure 9): WP = weather proof. Structural (Figure 18): WP. = work point. c) A good rule: "Read the abbreviation list and legend of the trade and sheet you are working from, and look at what the symbol is attached to. If it could mean two things, ask before you build."

Points a good answer covers: both meanings for each pair, the idea that context matters, and asking when unsure.

Task 12 (level 3)

a) London Square (Figure 2): "A.F.F. Finished Face". leləm̓ electrical (Figure 9): "A.F.F. Above Finished Floor". leləm̓ mechanical (Figure 14): "AFF Above Finished Floor". b) The London Square entry looks wrong. Its own symbols legend (Figure 1) uses A.F.F. as a height in the ceiling tag ("1'-0" A.F.F."), which only makes sense as "above finished floor". The same list already has F.F. for "Finished Face", so the A.F.F. line looks like a copy error. c) Read it as 18" above the finished floor only after you confirm it. Tell your supervisor and ask the architect in writing (an RFI) to confirm the meaning and correct the legend. Keep the written answer.

Task 13 (level 3)

a) 110C Gym. Storage: rubber sheet. 110 Gym: maple hardwood sport floor. b) The two floors are different materials and may not be the same height, so the door needs a transition or threshold, and the door bottom must clear both floors. The carpenter and the flooring installer need to agree on the finished floor heights before the door is set. If the drawings do not show the transition, ask. c) A-201 floor plan (door tag 119 and room tags 110 and 110C), A-081 door schedule (mark 119 gives the from and to rooms) and A-090 room finish schedule (room numbers 110 and 110C). The door mark 119 and the room numbers link them.

Check yourself 1

The legend and the abbreviation list for that trade in that set.

Check yourself 2

The top is the view number (or letter). The bottom is the sheet the view is drawn on.

Check yourself 3

Locate things and lay out the building. Everyone can name the same spot by its grid lines, and dimensions are measured from them.

Check yourself 4

Any three, for example: room tag → room finish schedule, door tag → door schedule, wall tag → wall assembly schedule, window tag → window schedule, fixture or equipment tag → that trade's schedule.

Check yourself 5

Each office and each trade writes its own list. The same letters can mean different things, even on one sheet (FD) or in one set (WP).
