Part 3 or Part 9 and the occupant load

Read the BC Building Code articles that decide whether a building falls under Part 3 or Part 9, classify a building by its major occupancy, and work out an occupant load from Table 3.1.17.1.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Answers were checked against the British Columbia Building Code 2024, Book I (General), and the Revision 3 Ministerial Order (BA 2024 03), on 2026-11-08. If a later revision changes these articles, the current Code governs.

Task 1 (level 2)

a) The building must be - 3 storeys or less in building height, - with a building area not exceeding 600 m², and - used for major occupancies in Group C, D or E, or Group F, Division 2 or 3.

b) "not exceeding 600 m²". Not exceeding means 600 m² or less, so exactly 600 m² still passes. 601 m² does not.

Task 2 (level 2)

a) Hair salon: Group D (Note A-3.1.2.1.(1) lists barber and hairdressing shops and beauty parlours under Group D). b) Supermarket: Group E. c) Motel: Group C. d) Church: Group A, Division 2. e) Arena: Group A, Division 3. f) Hospital: Group B, Division 2. g) Repair garage: Group F, Division 2. h) Paint factory: Group F, Division 1 (the Note lists "Paint, varnish and pyroxylin product factories").

Steps: find the use in Note A-3.1.2.1.(1), read which heading it sits under, then check the heading against Table 3.1.2.1.

Task 3 (level 2)

Building Answer Reason Sentence
A House Part 9 Group C, 2 storeys, 120 m². All three tests pass. 1.3.3.3.(1)
B Restaurant Part 3 Restaurants are Group A, Division 2. Group A is Part 3 at any size. 1.3.3.2.(1)(b)
C Hardware store Part 3 Group E, but 900 m² is over 600 m². 1.3.3.2.(1)(c)
D Apartment building Part 3 Group C, but 4 storeys is over 3. 1.3.3.2.(1)(c)
E Repair garage Part 9 Group F, Division 2, 1 storey, 450 m². All three tests pass. 1.3.3.3.(1)
F Daycare for children Part 3 Article 3.1.2.8. says a daycare facility for children shall be classified as Group A, Division 2. 1.3.3.2.(1)(b)
G Office building Part 9 Group D, 3 storeys is "3 storeys or less", 600 m² is "not exceeding 600 m²". 1.3.3.3.(1)

Building G is the trap. Learners who say Part 3 have read "less than 600" instead of "not exceeding 600".

Task 4 (level 2)

a) 140 m². Building area is the greatest horizontal area of the building above grade, so it is the bigger of the two floors. b) They added the two floors together (140 + 110 = 250). Building area is not the total floor area. It is the largest single footprint above grade.

Task 5 (level 3)

a) Floor 2. The first storey is the uppermost storey whose floor is not more than 2 m above grade. Floors 1 and 2 are not more than 2 m above grade (Floor 1 is below grade, Floor 2 is 0.3 m above). Floors 3 and 4 are more than 2 m above. The uppermost of Floors 1 and 2 is Floor 2. b) A basement: a storey below the first storey. c) 3 storeys. Building height counts the storeys from the floor of the first storey (Floor 2) up to the roof: Floors 2, 3 and 4. d) Part 9. Offices are Group D, the building height is 3 storeys, and 480 m² does not exceed 600 m².

A learner who answers "4 storeys, so Part 3" has counted the basement. Point them back to the definitions of first storey, basement and building height.

Steps: find the heading for the type of use (Assembly, Business and personal services, Mercantile, Other uses), find the row, read the area per person, then divide the floor area by it.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Group E (the store) and Group D (the offices). Sentence 3.1.2.1.(2) says a building with more than one major occupancy is classified by all of them. b) Part 9. Both E and D are on the Part 9 list, the building is 2 storeys, and 400 m² does not exceed 600 m². c) - Store: mercantile, first storey, 3.70 m² per person. 370 ÷ 3.70 = 100 persons. - Offices: 9.30 m² per person. 372 ÷ 9.30 = 40 persons. - Whole building: 100 + 40 = 140 persons.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Space with non-fixed seats: 0.75 m² per person. Standing space: 0.40 m² per person. b) Meetings: 30 ÷ 0.75 = 40 persons. Standing events: 30 ÷ 0.40 = 75 persons. c) 75 persons. Sentence 3.1.17.1.(4) says that if a room is intended for different occupancies at different times, use the value from the table that gives the greatest number of persons.

Task 9 (level 3)

a) Any two of: - an assembly building such as a restaurant, church, community hall or daycare (Group A) - a care, treatment or detention building such as a hospital or group home (Group B) - a high-hazard industrial building such as a paint factory (Group F, Division 1) - a post-disaster building - a Group C, D, E, F2 or F3 building of 3 storeys or less whose building area is over 600 m²

b) Revision 3, in effect August 27, 2024, repealed Article 1.3.3.3. and put in a new one. Sentence (1) has the same three tests and now starts "Except as provided in Sentence (2)". The new Sentence (2) says Part 9 does not apply to buildings designed and constructed under Subsection 3.2.10. (residential buildings with a single exit).

c) Points a good answer covers: - Height is only one of three tests. Building area (600 m²) and major occupancy also count. - Assembly, care and high-hazard buildings are Part 3 at any size. - The rule has been revised, so read the current Article and the revisions page, not memory. - On a real job, the designer's code review on the drawings says which Part applies. If it is not clear, ask the supervisor.

Check yourself 1

Division A, Subsection 1.3.3. (Application of Division B), mainly Articles 1.3.3.2. and 1.3.3.3.

Check yourself 2

3 storeys or less in building height, a building area not exceeding 600 m², and a major occupancy in Group C, D, E, F2 or F3.

Check yourself 3

No, as a rule. A restaurant is Group A, Division 2, and Group A falls under Part 3 at any size (Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1)). The small size does not change the Group. (Article 3.1.2.7. has a special case for a small assembly suite of not more than 30 persons. That is the designer's call.)

Check yourself 4

No. Building area is the greatest horizontal area of the building above grade, measured to the outside surface of the exterior walls. It is the biggest single footprint, not the floors added together.

Check yourself 5

Find "offices" in Table 3.1.17.1. (9.30 m² per person), then divide the office floor area by 9.30.
