# Part 3 or Part 9 and the occupant load

Answers were checked against the British Columbia Building Code 2024, Book I (General), and the Revision 3 Ministerial Order (BA 2024 03), on 2026-11-08. If a later revision changes these articles, the current Code governs.

## Task 1 (level 2)

a) The building must be
- **3 storeys or less** in building height,
- with a building area **not exceeding 600 m²**, and
- used for major occupancies in **Group C, D or E, or Group F, Division 2 or 3**.

b) **"not exceeding 600 m²"**. Not exceeding means 600 m² or less, so exactly 600 m² still passes. 601 m² does not.

## Task 4 (level 2)

a) **140 m².** Building area is the greatest horizontal area of the building above grade, so it is the bigger of the two floors.
b) They **added the two floors together** (140 + 110 = 250). Building area is not the total floor area. It is the largest single footprint above grade.

## Task 5 (level 3)

a) **Floor 2.** The first storey is the uppermost storey whose floor is not more than 2 m above grade. Floors 1 and 2 are not more than 2 m above grade (Floor 1 is below grade, Floor 2 is 0.3 m above). Floors 3 and 4 are more than 2 m above. The uppermost of Floors 1 and 2 is Floor 2.
b) A **basement**: a storey below the first storey.
c) **3 storeys.** Building height counts the storeys from the floor of the first storey (Floor 2) up to the roof: Floors 2, 3 and 4.
d) **Part 9.** Offices are Group D, the building height is 3 storeys, and 480 m² does not exceed 600 m².

A learner who answers "4 storeys, so Part 3" has counted the basement. Point them back to the definitions of *first storey*, *basement* and *building height*.

## Task 7 (level 3)

a) **Group E** (the store) and **Group D** (the offices). Sentence 3.1.2.1.(2) says a building with more than one major occupancy is classified by all of them.
b) **Part 9.** Both E and D are on the Part 9 list, the building is 2 storeys, and 400 m² does not exceed 600 m².
c)
- Store: mercantile, first storey, 3.70 m² per person. 370 ÷ 3.70 = **100 persons**.
- Offices: 9.30 m² per person. 372 ÷ 9.30 = **40 persons**.
- Whole building: 100 + 40 = **140 persons**.

## Task 8 (level 3)

a) Space with non-fixed seats: **0.75 m² per person**. Standing space: **0.40 m² per person**.
b) Meetings: 30 ÷ 0.75 = **40 persons**. Standing events: 30 ÷ 0.40 = **75 persons**.
c) **75 persons.** Sentence 3.1.17.1.(4) says that if a room is intended for different occupancies at different times, use the value from the table that gives the greatest number of persons.

## Task 9 (level 3)

a) Any two of:
- an assembly building such as a restaurant, church, community hall or daycare (Group A)
- a care, treatment or detention building such as a hospital or group home (Group B)
- a high-hazard industrial building such as a paint factory (Group F, Division 1)
- a post-disaster building
- a Group C, D, E, F2 or F3 building of 3 storeys or less whose building area is over 600 m²

b) Revision 3, in effect August 27, 2024, **repealed Article 1.3.3.3. and put in a new one**. Sentence (1) has the same three tests and now starts "Except as provided in Sentence (2)". The new **Sentence (2)** says Part 9 does not apply to buildings designed and constructed under **Subsection 3.2.10.** (residential buildings with a single exit).

c) Points a good answer covers:
- Height is only one of **three** tests. Building area (600 m²) and major occupancy also count.
- Assembly, care and high-hazard buildings are Part 3 at any size.
- The rule has been revised, so read the current Article and the revisions page, not memory.
- On a real job, the designer's code review on the drawings says which Part applies. If it is not clear, ask the supervisor.
