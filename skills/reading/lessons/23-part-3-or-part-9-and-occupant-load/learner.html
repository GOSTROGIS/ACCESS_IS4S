Part 3 or Part 9 and the occupant load

Read the BC Building Code articles that decide whether a building falls under Part 3 or Part 9, classify a building by its major occupancy, and work out an occupant load from Table 3.1.17.1.

  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Why this matters on the job

The BC Building Code has two different sets of rules for buildings. Part 9 covers houses and small buildings. Part 3 covers bigger buildings and buildings where people gather, sleep in care or are held. A garage, a corner store and a four-storey apartment building do not follow the same rules, so the first question on any job is "which Part are we under?" The designer answers it before the drawings are made, and it is often written on a code review sheet at the front of the set, like the London Square cover sheet in Reading lesson 16. The occupant load, the number of people a room or floor is designed for, is the number that many fire and exit rules are built on. You may have seen it on a sign beside the door of a restaurant.

This lesson builds on Reading lesson 6, Find a rule in the BC Building Code. Have the BC Building Code 2024 PDF open:

BC Building Code 2024 page: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/construction-industry/building-codes-standards/bc-codes/2024-bc-codes

Key ideas

1. Where the answer is. Which Part applies is set in Division A, Subsection 1.3.3. Application of Division B. Two articles do the work: - Article 1.3.3.3. Application of Part 9 - Article 1.3.3.2. Application of Parts 3, 4, 5 and 6

2. Part 9 needs three things, all at once. Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) says Part 9 applies to buildings - of 3 storeys or less in building height, and - with a building area not exceeding 600 m², and - used for major occupancies in Group C (residential), Group D (business and personal services), Group E (mercantile) or Group F, Division 2 or 3 (medium- and low-hazard industrial).

If even one of the three is not true, the building is not a Part 9 building.

3. Some buildings are always Part 3. Sentence 1.3.3.2.(1) says Part 3 applies to buildings that are - post-disaster buildings, or - used for Group A (assembly), Group B (care, treatment or detention) or Group F, Division 1 (high-hazard industrial), of any size, or - Group C, D, E, F2 or F3 buildings that are over 600 m² in building area or over 3 storeys in building height.

4. The words in italics have exact meanings. They are defined in Article 1.4.1.2. of Division A.

Defined word What the Code says, in short
Building area the greatest horizontal area of the building above grade, measured to the outside surface of the exterior walls. It is not all the floors added together.
Storey the space from the top of one floor to the top of the floor above
First storey the uppermost storey whose floor is not more than 2 m above grade
Basement a storey below the first storey
Building height (in storeys) the number of storeys from the floor of the first storey up to the roof. A basement is below the first storey, so it does not count.
Grade the lowest of the average levels of finished ground along each exterior wall

5. Major occupancy groups. Table 3.1.2.1. lists the groups. Note A-3.1.2.1.(1), in the notes at the end of Part 3, gives examples of each one.

Group Division Major occupancy Examples from Note A-3.1.2.1.(1)
A 1 Assembly, performing arts theatres, motion picture theatres
A 2 Assembly not classed elsewhere in Group A restaurants, churches, community halls, gymnasia, schools
A 3 Assembly, arena type arenas, rinks, indoor swimming pools
A 4 Assembly in the open air bleachers, grandstands, stadia
B 1 Detention jails, prisons
B 2 Treatment hospitals
B 3 Care group homes, care facilities without treatment
C – Residential houses, apartments, hotels, motels
D – Business and personal services offices, banks, dental offices, barber shops
E – Mercantile shops, stores, supermarkets
F 1 High-hazard industrial paint factories, spray painting operations
F 2 Medium-hazard industrial repair garages, woodworking factories, warehouses
F 3 Low-hazard industrial storage garages, creameries

Some uses show up in more than one list, such as warehouses and workshops in both F2 and F3. Then the designer decides from what is stored or done there. A building with more than one major occupancy is classified by all of them (Sentence 3.1.2.1.(2)).

The Code also has a few special cases. For example, Article 3.1.2.7. lets a small Group A, Division 2 suite (not more than 30 persons, and not a daycare) be classified as Group D if it meets the conditions in that Article. Special cases like this are the designer's call. In this lesson, use the main tables.

6. Occupant load. Article 3.1.17.1. (Part 3) and Article 9.9.1.3. (Part 9) both send you to Table 3.1.17.1. Occupant Load. For most areas, the table gives an area per person in m². Divide the floor area by that number:

occupant load = floor area ÷ area per person

The Code says the occupant load is not less than what the table gives, unless it can be shown that fewer people will use the area. Dwelling units are different: Part 9 uses 2 persons per bedroom or sleeping area. If a room is used for different things at different times, Sentence 3.1.17.1.(4) says to use the value that gives the greatest number of people. The numbers in this lesson are chosen so that every answer comes out to whole people.

7. Check the revisions. The Code changes. Revision 3 (in effect August 27, 2024) replaced Article 1.3.3.3. Sentence (1) kept the same three tests, and a new Sentence (2) says Part 9 does not apply to buildings designed under Subsection 3.2.10., a new set of rules for some residential buildings with a single exit stair. The PDF on the government page may not include every revision, so always check the revisions page too.

Worked example

Question: A small survey company is building a one-storey office with no basement. It measures 15.5 m by 12 m to the outside of the walls. Is it Part 3 or Part 9, and what is the occupant load of the 186 m² office floor?

  1. Occupancy. Offices are Group D (Table 3.1.2.1. and Note A-3.1.2.1.(1)). Group D is on the Part 9 list.
  2. Building height. One storey. That is 3 storeys or less.
  3. Building area. 15.5 × 12 = 186 m². That does not exceed 600 m².
  4. All three tests pass, so Sentence 1.3.3.3.(1) puts it under Part 9.
  5. Occupant load. Part 9 sends you to Table 3.1.17.1. (Article 9.9.1.3.). Under "Business and personal services uses", offices are 9.30 m² per person.
  6. 186 ÷ 9.30 = 20 persons.

Practice

For Tasks 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 9 you need the BC Building Code 2024 PDF open. All buildings in this lesson are fictional.

Task 1 (level 2) Go to Article 1.3.3.3. in Division A. a) Write the three things that must all be true for Part 9 to apply. b) Which words in the Sentence tell you that a building of exactly 600 m² can still be Part 9?

Task 2 (level 2) Use Table 3.1.2.1. and Note A-3.1.2.1.(1). Give the Group (and Division, if there is one) for each use. a) a hair salon b) a supermarket c) a motel d) a church e) an arena f) a hospital g) a repair garage h) a paint factory

Task 3 (level 2) Part 3 or Part 9? For each building, give your answer, the reason, and the Sentence you used.

Building Use Storeys (building height) Building area
A House 2 120 m²
B Restaurant 1 240 m²
C Hardware store 1 900 m²
D Apartment building 4 550 m²
E Repair garage 1 450 m²
F Daycare for children 1 200 m²
G Office building 3 600 m²

For building F, also read Article 3.1.2.8.

Task 4 (level 2) A two-storey house measures 140 m² on the main floor and 110 m² on the upper floor, both measured to the outside of the walls. There is no basement. a) What is its building area? b) A co-worker says the building area is 250 m². What mistake did they make?

Task 5 (level 3) A building has four floors. Their levels, measured from grade, are: - Floor 1: 2.4 m below grade - Floor 2: 0.3 m above grade - Floor 3: 3.3 m above grade - Floor 4: 6.3 m above grade

a) Which floor is the first storey? Use the definition. b) What is Floor 1 called? c) What is the building height in storeys? d) The building is an office with a building area of 480 m². Part 3 or Part 9?

Task 6 (level 2) Use Table 3.1.17.1. Find the occupant load of each area. a) an office floor of 279 m² b) a classroom of 74 m² c) a cafeteria (dining space) of 96 m² d) a store on the first storey, 185 m² of sales floor e) a storage room of 92 m²

Task 7 (level 3) A two-storey building has a building area of 400 m². The ground floor is a store with 370 m² of sales floor. The second floor is offices, 372 m². a) What are its major occupancies? b) Part 3 or Part 9? Why? c) What is the occupant load of each floor, and of the whole building?

Task 8 (level 3) A 30 m² hall in a Part 3 building is set up with loose chairs for meetings. On some nights it is used for standing-room events. a) What area per person does Table 3.1.17.1. give for each use? b) What is the occupant load for each use? c) Which number must the designer use, and which Sentence says so?

Task 9 (level 3) A co-worker says: "Part 9 is anything three storeys or less. Everybody knows that." a) Name two kinds of building that are three storeys or less and still fall under Part 3. b) Open the BC Code revisions page from the BC Building Code 2024 page. What did Revision 3 do to Article 1.3.3.3.? c) What would you tell your co-worker?
