Follow a commissioning procedure

Read a written procedure for starting up a new motor, find who does each step, the hold points and the lockout steps, and decide what to do when something goes wrong.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the information; Make connections between different parts of the document(s); Understand and apply the information; Evaluate the document(s)

Task 1 (level 2)

a) Millwright. The "Who does what" line at the top of Document A is the key. b) Steps 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 and 2.4. The electrician also does the [All] steps (1.2, 1.3, 3.1 and 4.4) along with everyone else. c) Step 1.1.

Task 2 (level 2)

Order: D, B, F, E, A, C, G

Start-up meeting (1.1), connect the leads (2.2), rotation check not coupled (2.3), soft foot (3.2), coupling and guard (3.4), bump test (4.3), sign CP-12A (5.1).

Task 3 (level 2)

a) Step 3.4. The coupling guard must be installed before anyone goes on to Section 4. b) Step 5.2. Production staff may run the conveyor only after the supervisor signs form CP-12A (step 5.1).

Other hold-point wording a strong learner may find: "before any work starts" in step 1.3, and "Do not start the motor again until the cause is found and fixed" in step 4.4.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) Lock out: 1.3, 2.4 (only if the direction is wrong), 3.1 and 4.4 (only if something is not normal). b) Locks come off: 2.3 and 4.1. c) The motor has to run for the rotation check and for the test. A locked-out motor cannot run. As soon as anyone needs to work on the machine again, the locks go back on.

Task 5 (level 2)

a) "So a wrong direction cannot drive the conveyor backward." b) Soft foot is a foot that does not sit flat on the base. It must be corrected with shims before aligning starts.

Task 6 (level 3)

Steps from 4.4, in order: 1. Press the emergency stop. 2. Lock out following LO-01: your own tagged lock, then check that the lockout works. 3. Tell the supervisor. 4. Do not start the motor again until the cause is found and fixed.

A good answer may add: the fix is done with the conveyor locked out, and the test starts again from step 4.1, with the supervisor checking that everyone is clear and each worker removing their own lock.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Step 3.4: "Do not go on to Section 4 until the guard is installed." The test run is in Section 4. b) Points a good answer covers: - The procedure says the guard goes on before any test. It is a hold point, not a suggestion. - Watch and listen from a safe place with the guard on. - If someone thinks the coupling needs to be seen running, that is the supervisor's call, not the crew's. Ask before doing anything different.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) The bump test proves the finished install: wiring, alignment, coupling and guard together. Section 3 must be done first, and step 3.4 says Section 4 cannot start until the guard is on. Running the motor coupled before it is aligned would test a job that is not finished. b) So that someone can stop the motor at once if anything is not normal (step 4.4). The person stays within reach for the whole test. c) Fraserline lockout procedure LO-01, which Document A names but does not include. On a real job, ask the supervisor for the employer's written lockout procedure for that equipment. The Regulation says lockout procedures must be made available to every worker who has to work on the machinery or equipment.

Check yourself 1

Proving that a new machine is installed right and runs safely before it is handed over to the people who will use it.

Check yourself 2

A letter or word at the start of a step, such as [E] or [M], that says who does that step. It matters because each trade does only its own steps, and some steps (like opening an electrical panel) are not everyone's to do.

Check yourself 3

Any two of: "Do not go on to…", "Stop", "until", "only then", "before any work starts".

Check yourself 4

That worker's own job. Each worker removes their own personal lock when their work is done.

Check yourself 5

They are the starting point for future maintenance. Later readings are compared with them to spot a problem early.
