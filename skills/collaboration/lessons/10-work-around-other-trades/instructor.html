Work around other trades

Follow the order of trades on a shared job, know who waits on whom, respect inspection hold points and keep the next trade moving.

Collaboration lesson 10 of 15 · 90 min · Level 2-3

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 10 of 15. This session runs 90 minutes.

Hook: "Seven trades, one small washroom. Who goes first?" Let learners call out trades and put them in order on the board. Leave any disagreements up. You will come back to them with the swimlane chart.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read a trade sequence on a swimlane chart and say who waits on whom (Achieve a common goal with others: assess and mitigate risks and manage resources)
  • explain inspection hold points and why work is not covered until it passes (Achieve a common goal with others)
  • work out how a delay in one trade moves the finish date (Achieve a common goal with others)
  • use habits that keep the next trade moving, and send problems between companies through the foremen (Work well with other people, Manage difficult interactions with other people)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. Have you ever waited on another trade? What happened?
  2. What does "rough-in" mean in your trade?

Post-assessment (6 min, step 3.1)

  1. Name a hold point in your trade, or in a trade you work beside.
  2. The tiler is two days late, and nobody follows the tiler. What happens to the finish date? What if the drywaller is two days late?
  3. Another trade has piled material where your crew needs to work. What do you do?

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • dependencies, rough-in and finish
  • inspection hold points, and never covering work that has not passed
  • reading a swimlane chart, and how one late trade moves every trade after it
  • six habits that keep the next trade moving
  • sending problems between companies through the foremen

Next session: running a pre-task huddle and handing over at shift end.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. It is a bridge to the Numeracy scheduling lessons, which teach bar charts, networks and float in detail. Here, keep the maths to counting days.
  • The swimlane is a teaching example. Real jobs vary: some cities do not need an insulation inspection for an interior wall, and plumbing and electrical inspections are often on different days by different authorities. Say so if learners ask.
  • Inspection requirements come from the permit and the local authority. Do not tell learners a rule applies everywhere in BC. Use the sources to show two real lists.
  • The post-assessment question 2 answer: the tiler is last, so the finish moves two days. The drywaller being two days late also moves the tiler and the finish two days.
