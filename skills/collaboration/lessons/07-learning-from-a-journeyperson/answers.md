# Learning from a journeyperson

## Task 1 (level 1)

| Moment | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a) On a ladder mid-cut | Wait | Interrupting can cause a slip or a bad cut. |
| b) About to start a new task | Now | Asking before you begin saves redoing work. |
| c) Crane signals | Wait | Signals need full attention. Ask once the lift is done. |
| d) Coffee break, bigger question | Now | A break is a good time for questions that need a conversation. |
| e) Not sure the power is off | Stop and ask right away | Never guess on safety. Do not touch it until you know. |
| f) Just shown a step, missed part | Now | Ask while it is fresh: "Can you show me that last part again?" |

## Task 2 (level 1)

**Examples**

a) "I've never set a door frame. Can you show me the first one, and I'll do the second while you watch?"
b) "I've checked the wall with the level and it's out about a bubble at the top. Is that close enough, or do I shim it?"
c) "The north wall is sheeted and I've cleaned up the offcuts. Should I start the south wall or help with the window bucks?"

**What makes a question clear:** it names the task, says what you tried or what you see, and asks one specific thing.

## Task 3 (level 2)

**a)** Response 2 is best. It accepts the feedback, fixes the problem right away and says what the apprentice will do from now on. Response 1 blames others. Response 3 is an excuse and does not fix anything.

**b)** Example: "Thanks for telling me. Can you show me what you check before you call a job done? ... Okay, I'll go through that every time before I say I'm finished." Next day: "I went through the check on the last three. Anything I'm still missing?"

**What a good response covers:** listens without arguing, says thanks, asks a question to understand, commits to a change, and follows up.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**a)** 40 × 30 = 1,200 hours
**b)** 1,200 − 960 = 240 hours not showing
**c)** 240 ÷ 40 = 6 weeks

**d)** Example: "Hi Carla, I checked my SkilledTradesBC Portal account. It shows 960 hours, but by my count I've worked 1,200 since I started on January 6, so about 240 hours (6 weeks) aren't showing yet. Could you check when you next report my hours? Thanks."

**What a good message covers:** polite, gives both numbers and the gap, says how the learner counted, and asks the sponsor to check. It does not accuse.

## Task 5 (level 2)

**Open task.** Good coaching:

- the task is broken into 4 steps or fewer, in order
- the coach watches the partner try it instead of doing it for them
- the partner says the steps back
- the coach changes something after watching, such as slowing down, showing instead of telling, or splitting a step in two

## Task 6 (level 2)

**a)** "Thanks for checking them."
**b)** Sample: "Can you show me where the undercut is, and what I should change, the angle or the travel speed?"
**c)** "I redid them. Is this better?"

## Task 7 (level 3)

**a)** Sample: 1. I show one cut while Noor watches. 2. Noor says the steps back. 3. Noor marks and cuts one while I watch. 4. I check it against the tape and we talk about what to change. A good plan has show, say back, try while watched and check.
**b)** Sample: "This one is 3 mm long. Your line is right, so the blade is cutting on the wrong side of it. Next one, keep the blade on the waste side of the line." It names the fact, the likely cause and one thing to do.
**c)** Sample: Noor cuts three pieces in a row to length without help, and can explain the steps to someone else.

## Check yourself 1

About 80%. The other 20% is in-class technical training.

## Check yourself 2

Your sponsor (often your employer), in the SkilledTradesBC Portal. You should sign in to your own account to check them.

## Check yourself 3

Any two of: watch first, ask at the right time, repeat back what you were shown, write it down, try it while they watch, ask what to change.

## Check yourself 4

Listen to the end without arguing or explaining.

## Check yourself 5

Any one of: show them a task you know well, break it into steps, watch them try it, answer their questions without making them feel stupid, tell them what they did well.
