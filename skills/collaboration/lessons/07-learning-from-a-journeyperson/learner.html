Learning from a journeyperson

Understand how BC apprenticeship puts you beside a journeyperson, ask good questions, take feedback well and pass on what you learn.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 70 min
  • Components: Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Reflect and improve on teamwork; Work well with other people

Why this matters on the job

In a BC apprenticeship, most of your learning happens on the job, beside a certified journeyperson. How well you learn depends a lot on that working relationship. An apprentice who asks clear questions, listens to feedback and shows progress gets taught more, and gets trusted with more. Later, you will be the one showing someone new.

Key ideas

How apprenticeship works in BC. SkilledTradesBC runs the system. These points come from its website:

  • About 80% of your training happens at work, under the supervision of a certified journeyperson. About 20% is in-class technical training with a SkilledTradesBC-designated training provider.
  • Most apprenticeships take four or more years. The average across all trades is about 4 years.
  • Your sponsor (often your employer) makes sure you get on-the-job training and mentorship from a certified journeyperson. You should know which journeyperson is responsible for supervising you.
  • Your sponsor reports your work-based training hours in the SkilledTradesBC Portal. SkilledTradesBC recommends every 3 to 6 months. One hour on the job counts as one hour of work-based training.
  • Sign in to your own Portal account often to check that your hours are there. If they are missing or wrong, contact your sponsor.
  • At the end, your sponsor and the journeyperson who supervised you give a Recommendation for Certification, confirming you work at a journeyperson's level.

Be easy to teach.

  • Watch first. Ask to see it done once.
  • Ask at the right time. Don't interrupt someone in the middle of a cut or a lift, unless it is about safety. Never guess on safety: stop and ask.
  • Repeat back what you were shown: "So first I check..., then I..."
  • Write it down. A small notebook in your pocket beats asking the same thing three times.
  • Try it while they watch, then ask, "What should I change?"

Ask clear questions. "How do I do this?" is hard to answer. "I've measured twice and I get 2 mm different each time. Am I hooking the tape wrong?" shows what you tried and where you are stuck.

Take feedback well.

  1. Listen to the end. Don't start explaining.
  2. Say thanks, even if it stings.
  3. Ask a question to understand: "Can you show me where it goes wrong?"
  4. Do it differently next time.
  5. Follow up: "Is this better?"

The five steps for taking feedback make a loop. Step 5 brings you back to listening.

Mentoring goes both ways. The Skills for Success model includes supporting others through coaching, mentoring and motivating. Once you can do a task well, you can show the next new person. Teaching something is also one of the best ways to check that you really know it.

Worked example: cuts running long

Tidewater Carpentry (a fictional company). Journeyperson Bev tells apprentice Eli: "Your stud cuts are coming out about 3 mm long."

Response A: "That's not on me. The saw's off." Eli defends himself before he knows the cause. Bev is less likely to spend time teaching him.

Response B: "Okay, thanks. Can you watch me do the next one and tell me where it's going wrong?" Bev watches and sees Eli is lining up the blade on the wrong side of his pencil line. She shows him once. Eli's next three cuts are right, and he writes the fix in his notebook. At the end of the day he says, "Checked the last ten, all on the mark."

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Is this a good time to ask your journeyperson a question? Write now, wait or stop and ask right away.

a) They are on a ladder in the middle of a cut. b) You are about to start a task you've never done. c) The crane operator is taking signals from your journeyperson. d) It's coffee break, and you have a bigger question about your training plan. e) You are not sure the power to the circuit you are about to touch is off. f) They have just shown you a step, and you didn't catch part of it.

Task 2 (level 1) Make each question clear and specific. Add what you tried or what you see.

a) "How do I do this?" b) "Is this right?" c) "What now?"

Task 3 (level 2) a) Your journeyperson says: "You left the extension cord across the walkway again." Which response is best, and why?

  1. "Everyone does it."
  2. "Sorry. I'll run it along the wall now and check the walkway before I start each task."
  3. "I was going to move it."

b) Your journeyperson says: "You're fast, but you're not checking your work before you call it done." Write your own response using the five feedback steps.

Task 4 (level 2) You started with your sponsor on January 6. You have worked 40 hours a week for 30 weeks. Your Portal account shows 960 work-based training hours.

a) How many hours have you worked? b) How many hours are not showing in the Portal? c) How many 40-hour weeks is that? d) Write a short, polite message to your sponsor about it.

Task 5 (level 2) Coach a partner. Pick a simple task you know well (a knot, folding a shirt for a drawer, a phone setting, a step in your trade). Explain it in 4 steps or fewer. Watch your partner do it. Ask them to say the steps back. Then switch.

What I changed in my explanation after watching my partner: ____

Task 6 (level 2) Use the loop. Your journeyperson looks at your fillet welds on a bracket and says: "These are undercut. Grind them out and redo them."

a) Step 2: what do you say? b) Step 3: write one question to understand. c) Step 5: what do you say after the redo?

Task 7 (level 3) Now you teach. You are a third-year carpentry apprentice. A new first-year, Noor (fictional), needs to learn to mark and cut blocking with a speed square.

a) Write four short teaching steps. Use the "be easy to teach" list from the other side: what will you show, what will Noor try, how will you check? b) Noor's first cut is 3 mm long. Write the feedback you give. Use facts and say what to do next. c) How will you know Noor has really learned it?

Check yourself

  1. About what share of BC apprenticeship training happens at work?
  2. Who reports your work-based training hours, and where?
  3. Name two things you can do to be easy to teach.
  4. What should you do first when you get feedback?
  5. Name one way you can support a newer worker.
