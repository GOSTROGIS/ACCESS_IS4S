Learning from a journeyperson

Understand how BC apprenticeship puts you beside a journeyperson, ask good questions, take feedback well and pass on what you learn.

Collaboration lesson 7 of 8 · 60 min · Level 1-2

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (3 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 7 of 8. This session runs 60 minutes.

Hook: "Think of the best teacher, coach, Elder, boss or older worker who ever showed you how to do something. What did they do that made it stick? And what did you do that made them want to keep teaching you?" Take two or three answers. Point out the second half: learning is a two-way job.

Objective (1 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • explain the roles of the apprentice, sponsor and supervising journeyperson in a BC apprenticeship (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others)
  • ask clear questions at the right time (Work well with other people)
  • respond to feedback in a way that leads to improvement (Reflect and improve on teamwork: use feedback constructively)
  • check their own work-based training hours and raise a gap politely (Work well with other people)
  • coach a partner through a simple task (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: support others through coaching, mentoring, and motivating)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. Who records an apprentice's on-the-job hours in BC?
  2. When a boss points out a mistake, what is your first reaction, usually?

Post-assessment (5 min, step 3.1)

  1. What does your sponsor do for your apprenticeship, and what do you need to check yourself?
  2. Turn "Is this right?" into a clear question about a task in your trade.
  3. Your journeyperson says your work area is messy. Give a response that follows the five feedback steps.

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • how BC apprenticeship works: about 80% at work, sponsors, supervising journeypersons, hours in the Portal and the Recommendation for Certification
  • being easy to teach: watch, ask at the right time, repeat back, write it down, try it while they watch
  • clear questions that show what you tried
  • five steps for taking feedback well
  • coaching someone newer than you

Next session: respectful workplaces, bullying and harassment.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson. There was no ESAF lesson on working with a mentor.
  • Rules and numbers differ by trade. Send learners to Find Your Trade on skilledtradesbc.ca for their own trade's hours and levels. Do not quote one trade's hours as if they apply to all.
  • Some learners will already have had a bad experience with a boss or journeyperson. If a story involves humiliation, threats or hazing, it belongs in lesson 8, not here. Acknowledge it and say you will come back to it.
  • The stud-cut example is a teaching story about feedback, not an instruction on how to cut.
