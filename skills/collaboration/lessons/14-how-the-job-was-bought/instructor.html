How the job was bought and who answers to whom

Know the three common ways an owner buys a building, which bonds protect whom, and how a shop drawing travels up the chain and back, so your questions and paperwork go to the right place.

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 14 of 15. This session runs 75 minutes. It builds on lesson 9 (who does what on a job site) and lesson 13 (RACI).

Hook: hold up any manufactured item in the room, such as a door closer or a light fixture. "Before this was made for this building, somebody drew exactly how it would be made, and somebody else had to stamp that drawing. Who do you think that was?" Take a few answers. Then ask, "And who paid for this building, and how did they pick the builder?" Today is about the deal behind the job, and why it decides where your questions go.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell design-bid-build, design-build and construction management apart, and say who their company usually signs with in each (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others)
  • send a design question up the right chain (Achieve a common goal with others: consult and share with others when needed and appropriate)
  • name the bid bond, performance bond and labour and material payment bond and who each protects, and work out a bond amount (Achieve a common goal with others: assess and mitigate risks and manage resources)
  • follow a shop drawing up the chain and back, read the review stamp, and count the days it takes (Work well with other people: adhere to social and organizational rules)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. On a job you know, who did your company have its contract with?
  2. Have you heard the words "shop drawing" before? What do you think one is?

Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

  1. A job was bought design-build. You have a question about a beam size. Who answers it?
  2. A GC's contract is $480,000 and the owner wants a 50% performance bond. How much is the bond? ($240,000)
  3. A shop drawing comes back "Revise and resubmit". Can the shop start cutting steel?

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • design-bid-build, design-build and construction management, and who signs with whom
  • the chain a design question travels in each
  • bid bonds, performance bonds and labour and material payment bonds
  • the shop drawing chain up to the consultant and back down, and what each stamp means
  • counting the working days a review takes, and what a resubmit does to the schedule

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. It is built on topics only. No contract wording is quoted. Do not hand out CCDC documents: they are copyrighted and sold through construction associations.
  • Keep it at crew level. Learners do not need to know how bonds are priced or how CM fees work. They need to know where their question goes and why the shop waits for a stamp.
  • The 5-day consultant review and the other durations in the example are job data written for this lesson. Real specifications set their own review times.
  • The 50% bond figure is what the Surety Association of Canada describes as typical. Owners can ask for other amounts. Say "typically" when you teach it.
  • Other ways to buy a job exist, such as integrated project delivery and public-private partnerships. Mention them only if a learner asks.
  • Under a construction management contract for services only, no single trade contractor runs the site. In BC, whoever is prime contractor still has to coordinate safety. If a learner works on such a job, they ask on day one who the prime contractor is (lesson 9).
