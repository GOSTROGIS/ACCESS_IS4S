# How the job was bought and who answers to whom

## Task 1 (level 1)

**a)** Design-build. **b)** Design-bid-build. **c)** Construction management.

## Task 2 (level 2)

**a)** You, your foreman, the GC, then the architect or engineer, usually by RFI.
**b)** You, your foreman, the design-builder. The design-builder's own designers answer.
**c)** You, your foreman, the construction manager, then the consultant. The drywall sub's contract is with the CM.

A good answer always goes through the learner's own foreman first and never jumps straight to the designer.

## Task 3 (level 2)

**a)** Bid bond.
**b)** Performance bond.
**c)** Labour and material payment bond. The electrical sub had a direct subcontract with the bonded GC.
**d)** No. The supplier's contract was with the electrical sub, not the GC. The standard payment bond covers only those with a direct contract with the bonded contractor.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**a)** 50% of $1,250,000 = 0.5 × $1,250,000 = **$625,000** for each bond.
**b)** **$625,000.** The surety is never liable for more than the bond amount.

## Task 5 (level 1)

1. The cabinet shop draws it.
2. The millwork sub checks the cabinet shop's drawing.
3. The GC checks field measurements and stamps it.
4. The architect reviews it for general conformance with the design.
5. The stamped drawing goes back to the cabinet shop.

## Task 6 (level 2)

**a)** 3 + 2 + 5 + 1 = **11 working days.**
**b)** The first pass took 12 days. 12 + 11 = **working day 23.**
**c)** 23 + 8 = **working day 31.**
**d)** 31 − 25 = **6 days late.**

## Task 7 (level 2)

**a)** Build it, and add the bracket at the landing.
**b)** Do not build. Fix the post spacing to match the architectural drawings and send the drawing up the chain again.
**c)** Do not change it on site. The stamped drawing is what was reviewed. The foreman raises it with the GC, and the GC asks the consultant (for example by RFI). Build the change only when it comes back in writing.

## Task 8 (level 3)

A good answer covers:

- Stop work on that post. Do not move it on your own.
- Tell your own foreman, with a photo and the location.
- Your foreman tells the GC. The GC talks to the electrical sub and, if the post or the floor box has to move, asks the consultant.
- The decision comes back down in writing: a revised shop drawing, a note on the stamp, or a site instruction.
- Why not just move it: the post spacing was reviewed by the consultant for the design. A moved post might not meet the design, and the shop still owns its measurements. Moving it without approval can mean doing the work twice and paying for it.
