Who does what on this task with a RACI chart

Use a RACI chart to show who does each task, who answers for it, who to ask first and who to tell after, so nothing falls between two people.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Achieve a common goal with others; Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Work well with other people

Task 1 (level 1)

a) I. b) R. c) A. d) C.

Task 2 (level 1)

a) Sam, the foreman. b) Ana. She is C on "Order the lumber", so Sam asks her before he orders. c) Ana. She is A on "Cut the stair stringers", so she checks and signs off. d) Choose the railing style.

Task 3 (level 2)

a) 2 tasks: dig and pour the footings (row 3) and check the railing before handover (row 6). b) 4 tasks: rows 1, 2, 3 and 6. Rows 1 and 2 are A/R, so they count. c) 4 blank boxes: Kyle in row 1, Kyle in row 2, Grace in row 4 and Kyle in row 5. The chart has 6 rows × 4 people = 24 boxes, and 20 of them have a letter.

Task 4 (level 2)

  1. Task A has two A's (foreman and lead hand). Keep one A, usually the foreman. Change the lead hand to C or R.
  2. Task B has no A. Nobody answers for the concrete order. Make the foreman A. The supplier stays R for the delivery, or the foreman becomes A/R for placing the order.
  3. Task D has no R. Nobody does the work. Make the lead hand or the apprentice R, or both.

A good answer names the row, the broken rule and a fix that leaves exactly one A and at least one R in every row.

Task 5 (level 2)

A model chart:

Task Marco (foreman) Lena (journeyperson) Tyler (apprentice) Painter
Cut the pipe A C R
Fit and weld A R
Grind the welds A C R
Prime A I R

Marco is A on every row because he answers for every step. Lena is C on cutting and grinding because she checks Tyler's work and guides him. Some learners write Lena as I on "Prime" and others leave it blank. Both are fine. Any chart is correct if every row has exactly one A, at least one R, and matches the notes.

Task 6 (level 2)

The A was missing, and so was a clear R. Nobody was named to book the inspection. A RACI chart with one name in the A box (and an R, often the same person as A/R) would have shown, before the drywall went up, who had to book it. Everyone else on the crew would be I, so they would know to wait until it passed.

Task 7 (level 2)

Open task. A good chart has at least three tasks and three people or roles, exactly one A in every row, at least one R in every row, and C only for people whose answer is needed before the work.

Check yourself 1

Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.

Check yourself 2

Exactly one.

Check yourself 3

C is asked before or during the work, and their answer goes into it. I is told after. C is two-way talk, I is a one-way message.

Check yourself 4

Yes. Write it as A/R. It is common on small crews.

Check yourself 5

The org chart shows who answers to whom for the whole job. A RACI chart works one task at a time, so the same person can be R on one task and I on another.
