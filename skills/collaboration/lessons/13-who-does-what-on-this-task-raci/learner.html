Who does what on this task with a RACI chart

Use a RACI chart to show who does each task, who answers for it, who to ask first and who to tell after, so nothing falls between two people.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Achieve a common goal with others; Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Work well with other people

All people and companies in this lesson are fictional.

Why this matters on the job

Lesson 9 showed who is who on a site: owner, general contractor, foreman, lead hand, journeyperson, apprentice. That tells you people's titles. It does not tell you who books the inspection on Tuesday, or who checks the stair stringers before they go in. When two people each think the other one will do a task, it does not get done. When two people both think they are in charge of it, they argue. A RACI chart fixes that one task at a time.

Key ideas

The four letters

A RACI chart is a grid. Each row is a task. Each column is a person or a role. Each box gets a letter.

Letter Stands for What it means for that task
R Responsible Does the work.
A Accountable Answers for it. Makes the final call and signs off that it is done right.
C Consulted Asked before or during the work. Their advice goes into it. Two-way talk.
I Informed Told after a decision or when the work is done. One-way message.

A box can also be blank. That person has no part in that task.

Three rules for a good chart

  1. Exactly one A on every task. Two A's means two bosses for one task. No A means nobody answers for it.
  2. At least one R on every task. Someone has to do the work.
  3. One person can be A and R on the same task. On a small crew the foreman often orders the lumber and answers for it. Write it as A/R.

C or I?

Ask yourself: do I need this person's answer before the work goes ahead? If yes, they are C. If they only need to know it happened, they are I. Too many C's slow a job down. Too few I's leave people guessing.

A RACI chart is not the org chart

The org chart in lesson 9 shows who answers to whom for the whole job. A RACI chart works task by task. An apprentice can be R on a task. A homeowner can be A on a choice that is theirs to make, such as the railing style.

Worked example: the backyard deck

Riverbend Decks is building a backyard deck for a homeowner, Grace. Sam is the foreman. Ana is the lead hand, a journeyperson carpenter. Kyle is a first-year carpentry apprentice working with Ana. On this job, Sam books all inspections with the city.

Task Grace (homeowner) Sam (foreman) Ana (lead hand) Kyle (apprentice)
1. Order the lumber I A/R C
2. Book the footing inspection I A/R I
3. Dig and pour the footings I A R R
4. Cut the stair stringers I A R
5. Choose the railing style A R C
6. Check the railing before handover I A R I

Read row 4 across. Kyle cuts the stringers (R). Ana answers for them, checks them and signs off (A). Sam is told when they are done (I). Grace has no part in it, so her box is blank.

Read row 5. The railing style is Grace's choice, so she is A. Sam brings her the options and prices (R). He asks Ana first whether each style can be built on this deck (C).

Check the rules. Count the A's in each row: every row has exactly one. Count the R's: every row has at least one. The chart passes.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Match the letter. Write R, A, C or I.

a) Is told after the work is done. ______

b) Does the work. ______

c) Answers for the task and signs off on it. ______

d) Is asked before the work goes ahead. ______

Task 2 (level 1) Read the deck chart. Use the worked example.

a) Who is accountable for digging and pouring the footings?

b) Who must Sam ask before he orders the lumber?

c) Kyle finishes the stringers. Who checks them?

d) On which task is Grace accountable?

Task 3 (level 2) Count the letters. Use the worked example.

a) On how many tasks is Ana R?

b) On how many tasks is Sam A, including A/R?

c) How many boxes in the whole chart are blank?

Task 4 (level 2) Fix the chart. A new foreman drew this chart for a garage slab. Find the three problems and write how you would fix each one.

Task Foreman Lead hand Apprentice Concrete supplier
A. Set the forms A A R
B. Order the concrete C R
C. Place and finish the slab A R R I
D. Strip the forms A I I

Task 5 (level 2) Fill in a shop chart. Ironwood Fabrication is building 10 sections of steel handrail in the shop. Read the notes. Then fill in the chart with R, A, C or I.

  • Marco, the shop foreman, answers for every step and signs off the finished sections.
  • Lena, a journeyperson fabricator, fits and welds the sections. She asks Marco before she changes the welding order.
  • Tyler, an apprentice, cuts the pipe and grinds the welds under Lena.
  • Lena checks Tyler's cuts before fitting.
  • The painter primes the finished sections. He is told when each section is ready.
Task Marco (foreman) Lena (journeyperson) Tyler (apprentice) Painter
Cut the pipe
Fit and weld
Grind the welds
Prime

Task 6 (level 2) It fell between two people. On a renovation, the electrical rough-in was covered with drywall before the inspection. Afterwards the foreman said, "I thought the lead hand booked it." The lead hand said, "I thought the foreman did." Which letter was missing from the task "Book the electrical inspection", and how would a RACI chart have stopped this?

Task 7 (level 2) Your own crew. Pick three tasks from your shop class or a job you know (for example, end-of-day clean-up, signing out a tool, the morning safety check). Make a RACI chart with at least three people or roles. Check it with the three rules.

Check yourself

  1. What do the four letters in RACI stand for?
  2. How many A's should each task have?
  3. What is the difference between C and I?
  4. Can the same person be A and R on one task?
  5. How is a RACI chart different from the org chart in lesson 9?
