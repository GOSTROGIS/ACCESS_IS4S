Who does what on this task with a RACI chart

Use a RACI chart to show who does each task, who answers for it, who to ask first and who to tell after, so nothing falls between two people.

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (3 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 13 of 15. This session runs 60 minutes. It builds on lesson 9 (who does what on a job site).

Hook: "A crew covered a wall with drywall before the electrical inspection. The foreman said, 'I thought the lead hand booked it.' The lead hand said, 'I thought the foreman did.' Whose job was it?" Take a few answers. Point out that both titles were clear, but the task was not. Today is about making each task clear.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read a RACI chart and say who does, answers for, is asked about and is told about each task (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others)
  • check a chart against three rules and fix one that breaks them (Achieve a common goal with others: take responsibility to make contributions and complete tasks)
  • tell when to consult someone before the work and when to inform them after (Achieve a common goal with others: consult and share with others when needed and appropriate)
  • build a simple chart for a crew they know (Work well with other people)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. On a job you know, who books the inspections?
  2. What goes wrong when two people both think they are in charge of the same task?

Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

  1. A task has two A's. What problem does that cause, and how do you fix it?
  2. The homeowner needs to know when the deck stairs are finished. Is she C or I on that task?
  3. An apprentice cuts the stringers and the journeyperson checks them. Who is R and who is A?

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • the four letters: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed
  • the three rules: exactly one A, at least one R, one person can be A and R
  • consulting before the work and informing after
  • reading, fixing and building a RACI chart for a deck and a shop job

Next session: how the job was bought and who answers to whom.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. There was no ESAF lesson on task-level roles.
  • RACI is a common project-management tool, not a legal or official term. Some companies use other letters for the same idea. The habit that matters is one person answering for each task.
  • Who books inspections depends on the city and on the company. In the deck example it is the foreman because the example says so. Do not teach it as a rule.
  • Task 5 has more than one fair answer for the "C" boxes. Accept any chart that follows the three rules and matches the notes.
  • The deck job uses the same fictional company as the deck network in Numeracy lesson 19, so learners who have done that lesson see the same job from a new angle.
