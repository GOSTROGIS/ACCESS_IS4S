# Preventing and resolving conflict on a crew

## Task 1 (level 2)

**Warning signs in the dialogue**

| Line | Warning sign |
|---|---|
| Mo: "Not me again. I'm always on the roof." | Intense comment, "always" |
| Rita: "Oh, here we go." | Sarcastic comment, impatience |
| Mo: "I'm just saying, I'm always on the roof." | Repeating the same point |
| Rita: "Well, you should have said something last week instead of..." | "Should", finger pointing |
| Mo: "Don't tell me what I should have done." | Intense comment, interrupting |
| Lead: "Okay, let's..." then Rita: "He does this every time." | The lead is cut off before finishing, and Rita talks about Mo instead of to him, which is taking sides against him |

The crew also has lots of talk and no decision about who is on the roof.

**Steps:** compare each line with the warning sign list on the learner page.

## Task 2 (level 2)

**Private, open task.** There are no right answers. A thoughtful response ticks honestly, names a type of person and a reason, and names one specific change (for example, "explain my side instead of keeping it to myself," or "stop changing the subject when it gets tense").

## Task 3 (level 2)

**Examples**

a) "So you've had cleanup three days in a row, and you want it shared around?"
b) "So the drawing change didn't reach the framers, and that's why the wall went up wrong?"
c) "So you're fine with an early start, as long as you're out by 3:30 for your kid?"

**What makes a good paraphrase:** it uses your own words, keeps the facts (three days, the drawing change, 3:30), names what the person needs, and ends so they can say "yes, that's it" or correct you.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**Examples**

a) "When the van comes back with the fuel light on, I have to stop for gas before I can get to the job, and I'm late. I need us to fill it up when it's under a quarter tank."

b) "I've done the cleanup every day for the last two weeks. I'd like to talk about how we share it. When is a good time?" (Facts and a request for time, no "I feel" with a boss.)

c) "When I get talked over at the toolbox talk, I can't get my point across, and it's about the hoist. I need to finish what I'm saying, then I'll hand it back."

**What makes a good W.I.N. statement:** describes what happened, not the person ("when the van comes back", not "when you're lazy"), says the real effect, and asks for one clear change.

## Task 5 (level 2)

**Open task.** A good third round:

- both partners say what they need the item for and by when
- each paraphrases the other at least once
- they find a solution both can accept, such as taking turns, sharing, or finding another item
- no one uses force, threats or put-downs

**What is usually different the third time:** it is faster, calmer, and both people get some or all of what they need. In rounds 1 and 2, pairs often argue about who deserves it, or one person gives in.

## Task 6 (level 2)

**Open task.** A good plan names a specific outcome, gives a W.I.N. statement that describes behaviour and not the person, and sets a real time and place (for example, "at coffee break Tuesday, away from the crew").

## Task 7 (level 2)

**a)** Give in (accommodate).
**b)** Push until you win (compete).
**c)** Meet halfway (compromise).
**d)** Work it out together (collaborate).
**e)** Put it off (avoid).

## Task 8 (level 3)

The samples below assume Kim needs two hours on the lift before a 10:00 inspection and Raj has suitable floor-level work until 9:00. These are invented role-play details, not facts supplied in the case. Accept other clear assumptions and agreements that meet both workers' needs safely.

**a)** Sample (attend and value): "Hold on, both of you. Let's take two minutes. I want to hear each of you."
**b)** Sample: "So, Kim, you need the lift at 7:00 because the inspector is coming at 10:00 to look at your hangers. Is that right?"
**c)** Sample: "When the lift is taken first thing, I can't finish my hangers before the 10:00 inspection. I need it from 7:00 to 9:00."
**d)** Sample: "Kim takes the lift from 7:00 to 9:00. Raj sets up his materials and does layout on the floor until 9:00, then takes the lift." This is work it out together if both people's needs are met (Kim makes the inspection and Raj loses no working time). If one person gives up something, it is meet halfway. Either is correct if the learner names it to match their agreement.

## Check yourself 1

Opinions, facts, perceptions and values.

## Check yourself 2

Any three of: sharp or sarcastic comments, impatience, ideas shot down before the person finishes, repeating the same point, taking sides, lots of talk and no decision, finger pointing, a lot of "you should".

## Check yourself 3

Saying back what you heard, in your own words. It shows you are listening and lets the other person correct you if you got it wrong, so you are both working from the same understanding.
