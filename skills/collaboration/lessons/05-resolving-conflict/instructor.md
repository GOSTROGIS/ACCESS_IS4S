# Preventing and resolving conflict on a crew

**Collaboration lesson 5 of 15 · 100 min · Level 2-3**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 5 of 15. This session runs 100 minutes.

Hook: "Think of the last argument you saw at work or on a team. Was it about the facts, the plan, or the person?" Take a few answers without names. Point out that arguments often start about the work and end up about the person. Today is about keeping it on the work.

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name the common causes and warning signs of conflict on a crew (Manage difficult interactions with other people: anticipate and address interpersonal barriers)
- describe their own usual response to conflict (Work well with other people: assess strengths and weaknesses of yourself)
- use attending, valuing, paraphrasing and questioning in a disagreement (Manage difficult interactions with other people: engage in productive discussions)
- state a need using the W.I.N. method and reach an agreement (Manage difficult interactions with other people: discuss, negotiate, and resolve difficult interactions in a sensitive and helpful manner)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

1. What usually happens when two people on a crew disagree?
2. What is one thing someone has said to you in a disagreement that made it better, or worse?

## Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

1. Name two warning signs that a conflict is building on a crew.
2. Paraphrase this: "I keep getting stuck with the jobs nobody else wants."
3. Turn "You never put the ladder back!" into a W.I.N. statement.

## Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- where conflict comes from: opinions, facts, perceptions and values
- warning signs, and how to prevent conflict before it starts
- our own usual response to conflict
- four listening skills: attend, value, paraphrase, question
- the W.I.N. method for saying what you need
- that threats, harassment and discrimination are not conflicts to negotiate

Next session: a team build task with a debrief.

## Notes for the instructor

- This lesson joins the two ACCESS conflict lessons. Kept from the starter lesson: the causes of conflict, the warning signs and the four skills. Kept from the intermediate lesson: the "You've got it, I want it" activity, the conflict questionnaire (shortened and reworded), the W.I.N. method and the goal sheet.
- Dropped: the Dr. Seuss story, the film suggestion and the "tug of war" quote (published material by others), and the Crime and Punishment ranking worksheet. Its cases included sexual crimes against children, which can harm learners and are not needed to practise consensus. Group decision practice is in lesson 6.
- In the role play, stop any pair whose play becomes personal or physical. Use the debrief to bring it back to the item and the need.
- If a learner's Task 6 conflict involves threats, harassment or discrimination, talk with them privately and point to the reporting steps in lesson 8.
