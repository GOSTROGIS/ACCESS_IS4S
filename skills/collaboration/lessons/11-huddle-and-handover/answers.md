# Run a pre-task huddle and hand over at shift end

## Task 2 (level 1)

**a)** Who does what. **b)** The hazards. **c)** The task. **d)** The controls.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Sample answers. Any three real hazards, each with a control that fits it.

| Hazard | Control |
|---|---|
| Cars driving through the work area | Close the area with cones, signs or barriers, and use a traffic control person if needed |
| Hot asphalt burns | Long sleeves, gloves and boots, and keep clear of the screed |
| Workers struck by the roller or paver backing up | A spotter, back-up alarms working, and nobody walks behind a reversing machine |

## Task 4 (level 2)

Missing or vague: how much is done ("most"), what is left and where ("some"), which room and what "stuff" is, what the hazard upstairs is, and who to call.

Sample rewrite: "Done: conduit in units 201 to 204. Not done: units 205 and 206. Hazards: open floor hatch at the top of the east stairs, taped off. Tools: bender and threader in the gang box in 203. Watch for: 205 ceiling is not inspected yet. Questions: Priya, 604-555-0177."

## Task 6 (level 2)

Samples: "What could hurt someone here today?", "What's changed since yesterday?", "What would you do if the lift stopped halfway?", "Who hasn't done this before?"

## Task 7 (level 2)

**a)** 6 (20 − 14). **b)** At C4. It is covered and tagged. **c)** Move the hole 50 mm because of rebar.

## Task 8 (level 3)

**Open task.** A strong huddle has all five steps in order and names a person for each job. Sample hazards and controls: load falling (rigging inspected, nobody under the load), crane contact with power lines (check distance, a spotter watches the boom), pinch points while landing the beam (tag lines, hands clear), wind (check before the lift, stop if it picks up). Who does what: a named rigger, a named signaller, two people on tag lines. Questions: invite them, and remind everyone that the crane operator must stop on a stop signal from anyone.

## Task 9 (level 3)

Any three, such as: ask a direct open question to one person by name ("Jess, what worries you about this lift?"), ask the worried apprentice privately after the huddle, slow down and walk the area together, use the newest person's eyes on purpose ("Tell me one thing you'd check first"), and next time ask for hazards before you give your own list so people are not just agreeing with you.

## Task 10 (level 3)

**a)** Sample: "Hazards: a lane closure sign blew over at 4:00. Its current condition and whether it has been restored are not confirmed. Contact the supervisor now and have the traffic controls checked and made safe before work continues." The case does not say the sign was put back up or that its base was weak. An active hazard needs action and reporting when discovered, not just a note at shift change.
**b)** Sample: "Every handover lists every sign, light and barrier that changed during the shift, and the next crew checks them before work starts."
**c)** Their supervisor or the employer, as soon as possible. Unsafe conditions must be reported (OHS Regulation section 3.10). A near miss should be reported the way the employer's procedures say.
