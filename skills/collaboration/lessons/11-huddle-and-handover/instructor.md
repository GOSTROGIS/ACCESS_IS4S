# Run a pre-task huddle and hand over at shift end

**Collaboration lesson 11 of 15 · 75 min · Level 2-3**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 11 of 15. This session runs 75 minutes.

Hook: hand one learner a note that says only "Finish the job by the door." Ask them to act it out. Then ask the group, "What do they need to know that the note does not say?" List the gaps. Today is about the two short talks that fill those gaps: the huddle and the handover.

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- run a five-step pre-task huddle that gets the whole crew talking (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others, Achieve a common goal with others: ensure opportunities for others to contribute)
- name hazards and controls for a task, and remind the crew of their duty to report unsafe conditions (Achieve a common goal with others: assess and mitigate risks and manage resources)
- write a complete shift handover with exact numbers and places (Achieve a common goal with others)
- reflect on a handover that went wrong and set a crew rule (Reflect and improve on teamwork)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

1. Have you ever started a shift without knowing what the last crew did? What happened?
2. What is the difference between a toolbox talk and a quick plan before a task?

## Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

1. Name the five huddle steps in order.
2. Give one example of a vague handover line and fix it.
3. In a huddle, what do you remind every worker about unsafe conditions?

## Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- the difference between a pre-task huddle and a toolbox talk
- five huddle steps: task, hazards, controls, who does what, questions
- getting the whole crew talking, including the newest person
- the six parts of a handover card, with exact numbers and places
- learning from a handover that went wrong

Next session: disagreeing with a lead hand and still getting the work done.

## Notes for the instructor

- This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. The huddle steps are a teaching pattern. Many sites use their own form, such as a field level hazard assessment. Learners follow their site's form when there is one.
- Do not teach crane rigging or signalling in Task 8. The point is the huddle. Lesson 9 of the Communication series covers crane signal rules.
- Silica dust from drilling concrete is a real hazard. If learners ask about controls, point them to WorkSafeBC's silica resources rather than teaching it here.
