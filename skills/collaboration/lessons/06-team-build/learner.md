# Plan and build together, then debrief

## Why this matters on the job

On a real job, nobody holds every piece of information. The super has the schedule, the engineer has the specs, the estimator has the budget and the client has what they want. The job only comes together when people share what they know, agree on one plan and adjust when a change order lands halfway through. Afterward, good crews ask, "What would we do differently next time?"

## Key ideas

**Share what you know.** If one person keeps part of the information to themselves, the team builds the wrong thing. Say your piece out loud, then check that everyone has it.

**Disagree, then decide.** Two good ideas can compete. A quick, fair way to decide:

1. Each side explains its idea in one minute.
2. The other side says it back in their own words.
3. Together, pick the best parts of both and agree on one plan.

**Agreement** does not mean everyone thinks it is the best plan. It means everyone can live with it and will work on it. Problems get solved best when each person takes responsibility for both hearing and being heard.

**Give everyone a part.** Someone keeps the team on time, someone keeps the budget, someone builds, someone tests. Make sure no one is left watching.

**Manage risk and resources.** Check the budget before you buy. Test before the client sees it. Think about what could go wrong.

**Debrief.** After the job, look back on the work and on how the team worked together. Make suggestions that are specific and kind, and use the feedback you get.

### Worked example: the purchase order

A team plans to buy 8 sheets of paper and 60 cm of tape. Paper costs $2 a sheet. Tape costs $1 for each 10 cm. The budget is $30.

- Paper: 8 × $2 = $16
- Tape: 60 cm ÷ 10 cm = 6 pieces, and 6 × $1 = $6
- Total: $16 + $6 = $22
- Left in the budget: $30 − $22 = $8

The team is within budget and has $8 left for a change order.

## The job

**Client brief.** The Lakeside Trail Society (a fictional client) wants a model footbridge to show at a meeting. Your team will design and build it from paper and tape.

Each person on your team gets one spec card from your instructor. **Read your card aloud to your team. Do not hand it over.** Your team needs every card to meet the brief.

**Task 5** (level 3)
Debrief as a team. Write short answers:

a) What went well in how we worked together?
b) What did not go well?
c) Did everyone get a real chance to contribute? How do we know?
d) How did we handle the change order?
e) One constructive suggestion for next time, starting with "Next time we will..."
f) One piece of feedback we got from the observer or instructor, and what we will do with it.

## Check yourself

3. A team buys 7 sheets and 90 cm of tape. Are they within the $30 budget? Show your work.
4. Name two questions a team can ask in a debrief.
