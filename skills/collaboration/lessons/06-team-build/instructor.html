Collaboration · Lesson 06 · Lesson 7: Learning from a journeyperson

Plan and build together, then debrief

Share information, agree on a plan, build a model bridge within a budget, handle a change order and look back on how the team worked.

Collaboration lesson 6 of 8 · 90 min · Level 2-3

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 6 of 8. This session runs 90 minutes, and most of it is hands-on.

Hook: "On a real job, who has the schedule? The specs? The budget? What the client wants?" List the answers (superintendent, engineer or drawings, estimator, client). "Does any one person have all of it?" Today each team member holds one piece, and the team has to put the pieces together.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • share their part of the information and build one spec list with the team (Achieve a common goal with others: consult and share with others when needed and appropriate)
  • use a fair method to choose between two ideas and make sure every member has a role (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others, Achieve a common goal with others: ensure opportunities for others to contribute)
  • work within a budget and handle a change (Achieve a common goal with others: assess and mitigate risks and manage resources)
  • reflect on team performance, make a constructive suggestion and use feedback (Reflect and improve on teamwork)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. What is one thing that makes a team project go well?
  2. What do you do when your idea and someone else's idea compete?

Set-up before class

  • Print one set of spec cards (below) for each team. Cut them apart.
  • Print one change order card for each team.
  • Per team: about 15 sheets of letter paper kept at the instructor's "store", one roll of masking tape (sold in 10 cm pieces, or teams measure and record), a ruler, scissors, one paper cup and about 100 washers or coins.
  • Set up a 30 cm gap for each team between two desks or two stacks of books of the same height.
  • Teams of 4. With 5, the fifth person is the observer and uses the observer checklist. With 3, one person reads two cards.

Spec cards

Card A: span and materials. The bridge must cross a 30 cm gap. Use only paper and masking tape. The bridge may rest on the two sides but may not be taped to them or touch the floor.

Card B: load test. The load is washers in a paper cup set at the centre of the bridge deck. The bridge passes if it holds the cup for 10 seconds. Your score is the number of washers held.

Card C: budget. Your budget is $30. Paper costs $2 a sheet. Tape costs $1 for each 10 cm. Buy from the instructor's store. No refunds.

Card D: the client. The deck must be at least 8 cm wide so a model person could walk across. Before the test, the team must give the client a 30-second explanation of how the bridge carries the load.

Change order (hand out 8 minutes into the build): The client has asked for a handrail on one side of the deck, at least 3 cm high. It must be in place for the test.

Observer checklist

  • Did every card get read aloud? Who checked?
  • Who spoke during planning? Was anyone cut off?
  • How was the choice between ideas made?
  • Who had a job during the build? Was anyone left watching?
  • What did the team do when the change order came?

Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

  1. What would have happened if one person had kept their card secret?
  2. How did your team choose between ideas? Was it fair to everyone?
  3. What is one constructive suggestion you made or heard today?

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • sharing information so the whole team works from the same brief
  • a fair three-step way to choose between two ideas, and what agreement means
  • giving everyone a role, managing a budget and handling a change order
  • testing before the client sees the work
  • debriefing: looking back on the work and on how the team worked together

Notes for the instructor

  • This lesson replaces four old files. The ACCESS team building lesson's idea (one group with clear instructions, one with none) and the knot-tying exercise's idea (learn fast from each other, not from a long document) are kept in the spec cards: the team only succeeds if each person shares their piece.
  • The Arctic survival game and the newspaper article on classroom debate are published works by others and are not reproduced. Their skills are trained here with new material: reaching agreement as a group (the merge step) and arguing two sides then finding a solution that works for both (the three-step method).
  • The ACCESS line "agreement means substantial agreement, not necessarily unanimity" and "accept responsibility for both hearing and being heard" come from the ACCESS conflict starter lesson's group task instructions.
  • Score is not the point. A team that held few washers but shared well, met every spec and debriefed honestly has done the lesson well.
