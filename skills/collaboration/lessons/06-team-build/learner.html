Collaboration · Lesson 06 · Learner

Plan and build together, then debrief

Share information, agree on a plan, build a model bridge within a budget, handle a change order and look back on how the team worked.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 100 min
  • Components: Achieve a common goal with others; Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Reflect and improve on teamwork

Why this matters on the job

On a real job, nobody holds every piece of information. The super has the schedule, the engineer has the specs, the estimator has the budget and the client has what they want. The job only comes together when people share what they know, agree on one plan and adjust when a change order lands halfway through. Afterward, good crews ask, "What would we do differently next time?"

Key ideas

Share what you know. If one person keeps part of the information to themselves, the team builds the wrong thing. Say your piece out loud, then check that everyone has it.

Disagree, then decide. Two good ideas can compete. A quick, fair way to decide:

  1. Each side explains its idea in one minute.
  2. The other side says it back in their own words.
  3. Together, pick the best parts of both and agree on one plan.

Agreement does not mean everyone thinks it is the best plan. It means everyone can live with it and will work on it. Problems get solved best when each person takes responsibility for both hearing and being heard.

Give everyone a part. Someone keeps the team on time, someone keeps the budget, someone builds, someone tests. Make sure no one is left watching.

Manage risk and resources. Check the budget before you buy. Test before the client sees it. Think about what could go wrong.

Debrief. After the job, look back on the work and on how the team worked together. Make suggestions that are specific and kind, and use the feedback you get.

The job runs as a loop. A change order sends the team back to the plan.

Worked example: the purchase order

A team plans to buy 8 sheets of paper and 60 cm of tape. Paper costs $2 a sheet. Tape costs $1 for each 10 cm. The budget is $30.

  • Paper: 8 × $2 = $16
  • Tape: 60 cm ÷ 10 cm = 6 pieces, and 6 × $1 = $6
  • Total: $16 + $6 = $22
  • Left in the budget: $30 − $22 = $8

The team is within budget and has $8 left for a change order.

The job

Client brief. The Lakeside Trail Society (a fictional client) wants a model footbridge to show at a meeting. Your team will design and build it from paper and tape.

Each person on your team gets one spec card from your instructor. Read your card aloud to your team. Do not hand it over. Your team needs every card to meet the brief.

Practice

Task 1 (level 2) Each person reads their card aloud. Together, write one list of everything the bridge must do. Check that every card is on the list.

Our spec list:

Task 2 (level 2) Sketch two different bridge ideas. Use the three-step method: each side explains for one minute, the other side says it back, then the team agrees on one plan that uses the best parts of both. Write down what you took from each idea.

Task 3 (level 2) a) Write your team's purchase order: how many sheets and how many centimetres of tape. Show that it is within the $30 budget. b) Team Pine buys 9 sheets and 80 cm of tape. Are they within budget? How much is left? c) Team Fir wants 12 sheets and 70 cm of tape. Are they within budget? By how much are they over or under? d) Name your roles: who keeps time, who keeps the budget, who builds, who tests, who talks to the client.

Task 4 (level 2-3) Build your bridge. Partway through, your instructor will hand your team a change order. Decide together how to handle it. At the end, test the bridge and give the client your 30-second explanation.

Number of washers held: _ Did we meet every spec? _

Task 5 (level 3) Debrief as a team. Write short answers:

a) What went well in how we worked together? b) What did not go well? c) Did everyone get a real chance to contribute? How do we know? d) How did we handle the change order? e) One constructive suggestion for next time, starting with "Next time we will..." f) One piece of feedback we got from the observer or instructor, and what we will do with it.

Task 6 (level 2) A real mini-job: the cut list. Your team frames a practice wall in the shop. The wall is 2,400 mm long. Studs go at 400 mm on centre, with a stud at each end. The wall has one bottom plate and one top plate, each 2,400 mm long. Each plate is 38 mm thick, and the wall is 2,400 mm tall overall.

All pieces are cut from 2,440 mm lengths of 2x4 at $5.25 each (a fictional price). The budget is $50.

a) How many studs do you need? b) How long is each stud? c) How many 2,440 mm lengths do you buy? d) What is the total cost? How much of the budget is left? e) Name a person for each role: cutter, layout, nailer, checker, budget keeper.

Task 7 (level 3) You are the lead hand. A change order lands. Halfway through, the client asks for one extra stud at 1,200 mm so a shelf can be fixed there. There is already a stud at 1,200 mm on centre, so the client wants a second stud beside it.

a) Can you cut the extra stud from offcuts? Show why or why not. b) How much over budget does the change take you? c) Write what you say to the client.

Check yourself

  1. Why could no one person build a bridge that met the brief using only their own card?
  2. In this lesson, what does "agreement" mean?
  3. A team buys 7 sheets and 90 cm of tape. Are they within the $30 budget? Show your work.
  4. Name two questions a team can ask in a debrief.
  5. What makes a suggestion constructive? Give an example.
