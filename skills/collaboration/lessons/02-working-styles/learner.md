# Working styles and style-stepping

## Why this matters on the job

A journeyperson says "clean up the area." To one apprentice that means sweep and stack the offcuts. To another it means tools away, cords coiled, floor swept and garbage hauled. Neither is lazy. They just have different ideas of "done." On a crew you work with people who decide fast and people who check twice, people who talk all day and people who want quiet. Knowing how you like to work, and how others do, saves time and arguments.

## Key ideas

**A working style is a preference, not a rule.** It is how you *tend* to work when you have a choice. Everyone can work in every style, and most people change with the job, the day and the crew.

This lesson uses two simple questions:

- **Pace:** do you tend to act quickly, or take your time?
- **Focus:** do you tend to think about the task first, or the people first?

Put them together and you get four working styles:

| | Task first | People first |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick** | Get it done | Get people going |
| **Steady** | Get it right | Keep the crew together |

**What each style brings, and what can go wrong when it is overused:**

| Style | Brings to a crew | When overused | Working with this style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Get it done | Speed, decisions, drive | Skips steps, gets impatient, talks over others | Be brief. Give the bottom line first. Offer choices. |
| Get people going | Energy, ideas, keeps spirits up | Talks more than works, loses track of details | Start friendly. Give the big picture, then write the details down for them. |
| Keep the crew together | Patience, listening, helps new people | Avoids conflict, says yes when they mean no | Tell them about changes early. Ask their view directly. Give them time. |
| Get it right | Accuracy, planning, checking | Slow, stuck on detail, can sound critical | Give facts and numbers. Give time to check. Ask specific questions. |

A crew needs all four. The fast one keeps the job moving, the careful one catches mistakes, the friendly one keeps people talking, and the steady one looks after the new hire.

**Style-stepping** means changing your approach for a moment so the other person can work with you. You don't change who you are. You change how you deliver the message.

### Be honest about what this is

The preference check below was written for this lesson. **It is not a scientific test and it does not measure your personality.** It is a way to start a conversation about how you like to work.

Popular type tests are widely used at work, but researchers still argue about how well they hold up. A 2019 review in *Social and Personality Psychology Compass* found that the theory behind the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator does not agree well with known data and is hard to test. The company that publishes that test says it should not be used in hiring. If an employer asks you to do a style or type test, it is fair to ask what it will be used for.

### Worked example: "Done"

Ridgeline Plumbing (a fictional company) sends apprentice Kai to finish a kitchen sink while journeyperson Lee works in the next unit. Kai is a *get it done* person. Lee is a *get it right* person.

Kai texts: "done."

Lee calls back with five questions. Was it tested? Any drips? Where is the box of fittings? Kai is annoyed: "I said it was done."

**Style-stepping:** Kai sends what a *get it right* person needs, in one message:

"Kitchen sink in unit 4 is done. Ran the water 5 minutes, no drips under the cabinet. Photo attached. Leftover fittings are back in the van, blue bin."

Lee has no more questions, and Kai gets the next job faster. Kai still works fast. He just added the facts Lee needs.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1)
For each pair, circle the statement that sounds more like you **at work**. There are no right or wrong answers.

| # | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I like to start right away and figure it out as I go. | I like to read the plan before I pick up a tool. |
| 2 | I decide fast. | I take time to think it over. |
| 3 | I talk while I think. | I think first, then talk. |
| 4 | I like the job to keep moving, even if we fix things later. | I would rather slow down than redo work. |
| 5 | Change of plan? Fine, let's go. | Change of plan? Tell me why first. |
| 6 | I get bored on long, repeated tasks. | I am happy doing the same task well all day. |
| 7 | At lunch I talk about the job. | At lunch I talk about people's weekends. |
| 8 | A good day is about what got done. | A good day is about how the crew got along. |
| 9 | I want instructions as a list. | I want someone to show me and talk it through. |
| 10 | With a problem, I go for the fix first. | With a problem, I check how everyone is doing first. |
| 11 | I like to work alone on my part. | I like to work beside someone. |
| 12 | Feedback: just tell me straight. | Feedback: tell me what is going well too. |

Count your A answers in rows 1 to 6. This is your **pace** score.
4 or more = Quick. 2 or fewer = Steady. 3 = you use both.

Count your A answers in rows 7 to 12. This is your **focus** score.
4 or more = Task first. 2 or fewer = People first. 3 = you use both.
