# Working styles and style-stepping

**Collaboration lesson 2 of 8 · 90 min · Level 1-2**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 2 of 8. This session runs 90 minutes.

Hook (adapted from the ACCESS working styles lesson): "A journeyperson tells an apprentice to clean up the area. The apprentice sweeps and says done. The journeyperson says it's not done: cords aren't coiled, tools aren't away. They argue about what 'clean' means." Ask: "Who is right?" Let learners see that both are describing a real standard. They just have different ideas of done. Today is about noticing those differences and working with them.

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- describe their own working preferences using pace and focus (Work well with other people: assess strengths and weaknesses of yourself and others)
- recognize the working style behind a co-worker's message (Value diversity and inclusivity of others: understand that people have different ways of thinking and acting)
- rewrite a message or adjust a conversation to fit another person's style (Value diversity and inclusivity of others: adapt to people's different styles of interaction when possible and appropriate)
- explain why a style check is a conversation tool and not a test

## Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.3)

1. Think of a time a co-worker, boss or customer drove you up the wall. What did they do that bothered you?
2. How did it get sorted out, or did it?

Keep answers general. No names of real people.

## Post-assessment (6 min, step 3.1)

1. What two questions make up the four working styles in this lesson?
2. What is style-stepping? Give one example from your trade.
3. Why should you not treat a style check, or a type test, as a measure of who you are?

## Summary (3 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- working styles as preferences based on pace and focus
- what each of the four styles brings and how it can go wrong
- style-stepping: changing how you deliver a message so the other person can work with you
- why a style check is a conversation tool and not a scientific test

Next session: respecting differences and culture on a job site.

## Notes for the instructor

- This lesson rebuilds two ACCESS lessons that used a four-quadrant personality worksheet and a kite graph. The worksheet and labels are new, written for ACCESS. The ACCESS role play and the idea of style-stepping are kept.
- The old lessons called the worksheet a test that the learner could "verify." Do not describe it that way. Present it as a way to talk about preferences.
- Keep results voluntary. A learner can keep their result private and still take part in Tasks 2 to 5.
- Do not put learners into boxes in later lessons ("you're the get it right one"). That is the stereotyping that lesson 4 warns about.
