Working styles and style-stepping

Talk about how you like to work, notice how others like to work, and adjust your approach so the job goes smoothly.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Work well with other people; Value diversity and inclusivity of others

Task 1 (level 1)

No right or wrong answers. Check the tally only.

Steps

  1. Count the A answers in rows 1 to 6. 4, 5 or 6 = Quick. 0, 1 or 2 = Steady. 3 = uses both.
  2. Count the A answers in rows 7 to 12. 4, 5 or 6 = Task first. 0, 1 or 2 = People first. 3 = uses both.
  3. Find the box where the two meet: Quick + Task = Get it done. Quick + People = Get people going. Steady + People = Keep the crew together. Steady + Task = Get it right.

Example: 5 A answers in rows 1 to 6 and 2 A answers in rows 7 to 12 gives Quick and People first, so get people going.

A learner with a 3 on either scale sits between two styles. That is a normal result, not a mistake.

Task 2 (level 1)

  • a) Get it done: speed first, fix later.
  • b) Get it right: checks the drawing before starting.
  • c) Keep the crew together: looks after the new person.
  • d) Get people going: excited about a new idea and wants to talk it through.

Steps: ask two questions of each message. Is it quick or steady? Is it about the task or about people?

Task 3 (level 2)

What a good rewrite covers: the time it came, the number ordered and the number that came, the shortage, when the shortage will arrive, and where the sheets are.

Example: "Delivery came at 7:30. Ordered 40 sheets, got 38, so 2 short. Driver says the other 2 come Thursday. All 38 are stacked in the garage."

The learner can keep a friendly word if they like. The facts must all be there.

Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good scene:

  • the tradesperson notices a sign of the other person's style (for example, the customer asks for every number, or the superintendent says "just give me the short version")
  • the tradesperson changes how they answer to fit that style, using the "Working with this style" column
  • the problem is sorted out by the last line, with a clear next step
  • 3 to 5 lines, respectful on both sides

Example (homeowner who is get it done): Homeowner: "You were supposed to be here yesterday. I took the day off for nothing." Electrician: "You're right, and I'm sorry. Here's where we are: panel work today, done by 3. I can't get the lost day back, but I've taken the service call fee off." Homeowner: "Fine. By 3?" Electrician: "By 3. I'll text you if anything changes."

Task 5 (level 2)

Things that could go wrong (any two): steps get skipped, measurements are not checked, work has to be redone, new or quieter people are left out, nobody notices when someone is struggling, people talk over each other.

Habits to add (any two): a quick check against the drawing before cutting, one person checks the work before the next step, a 2-minute crew check-in at the start, the lead asks each person by name for their view, someone checks on the newest crew member.

Check yourself 1

No. It was written for this lesson as a way to talk about how you like to work. It is not a scientific test and does not measure personality. Everyone uses all four styles, and people change with the situation.

Check yourself 2

Changing your approach for a moment so another person can work with you, for example adding the facts a get it right person needs. You change how you deliver the message, not who you are.

Check yourself 3

Strength (any one): accuracy, planning, catching mistakes, checking work. Risk when overused (any one): slow, gets stuck on detail, can sound critical.

Check yourself 4

Ask them directly and give them time: "Before we lock it in, what would you change?" or "Is there a part of this plan you're not sure about?" Ask one-on-one if they are quieter in a group, and tell them about changes early.

Check yourself 5

No. A type test should not be the way an employer chooses who to hire. The Myers-Briggs Company says the MBTI shouldn't be used in hiring, and the Myers & Briggs Foundation says it is not designed for hiring and that taking it is voluntary.
