Disagree with a lead hand and still get the work done

Tell a work disagreement from a safety refusal, raise your view once with facts, accept the decision and do the work well, and know how to take a problem further.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Manage difficult interactions with other people; Work well with other people; Reflect and improve on teamwork

Task 1 (level 2)

a) W. It may matter for the code or the drawings, so asking is fine, but it is not a hazard to a person right now. b) S c) W d) S e) W

Task 2 (level 2)

  • b) Roof edge: Do not do the task. Report it right away to your supervisor or employer (section 3.12). It is also an unsafe condition you must report as soon as possible (section 3.10).
  • d) Grinder without a guard: the same. Refuse the task and report it right away (section 3.12).

Task 3 (level 2)

Sample: "When we set the fixtures before the drain is roughed in, I think we'll have to pull them to fit the drain. I'd like to rough in the drain first. Can we talk about the order?" A good answer drops "dumb" and "everyone knows," states what the learner sees, the effect and the idea.

Task 4 (level 2)

Samples: "Can you walk me through why we're starting here?", "Is there something in the drawings or schedule that sets this order?", "What should I watch for doing it this way?"

Task 5 (level 3)

Sample sentence: "Okay, your way. I'll get started." Two things you will not do: work slowly or badly to prove a point, complain about it to the crew, say "I told you so" later, keep re-arguing it during the day.

Task 6 (level 3)

a) The lead hand first, in private, with facts ("I haven't been on layout in three weeks. I'd like a turn."). Next, the foreman. b) The lead hand or foreman. If it is not fixed, the superintendent, who runs the site and the facilities for the prime contractor. Washrooms can also be raised with the health and safety representative or joint committee. c) This is unsafe work. Refuse it and report it to your supervisor (section 3.12). If it is still not resolved, the supervisor investigates again with you and a worker representative, then WorkSafeBC. You can also raise it with your joint health and safety committee or representative.

Task 7 (level 3)

Bring: the dates of each mistake, photos, the drawing references, and the hours spent rebuilding.

Sample opening: "Can I show you something? We've torn out and rebuilt the same kind of wall three times this month: here are the dates and photos." A good answer is private, uses facts and does not name or blame a person.

Task 8 (level 3)

a) Sample: "Good question. Let's finish this lift, then talk it through at the gang box." Calm, no counter-attack, sets a time. b) Thank them for speaking up, ask for their reasons, and explain yours. Then ask that next time they raise it in private first, unless it is about safety. c) Sample: "Jess spotted a better way. We're switching to it. Thanks, Jess." A good lead hand admits it openly. That builds the trust that makes people speak up.

Task 9 (level 3)

Open task. Use the observer checklist. A strong role play shows every box ticked. If the disagreement turns out to be about safety, the worker names the rule instead of "committing."

Check yourself 1

Is it a safety issue?

Check yourself 2

Section 3.10: report an unsafe condition or act as soon as possible to a supervisor or employer. Section 3.12: do not do a task you have reasonable cause to believe would create an undue hazard, and report it right away.

Check yourself 3

You say your view once, then accept the lead hand's decision and do the work their way, and do it well.

Check yourself 4

It keeps the lead hand from feeling challenged in front of the crew, so they are more likely to listen. It also keeps the crew focused on the work.

Check yourself 5

Facts: dates, photos, drawings, measurements and the time or cost of the problem.
