Disagree with a lead hand and still get the work done

Tell a work disagreement from a safety refusal, raise your view once with facts, accept the decision and do the work well, and know how to take a problem further.

Collaboration lesson 12 of 15 · 75 min · Level 3

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 12 of 15. This session runs 75 minutes. It is the last Collaboration session.

Hook: "Hands up if a boss has ever told you to do something a way you thought was wrong." Most hands go up. "What did you do?" Take two or three answers. Sort them on the board into "argued," "went quiet," "said it once" and "refused." Today is about which one fits which situation.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell a work disagreement from a safety issue and name the rules for safety issues (Manage difficult interactions with other people: anticipate and address interpersonal barriers)
  • raise a disagreement once, in private, with facts, and listen to the reason (Manage difficult interactions with other people: engage in productive discussions)
  • accept a decision and do the work well, and take a repeated problem up the chain with facts (Work well with other people, Manage difficult interactions with other people: discuss, negotiate, and resolve difficult interactions in a sensitive and helpful manner)
  • handle disagreement as a lead hand and admit when someone else was right (Reflect and improve on teamwork)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. What is the difference between disagreeing with a task and refusing unsafe work?
  2. Who would you go to if your lead hand did not fix a problem?

Post-assessment (5 min, step 3.1)

  1. Your lead hand tells you to skip the lockout "just this once." Is that a work disagreement? What do you do?
  2. Give a W.I.N. statement for a disagreement in your trade.
  3. What does "disagree and commit" look like an hour later?

Summary (3 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • first asking, "Is it a safety issue?", and the duties in sections 3.10 and 3.12
  • six steps for a work disagreement: pick the moment, say it once with facts, ask and listen, let the lead hand decide, disagree and commit, bring facts if it keeps happening
  • going up the chain one step at a time
  • disagreeing as a lead hand and admitting when someone else was right

Across the first twelve Collaboration sessions we covered doing your share, working styles, respecting differences, checking assumptions, resolving conflict, building as a team, learning from a journeyperson, respectful workplaces, who does what on a site, working around other trades, huddles and handovers, and disagreeing well.

Next session: who does what on a task, using a RACI chart.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. It is a level 3 practice session. Lessons 13 to 15 extend the series with task roles, project delivery and crew ground rules.
  • Make the safety line very clear. Learners must never take "disagree and commit" to mean doing unsafe work. If a learner describes being pressured into unsafe work, point to Communication lesson 4 and WorkSafeBC.
  • Some learners come from families or communities where questioning an older person is not done. Respect that. Saying it once, in private, with facts is a way to speak up that many people find respectful. Nobody has to act out a conflict they find uncomfortable in Task 9. They can observe instead.
  • "Disagree and commit" is a common workplace phrase, not a legal or official term.
