# Disagree with a lead hand and still get the work done

## Task 1 (level 2)

## Task 2 (level 2)

- **b) Roof edge:** Do not do the task. Report it right away to your supervisor or employer (section 3.12). It is also an unsafe condition you must report as soon as possible (section 3.10).
- **d) Grinder without a guard:** the same. Refuse the task and report it right away (section 3.12).

## Task 3 (level 2)

Sample: "When we set the fixtures before the drain is roughed in, I think we'll have to pull them to fit the drain. I'd like to rough in the drain first. Can we talk about the order?" A good answer drops "dumb" and "everyone knows," states what the learner sees, the effect and the idea.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Samples: "Can you walk me through why we're starting here?", "Is there something in the drawings or schedule that sets this order?", "What should I watch for doing it this way?"

## Task 5 (level 3)

Sample sentence: "Okay, your way. I'll get started." Two things you will not do: work slowly or badly to prove a point, complain about it to the crew, say "I told you so" later, keep re-arguing it during the day.

## Task 6 (level 3)

**a)** The lead hand first, in private, with facts ("I haven't been on layout in three weeks. I'd like a turn."). Next, the foreman.
**b)** The lead hand or foreman. If it is not fixed, the superintendent, who runs the site and the facilities for the prime contractor. Washrooms can also be raised with the health and safety representative or joint committee.
**c)** This is unsafe work. Refuse it and report it to your supervisor (section 3.12). If it is still not resolved, the supervisor investigates again with you and a worker representative, then WorkSafeBC. You can also raise it with your joint health and safety committee or representative.

## Task 7 (level 3)

Bring: the dates of each mistake, photos, the drawing references, and the hours spent rebuilding.

Sample opening: "Can I show you something? We've torn out and rebuilt the same kind of wall three times this month: here are the dates and photos." A good answer is private, uses facts and does not name or blame a person.

## Task 8 (level 3)

**a)** Sample: "Good question. Let's finish this lift, then talk it through at the gang box." Calm, no counter-attack, sets a time.
**b)** Thank them for speaking up, ask for their reasons, and explain yours. Then ask that next time they raise it in private first, unless it is about safety.
**c)** Sample: "Jess spotted a better way. We're switching to it. Thanks, Jess." A good lead hand admits it openly. That builds the trust that makes people speak up.

## Task 9 (level 3)

**Open task.** Use the observer checklist. A strong role play shows every box ticked. If the disagreement turns out to be about safety, the worker names the rule instead of "committing."
