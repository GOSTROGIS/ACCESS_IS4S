Doing your share on a crew

Know your role, know who depends on you, and build trust by doing what you said you would do.

  • Level: 1-2

  • Components: Work well with other people; Achieve a common goal with others

Collaboration lesson 1 of 8 · 60 min · Level 1-2

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners. This is the first of eight Collaboration sessions, and it runs 60 minutes.

Hook: write on the board "Is a totem pole made by one person?" Let learners answer in a word or two, then leave the question up. Tell them you will come back to it with one real pole project. Add a second hook from the trades: "Has a job ever stopped because one person was missing or late?"

Objective (1 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • name their role in a crew task, who depends on them and whom they depend on (Achieve a common goal with others)
  • pick out actions that build or break trust on a crew (Work well with other people)
  • tell a lead early, in a clear message, when their part will be late (Work well with other people, Achieve a common goal with others)
  • name one strength and one area where they want help (Work well with other people)

Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.3)

Ask learners to answer on paper or out loud:

  1. What does "doing your share" on a crew mean to you?
  2. If you will be late for a shift, who do you tell, and when?

Note who already names "tell the lead early". You can pair them with learners who do not.

Post-assessment (5 min, step 3.1)

  1. What three questions help you know your share of a crew task?
  2. Give one example of telling a lead early. What should the message include?
  3. What did the Legacy Pole example show about roles in a big job?

Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • the Skills for Success definition of collaboration and the 2021 change from Working with Others
  • the three role questions: my job, who waits on me, whom I wait on
  • everyday actions that build trust on a crew
  • how to tell a lead early when your part will be late
  • one Haida pole project that needed many people working in order

Next session: working styles, and how to work with people whose style is different from yours.

Notes for the instructor

  • This lesson replaces the 2011 Working with Others plan, which opened with the totem pole question and a documentary. The documentary and the egg build are dropped. The team build activity is now lesson 6.
  • If a learner offers a cultural example, thank them and do not ask them to speak for their Nation or for Indigenous people in general.
  • Task 4 asks for a personal strength. Accept a job-related answer. No one has to share anything about family or community.
