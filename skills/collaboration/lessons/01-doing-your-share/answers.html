Doing your share on a crew

Know your role, know who depends on you, and build trust by doing what you said you would do.

Task 1 (level 1)

Answer

  • Dana: Priya is waiting on Dana's laid-out plates.
  • Marcus: Priya is waiting on Marcus's cut studs.
  • Priya: nobody in the table is waiting on Priya yet. The wall itself is what the next trade or the next step waits on.

Steps: read the "Needs before they can start" column. Find each person's name in that column. The person in that row is the one waiting on them.

Task 2 (level 1)

Builds trust: a, c, e, g

Breaks trust: b, d, f, h

Steps: ask of each action, "Does this help the next person do their job, or make them wait, guess or clean up after me?"

  • e builds trust because asking once is faster and safer than guessing.
  • h breaks trust even though the person is on time: without boots they can't start work, so the crew waits or the lead has to sort it out.

Task 3 (level 2)

What a good text covers

  • what is late: the cut list
  • when it will be done: about 12:30, or after lunch
  • what the crew can do meanwhile: a part that is ready, or a suggestion for Priya and Tomas
  • it is sent now, at 11:15, not at 12:00

Example: "Dana, cut list won't be done by 12. Need about 30 more min, so done by 12:30. Studs for the north wall are cut and stacked by the saw, Priya can start on those."

Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good response:

  • names one real strength, such as "I'm careful with measurements" or "I keep the work area clean"
  • names one task where the learner wants help the first time
  • gives the actual words, for example "I haven't run this saw before. Can you show me once and then watch me do one?"

Any honest, job-related answer is fine. Learners do not have to share personal or community information.
