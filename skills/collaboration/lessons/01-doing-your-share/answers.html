Doing your share on a crew

Know your role, know who depends on you, and build trust by doing what you said you would do.

Task 1 (level 1)

Answer

  • Dana: Priya is waiting on Dana's laid-out plates.
  • Marcus: Priya is waiting on Marcus's cut studs.
  • Priya: nobody in the table is waiting on Priya yet. The wall itself is what the next trade or the next step waits on.

Steps: read the "Needs before they can start" column. Find each person's name in that column. The person in that row is the one waiting on them.

Task 2 (level 1)

Builds trust: a, c, e, g

Breaks trust: b, d, f, h

Steps: ask of each action, "Does this help the next person do their job, or make them wait, guess or clean up after me?"

  • e builds trust because asking once is faster and safer than guessing.
  • h breaks trust even though the person is on time: without boots they can't start work, so the crew waits or the lead has to sort it out.

Task 3 (level 2)

What a good text covers

  • what is late: the cut list
  • when it will be done: about 13:00, allowing for the 12:00–12:30 lunch break
  • what the crew can do meanwhile: ask Dana to confirm another suitable task; do not claim materials are ready without checking
  • it is sent now, at 11:15, not at 12:00

Example: "Dana, the cut list will be late. I need 30 minutes beyond noon plus our 12:00–12:30 lunch break, so I expect to finish at 13:00. Can you confirm another suitable task for Priya and Tomas while they wait?"

Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. A good response:

  • names one real strength, such as "I'm careful with measurements" or "I keep the work area clean"
  • names one task where the learner wants help the first time
  • gives the actual words, for example "I haven't run this saw before. Can you show me once and then watch me do one?"

Any honest, job-related answer is fine. Learners do not have to share personal or community information.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) Dana's laid-out plates and Marcus's cut studs. b) Dana, who checks the wall, and the whole crew, who stand it up. c) Marcus, Priya (nothing to nail) and Dana (stops her layout to look for the cut list). Tomas may also wait if the crew needs him to cut. d) Open. A good chart has the learner in the middle, at least one box above (work they wait on) and one box below (work that waits on them), joined by arrows.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Tying the rebar on the north footing. The pump truck arrives at 7:30 and Jess arrives at 7:40. If the rebar is not tied, the pour waits and the truck sits. b) Ray, or the lead hand. Ray is fast at tying rebar, so the footing is most likely to be ready by 7:30. A good answer gives a reason based on strengths and the deadline. c) Moving material: chairs, bar and tie wire to Ray, and clearing the pump truck's path. Ali has never tied rebar, so 7:30 is not the time to learn it alone. d) Sample: "Thanks for the heads-up. Ray is starting your footing. When you get here, check his ties on the north side, then help with the pour." A good text thanks Jess for telling you early, says who is covering and gives Jess a clear job on arrival.

Check yourself 3

Any three of: show up on time with your gear, do what you said you would do, say early if you can't, ask instead of guessing, leave your work ready for the next person, put tools back, ask the lead what is next.

Check yourself 4

Tell the lead right away, at 9:00, not at 10:00. Say what is late, when it will be done, and anything the crew can do in the meantime.
