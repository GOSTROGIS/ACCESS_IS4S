# Who does what on a job site

## Task 1 (level 1)

**a)** Journeyperson. **b)** Superintendent. **c)** Owner. **d)** Inspector. **e)** Foreman.

## Task 2 (level 1)

**a)** The sub foreman or lead hand, who answers to the superintendent.
**b)** Off to the side, not connected to the crews. They check the work against the code or permit. They do not direct crews.
**c)** The owner.

## Task 3 (level 1)

**a)** Your sponsor or employer. **b)** Your journeyperson. **c)** Your supervisor, right away. It is an unsafe condition. **d)** Your lead hand or foreman.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**a)** Workers of two or more employers working at the site at the same time.
**b)** The owner.
**c)** The name of the person the employer has designated to supervise its workers at that site.

## Task 5 (level 2)

Tell your own foreman or lead hand. Your foreman talks to the drywall foreman or the superintendent. A good answer keeps it company to company through the foremen, not apprentice to drywaller.

## Task 6 (level 2)

**Open task.** A good chart has at least five roles, shows who answers to whom with lines or arrows, keeps inspectors apart from the crews and circles the learner.

## Task 7 (level 2)

Sample: "Who is my supervisor on this site, and who do I go to when they're not here?"

## Task 8 (level 3)

- **Lumber pile:** Say yes if it is safe and you agree, and show him where it can go. Or move it with your crew. Tell your foreman at 1:00 so the two foremen know. A good answer does not let another company's worker move your material without you knowing where it went.
- **Wobbling stairs:** An unsafe condition. Stop people using them (block or tag them if you can), and report it right away to your supervisor or the employer. With the foreman off site, call or text him and tell the superintendent, who runs the site for the prime contractor. Do not wait until 1:00.
- **Inspector:** Show the inspector where unit 204 is, be polite, and answer only what you know. Tell your foreman, by phone or text, that the inspector came and what they said.

## Task 9 (level 3)

Sample: "Hey, when you need a tool, come to me first, or the foreman. The super runs the whole site for every company, so if every apprentice goes to her, she can't do her job. I'd rather you ask me ten times than guess. Safety is the one thing you can take to anyone, right away." A good answer is private and friendly, gives the reason for the line and makes clear the apprentice should still ask questions.

## Check yourself 1

The superintendent.

## Check yourself 2

A workplace where workers of two or more employers are working at the same time.

## Check yourself 3

The prime contractor. That is usually the GC under a written agreement with the owner, or the owner if there is no agreement.

## Check yourself 4

Your supervisor or the employer, as soon as possible (OHS Regulation section 3.10).

## Check yourself 5

They work for the city, Technical Safety BC or another authority, not for the contractor. Their job is to check the work against the code or permit. Your foreman deals with them and directs the crew.
