Who does what on a job site

Know the people on a building site, from the owner to the apprentice, who you ask for what, and how BC law makes one party coordinate safety for everyone.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Work well with other people; Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Achieve a common goal with others

Why this matters on the job

On your first day on a big site you may meet 40 people from six companies. Some can tell you what to do. Some cannot. If you ask the wrong person, you lose time, or you get an answer that is not theirs to give. If you know who does what, you can find the right person fast, you know whose plan you are following, and you know who to tell when something is unsafe.

Key ideas

The owner pays for the building. The owner might be a person, a company, a First Nation or a school district. On most jobs the owner hires a general contractor to build it.

The general contractor (GC) runs the whole job for the owner. The GC hires subcontractors (subs) for parts of the work, such as plumbing, electrical, drywall or roofing.

The prime contractor is a BC legal role. When workers of two or more employers work at a site at the same time, it is a multiple-employer workplace. The prime contractor must make sure everyone's health and safety work is coordinated. The prime contractor is the contractor named in a written agreement with the owner. With no written agreement, the owner is the prime contractor. On most building sites, the GC is the prime contractor.

Each employer on a multiple-employer site must give the prime contractor the name of the person who supervises its workers there.

On the site, from the top down:

Role What they do Ask them about
Superintendent (super) Runs the whole site for the GC. Plans the order of work across trades. Which trade works where and when, site rules, access
Foreman Runs one company's crew or crews on the site. Your crew's plan for the week, hours, problems with another trade
Lead hand Leads a small crew day to day. Often a journeyperson. Today's task, tools, where to start
Journeyperson A certified tradesperson. Supervises and teaches apprentices. How to do the work right
Apprentice Learning the trade on the job, under a journeyperson. Ask up, help the next new person
Labourer Moves material, cleans up, helps the trades. Where things are on site
Inspector Checks that the work meets the code or permit. Works for a city, Technical Safety BC or another authority. Does not direct crews. Nothing directly. Your foreman deals with inspectors.

Job titles change from company to company. Some sites have a project manager in the office, a site safety coordinator or an engineer. Ask on your first day: "Who is my supervisor here, and who do I go to when they are not around?"

Your supervisor is the person named to supervise you. On most crews that is your foreman or lead hand. Unsafe conditions get reported to a supervisor or the employer as soon as possible. That is a rule in BC's Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, section 3.10.

The chart shows how the roles connect on a typical site.

Ask the right person. Most questions go to the closest person who can answer them. Go up one step when they cannot. Safety goes to your supervisor right away, every time.

Worked example: whose question is it?

Riverside Commons is a fictional three-storey building. The owner is Riverside Housing Society (fictional). Tallgrass Builders (fictional) is the GC and prime contractor. Maria is the superintendent. Coast Mechanical (fictional) has the plumbing sub. Its foreman is Dev. Jordan is a first-year plumbing apprentice working with Lou, a journeyperson.

Jordan has four questions on Monday morning.

Jordan's question Who Jordan asks Why
"How do I support this pipe run?" Lou, the journeyperson It is about how to do the work.
"Can I use the GC's scissor lift?" Dev, the foreman Shared equipment is arranged between companies. Dev asks Maria.
"The guardrail on level 3 is missing." Dev, right away, in person Unsafe condition. Section 3.10 says report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or employer.
"Is the inspector coming today?" Dev Inspections are booked and handled by the foreman and the GC.

Jordan did not go to Maria for every question. Maria runs the whole site. Dev speaks for Coast Mechanical. That keeps one clear line between the two companies.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Match the role. Write the role from the table.

a) Certified in the trade. Supervises and teaches apprentices. __

b) Runs the whole site for the general contractor. __

c) Pays for the building. __

d) Checks work against the code, but does not direct crews. __

e) Runs one company's crew or crews on the site. __

Task 2 (level 1) Read the chart. Use the organization chart.

a) Who does the plumbing subcontractor's crew answer to on site?

b) Where do inspectors sit on the chart, and why are they drawn apart?

c) Who hires the general contractor?

Task 3 (level 1) Ask first. Who do you ask first? Use the "Ask first" chart.

a) Your hours are missing in the SkilledTradesBC Portal. __

b) You don't know how to set the depth on the rotary hammer. __

c) The stair handrail is loose. __

d) You need to know which room to start in today. __

Task 4 (level 2) Prime contractor. Answer from the Key ideas.

a) What makes a site a multiple-employer workplace?

b) Who is the prime contractor if there is no written agreement with the owner?

c) What must each employer give the prime contractor?

Task 5 (level 2) Who speaks for whom? You are an apprentice with an electrical sub. The drywall crew keeps boarding over your boxes before you finish. Who do you tell, and who should talk to the drywall company?

Task 6 (level 2) Draw your site. Draw an organization chart for a job you have worked on or seen, or for the Riverside Commons example. Include at least five roles. Circle yourself.

Task 7 (level 2) First-day question. Write the question you ask on your first morning to find out who your supervisor is and who to go to when they are away.

Task 8 (level 3) You are the lead hand. You lead a four-person framing crew for Tallgrass Builders. Your foreman is off site until 1:00. At 9:30:

  • The electrical sub's apprentice asks you if he can move your lumber pile to get to a panel.
  • One of your crew says the temporary stairs to level 2 are wobbling.
  • A city building inspector asks where the framing for unit 204 is.

For each one, write what you do and who you tell.

Task 9 (level 3) The shortcut. A new apprentice on your crew goes straight to the superintendent every time he needs a tool. The super tells you, "Your guy keeps coming to me." Write what you say to the apprentice. Explain why the line matters without making him feel stupid.

Check yourself

  1. Name the role that runs the whole site for the general contractor.
  2. What is a multiple-employer workplace in BC?
  3. Who must coordinate health and safety on a multiple-employer workplace?
  4. Who do you tell first about an unsafe condition, and how soon?
  5. Why do inspectors not give orders to your crew?
