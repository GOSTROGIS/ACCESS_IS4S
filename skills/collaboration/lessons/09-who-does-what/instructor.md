# Who does what on a job site

**Collaboration lesson 9 of 15 · 75 min · Level 1-3**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 9 of 15. This session runs 75 minutes. Lessons 9 to 12 take the Collaboration skills onto a real site with other trades.

Hook: "You walk onto a site on day one. There are 40 people in hard hats. Who do you ask where to park?" Take a few answers. Then ask, "Who do you tell if a guardrail is missing?" Point out that the two answers are different people, and that today is about knowing which is which.

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name the main roles on a building site and what each one does (Work well with other people)
- explain the BC prime contractor role on a multiple-employer workplace (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others)
- choose the right person for a question and report unsafe conditions to their supervisor (Achieve a common goal with others: consult and share with others when needed and appropriate)
- act as a lead hand who keeps the line between companies clear (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

1. Name every job title you can think of on a construction site.
2. On a site you know, who would you tell about a safety problem?

## Post-assessment (4 min, step 3.1)

1. Who is usually the prime contractor on a building site, and what must they do?
2. A wire is hanging loose at head height. Who do you tell, and when?
3. Why does an apprentice usually go through their own foreman, not the superintendent, for shared equipment?

## Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- the owner, general contractor, subcontractors and the site roles from superintendent to apprentice
- the BC prime contractor and multiple-employer workplaces
- asking the closest person who can answer, and going up one step when they cannot
- reporting unsafe conditions to a supervisor as soon as possible
- the lead hand's job of keeping one clear line between companies

Next session: working around other trades.

## Notes for the instructor

- This is a new lesson. There was no ESAF lesson on site roles. It answers the gap named in the 2026-11-08 skills audit.
- Job titles vary. Some companies use "foreman" for what others call "lead hand." Ask learners what titles they have heard and add them to the board. The point is the line of who answers to whom.
- Do not teach prime contractor duties beyond what is in the lesson. If a learner works as a supervisor, send them to WorkSafeBC's information sheet.
- If a learner has been told to report safety concerns to someone other than their supervisor, that is fine. The rule says a supervisor or the employer.
