A new crew's rough start with ground rules and conflict styles

Expect the rough patch when a crew forms, write ground rules people can follow, pick the conflict style that fits, and settle a fight about site paperwork before it costs money.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Manage difficult interactions with other people; Work well with other people; Reflect and improve on teamwork

Task 1 (level 1)

a) Adjourning. b) Storming. c) Norming. d) Forming. e) Performing.

Task 2 (level 2)

Sample rewrites. Accept any rule you could check.

a) "No name-calling and no jokes about anyone's culture, body or family. If it happens, the lead hand deals with it that day." b) "Raise a problem at the 7:00 huddle or tell the lead hand the same day. Don't save it up." c) "Sign tools out and back in on the gang box sheet. Report a damaged tool to the lead hand before the end of the shift."

Task 3 (level 2)

Open task. A good list has five rules or fewer, each one specific enough to check (a time, a place, an action), written in plain words. None of them weakens a safety rule or the company's bullying and harassment policy. Rules about the work are fine. Rules that police someone's culture, language or family duties are not.

Task 4 (level 2)

a) Put it off or give in. It is small. Take turns or let it go. b) Push until you win. It is a safety rule, so it is not up for a deal. (Lesson 12: safety issues are not work disagreements.) c) Meet halfway. Both need the lift equally, there is one lift, and the week starts tomorrow. For example, one crew has it mornings and the other afternoons. d) Give in. Both ways work and it matters more to him. You are learning his way. e) Work it out together. Both care a lot, they will work together for four months, and there is time to sit down.

Accept a different habit if the reason fits the "fits when" column.

Task 5 (level 2)

Sample:

  • "Just make them do it" pushes until Northgate wins. Dale's crew might send photos, but they will drag their feet and the relationship will get worse for four more months. It does not fix why the photos were incomplete.
  • "Just ignore her" puts it off. The claims stay held, Silverline does not get paid, and the plumbing still gets covered without proof.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) $18,400 + $22,600 = $41,000. b) 4 × $20,000 = about $80,000 more. c) Any one: the two people barely speak, the crew picks up the "busywork" attitude, trust between the companies drops, the inspection risk if pipe is covered without proof, stress, time spent arguing.

Task 7 (level 3)

Open task. The observer's notes should show:

  • one W.I.N. statement from each person about the work, not the person (for example, Dale: "When the photos get sent back at month end, I lose a whole month's pay on that work. I need to hear about gaps the same day.")
  • one open question from each (for example, Priya: "What would make the photos quick for your lead hand?")
  • one agreed change that keeps the proof Northgate needs and is quick for Silverline's crew

Task 8 (level 3)

Open task. A good answer names one stage, gives a sign that fits it ("we argue every morning about who cleans up" fits storming), and gives one specific suggestion, such as a written clean-up rule or a five-minute check-in.

Check yourself 1

Forming, storming, norming, performing.

Check yourself 2

It is specific enough to check, the list is short, the crew agreed to it, and it is written where people can see it.

Check yourself 3

Push until you win. Safety rules are not up for a deal.

Check yourself 4

Work it out together.

Check yourself 5

The process: how much paperwork, and in what form, before the plumbing was covered. It was not about the quality of the pipe work.
