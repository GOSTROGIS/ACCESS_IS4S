# A new crew's rough start with ground rules and conflict styles

All people, companies and jobs in this lesson are fictional.

## Why this matters on the job

Every new job puts people together who have not worked side by side before. The first week is often polite. Then the friction starts: who sets the pace, how clean is clean, whose way of doing it wins. That rough patch is normal. Crews that get through it fast have a few clear ground rules and know more than one way to handle a disagreement. Crews that do not get through it lose time, lose people and sometimes lose money, as the paperwork fight in this lesson shows.

## Key ideas

### Crews go through stages

In 1965 a researcher named Bruce Tuckman reviewed 50 studies of small groups. He described four stages that many groups go through. In 1977 he and Mary Ann Jensen added a fifth.

| Stage | What it looks like on a crew |
|---|---|
| **Forming** | Everyone is polite and careful. People wait to be told what to do and test where the lines are. |
| **Storming** | Friction. People push back on the plan, the pace or each other. Some take sides. |
| **Norming** | The crew settles on how it works: who does what, how fast, how problems get raised. |
| **Performing** | The work flows. People cover for each other and fix problems without drama. |
| **Adjourning** | The job ends and the crew splits up. |

This is a model, not a rule. Some crews skip a stage or slide back when someone new joins. The point is: **storming is normal**. It does not mean the crew is broken. It means the crew has not agreed how it works yet.

### Ground rules get a crew to norming faster

Ground rules are a short list the crew agrees on. Good ground rules are:

- **specific:** you can tell if someone followed them
- **few:** five is plenty
- **agreed:** the crew talks them through, not just the lead hand
- **visible:** written on the gang box lid, the lunchroom wall or the group chat

| Weak rule | Better rule |
|---|---|
| Be on time. | Be at the gang box, dressed and ready, by 7:00. |
| Keep it clean. | Last 15 minutes of the shift, everyone cleans their own area and puts tools back in the gang box. |

Ground rules never replace the law or the company's policies. Safety rules and the bullying and harassment policy (lesson 8) apply whether the crew writes them down or not.

### Five ways to handle conflict, and when each fits

Lesson 5 showed five habits people use in a conflict. None of them is always right. Pick the one that fits.

| Habit | Fits when | Does not fit when |
|---|---|---|
| **Put it off** (avoid) | The issue is small, or people need to cool down before they talk. | It is about safety, or it keeps coming back. |
| **Give in** (accommodate) | It matters more to them than to you, or you find out you were wrong. | You give in every time and start to resent it. |
| **Meet halfway** (compromise) | Both sides have a fair point, time is short and you have about equal say. | Splitting the difference gives a half-done job. |
| **Push until you win** (compete) | Safety, the law, or an emergency where someone has to decide now. | It is just a preference. It damages trust. |
| **Work it out together** (collaborate) | Both sides care a lot, you will work together for a long time, and there is time to talk. | The issue is tiny or there is no time. |

## Worked example: when paperwork causes a fight

Northgate Construction is the GC on a four-storey rental building. Silverline Mechanical has the plumbing subcontract. Dale is Silverline's foreman. Priya is Northgate's site coordinator. She is newer to the trade than Dale.

**The rule on this job.** Northgate's quality plan says plumbing in a wall is a **hold point**. Before drywall covers it, the plumbing crew must take one photo of each wall section with a tag showing the unit and wall, and the lead hand must sign a short checklist. Northgate counts the work as complete on a monthly progress claim only when the photos and the signed checklist are in.

**What happened.** For two months Dale's crew sent photos with no tags and checklists with no signature. Priya sent the work back. Silverline's progress claims for those two months, $18,400 and $22,600, were held. Dale told his crew, "It's busywork. She's micromanaging us." Priya told her boss, "Silverline doesn't care about quality." Now they barely speak, and there are four more months on the job.

**What kind of conflict is it?** It started as a disagreement about **the process** (how much paperwork, in what form), not about the quality of the pipe work. It is sliding into a **personal** conflict ("she's micromanaging", "they don't care"). Bring it back to the process.

**What does each side need?**

| Dale and Silverline need | Priya and Northgate need |
|---|---|
| To get paid on time | Proof the pipe is right before it is hidden |
| A routine that does not slow the crew down | Photos that show which unit and which wall |
| To be treated as the experienced trade he is | Checklists signed by someone who answers for them |

**Which habit fits?** Both care a lot, they have four more months together, and there is time to sit down. That points to **work it out together**.

**What they agreed.** Dale and Priya met with their bosses for 20 minutes. They agreed that Silverline's lead hand photographs each wall with a pre-printed tag before he leaves the unit, uploads the photos to the shared folder by the end of the shift, and signs one checklist per unit instead of one per wall. Priya checks the folder each morning and flags gaps the same day, not at month end. Dale's held claims were paid once the missing photos for the two months were in.
