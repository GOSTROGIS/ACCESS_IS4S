A new crew's rough start with ground rules and conflict styles

Expect the rough patch when a crew forms, write ground rules people can follow, pick the conflict style that fits, and settle a fight about site paperwork before it costs money.

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

Opening (4 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 15 of 15. This session runs 75 minutes. It builds on lesson 5 (resolving conflict), lesson 8 (respectful workplaces) and lesson 12 (disagreeing with a lead hand).

Hook: "Think of the first week of any new crew, team or class you joined. How did people act on day one? How did they act in week three?" Take two or three answers. Draw a line on the board from "polite" to "friction" to "settled". Today is about getting through the friction faster, and about one fight that cost a company $41,000 in held payments.

Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • name the stages a crew goes through and expect storming as normal (Reflect and improve on teamwork: reflect on team performance)
  • write specific ground rules a crew can follow and check (Work well with other people: encourage supportive and cooperative behaviours, language, attitudes, and approaches)
  • pick the conflict habit that fits a situation and say why (Manage difficult interactions with other people: anticipate and address interpersonal barriers)
  • work through a site paperwork conflict by naming what each side needs and agreeing a change (Manage difficult interactions with other people: discuss, negotiate, and resolve difficult interactions in a sensitive and helpful manner)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.3)

  1. Name the five conflict habits from lesson 5.
  2. What is one rule your class crew follows that nobody ever wrote down?

Post-assessment (5 min, step 3.1)

  1. A crew is arguing about the pace in week two. What stage is it in, and is that a bad sign?
  2. Rewrite "Be safe" as a ground rule you could check.
  3. When does "push until you win" fit, and when does it not?

Summary (4 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

  • forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning, and why storming is normal
  • ground rules that are specific, few, agreed and visible
  • the five conflict habits and when each one fits
  • a paperwork conflict on site: process versus personal, what each side needs, and agreeing a change

Across the fifteen Collaboration sessions we covered doing your share, working styles, respecting differences, checking assumptions, resolving conflict, building as a team, learning from a journeyperson, respectful workplaces, who does what on a site, working around other trades, huddles and handovers, disagreeing well, RACI charts, how the job was bought, and getting a new crew through its rough start.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. The Silverline case is fictional. It is based on a common kind of site conflict, retold with invented people, companies and amounts.
  • Tuckman's stages come from a 1965 review of research on small groups, with a fifth stage added in 1977. Present them as a useful model, not a law of nature. Some learners will say their crews never "stormed". That is fine.
  • Do not let "ground rules" become a way to police people's culture, language or family duties. If a learner suggests a rule like that, ask the group whether it is about the work or about the person.
  • Keep the safety line from lesson 12: no habit means giving in on safety. "Push until you win" in Task 4 b) is about holding the safety rule, not about winning an argument.
  • Some learners find role-play uncomfortable. In Task 7 they can be the observer the whole time.
  • Hold points, photo routines and when a GC counts work as complete vary by job and by contract. In the case they are the rules of that fictional job.
