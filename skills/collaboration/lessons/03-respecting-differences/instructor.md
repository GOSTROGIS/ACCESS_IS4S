# Respecting differences on a job site

**Collaboration lesson 3 of 15 · 100 min · Level 1-3**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 3 of 15. This session runs 100 minutes.

Hook: "How many languages can the people in this room speak, or speak a few words of? Count them with me." Write the count on the board. Include English, French, sign languages, Indigenous languages, family languages, and a few words learned on a job site. Then ask: "On a crew, is that a problem or an advantage?"

## Objective (2 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- explain culture in their own words and name groups they belong to (Value diversity and inclusivity of others)
- spot crew habits that include or leave out people (Value diversity and inclusivity of others, Work well with other people)
- respond without judging in common job-site situations (Value diversity and inclusivity of others: respond without judging people for their different opinions, ideas and views)
- name the grounds protected in employment under the BC *Human Rights Code* and explain the duty to accommodate (Value diversity and inclusivity of others)

## Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.3)

1. What is one thing about how you were raised that a co-worker might not know or expect?
2. Have you ever been on a crew or team where someone was left out? What was going on?

Learners can answer on paper and keep it private.

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 3.1)

1. Give one example of crew culture that helps everyone and one that leaves people out.
2. What is a respectful way to answer someone who asks you to speak for your whole culture?
3. Name three grounds protected in employment under the BC *Human Rights Code*.

## Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- what culture is, and that everyone belongs to more than one group
- crew culture and unwritten rules that include or leave out people
- First Nations, Inuit and Métis as distinct peoples, and the 204 First Nations in BC
- responding without judging in common job-site situations
- the BC *Human Rights Code* grounds in employment and the duty to accommodate

Next session: checking assumptions before you act.

## Notes for the instructor

- This lesson rebuilds the ACCESS Defining Culture lesson. Tiny talents and the alike-and-different exercise are kept. The definition is reworded in plain language. The exercises on naming cultural groups and labels are replaced by crew culture and the Human Rights Code.
- Learners decide how much to share about their own family, Nation or faith. Never call on a learner to explain their culture to the class.
- If a learner shares an experience of discrimination, thank them, do not ask for details in front of the group, and offer to talk after class. Lesson 8 covers reporting.
- Task 5 is about what the law protects. Some unfair treatment (case e) is not a human rights matter but may still be a problem to raise at work.
