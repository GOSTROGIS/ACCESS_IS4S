# Respectful workplaces, bullying and harassment

**Collaboration lesson 8 of 8 · 60 min · Level 2**

Every minute of the session is in the Learning tasks table. The blocks below point to their step numbers.

## Opening (3 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to the last of the eight Collaboration sessions. This session runs 60 minutes.

Say at the start: this topic can bring up hard memories. Learners can step out at any time, and no one has to share a personal story. The support line is at the end of the learner page.

Hook: "On some crews, new apprentices get pranked or put through an 'initiation.' Where is the line between a joke and something that isn't okay?" Take a few answers without stories about real people.

## Objective (1 min, step 1.2)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- use WorkSafeBC's definition to tell bullying and harassment apart from reasonable direction and feedback (Manage difficult interactions with other people: anticipate and address interpersonal barriers)
- name the duties of employers, supervisors and workers (Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others: acknowledge roles of yourself and others)
- plan what to do as a bystander and write a factual report (Manage difficult interactions with other people, Work well with other people: encourage supportive and cooperative behaviours)
- find WorkSafeBC's reporting steps on its website

## Pre-assessment (3 min, step 1.3)

1. Is a foreman telling you to redo sloppy work a form of bullying? Why or why not?
2. If you saw a co-worker being picked on, who would you tell?

## Post-assessment (3 min, step 3.1)

1. Give one example of bullying and harassment and one example of reasonable management action.
2. Name the three duties of a worker.
3. Where do you report bullying and harassment first?

## Summary (2 min, step 3.2)

During this session we covered:

- WorkSafeBC's definition of bullying and harassment, and what it does not include
- the duties of employers, supervisors and workers
- what to do as a bystander
- how to report: employer first, then WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line, then the questionnaire
- the Builders Code and the link to the BC *Human Rights Code*

Across the eight Collaboration sessions we covered doing your share, working styles, respecting differences, checking assumptions, resolving conflict, building as a team, learning from a journeyperson and respectful workplaces.

## Notes for the instructor

- This is a new lesson. There was no ESAF lesson on bullying and harassment. The W.I.N. example about jokes on a person's race, in the old ACCESS conflict lesson, is handled here as harassment to report, not as a conflict to negotiate.
- Do not role-play hazing or harassment. Tasks 3 and 4 use a written case.
- If a learner discloses current bullying or harassment, thank them, talk privately after class, and help them find their employer's reporting procedure. Do not promise to keep a disclosure secret if someone is at risk of harm. Follow ACCESS's own policy.
- The Summit Steel case calls the "bucket of steam" a well-known gag. Do not spend time debating whether gags are funny. The teaching point is that singling someone out, physical acts and posting video cross the line.
- WorkSafeBC policy numbers changed when the *Workers Compensation Act* was renumbered on April 6, 2020. Older documents call the policies D3-115-2, D3-116-1 and D3-117-2. The current numbers are P2-21-2, P2-22-1 and P2-23-2.
