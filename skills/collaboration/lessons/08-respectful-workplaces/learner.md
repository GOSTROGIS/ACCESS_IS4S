# Respectful workplaces, bullying and harassment

## Why this matters on the job

Some crews still treat hazing and put-downs as part of "paying your dues." WorkSafeBC does not. It treats workplace bullying and harassment as a health and safety hazard, and its policies say it can lead to injury, illness or death. Every BC worker has duties here, including you. Knowing the rules helps you protect yourself, back up a co-worker and keep good people in the trade.

## Key ideas

**What it is.** WorkSafeBC defines bullying and harassment as any inappropriate conduct or comment by a person toward a worker that the person knew, or reasonably ought to have known, would cause that worker to be humiliated or intimidated. It does **not** include reasonable action by an employer or supervisor to manage and direct workers or the workplace.

The "person" can be anyone at work: a boss, a co-worker, a client or a member of the public.

| Can be bullying and harassment | Is not bullying and harassment |
|---|---|
| Verbal aggression or yelling | Expressing a different opinion |
| Humiliating initiation practices or hazing | Constructive feedback, guidance or advice about work |
| Spreading malicious rumours | Reasonable management action, such as managing your performance, reasonable discipline or assigning work |
| Calling someone derogatory names | |
| Any other conduct or comment that would make someone feel humiliated or intimidated | |

**Who must do what.** WorkSafeBC policy sets out duties for each person.

**Employers** must, among other things:
- have a policy statement that bullying and harassment is not acceptable or tolerated
- take steps to prevent it where possible, or otherwise minimize it
- have procedures for workers to report it, including what to do if the employer or supervisor is the one doing it
- have procedures for dealing with reports, including investigation
- tell workers about the policy, and train supervisors and workers
- review the policy and procedures every year
- not bully or harass, and follow their own policy

**Supervisors** must not bully or harass, and must follow the employer's policy and procedures.

**Workers** must:
- not bully or harass anyone at work
- report bullying and harassment they see or experience
- follow the employer's policy and procedures

**How to report.** WorkSafeBC lists these steps:

1. Report it to your employer as soon as possible, following your employer's procedures.
2. If your employer has no policy or procedures, or has not taken reasonable steps, call WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line at 604.276.3100 (1.888.621.7233 toll free) and speak with a prevention officer.
3. After that, you can send WorkSafeBC a Bullying and Harassment Questionnaire.

WorkSafeBC checks that employers have proper policies and deal with reports. It does not mediate disputes between people.

**Harassment linked to a protected ground**, such as Indigenous identity, race, sex or religion, can also be discrimination under the BC *Human Rights Code* (see lesson 3).

**Industry action.** The Builders Code, managed by the BC Construction Association, sets a code of conduct for construction sites. Companies sign a pledge for an "Acceptable Worksite" that is free from hazing, harassment, bullying and discrimination.

**If you are standing nearby,** you can: refuse to join in or laugh, say something short if it is safe ("Not cool, leave it"), check on the person afterward, and report it. Reporting what you see is one of your duties as a worker.

### Worked example: the "initiation"

Summit Steel (a fictional company). On Nico's first week, the crew sends him to the tool crib for a "bucket of steam." Everyone laughs, and so does Nico. The next day two crew members tape him to a column during lunch, film it and post the video in the crew group chat with "Welcome, rookie."

- The first joke is a well-known gag. Many people would shrug it off. But if Nico is singled out again and again, it can become humiliating.
- Taping a worker to a column and posting a video of it is a **humiliating initiation practice**, or hazing. It fits WorkSafeBC's list.
- Every worker who saw the video has a duty to report it. The employer must deal with the report using its procedures.
- What does not count: the next week the foreman tells Nico his bolt-up was sloppy and has him redo it. That is reasonable direction of work, as long as it is done reasonably.
