Respectful workplaces, bullying and harassment

Know what counts as workplace bullying and harassment in BC, what employers, supervisors and workers must do, and how to report it.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 70 min
  • Components: Manage difficult interactions with other people; Facilitate an environment where you can collaborate with others; Work well with other people

Task 1 (level 2)

Situation Answer Matches
a) Welds failed, redo them Not bullying and harassment Reasonable management action, constructive feedback about work
b) Yelling and name-calling every morning Likely bullying and harassment Verbal aggression or yelling, calling someone derogatory names
c) Two journeypersons disagree, then agree Not bullying and harassment Expressing a different opinion
d) Rumour about how she got hired Likely bullying and harassment Spreading malicious rumours
e) Apprentices taped to a post Likely bullying and harassment Humiliating initiation practices or hazing
f) Assigned to cleanup for the afternoon Not bullying and harassment Reasonable management action (assigning work)
g) Customer's repeated comments about her body Likely bullying and harassment Inappropriate comment that would humiliate or intimidate. The "person" can be a customer. It may also be sexual harassment under the Human Rights Code
h) Shown a safer way to hold the grinder Not bullying and harassment Guidance or advice about work

Steps: compare each situation with the "can be" and "is not" table. Ask: would a reasonable person know this would humiliate or intimidate the worker? Is it a reasonable part of directing the work?

Task 2 (level 2)

Duty Who holds it
a) Not bullying or harassing anyone Employer, supervisor and worker
b) Reporting bullying and harassment you see or experience Worker
c) Training workers and supervisors Employer
d) Reviewing the policy every year Employer
e) Following the employer's policy and procedures Employer, supervisor and worker
f) Having a way to report when the supervisor is the one doing it Employer

Task 3 (level 2)

Open task. A good plan, in order:

  1. Does not like, share, laugh at or add to the video.
  2. If safe, posts something short in the chat or says it in person, such as "Not cool. Take it down." Or chooses not to post and goes straight to reporting.
  3. Checks on Nico privately: "Are you okay? That wasn't fair to you."
  4. Reports it to the supervisor or the person named in the employer's procedure, as soon as possible, because reporting is a worker duty.
  5. Keeps a note of what they saw and when.

The plan should not include getting even with the crew members or confronting them in a way that could start a fight.

Task 4 (level 2)

What a good report covers: facts only, no labels or guesses about why people did it.

  • what happened: Nico was taped to a column during lunch, it was filmed, and the video was posted in the crew group chat with "Welcome, rookie"
  • when and where: the day and time, the location on site, the time of the post
  • who was involved: the people seen taping and filming, if known
  • who else saw it: people in the group chat or at lunch
  • what the writer has done: did not share the video, checked on Nico, is reporting now

Example: "On Tuesday at about 12:15, during lunch at the level 2 laydown area, two crew members taped Nico to a column. One filmed it. At 12:40 the video was posted in the crew group chat with the words 'Welcome, rookie.' About 12 people are in the chat. I did not share it. I spoke to Nico at 1:00. I'm reporting it under our bullying and harassment policy."

Task 5 (level 2)

a) Report it to your employer as soon as possible, following your employer's policies and procedures. b) 1.888.621.7233. The page also gives the number 604.276.3100. c) A Bullying and Harassment Questionnaire.

Steps: open the page, read the numbered steps in order.

Task 6 (level 2)

a) Report it to her employer as soon as possible, using the employer's procedures. b) Call WorkSafeBC's Prevention Information Line (604.276.3100 or 1.888.621.7233) and speak with a prevention officer. c) She can send WorkSafeBC a Bullying and Harassment Questionnaire.

Task 7 (level 3)

a) Yes, it could be. It is conduct toward a worker that the person reasonably ought to have known would humiliate or intimidate him. Hiding tools every day for a week, from a new worker, is not reasonable management of work. Calling it a joke does not change that. b) Not bully or harass, and follow the employer's policy and procedures. That includes reporting it and dealing with it the way the policy says. c) Sample: "The tools stop going missing today. It's not a joke to him, and it's costing us time every morning." A good answer is short, private if possible, names the behaviour and says it stops. d) Sample: "I've seen what's been happening with your tools. It stops now, and I'm reporting it the way our policy says. If anything like it happens again, tell me or the foreman." A good answer checks on the apprentice and tells him how to report.

Check yourself 1

Any inappropriate conduct or comment toward a worker that the person knew, or should have known, would humiliate or intimidate that worker. It does not include reasonable action by an employer or supervisor to manage and direct the work.

Check yourself 2

Any two of: expressing a different opinion, constructive feedback or advice about work, reasonable management action such as managing performance, reasonable discipline or assigning work.

Check yourself 3

Not bullying or harassing anyone, reporting bullying and harassment you see or experience, and following the employer's policy and procedures.

Check yourself 4

Report it to your employer as soon as possible, following your employer's procedures.

Check yourself 5

A code of conduct for construction sites, managed by the BC Construction Association. Companies sign a pledge for an "Acceptable Worksite" free from hazing, harassment, bullying and discrimination.
