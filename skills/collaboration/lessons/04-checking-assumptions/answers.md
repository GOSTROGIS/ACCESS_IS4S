# Checking assumptions before you act

## Task 1 (level 1)

| # | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | T | The pickup arrived at 6:40. |
| 2 | ? | The story says a person walked to the trailer. It does not say that person drove the pickup. |
| 3 | T | The person in the hard hat opened the lock box and took out a key. |
| 4 | ? | The story does not say which key it was. |
| 5 | T | The story says 7:05. |
| 6 | ? | The lift was by the east wall. The story does not say who moved it, or whether it was moved at all. |
| 7 | F | The door was open when the superintendent arrived. |
| 8 | ? | Nothing in the story says who the person is. |
| 9 | T | That is what the note said. |
| 10 | ? | The note says so, but the story does not say anyone checked. A note is a claim, not a test. |
| 11 | ? | The story does not give the superintendent's sex. |
| 12 | F | The story mentions at least two people: the person in the hard hat and the superintendent. |

**Steps:** for each statement, find the exact words in the story that prove it true or false. If you can't point to the words, mark ?.

**Common slips:** 2, 4, 6 and 10 feel true because the story seems to flow. 11 catches people who picture a man.

## Task 2 (level 1)

| Line | Answer |

| e | O |

| g | O |

**Steps:** ask, "Could a camera or a tape measure record this?" If yes, it is an observation. If it is about what someone thinks, feels or always does, it is an assumption.

## Task 3 (level 2)

**Open task.** A good group discussion:

- names more than one source of a belief (family, media, friends, past experience, culture)
- gives at least one reason people keep beliefs (they feel true, everyone around them agrees, they once had a bad experience, it saves thinking)
- names a real harm on a crew (someone is left out of training, a person is blamed unfairly, a safety concern is ignored because of who raised it, good workers leave)
- includes at least one person's feeling about being judged, if anyone chooses to share

There is no correct corner for any statement. The point is to notice how fast we react.

## Task 4 (level 2)

**What a good rewrite covers:** only what was seen, the times, no name or label, and a question to get the facts.

**Example:** "At 7:05 the trailer door was open and the scissor lift was by the east wall with a 'Battery low' note. Who used the lift this morning? Please let me know so we can check the battery and lock up."

## Task 5 (level 2)

**Private, open task.** A thoughtful plan:

- names a real bias, either favourable or unfavourable
- gives a likely source
- names a concrete effect at work (who gets asked for help, who gets trusted with a task, who gets blamed)
- names one checking action, such as "ask before I decide," "give the new person the same task I'd give anyone," or "notice when I'm quick to blame"

Do not mark this task for content. The learner keeps it.

## Check yourself 1

An observation is something you can see, hear or measure. An assumption is what you think it means, filling a gap in what you know.
