# Weigh your options and make a decision

## Why this matters on the job

Every day on a BC site someone has to decide: rent the lift or carry it up, take the job offer now or wait for the apprenticeship, pour today or wait out the rain. A quick decision can be the right one, but a decision made without looking at the options often costs more later. This lesson gives you a way to decide that you can explain to a supervisor, a crew or your family.

## Key ideas

### Decision styles

Everyone has a usual way of deciding. None is always wrong, but each has a cost. These styles come from the ACCESS lesson this one replaces.

| Style | What it looks like | Motto |
|---|---|---|
| Impulsive | Little thought, take the first option | "Don't look, just leap!" |
| Fatalistic | Let things decide for you | "It's out of my hands." |
| Compliant | Let someone else decide, offer no opinion | "Whatever you say." |
| Delaying | Put it off, drag it out | "What's the rush?" |
| Agonizing | Lost in the details, overwhelmed | "I'm so confused!" |
| Planning | Use a process to reach a good result | "Let's look at the facts and the consequences." |
| Intuitive | Go with a gut feeling | "It just feels right." |
| Paralysis | Accept the decision but cannot act | "I can't face it." |

Intuition built on years of experience can be fast and good. Compliance is right when the decision really belongs to your supervisor. The planning style is the one to reach for when the stakes are high.

### Steps to an effective decision

1. Define the decision to be made.
2. List the options you already know about.
3. Look for new options: ask others, check what worked before.
4. Sort what **must** happen from what you would **like** to happen.
5. Check each option against the musts first. An option that fails a must is out, however cheap or easy it is.
6. Compare the options that are left on pros and cons, short term and long term.
7. Choose, act, and follow up.

### If-then thinking

Write what you will do if something changes: "**If** the boom truck cancels by noon Thursday, **then** the crew carries the sheets up Thursday afternoon." If-then statements give you a backup before you need one.

### People value different things

Two careful people can look at the same facts and decide differently, because they put different weight on safety, money, time, loyalty or fairness. When you disagree, explain what you value and why. That usually gets further than arguing about who is right.

### Worked example

Riverbend Framing (fictional) must get 80 sheets of drywall to the third floor of a building by **Friday 7 a.m.** for the drywall crew.

- **Musts:** on the third floor by Friday 7 a.m., done safely, extra cost no more than $400.
- **Likes:** least crew time, least heavy carrying up stairs.

| Option | Facts (fictional) | Meets the musts? |
|---|---|---|
| A. Crew carries it up the stairs Thursday afternoon | 4 workers for about 3 hours, no extra cost, lots of carrying on temporary stairs | Yes, if done with safe lifting and two-person carries |
| B. Rent a material hoist Thursday | $350 rental. The rental company requires a trained operator. The site's only trained operator is off Thursday. | No. Nobody trained can run it on Thursday. |
| C. Supplier's boom truck places it through third-floor window openings | Thursday 2 p.m., $150 extra. Needs the openings clear and two people to receive. Must be booked by Wednesday noon. | Yes |
| D. Leave it on the ground for the drywall crew | No cost | No. It misses Friday 7 a.m. |

Options A and C are left. A costs 4 × 3 = 12 crew hours and a lot of carrying. C costs $150 and about 2 people for a short time. **Decision:** book the boom truck by Wednesday noon. **If** the supplier cancels by Thursday noon, **then** the crew carries the sheets up Thursday afternoon (option A).

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Past decisions. Write three decisions you have made in the last year. You do not have to share them. Beside each, write the letter or letters that describe how you made it.

- A. It felt right.
- B. I left the decision to someone else.
- C. I put off the decision.
- D. I did the first thing that came to mind.
- E. I took the safest choice.
- F. I waited until things got so bad I had to act.
- G. I left it up to fate.
- H. I decided what mattered most to me and acted on it.
- I. I did what I wanted without thinking about what would happen.

Do you see a pattern? Which style from the table is closest to yours?

**Task 2** (level 1) Match each saying to a decision style.

- a. "Flip a coin, whatever happens happens."
- b. "Let me just get through the weekend and I'll think about it."
- c. "I've read six reviews of every saw and I still can't pick one."
- d. "Let's write down what we need, then compare."
- e. "You're the foreman, your call."
- f. "Grab that one, it'll do."

**Task 3** (level 2) Jordan is finishing a trades prep program and has two offers (both fictional).

- **Offer 1:** labourer with Northline Civil, starts Monday, $26 an hour, no apprenticeship, 90-minute drive each way.
- **Offer 2:** apprentice carpenter with Cedar Flats Builders, starts in 6 weeks, $22 an hour, the employer will sign Jordan up as an apprentice, 20-minute drive each way.

Make a table with pros and cons of each offer, in the short term (next 3 months) and the long term (next 3 years). Then write one question Jordan should answer before choosing.

**Task 4** (level 2) The drywall plan in the worked example could still go wrong. Write three more if-then statements that would keep the job on track.

**Task 5** (level 3) Read the story, then rank the five people from **1** (most responsible for the lost load) to **5** (least responsible). Write one reason for each. Then compare with a group and try to agree on one ranking.

> **The rejected load** (fictional)
>
> A wall pour at a Lakeview Concrete Forming site was booked for 10:00 a.m. The evening before, superintendent **Dale** told crew lead **Priya** to "make sure the forms are checked first thing," then left to manage another site. He did not answer his phone the next morning.
>
> At 6:00 a.m. Priya asked apprentice **Sam** to check the form bracing. Sam found two loose braces on the north wall. He was not sure it mattered and Priya looked busy, so he told no one.
>
> At the supplier, dispatcher **Ray** batched the load at 8:45 a.m. to get ahead of traffic, without calling the site. The truck arrived at 9:15. The supplier's ticket says concrete must be placed within 90 minutes of batching.
>
> At 9:30 the pump operator, **Gwen**, walked the forms, saw the loose braces and refused to pump until they were fixed. The crew finished the repairs at 10:20 a.m. The load was too old to use and was rejected. It cost the company $1,200.

**Task 6** (level 2) In the story, Gwen refused to pump. Look up WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.12 at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-03-rights-and-responsibilities and explain why her decision was not just her style but her duty. When did the load's 90 minutes run out?

## Check yourself

1. Name two decision styles and one situation where each one works well.
2. What is the difference between a must and a like?
3. Write an if-then statement for getting to work on a day the bus might not run.
4. Why might two careful people rank the people in "The rejected load" differently?

## More practice: compare quotes with a weighted decision table

When several options all meet the musts, a **weighted decision table** helps you compare them on the likes. Give each thing you care about a **weight** so the weights add up to 100. Score each option from 1 (poor) to 5 (best) on each thing. Multiply each weight by its score, then add the products for each option (BEDMAS: multiply first, then add). The highest total is the best fit for what you said matters.

**Task 7** (level 2) Northshore Pipe and Steel (fictional) needs steel pipe for the 10-section handrail job. Every supplier must supply mill certificates (the steel's test reports), because the job specification asks for them. Three fictional suppliers quoted.

| | Fraser Steel Supply | Delta Metals | Coastline Steel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | $4,200 | $4,450 | $4,900 |
| Delivery | 8 working days | 3 working days | 5 working days |
| Mill certificates | Sent with the steel | Only on request, 5 days after delivery | Sent with the steel |
| Distance from the shop | 45 km | 12 km | 30 km |

The foreman sets these weights and scores:

| What matters | Weight | Fraser | Delta | Coastline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Price | 40 | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Delivery | 30 | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| Mill certificates | 20 | 5 | 2 | 5 |
| Distance | 10 | 3 | 5 | 4 |

- a. Check that the weights add up to 100.
- b. Work out the weighted total for each supplier. Show the BEDMAS steps for one.
- c. Which supplier has the highest total?
- d. The general contractor now says the steel must be in the shop within **5 working days**. That is a must. Which supplier is out? Of the two left, which has the higher total?
- e. Instead of part d, suppose the foreman decides delivery matters most and changes the weights to price 30, delivery 40, certificates 20, distance 10. Work out the new totals. Which supplier wins now?
- f. Write two sentences to the foreman defending your choice for the 5-day must in part d.
