Problem solving · Lesson 06 · Answers

Weigh your options and make a decision

Know your decision style, build more than one option, check each against what must happen, and choose with if-then thinking.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Develop multiple routes of action; Address the issue

Task 1 (level 1)

Personal task with no right answer. Points a good response covers:

  • three real decisions (kept private if the learner chooses)
  • one or more letters for each
  • a pattern, for example "I often let others decide (B)" or "I usually decide by what matters most (H)"
  • the closest style from the table: A is intuitive, B compliant, C delaying, D impulsive, E often planning or cautious, F delaying or paralysis, G fatalistic, H planning, I impulsive

Task 2 (level 1)

  • a. Fatalistic
  • b. Delaying
  • c. Agonizing
  • d. Planning
  • e. Compliant
  • f. Impulsive

Task 3 (level 2)

Example table. Other well-reasoned points are accepted.

Offer 1: labourer, Northline Civil Offer 2: apprentice, Cedar Flats Builders
Short term, pros Pay starts Monday. $4 an hour more. Short drive. Learning a trade from the start.
Short term, cons 3 hours of driving a day. Fuel costs. Tiring. 6 weeks with no pay from this job.
Long term, pros Steady work experience. Working toward a trade. Less driving every day for years.
Long term, cons No apprenticeship, so no progress toward a trade. Lower starting pay.

Steps: 3 hours a day of driving comes from 90 minutes each way: 2 × 90 = 180 minutes. Over a 5-day week that is 5 × 3 = 15 hours.

Good questions for Jordan: "Can I cover my bills for 6 weeks without pay?" "Could I work the labourer job for 6 weeks, then start the apprenticeship, without breaking a promise?" "What matters more to me in three years: more pay now or a trade?"

Task 4 (level 2)

Any three workable if-then statements, for example:

  • If the window openings are not clear by Thursday noon, then two workers clear them before 2 p.m.
  • If the boom truck is late past 3 p.m., then call the supplier and start carrying by hand at 3:30 p.m.
  • If only one receiver is free at 2 p.m., then the foreman assigns a second person from another task.
  • If it rains, then cover the sheets on the third floor right away.

Each must name a trigger (if) and a clear action (then).

Task 5 (level 3)

There is no single correct ranking. Points a strong response covers:

  • a reason for each person tied to a fact in the story
  • Dale: gave a vague instruction ("make sure the forms are checked") and could not be reached
  • Priya: passed the check to an apprentice without saying what to look for or asking for a report
  • Sam: found the loose braces and did not report them. The story does not say anyone told him to report, and new workers often hesitate
  • Ray: batched the load early without calling the site, which started the 90-minute clock before the site was ready
  • Gwen: refused to pump because of a real hazard. Most groups rank her least responsible, because refusing was her duty (Task 6)
  • in the group step, the learner explains what they value most (clear instructions, speaking up, following the booking, safety first) and how the group reached or failed to reach one ranking

Task 6 (level 2)

WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.12(1) says a person must not carry out a work process or operate a tool, appliance or equipment if they have reasonable cause to believe it would create an undue hazard to anyone's health and safety. Loose form bracing during a pour is a reasonable cause to believe there is an undue hazard, so Gwen was required to stop. Under section 3.12(2), a worker who refuses must report it to the supervisor or employer right away.

The 90 minutes ran out at 10:15 a.m.: 8:45 + 90 minutes = 8:45 + 1 hour 30 minutes = 10:15. The repairs finished at 10:20, five minutes too late.

Check yourself

  1. Any two with a fitting example, such as intuitive (a routine choice you have made many times), compliant (a decision that belongs to your supervisor), planning (a big purchase or a safety decision). (Level 1)
  2. A must has to be met or the option is out. A like is something you would prefer but can do without. (Level 1)
  3. Example: "If the bus is not running by 6:15 a.m., then I call my cousin for a ride and text my supervisor." Any statement with a trigger and an action. (Level 2)
  4. Because they put different weight on things such as clear instructions, speaking up, timing and safety. Different values can lead careful people to different answers from the same facts. (Level 2)
