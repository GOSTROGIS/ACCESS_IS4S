# Weigh your options and make a decision

## Opening (6 min)

**Session 6 of 9 in Problem Solving. Length: 90 min.**

Welcome learners. Run the ACCESS agree and disagree line: learners stand in a line. Read each statement. They step forward if they agree, back if they disagree, and stay if they cannot decide. Return to the line after each one.

1. Time is money.
2. Everyone should have a job.
3. Decision making means making snap decisions.
4. Tossing a coin is the best way to decide.
5. The boss should make every decision on a job site.

Ask: "How did it feel to decide that fast? Is that how you usually decide? What matters when you make a decision?"

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- name their usual decision style and when other styles work better (**Address the issue**: use thinking strategies to choose the best course of action)
- build several options, check them against musts, and compare pros and cons in the short and long term (**Develop multiple routes of action**)
- use if-then thinking to plan a backup (**Address the issue**)
- explain a ranking of responsibility in terms of their own values, and work toward a group decision (**Address the issue**)

## Pre-assessment (2 min)

1. "Think of the last big decision you made. How did you make it?"
2. "What is one thing that makes decisions hard for you?"

Facilitation note for "The rejected load": this replaces two published story exercises from the old files. Like them, it has no single right answer. Keep the debrief on values and reasons, not on blame. Some learners may connect Sam's silence to their own experience of being new on a crew. Let them speak to it if they wish, and do not ask them to.

## Post-assessment (6 min)

1. "Your truck needs $900 of repairs and you start a new job in two weeks. List one must, one like and two options."
2. "Write one if-then statement for that decision."
3. "Which decision style would you like to use more often, and in what kind of situation?"

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- eight decision styles and when each helps or gets in the way
- the seven steps to an effective decision
- musts before likes, and short-term and long-term pros and cons
- if-then backup plans
- how values shape decisions, and the right and duty to refuse unsafe work
