# Define the problem before you fix it

## Opening (5 min)

**Session 1 of 10 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.**

Welcome learners. Write two words, one under the other, on the flip chart: **NEW** and **DEER**. Say: "Rearrange the letters in these two words to spell one word. Stand up when you have it." Give about a minute, then confirm RENEWED. Now write **NEW** and **DOOR** and give the same instruction: "Rearrange the letters in these two words to spell one word." After a minute, write ONE WORD on the flip chart.

Debrief: we often assume a problem must be harder than it is, and we skip reading the instruction exactly. On a job, the same habit sends people off to fix a problem nobody asked them to fix. (This hook comes from the ACCESS ESAF Thinking Skills file it replaces.)

Background for the instructor: in May 2021 the Government of Canada replaced its older skills framework with the Skills for Success model. Thinking Skills, which included critical thinking and decision making, became Problem Solving. This is the first of nine Problem Solving lessons.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- tell a symptom from a cause and a fix, and sort a work order into given, required, not needed and goal (**Analyze the issue**)
- decide whether an issue is familiar or new, simple or complex, and whether the decision is theirs (**Identify the issue to address**)
- write a one-sentence problem statement that names the goal and limits without choosing a fix (**Identify the issue to address**)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

Ask the group and note answers on the flip chart:

1. "Your truck makes a grinding noise when you brake. Is that a problem, a cause or a fix?"
2. "What do you need to know before you start a job someone gives you?"

## Learning tasks (44 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 1.1 Explain symptom, cause and fix with the closet door example. Ask for one more example from learners' own work or home. | 1.1 Listen, give an example, name its symptom, cause and fix. | Learner page, flip chart | 7 min |
| 1.2 | 1.2 Assign Task 1. Check answers aloud. | 1.2 Complete Task 1 alone, then check with a partner. | Learner page | 5 min |
| 2.1 | 2.1 Walk through the Unit 4 work order: given, not needed, required, goal. Point out that the cat is not needed for the repair but still changes how you work. | 2.1 Follow along and mark each part of the work order. | Learner page, highlighters | 6 min |
| 2.2 | 2.2 Assign Task 2. Ask two learners to read out their "required" line. | 2.2 Complete Task 2 and share the required line. | Learner page | 6 min |
| 3.1 | 3.1 Explain the three questions (familiar or new, simple or complex, whose decision). Stress that an unsafe condition is reported to a supervisor right away. Assign Task 4 in pairs. | 3.1 Discuss Task 4 in pairs and report one case to the group. | Learner page | 8 min |
| 4.1 | 4.1 Model the four parts of a problem statement. Assign Task 3. Read two statements aloud and ask: "Does it choose a fix?" | 4.1 Write the Task 3 statement and test it against the four parts. | Learner page | 8 min |
| 4.2 | 4.2 Assign Task 5. Collect one good question from each pair. | 4.2 Write two questions and say what each answer would change. | Learner page | 4 min |

Learning tasks: 7 + 5 + 6 + 6 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 44 min. Whole session: 5 + 2 + 3 + 44 + 4 + 2 = 60 min.

## Post-assessment (4 min)

1. "Name the symptom, the cause and the fix in this sentence: The garage door won't open because the opener's remote battery is dead, so I replaced it."
2. "What four things go into a problem statement?"
3. "A ladder on site has a cracked rail. Whose decision is the repair, and what do you do now?"

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- symptom, cause and fix
- given, required, not needed and goal
- three questions: familiar or new, simple or complex, whose decision
- writing a problem statement that leaves the fix out
