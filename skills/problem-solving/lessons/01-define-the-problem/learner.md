# Define the problem before you fix it

## Why this matters on the job

A supervisor texts you: "Unit 4 closet door is wrecked, sort it out." If you buy a new door and drive to the site, you may bring the wrong size, the wrong colour, or a fix nobody needed. On BC job sites, a job that starts from a guess costs fuel, materials and hours. A few minutes spent defining the problem first saves trips and arguments later.

## Key ideas

### Symptom, cause and fix are different things

- A **symptom** is what you notice: "The door won't stay shut."
- A **cause** is why it happens: "The latch does not line up with the strike plate."
- A **fix** is what you do about it: "Move the strike plate so the latch catches."

People often report a symptom and suggest a fix in the same sentence. Your first job is to pull them apart.

### Given, required, goal

- **Given:** the facts you already have.
- **Required:** what you must find out or produce.
- **Goal:** what "done" looks like, with its limits: time, money, rules and safety.
- **Not needed:** information that does not change the job. Set it aside, but keep it in case it matters later.

### Three questions for any new issue

1. **Is it familiar or new?** A familiar problem may already have a procedure. Use it.
2. **Is it simple or complex?** A complex problem has many parts or many people. Break it into smaller parts.
3. **Do I need to make a decision, or does someone else?** If the issue is unsafe or outside your role, report it to your supervisor.

### A one-sentence problem statement

Put four things in one sentence: **what is wrong, where, what done looks like, and the limits.** Leave the fix out. You do not know the fix yet.

### Worked example

A work order from Harbourview Property Services (a fictional company):

> Unit 4, 1180 Alder Lane. Tenant reports the bedroom closet door "won't stay shut." Door is a white hollow-core slab, 762 mm wide. Tenant works nights: no entry before 1 p.m. Tenant has a cat. Finish by Friday. Budget for parts: $60.

- **Given:** the unit and address, the tenant's report, the door type and width, entry after 1 p.m., the Friday deadline, $60 for parts.
- **Not needed for the repair itself:** the cat. It still matters for how you work: keep the suite door closed.
- **Required:** the cause, and a repair.
- **Not yet known:** is it the latch, the strike plate, the hinges or the door itself?
- **Goal:** the door closes and stays shut, by Friday, with $60 or less in parts, working after 1 p.m.

**Problem statement:** "The bedroom closet door in Unit 4 does not stay shut. Find the cause and repair it so it latches, by Friday, with no more than $60 in parts, working after 1 p.m."

The statement does not say "replace the door." That might turn out to be the fix, but nobody knows yet.

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Label each statement **S** (symptom), **C** (cause) or **F** (fix).

a. The shop heater blows cold air.
b. The thermostat batteries are dead.
c. Put new batteries in the thermostat.
d. Water is dripping through a ceiling tile in the lunchroom.
e. A roof drain above the lunchroom is plugged with leaves.
f. Clear the leaves out of the roof drain.
g. The chop saw cuts are coming out crooked.
h. The fence on the chop saw has come loose.

**Task 2** (level 1) Read this work order. Sort every piece of information into **given**, **not needed** or **goal**, then write what is **required**.

> Coldwater Fabrication (fictional). Customer Silver Creek Farms needs 12 steel brackets like the sample in the bin by the door. The sample is 6 mm plate, 100 mm × 150 mm, with two 14 mm holes. Use plate from the offcut rack first. Paint them black. The customer will pick them up Thursday at 3 p.m. The customer drives a blue truck.

**Task 3** (level 2) Your foreman sends this message. Write a one-sentence problem statement. Do not choose a fix.

> "Pour is booked for 7 a.m. Thursday. Forecast says heavy rain Wednesday night. Our tarps only cover half the slab forms. The concrete supplier needs 24 hours' notice to change the pour time. Figure out what we do."

**Task 4** (level 2) For each case, decide: is it familiar or new, simple or complex, and who should decide what happens next?

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between a symptom and a cause?
2. In a work order, what does "required" mean?
3. Why should a problem statement leave out the fix?
4. You notice a loose guardrail on a deck. What must you do, and who decides on the repair?
