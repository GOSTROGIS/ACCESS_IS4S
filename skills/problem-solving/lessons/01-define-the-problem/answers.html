Define the problem before you fix it

Sort what you are given from what you need to find, and name the goal before you start the work.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Identify the issue to address; Analyze the issue

Task 1 (level 1)

Statement Answer
a. The shop heater blows cold air. S
b. The thermostat batteries are dead. C
c. Put new batteries in the thermostat. F
d. Water is dripping through a ceiling tile. S
e. A roof drain is plugged with leaves. C
f. Clear the leaves out of the roof drain. F
g. The chop saw cuts are coming out crooked. S
h. The fence on the chop saw has come loose. C

Steps: ask "Is this what I notice (S), why it happens (C), or what I do (F)?" A sentence with an action verb telling someone what to do is usually a fix.

Task 2 (level 1)

  • Given: customer Silver Creek Farms, 12 brackets, sample in the bin by the door, 6 mm plate, 100 mm × 150 mm, two 14 mm holes, use offcuts first, paint black, pickup Thursday at 3 p.m.
  • Not needed: the colour of the customer's truck.
  • Goal: 12 black-painted brackets that match the sample, made from offcuts first, ready by Thursday 3 p.m.
  • Required: cut, drill and paint 12 brackets that match the sample. Before cutting, check the sample against the written size, since the work order says "like the sample."

Steps: underline every number and every instruction, then cross out anything that would not change the brackets or the deadline.

Task 3 (level 2)

A good statement: "Half the slab forms will be uncovered in heavy rain the night before Thursday's 7 a.m. pour. Decide by 7 a.m. Wednesday, while the supplier can still accept 24 hours' notice, how to keep the forms ready for the pour or whether to move it."

Points a good response covers:

  • what is wrong (half the forms uncovered, rain Wednesday night)
  • when it matters (7 a.m. Thursday pour)
  • the limit hidden in the numbers: 24 hours before 7 a.m. Thursday is 7 a.m. Wednesday, so that is the latest time to call the supplier
  • no fix chosen: "buy more tarps" or "cancel the pour" are options, not the problem

Task 4 (level 2)

Case Familiar or new Simple or complex Who decides
a. Staple gun empty Familiar Simple You: reload it from the usual supply.
b. Loose guardrail May be new to you Could be complex Report it to your supervisor right away, as WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10 requires. Stay clear of the edge. The supervisor or employer decides the repair.
c. Client wants an outlet moved New, outside the job Complex: changes the scope and involves electrical work Not you. Tell the client you will pass it on, and tell your supervisor.
d. Two drawings disagree New Complex Not you. Ask your supervisor which drawing is current before you cut or build.

Task 5 (level 2)

Points a good response covers. Any two well-formed questions with a stated effect are correct, for example:

  • "Does the door rub or hit anything when you close it?" If yes, the hinges or the door may be the cause, not the latch.
  • "When did it start, and did anything change, like a new floor or a move?" New flooring could mean the door needs trimming.
  • "Can I see a photo of the latch and strike plate?" A photo could show a loose screw, so you bring screws instead of a new door.
  • To the supervisor: "Is the $60 for parts only, or does it include a new door if needed?" This changes whether replacing the door is even possible.

Check yourself

  1. A symptom is what you notice. A cause is why it happens. (Level 1)
  2. Required is what you must find out or produce to finish the job. (Level 1)
  3. Because you do not know the cause yet. Naming a fix too early can send you after the wrong one. (Level 2)
  4. Report it to your supervisor as soon as possible and keep yourself and others away from it. The supervisor or employer decides on the repair and must make sure it is fixed without delay (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10). (Level 2)
