Tell facts from opinions and assumptions

Sort what you know from what you believe or take for granted, and check the assumptions that could hurt the job.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue

Opening (5 min)

Session 2 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.

Welcome learners. Draw a line down the middle of the board. Label the halves Turned out well and Turned out badly. Start with a few everyday assumptions: "I'll be at work tomorrow." "I'll pick you up at noon." "The next three turns are left." Ask: what did each one take for granted? (No flat tire, no detour, no sick day.) Ask learners for their own and sort them onto the two halves.

Point out what the ESAF lesson this replaces said well: noticing patterns and making assumptions is how we get through a day without re-thinking everything. "Don't assume" is not useful advice. "Check the assumptions that matter" is.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • label statements as fact, opinion or assumption (Gather information to help you address the issue: differentiate fact from opinion)
  • say what a message states explicitly and what it implies (Analyze the issue)
  • name a bias, preconception or habit in a work story and list the facts that would test it (Gather information to help you address the issue: recognize and manage biases, pre-conceptions and habits)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. "Is 'that supplier is always late' a fact? How would you find out?"
  2. "Tell us about a time someone assumed something about you that wasn't true." (Optional: learners may pass.)

Learning tasks (43 min)

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 1.1 Explain fact, opinion and assumption with the key ideas. Use the board examples from the opening. 1.1 Listen and move two board examples to the right label. Learner page, board 5 min
1.2 1.2 Assign Task 1. Check answers aloud and discuss any disagreements. 1.2 Complete Task 1 alone, then compare with a partner. Learner page 5 min
2.1 2.1 Explain explicit and implicit. Read the pump truck example. Assign Task 2. 2.1 Complete Task 2 and share one implied meaning. Learner page 5 min
2.2 2.2 Assign Task 3. Have learners open WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 8.14 on a phone or class computer, or hand out a printout of section 8.14. 2.2 Complete Task 3 in pairs using section 8.14. Learner page, devices or printout of section 8.14 8 min
3.1 3.1 Form teams of three or four. Hand each team a tray and say only: "Build the tallest structure you can." Answer questions only by repeating the instruction. Stop after 8 minutes. 3.1 Build the structure. Trays with straws, pins and paper cups, timer 8 min
3.2 3.2 Debrief Task 4: compare heights and how each team read "tallest you can." List the assumptions on the board. 3.2 Write the team's assumptions for Task 4 and explain them. Learner page, board 4 min
4.1 4.1 Explain bias, preconceptions and habits using the Tasha worked example. Assign Task 5. 4.1 Read the worked example and complete Task 5 in pairs. Learner page 8 min

Learning tasks: 5 + 5 + 5 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 = 43 min. Whole session: 5 + 2 + 3 + 43 + 5 + 2 = 60 min.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. "Label this: 'The scaffold was inspected this morning.' What would make it a checked fact for you?"
  2. "A sign says 'New and improved formula.' What does it state, and what does it imply?"
  3. "Write one justified and one unjustified assumption from your own week."

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • fact, opinion and assumption
  • explicit and implicit messages, including product labels
  • what BC requires of eye protection, checked at the source
  • bias, preconceptions and habits, and the question "What fact would change my mind?"
