Tell facts from opinions and assumptions

Sort what you know from what you believe or take for granted, and check the assumptions that could hurt the job.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue

Why this matters on the job

On a site, people pass on information all day: "the lumber's dry," "that supplier is always late," "the power's off in there." Some of it is fact, some is opinion, and some is a guess that nobody has checked. If you act on the wrong kind, you can waste a morning or get hurt. Knowing which is which tells you what to check before you act.

Key ideas

Fact, opinion, assumption

  • A fact can be checked and shown to be true or false: "The delivery arrived at 9:40." "The tape reads 2440 mm."
  • An opinion is a belief or a judgement. It can be honest and useful, but it is not proof: "That supplier is useless." "This is the best saw on the market."
  • An assumption is something taken for granted, usually about what we cannot see or about the future: "The lumber will be dry by tomorrow." "Nobody is working on the other side of this wall."

Assumptions are not bad. We make hundreds a day, and most work out. "I'll be at work tomorrow" is an assumption that saves you planning for every flat tire. The trouble starts when the stakes are high and nobody checks.

Explicit and implicit

  • Explicit means stated clearly: "The pump truck is stuck at the gate."
  • Implicit means suggested but not said: the pour will start late, the crew will be standing around, someone needs to call the foreman.

Messages on labels, signs and ads often lean on what is implied. "Industrial strength" sounds like a promise, but it states nothing you can check.

Bias, preconceptions and habits

A bias is a leaning that makes us favour one answer before we look at the facts. It often comes from habit ("we've always done it this way"), from a first impression, or from who is talking. Everyone has biases. The fix is to ask: What fact would change my mind? Have I checked it?

Worked example

Tasha, an apprentice with Lakeview Concrete Forming (fictional), sees a labourer on the deck waving both arms and shouting toward the crane. She thinks: "He's fooling around. Somebody's going to get hurt."

  • Fact: a worker is waving his arms and shouting toward the crane.
  • Assumption: he is fooling around.
  • What she does not know: whether he is the signaller giving signals to the operator.
  • Check: ask her lead hand who is signalling the crane today, before she reports horseplay.

It turns out he is the assigned signaller and the radio has died. Her first idea was not silly. It just was not checked.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Label each statement F (fact), O (opinion) or A (assumption).

  • a. The drywall delivery arrived at 9:40 a.m.
  • b. That supplier is always late.
  • c. The mud will be dry enough to sand by tomorrow morning.
  • d. The plan shows the window rough opening at 1220 mm wide.
  • e. Our old foreman ran a better crew.
  • f. Nobody is in the mechanical room, so it's fine to start cutting.
  • g. The thermometer in the trailer reads 4°C.
  • h. The new hire won't last a month.

Task 2 (level 1) Each sentence states something explicitly. Write one thing each one implies.

  • a. The pump truck is stuck at the gate.
  • b. They ran out of 3/8-inch drill bits at the supply store.
  • c. The SkyTrain isn't running this morning.
  • d. Everybody passed the fall protection quiz.
  • e. The forecast says snow overnight.

Task 3 (level 2) A box of safety glasses on a store shelf says:

PRO-SHIELD SPORT. Industrial strength! Meets or exceeds industry standards. As worn by pro riders.

  • a. What does the box state explicitly?
  • b. What does it want you to believe?
  • c. In BC, what must eye protection meet when a worker is required to wear it? Look it up in WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 8.14 at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-08-personal-protective-clothing-and-equipment
  • d. What would you need to see on this product before you trusted it for grinding?

Task 4 (level 2) Team build. Your instructor gives each team a tray of straws, pins and paper cups with one instruction: "Build the tallest structure you can." After the build, write down every assumption your team made about that instruction. For example: Did you assume it had to stand on its own? That you could only use the tray? That there was a time limit?

Task 5 (level 2) Read the story and answer the questions.

At Fraser Bend Mechanical (fictional), a run of copper pipe on the second floor started leaking at three joints the day after it was installed. The superintendent said, "It's the new apprentice. The new ones never clean their joints." The new apprentice, Devon, had soldered that run. So had Marco, who has 12 years in the trade. Nobody wrote down which joints each person made.

  • a. Which part of the superintendent's statement is a fact, and which is an assumption?
  • b. What preconception or habit might be behind it?
  • c. List two facts you would gather before deciding who or what caused the leaks.

Check yourself

  1. How can you tell a fact from an opinion?
  2. Give one assumption you made today that turned out fine.
  3. What is the difference between explicit and implicit?
  4. What question helps you catch your own bias?
