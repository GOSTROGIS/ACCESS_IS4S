# Tell facts from opinions and assumptions

## Task 1 (level 1)

| Statement | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a. Delivery arrived at 9:40 a.m. | F | It can be checked on the delivery slip or a clock. |
| b. That supplier is always late. | O | A judgement. "Always" is a generalization. The delivery records could test it. |
| c. Mud will be dry enough to sand by morning. | A | A guess about the future. |
| d. Plan shows rough opening 1220 mm. | F | Check the plan. |
| e. Our old foreman ran a better crew. | O | A judgement. |
| f. Nobody is in the mechanical room. | A | Taken for granted, not checked. High stakes before cutting. |
| g. Trailer thermometer reads 4°C. | F | Read the thermometer. |
| h. The new hire won't last a month. | A (also an opinion) | A prediction. Accept A or O if the learner explains. |

## Task 2 (level 1)

Any reasonable implied meaning is correct. Examples:

- a. The pour will start late. The crew may stand around. Someone should tell the foreman.
- b. You may need another store, another bit size, or a delay.
- c. Some workers will be late. Carpools or a later start may be needed.
- d. The crew can move to the next step of training. The quiz may have been easy.
- e. The site may need snow clearing, a later start, or cover for materials.

## Task 3 (level 2)

- a. Explicit: the name, "industrial strength," "meets or exceeds industry standards," and "as worn by pro riders."
- b. Implied: they are safe enough for work, tested to a real standard, and good because professionals wear them. None of that can be checked from the box.
- c. Under WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 8.14, required eye protection must fit the worker properly, give appropriate protection from the eye hazards present, and meet CSA Standard CAN/CSA-Z94.3-07 or Z94.3-15, or ANSI/ISEA Z87.1-2015.
- d. The product or its packaging should name one of those standards. You also need a proper fit and protection suited to grinding. "Industry standards" names no standard.

Steps: separate the words printed from the message intended, then compare the claim with the rule at the source.

## Task 4 (level 2)

Open task. Points a good response covers:

- at least three assumptions, such as: it must stand on its own, only tray materials may be used, it must stay up after we let go, it is a race, teams cannot share, it must be on the table and not on a chair
- how one assumption changed what the team built
- a note that the instruction allowed more than the team took for granted, or less

## Task 5 (level 2)

- a. Fact: three joints leaked the day after installation, and both Devon and Marco soldered that run. Assumption: the new apprentice caused the leaks.
- b. The preconception that new workers do poor work ("the new ones never..."). It may also be a habit of blaming the newest person.
- c. Any two facts that would test it, for example: which joints each person made (mark or photograph them next time), whether the leaking joints share something (location, fitting type, time of day), whether the flux and cleaning supplies were the same, whether the system was tested at the same pressure, and what the manufacturer's or company's procedure says.

## Check yourself

1. A fact can be checked and shown true or false. An opinion is a belief or judgement. (Level 1)
2. Any everyday example, such as "the bus would come on time" or "the store would be open." (Level 1)
3. Explicit is stated clearly. Implicit is suggested but not said. (Level 1)
4. "What fact would change my mind, and have I checked it?" (Level 2)
