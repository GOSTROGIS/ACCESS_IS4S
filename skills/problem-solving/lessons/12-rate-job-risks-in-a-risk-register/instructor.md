# Rate job risks in a risk register

## Opening (5 min)

**Session 12 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.**

Welcome learners. Ask: "Before you drive somewhere important, like a job interview, what could go wrong? What do you do about it ahead of time?" List answers on the board (traffic, no gas, a flat tire, a sick child). Beside each, ask what people do: leave early, fill up the night before, ask a cousin to be on call. Tell the group they just made a risk plan. Today they do the same thing for a job.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- tell a risk from an issue and write a risk as cause, event and effect (**Identify the issue to address**: identify the nature of the issue)
- score likelihood and impact and rank risks (**Analyze the issue**: identify possible cause-and-effect linkages)
- choose a response, an owner and a trigger for each risk (**Develop multiple routes of action**: create multiple options for action)
- write an if-then backup and update the register when a risk happens (**Address the issue**: if-then thinking, monitor and adjust)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. "What is one thing that could go wrong on a deck job?"
2. "What does 'likelihood' mean?"

## Learning tasks (44 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 1.1 Teach risk versus issue and cause, event, effect. Assign Tasks 1 and 2. | 1.1 Complete Tasks 1 and 2. Read one rewritten risk aloud. | Learner page | 8 min |
| 1.2 | 1.2 Teach the scoring table and the high, medium and low bands. Walk through the deck register on the board. Stress the safety section: a register never replaces hazard checks, and section 3.10 applies at once. | 1.2 Follow the worked example and check each score. | Learner page, board | 7 min |
| 1.3 | 1.3 Assign Task 3. Draw a 3 × 3 grid (likelihood across, impact up) and have learners place H1 to H5 on it. | 1.3 Complete Task 3 and place each risk on the grid. | Learner page, board | 7 min |
| 2.1 | 2.1 Teach the four responses, owners and triggers. Assign Task 4 in pairs. | 2.1 Complete Task 4. | Learner page | 9 min |
| 2.2 | 2.2 Assign Task 5 in small groups. Compare possible impact ratings and the assumptions behind them. Explain that likelihood is missing, so overall risk cannot yet be scored. | 2.2 Complete Task 5. | Learner page | 7 min |
| 2.3 | 2.3 Assign Task 6. Discuss how a register changes as the job moves. | 2.3 Complete Task 6. | Learner page | 6 min |

Learning tasks: 8 + 7 + 7 + 9 + 7 + 6 = 44 min. Whole session: 5 + 2 + 3 + 44 + 4 + 2 = 60 min.

Facilitation note: learners who have done safety training may mix up a risk register with a hazard assessment. Keep them apart. A hazard that could hurt someone is reported and controlled now, through the employer's safety program. The register in this lesson is for time, cost and quality. Lesson 13 shows how to find the biggest causes of problems that keep happening.

## Post-assessment (4 min)

1. "A risk is likely (3) and small (1). What is the score, and what band is it in?"
2. "Write one risk for a drywall job as cause, event, effect."
3. "Give one example of sharing a risk."

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- risks (might happen) and issues (have happened)
- writing a risk as cause, event and effect
- scoring likelihood × impact and ranking risks
- the four responses: avoid, reduce, share, accept
- owners, triggers and if-then backups
- keeping safety hazards out of the register and reporting them at once
