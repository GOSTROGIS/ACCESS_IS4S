# Decide whether an online answer can be trusted

## Opening (5 min)

**Session 9 of 9 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.**

Welcome learners. Read this out in a confident voice, as if from a phone: "Good news! For grinding, any sunglasses with UV protection are fine, as long as they're dark enough. Most pros prefer polarized." Ask: "Would you bet your eyes on that? Where do you think it came from? How would you check it?"

Tell the group it was written for this lesson and it is wrong. Then ask: "Where do you go first when you need an answer on the job?" List the answers on the board. Today we learn to check those answers before acting on them.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- use five questions (who, when, where, why, can I check it) to judge an online answer (**Gather information to help you address the issue**: differentiate fact from opinion)
- spot warning signs on a web page and in an AI chatbot answer (**Analyze the issue**)
- find the official BC source for a trades question and compare an answer against it (**Gather information to help you address the issue**: conduct research and collect relevant information)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. "Have you ever followed an online answer that turned out to be wrong? What happened?"
2. "Who makes the health and safety rules for BC workplaces?"

If devices are not available, print WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation sections 8.14, 10.1, 10.2 and 10.11, and the two BC Code pages, before the session.

For learners who want more practice with AI tools, the Digital skill's AI literacy lessons are at `../../../digital/ai-literacy/learner.html` on this site.

## Post-assessment (4 min)

1. "A video says BC requires a hard hat on every job site at all times. What are your next two steps to check it?"
2. "Name two things that make a web page less trustworthy."
3. "Which edition of the BC Electrical Code is current, and since when?"

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- five questions: who, when, where, why, can I check it
- warning signs on web pages, and how to treat AI chatbot answers
- the official BC sources for safety, building and electrical rules
- checking an answer against the source before acting on it

This is the last of the nine Problem Solving lessons. Ask learners which of the nine they will use first on the job.
