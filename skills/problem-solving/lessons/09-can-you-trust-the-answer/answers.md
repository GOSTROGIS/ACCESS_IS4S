# Decide whether an online answer can be trusted

## Task 1 (level 2)

A strong ranking:

1. **d. WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 8.14.** The official rule for BC workplaces, current, and it applies where you work.
2. **f. Your supervisor and your employer's written safety rules.** They apply the regulation to your actual job and may require more.
3. **e. The grinder's manual.** Written by the maker for that exact tool. It may say what protection to wear with it.
4. **c. AI chatbot with no sources.** Can help you find the name of the rule, but may be wrong and must be checked.
5. **b. Sponsored video.** Paid by a glasses brand, so it may push one product. May not be about BC.
6. **a. 2014 US forum post.** Old, written for another country, no named expert.

Accept f or e in second place, and c, b, a in a different order, if the reasons use who, when, where and why. The regulation must rank first or be named as the source the others are checked against.

## Task 2 (level 2)

- **Right:** ANSI/ISEA Z87.1 is one accepted standard. Section 8.14(3)(c) lists ANSI/ISEA Z87.1-2015.
- **Wrong:** CSA is accepted. Section 8.14(3)(c) also lists CSA Standard CAN/CSA-Z94.3-07 or Z94.3-15.
- **Left out:** the eye protection must also fit the worker properly and give appropriate protection from the eye hazards present (section 8.14(3)(a) and (b)). The chatbot also does not mention section 8.17, which requires face protection over eye protection where there are hazards to the face.

Steps: read the claim, find the section it is about, compare each part of the claim with the section's wording.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Warning signs:

- the address is worksafe-bc-rules.net, not the verified WorkSafeBC domain worksafebc.com; .net alone is not proof of a problem
- no author, no date, no contact page
- it is selling a product ("50% off")
- "made simple" and "good news" promise an easy answer

The main claim is wrong. WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Part 10 does not limit lockout to machines over 600 volts. Energy sources include electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical and thermal energy (section 10.1). Wherever unexpected startup or release of energy could cause injury, the energy must be isolated and effectively controlled (section 10.2), and lockout is required as set out in section 10.3. An off switch alone is not lockout.

## Task 4 (level 2)

- "BC builds to the 2018 BC Building Code": **out of date.** The BC Building Code 2024 came into effect March 8, 2024, with its earthquake and adaptable dwelling requirements applying to permits applied for on or after March 10, 2025.
- "BC electrical work follows the 2021 Canadian Electrical Code": **out of date.** Since March 4, 2025, the BC Electrical Code is the Canadian Electrical Code, Part I, 26th edition (2024), adopted with some BC changes.

What to say, respectfully: "That post was right in 2022, but both codes have new editions now. Let's check the official pages, and ask the supervisor which edition applies to this job, since older permits can follow transition rules."

## Task 5 (level 3)

Open task. Points a good response covers:

- a check card with the five questions: who, when, where, why, can I check it
- a real, specific question from the learner's trade
- the address of an official BC source, such as a WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation page, the BC Building Code 2024 page, the BC Electrical Code page or a manufacturer's manual, and the section or page used
- the answer in the learner's own words, without adding claims the source does not make
- a note of anything still unclear and who they would ask

## Check yourself

1. Who wrote it, when, where does it apply, why was it written, and can I check it against the official source? (Level 1)
2. Any two of: a web address with typos or one impersonating the expected organization, no author, no date, no contact information or physical address, no About Us page, selling something, out of date. (Level 1)
3. It can help you get started or find the name of the rule. Before acting, check its content against the official source. (Level 2)
4. WorkSafeBC, through the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation. (Level 1)
