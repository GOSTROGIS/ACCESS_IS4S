# Plan and organize the work

## Why this matters on the job

A job that is not planned turns into trips back to the truck, a crew waiting for material and a finish time nobody can predict. Employers notice the apprentice who shows up on time with the right gear and knows what comes next. Planning is also problem solving: you find the problems before they happen.

## Key ideas

### Break the job into tasks

Write every task as a short action: "cut shelves," "sand edges." Small tasks are easier to order, time and hand out.

### Put the tasks in order

Ask of each task: **what has to be done before this can start?** Some tasks depend on others: you cannot assemble parts you have not cut. Others can happen at the same time or in any order.

### List what you need

From the drawing or cut list, write a checklist of materials, tools and safety gear. Check it before you start, not halfway through.

### Estimate the time

Add up the time for each task. Use BEDMAS: brackets first, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Add time for setup, cleanup and breaks. Those are the parts people forget.

### Use an action planner

This planner comes from the ACCESS lesson this one replaces:

- **What** needs to be done?
- **When** (time frame and deadline)?
- **Where**?
- **How**?
- **Resources** needed: people, materials, tools, money?
- **Anything else** that could get in the way?

### Digital tools help, if you check them

A calendar alarm, a checklist app or a photo of the cut list on your phone can keep you organized. Check that the alarm is set for a.m., not p.m., that the date is right, that your phone is charged, and that anyone who needs the plan can see it.

### Worked example

You are building two plywood shelf units for the tool crib in a training shop (fictional). Time per task:

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| Set up and check the saw | 10 min |
| Cut parts | 25 min per unit |
| Sand edges | 15 min for both |
| Assemble | 20 min per unit |
| Clean up | 15 min |

Total time = 10 + 2 × 25 + 15 + 2 × 20 + 15

Multiplication first (BEDMAS): 2 × 25 = 50 and 2 × 20 = 40.

Then add: 10 + 50 + 15 + 40 + 15 = **130 min**, which is 2 hours 10 minutes.

Starting at 8:00 a.m. with a 15-minute coffee break, you would finish at 8:00 + 2 h 10 min + 15 min = **10:25 a.m.**

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Put these tasks for the shelf job in order. Mark any two that could swap places.

- Sweep up and put tools away
- Cut the parts
- Check the cut list against the drawing
- Sand the edges
- Assemble the units with glue and screws
- Get the plywood and screws from the rack
- Set up and check the saw
- Send the supervisor a photo of the finished units

**Task 2** (level 1) Here is the cut list for the two shelf units (fictional). Make a materials and tools checklist.

> **Cut list, 2 shelf units.** 19 mm plywood. Sides: 4 pieces, 300 mm × 1200 mm. Shelves: 8 pieces, 300 mm × 800 mm. Fasten each shelf with 6 screws, 45 mm long. Wood glue on every joint. Sand all edges with 120-grit paper. Wear safety glasses and hearing protection at the saw.

How many screws do you need? Screws come in boxes of 100. How many boxes?

**Task 3** (level 2) Plywood comes in sheets 1220 mm × 2440 mm. Each sheet can be ripped into 4 strips 300 mm wide (the leftover allows for saw cuts). From one 2440 mm strip you can cut either 2 sides (1200 mm) or 3 shelves (800 mm). How many strips do you need for 4 sides and 8 shelves? How many sheets is that?

**Task 4** (level 2) You start a new job with Cedar Flats Builders (fictional) on Monday. Read the email and fill in an action planner. Work out the latest time you should leave home.

> Start time is 7:00 a.m. sharp at 2250 Mountview Road. Please arrive 15 minutes early to sign in and do your site orientation. Bring: hard hat, safety boots, safety glasses, high-visibility vest, work gloves, lunch and water. Parking is on the street. Questions? Reply to this email.
>
> Lena, site office

Your drive takes 45 minutes. You want 15 extra minutes in case of traffic.

**Task 5** (level 2) You decide to use your phone to keep the Monday plan. Write the reminder or checklist entries you would set up. Then list three things you would check so the phone does not let you down.

**Task 6** (level 2) Back at the shop, the plywood delivery is delayed until 10:00 a.m. You were going to start at 8:00. What can you still do before 10:00? Using the times in the worked example, when will the job now finish if the saw is set up before the plywood arrives and you take no break?
