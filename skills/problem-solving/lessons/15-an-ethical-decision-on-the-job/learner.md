# Make an ethical decision on the job

## Why this matters on the job

Sooner or later someone on a job will ask you to do something that does not sit right: sign for hours nobody worked, take a gift from a supplier, skip a step "just this once". Saying yes can feel easier in the moment, especially when you are new and want to fit in. But your name, your ticket and other people's safety can be on the line. A clear way to think it through helps you make a choice you can stand behind, and explain it calmly.

## Key ideas

### What makes it an ethical dilemma

An **ethical dilemma** is a choice where doing what is right pulls against something else you care about: keeping your job, getting along with the crew, saving time, earning more. A choice is not a dilemma just because it is hard. "Which saw blade?" is a hard choice. "Do I sign a timesheet I know is wrong?" is a dilemma.

### Four steps

1. **Name the dilemma.** In one sentence: what is right, and what is pulling against it?
2. **List who is affected.** You, the crew, your employer, the client, the public, your family. Who gains and who pays?
3. **List the pressures.** Sort them into three kinds:
    - **Personal:** your values, your money, your fear of looking bad
    - **Company:** your boss, the crew, deadlines, company policy
    - **Outside:** the law, safety rules, the client, the community
4. **Decide, act and say why.** Choose, then check your choice with three questions:
    - Is it legal, and does it follow the safety rules and company policy?
    - Would I be comfortable if my crew, my family and the news knew?
    - Is it fair to everyone it affects?

If you are stuck, ask for help: a supervisor you trust, the safety representative, your union, an instructor or a mentor.

### Some answers are already set by law

For some dilemmas the rule decides for you. In BC:

- Anyone who sees what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition or act must report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10).
- A person must not carry out work or run equipment if they have reasonable cause to believe it would create an undue hazard to anyone's health and safety. A worker who refuses must report it right away to the supervisor or employer (section 3.12).
- A worker must not be subject to prohibited action (such as being disciplined or fired) for refusing unsafe work under section 3.12 (section 3.13).
- A personal lock may only be removed by the worker who put it on. If that is not possible, the supervisor or manager in charge takes over. They must make every reasonable effort to reach that worker and must make sure the equipment can be run safely before removing the lock (section 10.8).

### Worked example: the free impact driver

Dez is an apprentice who checks in deliveries for Cedar Flats Builders (fictional). Last week the company chose Coastline Fasteners (fictional) for a big hardware order. Today the Coastline driver hands Dez a new impact driver still in the box: "From the rep. A thank-you for the business. Keep it."

1. **Name the dilemma.** Taking a $300 gift is tempting, but it could look like Dez is being paid to favour this supplier.
2. **Who is affected.** Dez. The company, which needs fair prices on future orders. Other suppliers. The foreman, who trusts Dez to check deliveries honestly.
3. **Pressures.** Personal: Dez needs tools and the gift is free. Company: Dez does not know the company's gift policy. Outside: the supplier wants more business.
4. **Decide and say why.** Dez does not keep it. Dez thanks the driver, says the gift has to go through the office, and tells the foreman the same day. Dez can explain it: "I check their deliveries. If I keep a gift from them, nobody can trust my count."

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 1) Which of these are ethical dilemmas? Write D for dilemma or C for just a hard choice.

- a. Choosing between two brands of safety boots that both meet the job's rules.
- b. Your cousin asks you to say he worked on the site last week so he can get paid.
- c. Deciding whether to take the bus or drive to the site.
- d. You find the extra 20 sheets of plywood on the delivery were not on the invoice.
- e. Choosing which day to book your level 1 exam.
- f. The lead hand tells you not to report a small spill of fuel near the storm drain.

**Task 2** (level 2) Read the case and use the four steps.

> On a renovation job for Harbourline Interiors (fictional), the crew of 4 works 8 hours a day. The lead hand asks you to sign the weekly timesheet showing 10 hours a day for everyone. "The general contractor never checks. Everyone does it. You want to stay on this crew, right?" On this job the company bills the client $45 an hour for each worker.

- a. Write the dilemma in one sentence.
- b. How many hours a week would be billed that nobody worked (5 days)? How many dollars a week is that?
- c. List who is affected and the three kinds of pressure.
- d. What do you decide, and what exactly do you say to the lead hand?

**Task 3** (level 2) Read the case, then answer.

> At 3:30 p.m. on a shop floor at Northshore Pipe and Steel (fictional), the lead hand says: "Jay left his lock on the saw disconnect and went home. Grab the bolt cutters and cut it off so the next shift can start."

- a. What does WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 10.8 say about removing a personal lock? Look it up at https://www.worksafebc.com/en/law-policy/occupational-health-safety/searchable-ohs-regulation/ohs-regulation/part-10-de-energization-and-lockout
- b. Is this an ethical dilemma, or has the rule already decided it? Explain.
- c. What do you say to the lead hand, and what should happen next?

**Task 4** (level 3) Read the case and use all four steps.

> Priya is an estimator for a small contractor (fictional). This week she is comparing three supplier quotes for a large lumber order. One supplier invites her on a free fishing weekend. She knows her own boss went on the same trip last year. The supplier's quote is the second lowest.

**Task 5** (level 2) You are new on a framing crew. To save 20 minutes before lunch, the crew does not put the guardrail back after a lift and keeps working at the open edge on the second floor.

- a. Which sections from the Key ideas apply?
- b. Write what you would say to the lead hand in two or three calm sentences.
- c. Why can you speak up without fear of being punished for refusing the unsafe work?

**Task 6** (level 3) Think of a dilemma you have faced at work, at school or that you could face in the trade you are heading into. You do not have to share it. Write it out with the four steps. At the end, write who you could ask for help if it happened again.
