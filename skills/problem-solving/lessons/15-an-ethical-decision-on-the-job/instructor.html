Make an ethical decision on the job

Name the dilemma, list who is affected and the pressures on you, then decide and explain why, using four steps that work for gifts, timesheets and safety shortcuts.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Identify the issue to address; Analyze the issue; Develop multiple routes of action; Address the issue

Opening (5 min)

Session 15 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 75 min.

Welcome learners. Read this aloud: "You are at a gas station. The cashier hands you change for a fifty, but you paid with a twenty. Nobody else saw." Ask: "What do you do? Does it change anything if the cashier is rude? If you are broke this week? If the cashier is a friend?" Take a few answers without judging. Point out that the right answer did not change, but the pressure did. Today learners use four steps for choices like this on the job.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell an ethical dilemma from a hard choice and name it in one sentence (Identify the issue to address: identify the nature of the issue, identify if you need to make a decision)
  • list who is affected and the personal, company and outside pressures (Analyze the issue: think critically about the issue using the information gathered)
  • weigh more than one response and its short- and long-term effects (Develop multiple routes of action: consider the short- and long-term implications of different options)
  • decide, check the decision, and say it calmly, using the BC safety rules where they apply (Address the issue: use thinking strategies to choose the best course of action)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. "What does the word 'ethics' mean to you?"
  2. "Who could you go to if a lead hand asked you to do something wrong?"

Learning tasks (57 min)

1.1 1.1 Teach dilemma versus hard choice. Assign Task 1. 1.1 Complete Task 1 and explain one answer. Learner page 6 min

2.2 2.2 Assign Task 5. Read sections 3.10, 3.12 and 3.13 aloud from the WorkSafeBC Part 3 page. 2.2 Complete Task 5. Learner page, printout of sections 3.10 to 3.13 8 min
3.1 3.1 Assign Task 4 in groups of three. Each group presents its decision in one minute. Do not give a single right answer. 3.1 Complete Task 4 and present. Learner page 10 min
3.2 3.2 Assign Task 6 privately. Invite, do not require, sharing. 3.2 Complete Task 6. Learner page 6 min

Learning tasks: 6 + 7 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 6 = 57 min. Whole session: 5 + 2 + 3 + 57 + 5 + 3 = 75 min.

Facilitation notes:

  • Some learners may have been pressured on a job, or let go after speaking up. Let them speak if they wish and do not ask them to. Keep the debrief on choices and reasons, not on blaming people in the room.
  • Task 3 and Task 5 are not open questions: the regulation decides them. Make the difference clear. Values matter most where the law leaves the choice to you, as in Tasks 2, 4 and 6.
  • If devices are not available, print WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation sections 3.10, 3.12, 3.13 and 10.8 before the session.

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. "A supplier offers to fix your personal truck for free. You order from them at work. Name the dilemma in one sentence."
  2. "Name one personal, one company and one outside pressure in that case."
  3. "A co-worker's lock is on a machine and she has gone home. What must happen before the lock comes off?"

Summary (3 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the difference between an ethical dilemma and a hard choice
  • four steps: name the dilemma, list who is affected, list the pressures, decide and say why
  • three questions to check a decision
  • BC rules that already decide some dilemmas: reporting unsafe conditions, refusing unsafe work, no prohibited action, and who may remove a personal lock
  • where to go for help
