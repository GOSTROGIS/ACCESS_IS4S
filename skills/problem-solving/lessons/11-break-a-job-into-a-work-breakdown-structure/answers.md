# Break a job into a work breakdown structure

## Task 1 (level 1)

- **Pieces:** Decking, Railings, Posts and beams, Final inspection
- **Activities:** Install joists, Cut stair stringers, Dig post holes, Sweep the yard

"Final inspection" is a piece because it is something the job must deliver (a passed inspection). If a learner calls it an activity and explains that the inspector does it on a day in the schedule, accept it and point out that in the WBS it is written as the thing delivered.

```
1.2     Walls

1.3     Roof
1.3.1     Roof trusses
1.3.2     Roof sheathing and shingles
1.4     Close-out

```

Steps: put each of the ten pieces under the big piece it belongs to, then number them in order under that piece. The order inside each big piece may differ (for example 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 could swap). The door and window go under walls, in one place only. The numbers must match the place in the tree.

## Task 3 (level 2)

1. **The roof is missing.** Fails question 1: not everything in the job is in the list. Add 1.5 Roof with trusses, sheathing and shingles (and renumber close-out if the company wants close-out last).
2. **"Door and window" is in two places** (1.2.3 and 1.3.1). Fails question 3. Keep one and remove the other. If 1.3 Openings is removed, 1.4 Close-out becomes 1.3, or the roof takes 1.3.
3. **"Paint the owner's fence" is not part of this job.** Fails question 2. Remove it unless the owner signs a change and pays for it.

A learner may also notice that the concrete deck blocks from Task 2 are missing from the base (question 1). Accept it as an extra find.

## Task 4 (level 3)

a. Example WBS. Other sensible splits are accepted if every piece in the notes has one home.

```
1     Steel handrail, 10 sections
1.1     Approved shop drawings
1.2     Steel (ordered and received)
1.3     Fabricated sections
1.4     Primed sections
1.5     Installed handrail
1.6     Close-out
1.6.1     General contractor's check
1.6.2     Primer touch-up
```

b. Work package card:

- **1.3 Fabricated sections**
- **Who:** shop fabricator and welder
- **How long:** 10 × (3 + 2 + 1) hours. Brackets first: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. Then 10 × 6 = **60 hours**
- **Cost:** 60 × $50 = **$3,000** labour (the steel is costed under 1.2, so it is not counted again here)
- **Done when:** all 10 sections are cut, fitted, welded and ground to the approved shop drawings, checked against the drawing and tagged with their section numbers

c. Priming: 10 × 0.5 = **5 hours**. Install: 10 × 1.5 = **15 hours**. Together 5 + 15 = **20 hours**.

## Task 5 (level 2)

| WBS piece | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1.1.1 Layout and post holes | Lay out and dig post holes |
| 1.1.2 Pier footings | Pour pier footings |
| 1.2.1 Posts and beams | Set posts and beams |
| 1.2.2 Joists | Install joists |
| 1.3 Decking | Install decking |
| 1.4.1 Stair stringers | Cut stair stringers |
| 1.4.2 Finished stairs | Build stairs |
| 1.5 Railings | Install railings |
| 1.6.1 Final inspection | Final inspection |
| 1.6.2 Cleanup and handover | Clean up and hand over |

**1.6.2 Cleanup and handover** has no activity in the Numeracy lesson 19 table (activities A to I). It still belongs in the WBS because the owner is paying for a clean site and a handover, and it takes crew time. Leaving it out of the WBS is how it gets left out of the price and the schedule. The crew should add it to the schedule or say which activity includes it.

## Check yourself

1. A piece is a thing you hand over, written as a noun ("Railings"). An activity is an action in the schedule, written as a verb and a thing ("Install railings"). (Level 1)
2. **1.4.2.** (Level 1)
3. Is everything in the job in the list? Is anything in the list that is not part of the job? Is any piece in two places? (Level 2)
4. When one person or crew can do it, you can say how long it takes and what it costs, and someone can check that it is done. (Level 2)
5. The number and name, who does it, how long it takes, what it costs, and how you know it is done. (Level 1)
