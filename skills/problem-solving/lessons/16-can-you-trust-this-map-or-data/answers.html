Decide whether a map or data is good enough to act on

Tell accuracy from precision, ask whether a map or a set of numbers is fit for the job, find out why a map pin can be wrong, and confirm buried lines on site before anyone digs.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue

Task 1 (level 1)

  • a. Precise, not accurate. Same reading every time, but 6 mm off.
  • b. Both. The readings agree with each other and with the true length.
  • c. Neither. A rough guess, and 6 feet off.
  • d. Accurate on average, not precise. The readings jump around 25 kg. If a learner says "neither" because no single reading is exactly right, accept it with that reason.

Task 2 (level 2)

  • a. No, or not without checking. A new subdivision may not be on the map yet. Call the site or ask for a dropped pin and a landmark.
  • b. No. An online utility map helps you plan, but before you drive posts with powered equipment, the location of every underground utility service must be accurately determined (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79). Request a locate first.
  • c. Yes. A written dimension on an approved drawing is what you lay out from. (Check it against other dimensions if anything looks wrong.)
  • d. Yes. Pointing a driver to the right side of a farm does not need survey accuracy.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • a. The address was sent without the town, so the delivery system or driver picked 1540 Mill Road in Cedarvale instead of Fernhill.
  • b. Example: "1540 Mill Road, Fernhill, BC. Confirm the postal code, delivery entrance, site contact and map pin with the site office before dispatch." The scenario supplies no postal code, driveway, contact person or attached pin. Do not invent them. Accept a complete known address plus a clear request to confirm the missing delivery details.
  • c. Any two: check the pin on a map before sending, include the town and postal code on every order, attach a dropped pin, ask the driver to call the site contact before leaving the yard, read the address back to the supplier.

Task 4 (level 2)

  • a. 2.8 − 1.5 = 1.3 m closer than the drawing showed.
  • b. 234 × 1000 = 234,000 litres.
  • c. 9.8 − 4 = 5.8 m.
  • d. When: the drawings were based on a 1957 record, so the information was old. Can I check it: the line could have been located on site along the whole street before digging. Either answer, with the reason, is accepted. Both is best.

Task 5 (level 3)

  • a. Estimated radius 12 m, estimated diameter 12 + 12 = 24 m. This is not a guaranteed maximum error; the true point may lie outside the estimated area.
  • b. No. Even the estimated area (24 m across) is 8 times the width of the driveway: 24 ÷ 3 = 8.
  • c. Many decimal places make a number look precise, but the stated accuracy is an estimate of uncertainty, not a guarantee of the true location. Extra digits do not make it more accurate.

Task 6 (level 2)

Steps in a sensible order:

  1. Mark the planned post line and mark where each of the 12 holes will go.
  2. Request a locate from BC 1 Call online at bc1c.ca or by phone at 1-800-474-6886. Have ready: contact information, the exact location, the depth of the holes, the start date, whether it is public or private property, and where on the property you will dig.
  3. Wait for the responses. For a regular request, BC 1 Call members have 3 business days to respond. Do not dig until every owner has responded.
  4. Ask the property owner about private lines they installed themselves, such as power to a shed or an irrigation line.
  5. On site, check the paint marks or plans from each owner against your post layout. Follow each owner's requirements for working near their line (section 20.79(2)). Do not use pointed tools to probe for gas or electrical lines (section 20.79(3)).
  6. Move any post that falls too close to a marked line, as the owner's instructions say, then auger the first hole.

A strong answer includes the locate request, the wait for responses, the on-site check of marks, and following the owners' requirements before any powered digging.

Check yourself

  1. Accurate means close to the true value. Precise means repeated measurements agree closely. Many displayed digits alone do not establish precision or accuracy. (Level 1)
  2. Good enough for what you are about to do with it. (Level 2)
  3. Any two: the city was left out, a number or street type is wrong, the street is new, the pin sits at the middle of the lot or road. (Level 1)
  4. The location of all underground utility services in the area must be accurately determined, and any danger to workers from them must be controlled. (Level 2)
  5. No. It shows the best records someone had, which may be old or wrong. Lines must be located on site before digging. (Level 2)
