Problem solving · Lesson 16 · Instructor

Decide whether a map or data is good enough to act on

Tell accuracy from precision, ask whether a map or a set of numbers is fit for the job, find out why a map pin can be wrong, and confirm buried lines on site before anyone digs.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue

Opening (5 min)

Session 16 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.

Welcome learners. Ask: "Has your phone ever sent you to the wrong place? What happened?" Take two or three stories. Then ask: "If the phone can be wrong about a whole house, how sure can a drawing be about a pipe a metre underground?" Tell the group that today they decide when a map or a set of numbers is good enough to act on, and what to do when it is not.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell accuracy from precision in measurements and map data (Analyze the issue: think critically about the issue using the information gathered)
  • judge whether a map, drawing or GPS point is fit for a given job (Gather information to help you address the issue: differentiate fact from opinion, conduct research and collect relevant information)
  • find likely reasons a map pin is wrong and fix the address (Analyze the issue: identify possible cause-and-effect linkages)
  • plan a locate with BC 1 Call before digging, following WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79 (Gather information to help you address the issue: identify existing procedures)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. "What does BC 1 Call do?"
  2. "Is a number with more decimal places always more correct?"

Facilitation notes:

  • The Burnaby case is a public investigation. Keep the focus on the drawing and the missing field check, not on blaming one company or worker.
  • For learners who want more map practice, use Digital lesson 9.

Post-assessment (4 min)

  1. "A tape reads 1 mm short every time. Is it accurate, precise, both or neither?" (Precise, not accurate)
  2. "A homeowner says the gas line 'runs along the fence, I'm sure'. What do you do before digging a post hole with a powered auger?"
  3. "Write a delivery address the right way for a site at 220 Lakeview Drive."

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • accuracy versus precision, and why extra digits prove nothing
  • fit for the job: good enough for what you are about to do
  • why map pins land in the wrong place and how to prevent it
  • why drawings of buried lines are records, not the ground
  • the locate rule in WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79 and how to use BC 1 Call

This is the last lesson in the Problem Solving set. Ask learners which three lessons they expect to use most on the job, and why.
