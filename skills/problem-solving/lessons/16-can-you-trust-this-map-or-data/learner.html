Decide whether a map or data is good enough to act on

Tell accuracy from precision, ask whether a map or a set of numbers is fit for the job, find out why a map pin can be wrong, and confirm buried lines on site before anyone digs.

Why this matters on the job

Trades work runs on maps, drawings and numbers: a site address on a phone, a drawing that shows where a gas line runs, a GPS point for a sign post. Most of the time they are close enough. Sometimes they are not, and the cost can be a wasted trip or a punctured pipe. This lesson takes the checking questions from lesson 9 and applies them to maps and data. It builds on Digital lesson 9, where you found and measured a site on a map.

Key ideas

Accuracy and precision are different

  • Accurate means close to the true value.
  • Precise means repeated measurements agree closely. A display with many digits has fine displayed resolution, which does not by itself prove precision or accuracy.

A tape measure with a bent hook can read 2 mm long on every cut. It is precise (the same every time) but not accurate. A number like 49.204517 looks exact because of all the digits, but the digits do not prove it is right. Always ask: how close to the truth is this, and how do I know?

Fit for the job

Data does not have to be perfect. It has to be good enough for what you are about to do.

Source Good enough for Not good enough for
A phone map Driving to the site Finding the property line
A city's online utility map Planning which utility owners to contact Deciding where it is safe to dig
A written dimension on an approved drawing Laying out a wall Proving where a buried line is
A phone GPS point Telling a crew which gate to meet at Setting a survey corner

Why a map pin can be wrong

  • The address was typed without the city, so the map picked a street with the same name in another town.
  • A number or street type is wrong (Road instead of Street, 1450 instead of 1540).
  • The street or subdivision is new and is not on the map yet.
  • The pin sits at the middle of the lot or the middle of the road, not at the gate.

Fix it by sending the full address with the city and postal code, checking the pin on the map before you leave, and adding a landmark or a dropped pin.

Drawings show the best records, not the ground

Drawings of buried lines are often based on older records. A line can be off by metres from where the drawing shows it. A real case from BC shows what that can cost.

In July 2007, a contractor in Burnaby was digging a trench for a new storm sewer. The design drawings, based on a 1957 drawing, showed a crude oil pipeline at a constant 8.5 m from the property line. After the strike, a survey found the pipeline was really between 4 m and 9.8 m from that line. Where it was hit, it was only 1.5 m from the centre of the trench instead of the 2.8 m on the drawings. The excavator bucket punctured the pipe and about 234 cubic metres of crude oil was released. The Transportation Safety Board of Canada found that the pipeline's location had not been verified along the street before construction, as the crossing agreement and the federal crossing rules required, so the difference between the drawing and the ground was not found in time.

In BC, locate before you dig

  • WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79: before excavating or drilling with powered tools and equipment, the location of all underground utility services in the area must be accurately determined, and any danger to workers from them must be controlled. Work near a utility service must follow that owner's requirements. Pointed tools must not be used to probe for underground petroleum and electrical services.
  • BC 1 Call passes your locate request to its members, the owners of buried lines in the area. A request is free. Submit it online at bc1c.ca or call 1-800-474-6886. For a regular request, the owners have 3 business days to respond. BC 1 Call itself does not confirm that it is clear to dig, so follow each owner's response.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) For each case, write accurate, precise, both or neither.

  • a. A laser level that is out of adjustment reads 6 mm high every time, at every spot.
  • b. Three workers measure a wall and get 3048 mm, 3048 mm and 3049 mm. The wall really is 3048 mm.
  • c. Someone guesses a room is "about 20 feet". It is really 14 feet.
  • d. A scale reads 25.03 kg, then 24.71 kg, then 25.38 kg for the same 25 kg bag.

Task 2 (level 2) Is the source fit for the job? Write yes or no and one reason.

  • a. Using a phone map to find a new subdivision that was finished last month.
  • b. Using a city's online sewer map to decide where to drive fence posts with a post driver.
  • c. Using the written dimension 3600 on an approved floor plan to lay out a wall.
  • d. Using your phone's GPS point to tell a delivery driver which side of a farm to come to.

Task 3 (level 2) Riverbend Decks (fictional) booked a lumber delivery to "1540 Mill Rd". The truck went to 1540 Mill Road in Cedarvale, 40 minutes away. The real site is 1540 Mill Road in Fernhill, the next town over. (Both towns are fictional.)

  • a. What most likely went wrong?
  • b. Write the delivery address and instructions the way they should have been sent.
  • c. Name two things the office can check before the next delivery.

Task 4 (level 2) Use the Burnaby case in the Key ideas.

  • a. The drawings showed 2.8 m between the pipeline and the centre of the trench. The real distance where it was hit was 1.5 m. How much closer was the pipe than the drawing showed?
  • b. One cubic metre is 1000 litres. How many litres of crude oil were released?
  • c. The pipeline was between 4 m and 9.8 m from the property line. What is the difference between the closest and farthest?
  • d. Which question from lesson 9 ("who, when, where, why, can I check it") would have caught the problem? Explain.

Task 5 (level 3) A phone app shows a point as 49.204517, −122.910812 and says "accuracy ±12 m". (Digital lesson 19, GPS and location sharing, explains what that accuracy number means.)

  • a. Treat 12 m as the app's estimated error radius. What is the radius and the diameter of that estimated area? Is that estimate a guarantee that the true point lies inside it?
  • b. A driveway is 3 m wide. Could the app tell you which side of the driveway a buried line is on?
  • c. What does this show about the number of decimal places?

Task 6 (level 2) Cedar Flats Builders (fictional) will set 12 fence posts along a back lot line with a powered auger. Write the steps, in order, from planning to the first hole. Include who you contact, how long you wait, and what you check on site.

Check yourself

  1. What is the difference between accurate and precise?
  2. What does "fit for the job" mean for data?
  3. Give two reasons a map pin can land in the wrong place.
  4. Under WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79, what must happen before excavating with powered equipment?
  5. Is a drawing of buried lines proof of where they are? Why or why not?
