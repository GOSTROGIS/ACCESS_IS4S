# Spot patterns and possible causes

## Opening (4 min)

**Session 4 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 60 min.**

Welcome learners. Write **2, 4, 6, …** on the board and ask what comes next. Most will say 8. Then write a different ending that also fits: **2, 4, 6, −2, −4, −6**. Do the same with colours: blue, yellow, red, blue, yellow, red, and then reveal blue, yellow, red in lower-case letters. (This opening comes from the ACCESS ESAF assumption and bias lesson.)

Point: we build quick mental models from patterns, which saves time. A pattern can also lead us to the wrong answer if we stop looking. Today we use patterns as clues and then test them.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- continue a number or layout pattern and spot where it breaks (**Analyze the issue**: seek patterns, make connections across information)
- read a work log, find what repeats, and name possible cause-and-effect links (**Analyze the issue**: identify possible cause-and-effect linkages)
- break a problem into parts under five headings and choose where to look first (**Analyze the issue**: break down the issue into smaller parts)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. "Your phone dies early every Monday. What would you compare to find out why?"
2. "If two things happen together, does one cause the other? Give an example."

## Learning tasks (44 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 1.1 Assign Task 1. Ask for a second way 2, 4, 6, 8 could carry on. | 1.1 Complete Task 1 and offer a second pattern. | Learner page | 5 min |
| 1.2 | 1.2 Work through the stud layout worked example on the board. Show (9 − 1) × 400 with BEDMAS. Assign Task 2. | 1.2 Follow the worked example and complete Task 2. | Learner page, calculator | 8 min |
| 2.1 | 2.1 Teach the four kinds of things to compare and "a pattern is a clue, not proof." | 2.1 Listen and name one example of each kind from their own trade. | Learner page | 4 min |
| 2.2 | 2.2 Assign Task 3 in pairs. Check the percentages aloud. Ask: "What would you change first, and what would you keep the same?" | 2.2 Complete Task 3 and propose a one-change test. | Learner page, calculator | 10 min |
| 2.3 | 2.3 Teach the "If ___ then we should see ___" form. Assign Task 4. Stress that electrical faults are reported, not fixed by learners. | 2.3 Complete Task 4. | Learner page | 7 min |
| 3.1 | 3.1 Draw five headings on the board. Assign Task 5 in groups of three. Each group reports the heading it would look into first. | 3.1 Sort the facts, choose a heading, explain the choice. | Learner page, flip chart paper | 10 min |

Learning tasks: 5 + 8 + 4 + 10 + 7 + 10 = 44 min. Whole session: 4 + 2 + 3 + 44 + 5 + 2 = 60 min.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. "Marks are 300 mm apart starting at 0. Where is the 11th mark? Show BEDMAS."
2. "Leaks show up only on the north side of a building after rain. Name two possible causes and one fact that would tell them apart."
3. "Why change only one thing at a time when you test a possible cause?"

## Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

- continuing a pattern and noticing where it breaks
- comparing time, place, people and tools, and conditions
- why a pattern is a clue, not proof
- if-then statements that can be tested
- breaking a problem into five parts
