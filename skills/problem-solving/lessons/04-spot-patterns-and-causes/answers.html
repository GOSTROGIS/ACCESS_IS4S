Spot patterns and possible causes

Find what repeats in a log or a layout, break a problem into parts, and test a possible cause before you act on it.

  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Analyze the issue

Task 1 (level 1)

  • a. 10, 12. Add 2 each time. A second way that still fits 2, 4, 6, 8: it could repeat as 2, 4, 6, 8, 2, 4 or run back down as 8, 6, 4, 2. Any rule that fits the first four numbers is accepted if explained.
  • b. 1600, 2000. Add 400 each time.
  • c. 125, 150. Add 25 each time.
  • d. 2, 4. The group 2, 4, 6 repeats.

Task 2 (level 2)

14th mark: the 1st mark is at 0, so the 14th mark is 13 spaces along.

(14 − 1) × 400 = 13 × 400 = 5200 mm

The mark that breaks the pattern is 2440. The pattern says it should be 2400. Check the plan: it may be an error, or it may be there on purpose, for example to land on a sheet edge or beside an opening. Ask before you cut or fasten.

Task 3 (level 2)

  • a. Day shift: 12 + 12 = 24 tested, 1 + 0 = 1 failed. 1 ÷ 24 = 0.042, about 4%. Night shift: 12 + 12 = 24 tested, 4 + 5 = 9 failed. 9 ÷ 24 = 0.375, or 37.5%.
  • b. Most failures are on the night shift in booth 3, for both night welders.
  • c. No. Both night welders failed samples, and both passed in booth 1 last month. That points away from one welder and toward something they share.
  • d. The shift (for example time of night, temperature, supervision, fatigue) and the booth (for example booth 3's equipment or the draft from the overhead door). Night shift only uses booth 3, so the record cannot separate them.
  • e. Any one-change test, for example: have Wes and Kiri weld samples in booth 1 on the night shift, with everything else the same. Or have a day welder weld samples in booth 3 on the day shift. If failures follow the booth, look at booth 3. If they follow the shift, look at night conditions.

Task 4 (level 2)

  • a. The breaker trips on weekday mornings between 7:00 and 7:10 a.m., when the heater, kettle and microwave are all on. It did not trip on Wednesday, when the kettle was broken, or on Saturday.
  • b. Example: "If running the heater, kettle and microwave at the same time is causing the trips, then we should see no trip on a morning when the kettle and microwave are not used while the heater is on." Other testable statements are accepted.
  • c. Give the log to your supervisor. Reporting is your part (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10 requires anyone who sees what appears to be an unsafe condition to report it to a supervisor or the employer). Opening a panel or changing wiring is electrical work outside your role and training, so the employer should have a qualified electrician check it. Your log saves that person time.

Task 5 (level 3)

Heading Facts
People Two of five crew leave at 2 p.m. on Fridays.
Materials Concrete arrives at 10 a.m. on Fridays, 7 a.m. other days.
Equipment The vibrator broke on two of the four Fridays.
Method On Fridays the crew also strips last week's forms before the pour.
Environment It rained on one of the four Fridays.

The fact "Monday to Thursday the crew finishes on time" is the comparison that makes the Friday pattern visible.

Points a good choice covers. Any heading is accepted with a reason based on the facts. Strong answers notice what happens on all four Fridays: the late delivery, the smaller crew after 2 p.m., and the extra stripping work. The vibrator (two of four) and rain (one of four) cannot explain every late Friday on their own. A strong answer picks materials, people or method, and suggests one change to test, such as asking for a 7 a.m. Friday delivery.

Check yourself

  1. Time, place, people and tools, and conditions. (Level 1)
  2. Two things can happen together without one causing the other. Something else they share may be the cause. (Level 2)
  3. (6 − 1) × 400 = 5 × 400 = 2000 mm. (Level 1)
  4. Which change made the difference. If you change two things at once, you cannot tell which one worked. (Level 2)
