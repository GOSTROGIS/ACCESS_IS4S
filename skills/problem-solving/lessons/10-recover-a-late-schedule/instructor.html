Recover a late schedule

Measure a delay with the critical path, build recovery options (resequence, add crew, overtime, fast-track), weigh their days, costs and risks, then decide, justify the choice and check the new schedule.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Analyze the issue; Develop multiple routes of action; Address the issue; Evaluate the effectiveness of the solution or decision

Opening (5 min)

Session 10 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 90 min.

Welcome learners. Tell a short story: "Tuesday morning. The inspector walks into the washroom you are renovating, looks at the shower drain and writes 'fail'. The tenant moves in 16 days from now." Ask: "What do you do first? Who do you call? What could you do to still make the date?" List every idea on the board without judging. Say: "Today you turn that list into a decision you can defend with numbers."

Learners should have done Numeracy lessons 17 to 21 (time math, Gantt charts, networks, the passes and float) and Problem Solving lesson 6 (weigh options and decide).

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • measure a delay with the critical path and say how many working days and which date it costs (Analyze the issue: break down the issue into smaller parts, identify cause-and-effect linkages)
  • build recovery options of four kinds, with days saved, cost and risk for each (Develop multiple routes of action: create multiple options, consider short- and long-term implications)
  • check options against a must, compare them on wants and choose, with an if-then backup (Address the issue: use thinking strategies to choose the best course of action)
  • check the recovered schedule for float and explain the result to the owner (Evaluate the effectiveness of the solution or decision)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. "An activity has 2 days of total float and slips 3 days. How late is the job?" (1 day)
  2. "What is the difference between a must and a want?" (A must has to be met or the option is out. A want helps compare the options that are left.)

Post-assessment (5 min)

  1. "A delay makes a job 2 days late. Option X saves 1 day for $500. Option Y saves 2 days for $1,200 on an activity with 4 days of float. Which helps?" (Only X. Y is not on the critical path, so it saves nothing.)
  2. "Name the four ways to buy time back." (Resequence, add crew, overtime, fast-track)
  3. "Why did the class check the float after choosing A + B?" (To see that every remaining activity is critical and plan what to watch)

Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

  • six steps: measure, find the critical path, list options, check musts, choose and justify, check and tell people
  • four ways to buy time back and their costs and risks
  • why only critical-path savings move the finish date, and why mixes of options do not always add up
  • using a must to filter options and wants to choose
  • checking the float that is left and telling the owner

This is the last of the ten Problem Solving lessons. Ask learners which steps they would use on their next job.
