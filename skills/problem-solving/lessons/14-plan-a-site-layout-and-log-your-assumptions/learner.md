# Plan a site layout and log your assumptions

## Why this matters on the job

Before the first truck arrives, someone decides where the gate goes, where the crane stands, where the lumber is stacked and where everyone meets if the alarm sounds. A bad layout means trucks blocking a busy street, material moved three times, a crane that cannot reach the far corner, and workers walking under loads. A good layout comes with an **assumption log**: a short list of every choice and the reason for it, so the superintendent, the crane company and the city can check it. Superintendents and site coordinators on BC jobs do this at the start of every build.

## Key ideas

### Start with what you cannot change

Read the job information and sort it into two lists:

- **Fixed:** the lot lines, the building footprint, the streets, the hydrant, the power line, the neighbours, the permit rules.
- **Your choices:** the gate, the crane spot, the trailers, the laydown area, the bins, the walkways, the muster point.

Plan your choices around the fixed things.

### Think about how things move

Follow a delivery truck from the street to where it unloads and back out. Follow a worker from the gate to the lunch trailer and to the muster point. Follow a load from the laydown area up to the building. Where those paths cross, there is a risk.

### Check the crane reach with the Pythagorean theorem

Put the lot on a grid. Measure each point as **x** metres east and **y** metres north of one corner. The straight-line distance between two points is

**distance = √(difference in x² + difference in y²)**

Square the differences first, then add, then take the square root (BEDMAS). This is the same right triangle as in [Numeracy lesson 15](../../../numeracy/lessons/15-right-triangles-and-squaring/learner.html). Check the crane against the **farthest** point it must reach, and leave a margin: round in the safe direction, never the hopeful one.

### Write an assumption log

Each line has five parts:

| No. | Decision or assumption | Why | How we will check it | Who confirms |
|---|---|---|---|---|

An **assumption** is something you are treating as true that nobody has confirmed yet, such as "the lumber truck can turn into the lane". Writing it down tells everyone what to check.

### Example log lines

These lines come from the job below. They are examples only. Your layout may be different.

| No. | Decision or assumption | Why | How we will check it | Who confirms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Keep a clear space at least as wide as a fire truck in front of the hydrant. Nothing is stored there. | Fire crews must reach the hydrant at any time. | Walk the frontage each morning. | Site super |
| A2 | Waste bins go away from the neighbour's windows. | Bins are noisy and dusty when they are loaded and swapped. | Ask the neighbour after the first week. | Site super |
| A3 | Assume deliveries arrive between 7 a.m. and 3 p.m. | That is what the suppliers quoted. | Confirm each delivery time when it is booked. | Site office |

## The job

Kestrel Ridge Construction (a fictional company) is building a three-storey wood-frame building with shops at street level and apartments above. Here is the job sheet. Every rule in it is job data for this fictional site.

1. **Lot:** 40 m east to west by 30 m north to south. The south-west corner is point (0, 0). x is metres east, y is metres north.
2. **Building footprint:** 24 m by 14 m, from (4, 4) to (28, 18).
3. **Cedar Street** runs along the south side. It is busy, it is a bus route, and there is a school across the street. A bus stop sits on the sidewalk at the south-west corner. A fire hydrant is on the sidewalk near x = 36. A storm drain catch basin is in the curb near x = 20.
4. **Mill Lane** runs along the west side. It is a quiet two-way lane. An **overhead power line** runs along Mill Lane on the lot side, with a pole near the north-west corner.
5. **The neighbour's house** is on the east side, with windows facing the lot.
6. **City permit for this job:** Cedar Street and its sidewalk stay open. Solid hoarding (fencing) goes along both street sides. A covered walkway protects the Cedar Street sidewalk while the building is framed. No deliveries on Cedar Street from 8:00 to 9:00 a.m. or from 2:30 to 3:30 p.m. (school times). Wash water from the wheel wash must not run into the Cedar Street catch basin.
7. **Crane:** a self-erecting tower crane with a 30 m jib. The supplier says it can lift the heaviest planned load (a 1,000 kg bundle of wall panels) out to 30 m. Kestrel's rule: plan every lift no farther than **28 m** from the crane centre, for a margin. The crane stands on a 6 m by 6 m pad on flat ground, at least 2 m from the building. Loads must not be swung over Cedar Street, the covered walkway, the school or the neighbour's house. The crane supplier and the power utility decide how far the crane must stay from the power line.
8. **Things to place:** one vehicle gate 6 m wide, one pedestrian gate, a wheel wash at the vehicle gate, a site office trailer 3 m by 12 m (with the first aid room), a lunch and washroom trailer 3 m by 6 m, a lockable storage container 2.5 m by 6 m, a lumber laydown area 6 m by 10 m, two waste bins 2.5 m by 6 m each, a muster point and the crane pad.
9. **Parking:** workers park off site, in a lot two blocks away.
10. **Before digging** the foundation, every underground utility service must be located (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79).

## Practice

**Task 1** (level 2) From the job sheet, make two lists: **fixed** (what you cannot move or change) and **your choices** (what you decide). Then list three things you would ask the superintendent before you start.

**Task 2** (level 2) Where should the vehicle gate go: on Mill Lane or on Cedar Street? Give three reasons from the job sheet. Write it as line 1 of your assumption log.

**Task 3** (level 3) The crew marked three possible crane spots: **P1** (34, 22), **P2** (16, 26) and **P3** (2, 26). The crane must reach every corner of the building and the centre of the lumber laydown, **L** (34, 10). The building corners are (4, 4), (28, 4), (4, 18) and (28, 18).

- a. For each crane spot, find the distance to the farthest building corner and to L. Show the BEDMAS steps for at least one.
- b. Which spots meet Kestrel's 28 m rule for every point?
- c. Which spot has another problem, not about distance? What is it?
- d. Choose a spot and write it as a line in your assumption log.

**Task 4** (level 3) Make your full layout. Sketch the lot on grid paper (one square = 2 m) or on a printed copy of the plan. Place every item in item 8 of the job sheet, plus the hoarding and the covered walkway. Draw the truck route in one colour and the walking route from the pedestrian gate to the trailers in another. Then complete your assumption log with **at least 8 lines**.

**Task 5** (level 2) Swap layouts with a partner. Check theirs with these questions and write down any **no** answers.

- Can a fire truck reach the hydrant, with nothing parked or stored in front of it?
- Do trucks turn in and out without backing onto Cedar Street?
- Does any lift pass over a trailer, the walkway, the street or the neighbour's house?
- Can every worker walk from the pedestrian gate to the trailers without crossing the truck route or walking under a lift?
- Can everyone reach the muster point quickly, and can a supervisor count people there?
- Does wash water from the wheel wash stay away from the Cedar Street catch basin?
- Are the waste bins away from the neighbour's windows?
- Is the crane pad at least 2 m from the building and well away from the power line?

**Task 6** (level 2) Two days before the first lumber delivery, the supplier calls: their truck is 16 m long and cannot make the turn from Mill Lane into the gate. What are two options? Which do you choose? Write the new or changed lines in your assumption log.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between a fixed thing and a choice on a site plan?
2. What is an assumption, and why write it down?
3. A crane at (10, 20) must reach a corner at (34, 2). What is the distance?
4. Why check the farthest point instead of the nearest?
5. Name the five parts of an assumption log line.
