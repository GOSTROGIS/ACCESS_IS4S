Problem solving · Lesson 14 · Answers

Plan a site layout and log your assumptions

Place the gate, crane, trailers, storage, bins and safety points on a building lot, check the crane reach with the Pythagorean theorem, and write an assumption log that says why each choice was made.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 120 min
  • Components: Identify the issue to address; Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue; Develop multiple routes of action; Address the issue

Task 1 (level 2)

Fixed: the lot lines (40 m by 30 m), the building footprint, Cedar Street and its sidewalk, the bus stop, the school, the hydrant, the catch basin, Mill Lane, the overhead power line and pole, the neighbour's house and windows, the permit rules (street and sidewalk open, hoarding, covered walkway, delivery times, wash water), the crane's 30 m jib and Kestrel's 28 m rule, the pad size and the 2 m setback, the sizes of the trailers, container, laydown and bins, off-site parking, and the locate before digging.

Your choices: where the vehicle gate, pedestrian gate and wheel wash go, the crane spot, where each trailer, the container, the laydown area and the bins go, the truck route, the walking route and the muster point.

Good questions for the superintendent, for example:

  • "How big are the delivery trucks, and can they turn into Mill Lane?"
  • "Who are the crane supplier and the power utility contacts for the power line clearance?"
  • "Where does the city want the wash water to go?"
  • "Has the locate been requested yet, and when are the responses due?"

Task 2 (level 2)

Mill Lane. Reasons from the job sheet:

  1. Cedar Street is busy and a bus route, and the permit says it must stay open.
  2. There is a school across Cedar Street, and no deliveries are allowed on Cedar Street at school times.
  3. The bus stop sits at the south-west corner on Cedar Street, so trucks turning there would cross people waiting for the bus.
  4. Mill Lane is quieter, making it a candidate for access. Confirm truck turning, queuing and traffic control; quiet does not mean it is safe to reverse or block the lane.
  5. The catch basin is on Cedar Street. A Mill Lane wheel wash may help separation, but collected water and an approved disposal route are still needed; location alone does not prevent runoff reaching the drain.

Any three are accepted. A good spot is on the north half of Mill Lane, so trucks enter the open space north of the building.

Example log line:

No. Decision or assumption Why How we will check it Who confirms
1 Vehicle gate, 6 m wide, on Mill Lane, north of the building. Wheel wash just inside. Keeps trucks off busy Cedar Street, away from the school, bus stop and catch basin. Walk a truck route with the superintendent. Ask each supplier about truck size. Site super

Task 3 (level 3)

a. Distance = √(difference in x² + difference in y²).

P1 (34, 22)

  • Farthest corner (4, 4): differences 30 and 18. 30² + 18² = 900 + 324 = 1,224. √1,224 = about 35.0 m.
  • Corner (4, 18) is also too far: 30² + 4² = 900 + 16 = 916, √916 = about 30.3 m.
  • L (34, 10): differences 0 and 12, so 12 m.

P2 (16, 26)

  • Farthest corners (4, 4) and (28, 4): differences 12 and 22. 12² + 22² = 144 + 484 = 628. √628 = about 25.1 m.
  • L (34, 10): differences 18 and 16. 18² + 16² = 324 + 256 = 580. √580 = about 24.1 m.

P3 (2, 26)

  • Farthest corner (28, 4): differences 26 and 22. 26² + 22² = 676 + 484 = 1,160. √1,160 = about 34.1 m.
  • L (34, 10): differences 32 and 16. 32² + 16² = 1,024 + 256 = 1,280. √1,280 = about 35.8 m.

b. Only P2 keeps every point within 28 m (largest distance about 25.1 m, a margin of about 2.9 m).

c. P3 sits right beside the overhead power line on Mill Lane, so the crane supplier and the power utility would have to approve any crane there. Its 6 m pad would also stick out past the west lot line, because the centre is only 2 m from it. It also fails the reach check.

d. Example log line:

No. Decision or assumption Why How we will check it Who confirms
2 Crane pad centred at P2 (16, 26). Assume the supplier's 30 m figure is for our heaviest load. Farthest lift is about 25.1 m, inside our 28 m rule. Pad is 5 m from the building and away from the power line. Send the layout and the load list to the crane supplier for written confirmation. Crane supplier and site super

Task 4 (level 3)

There is no single correct layout. One sound layout, by area:

  • North strip (between the building and the north lot line): vehicle gate on Mill Lane, wheel wash just inside with its water collected or sent where the city approves, crane pad at P2, a truck lane about 5 m wide between the building and the pad, and the site office trailer along the north lot line east of the pad. Workers reach the office by one marked crossing of the truck lane.
  • East strip (between the building and the neighbour): the lumber laydown centred on L (34, 10), with room for a truck to pull alongside. Trucks turn in the open north-east area and leave by the same gate.
  • West strip (4 m wide, between the building and Mill Lane): the pedestrian gate on Mill Lane at the north end of this strip, just south of the truck lane, then the lunch and washroom trailer and the storage container in the strip. Both fit because they are 3 m wide or less, and 6 m + 6 m = 12 m fits in the 14 m length. Workers walk in without crossing the truck lane. Lifts to the building's west side are planned for times when nobody is in the strip.
  • Waste bins: in the north strip west of the crane pad, beside the vehicle gate, where a bin truck can swap them from the truck lane. That is the far side of the site from the neighbour's windows.
  • Hoarding along the Cedar Street and Mill Lane sides, with gates in it, and the covered walkway over the Cedar Street sidewalk.
  • Muster point: outside the hoarding near the pedestrian gate on Mill Lane, away from the vehicle gate, the bus stop and the hydrant, where a supervisor can count everyone.

Points a good layout and log cover:

  • every item from item 8 of the job sheet is placed, at its size
  • the truck route and the walking route are drawn and do not cross, or where they must cross, the crossing is marked and controlled
  • no planned lift passes over a trailer, the walkway, the street, the school or the neighbour's house
  • the hydrant and bus stop are kept clear
  • at least 8 log lines, each with a decision or assumption, a reason, a way to check it and a person who confirms
  • at least one line about the locate before digging, for example: "The foundation dig does not start until every underground utility service is located (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 20.79). Check: all locate responses received and marks on the ground. Confirms: site super."

Task 5 (level 2)

The learner writes down every question that gets a no answer on the partner's layout and suggests a fix for each. Good feedback names the item, the problem and a fix, for example: "The bins block the hydrant. Move them to the north-east corner, long side away from the neighbour." Feedback is respectful and about the plan, not the person.

Task 6 (level 2)

Options, for example:

  • Ask the supplier for a smaller truck, or two smaller loads. No change to the site plan. It may cost more for delivery.
  • Widen the Mill Lane gate or move it so the 16 m truck can make the turn. Check with the superintendent that the hoarding can move and the truck still clears the power pole.
  • Unload from Cedar Street. This needs the city's agreement, traffic control, and must stay outside school times. The crane cannot lift over Cedar Street, so the lumber would be moved in by forklift or by hand. This is the hardest option.

A common choice is the first option, because it changes nothing else on the site. Example log lines:

No. Decision or assumption Why How we will check it Who confirms
9 Lumber comes in two loads on trucks that fit the Mill Lane gate. The 16 m truck cannot make the turn. Nothing else on the plan has to change. Supplier confirms vehicle dimensions and turning requirements against the actual gate and route before each delivery. Site office
10 Assume the extra delivery charge is acceptable. Costs of smaller loads, gate changes and any permitted alternative must be compared; no prices have been supplied. Superintendent approves the extra cost. Site super

Check yourself

  1. A fixed thing cannot be moved or changed (a lot line, a street, a permit rule). A choice is something you decide (where the gate or the bins go). (Level 1)
  2. Something you treat as true that nobody has confirmed yet. Writing it down tells everyone what still needs checking. (Level 2)
  3. Differences 24 and 18. 24² + 18² = 576 + 324 = 900. √900 = 30 m. (Level 3)
  4. If the crane can reach the farthest point, it can reach every nearer one. (Level 2)
  5. Number, decision or assumption, why, how we will check it, who confirms. (Level 1)
