Find the biggest problem first

Tally problems from a list, sort them in a Pareto chart, draw a fishbone diagram for the biggest one, and test a fix with Plan, Do, Check, Act.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)

  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue; Address the issue; Evaluate the effectiveness of the solution or decision

Check: 14 + 10 + 8 + 5 + 3 = 40, the number of parts in the log.

Task 2 (level 2)

a.

Type Count Percent of total Running total
Burrs (B) 14 35% 35%
Wrong length (L) 10 25% 60%
Hole in wrong place (H) 8 20% 80%
Weld undercut (U) 5 12.5% 92.5%
Paint runs (P) 3 7.5% 100%

Steps: percent = count ÷ 40 × 100. For burrs, 14 ÷ 40 × 100 = 35%. Running total adds each percent to the one before: 35 + 25 = 60, 60 + 20 = 80, and so on, ending at 100%.

b. Bars from left to right: B, L, H, U, P, tallest to shortest. The running total line passes 35%, 60%, 80%, 92.5% and 100%.

c. Burrs and wrong length, 60% together.

  • f. Method
  • g. Equipment

Note g needs action today. A grinder with its side handle missing is a safety hazard, not just a cause of burrs. Stop using it and report it to the supervisor right away (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10). Some learners may also say the broken light (e) should be reported today. Accept that with the same reason.

Task 4 (level 3)

  • a. Before: 14 ÷ 200 × 100 = 7%. After: 3 ÷ 150 × 100 = 2%.
  • b. Yes. The burr reject rate fell from 7% to 2%, 5 percentage points lower. The blade was the only planned change, making it a plausible explanation. These before-and-after records alone do not prove causation: check that material, operators, inspection methods and other conditions stayed comparable, and continue recording to see whether the improvement lasts. The two counts cannot be compared directly, because a different number of parts was made in each period, which is why rates are used.
  • c. Example Act step: "Make sharp blades the standard: check and change the cold saw blade on a set schedule and add a blade check to the start-of-week list. Keep counting burr rejects for two more weeks to confirm. Then start the next PDCA cycle on wrong lengths."

c. Either answer is accepted with a sound reason. Example for doors: "Doors are only 10% of the items but almost 41% of the cost, so fixing the door problem saves the most money. Drywall dents are the most common, so the corner guard fix should still go on every job's start-up list." Example for drywall: "Drywall dents are 40% of all items and corner guards may reduce those dents, although the trial still recorded three.
