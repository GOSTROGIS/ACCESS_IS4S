# Find the biggest problem first

## Opening (5 min)

**Session 13 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 90 min.**

Welcome learners. Ask: "If you had 30 things wrong with your car and one free Saturday, how would you decide what to fix first?" Take answers. Draw out two ideas: fix what causes the most trouble, and fix what is cheapest or most dangerous.

Then describe a stop-the-line cord: "In many factories, any worker can pull a cord or push a button to stop the line and call a lead hand when something is wrong. The problem gets fixed where it starts, instead of being passed down the line." If you have your own shop-floor story of stopping work to fix a problem at the source, tell it briefly. Today learners use four tools to find and fix the biggest problem first.

## Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- turn a list of problems into counts with a tally sheet (**Gather information to help you address the issue**: collect relevant information)
- sort the counts in a Pareto chart and name the few types that cause most of the trouble (**Analyze the issue**: seek patterns, make connections across information)
- sort possible causes on a fishbone diagram (**Analyze the issue**: identify possible cause-and-effect linkages)
- plan one change with Plan, Do, Check, Act and adjust it (**Address the issue**: monitor and adjust)
- check whether the change worked from before-and-after rates (**Evaluate the effectiveness of the solution or decision**: reflect on the success of the process and the end result)

## Pre-assessment (3 min)

1. "What is a tally mark?"
2. "Name one problem on a job site that keeps coming back."

## Learning tasks (70 min)

| Step | Instructor activities | Learner activities | Resources | Time |

| 2.1 | 2.1 Teach the fishbone with the drywall example. Draw the fish on the board and add each cause to its bone. | 2.1 Copy the fish and add one more possible cause of their own. | Board, learner page | 7 min |
| 2.2 | 2.2 Assign Task 3. Discuss the grinder with no side handle: a safety hazard is reported at once, not saved for a fishbone. | 2.2 Complete Task 3 and name the note that needs action today. | Learner page | 8 min |

For step 2.2: WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 3.10 requires anyone who sees what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition to report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer. Make the link to lesson 5, where the same rule starts the troubleshooting steps.

## Post-assessment (5 min)

1. "A shop has 50 rejects: 30 wrong length, 10 bad welds, 10 other. What percent are wrong length?" (60%)
2. "Name the bone for this cause: the tape measures on site are all different brands and some hooks are bent." (Equipment)
3. "You tried a new checklist for one week and rejects went from 10 of 100 to 4 of 100. What do you do in the Act step?"

## Summary (5 min)

During this session we covered:

- tally sheets to count problem types
- Pareto charts and the running total, and why the few biggest types come first
- fishbone diagrams with five families of causes
- Plan, Do, Check, Act, with one change at a time and before-and-after rates
- how the biggest problem can change when you count cost instead of number

Ask learners where they could use a tally sheet this week, in class, at home or on the job.
