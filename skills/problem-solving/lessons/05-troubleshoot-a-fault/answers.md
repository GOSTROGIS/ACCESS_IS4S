# Troubleshoot a fault step by step

## Task 1 (level 2)

1. Stop and tell the supervisor.
2. Describe the symptom exactly.
3. Read the manual's table.
4. Check the simplest likely cause first.
5. Unplug and release the pressure (before any hands-on check or repair).
6. Test and record the result.

Accept steps 4 and 5 in either order if the learner explains that a no-tool check such as reading a gauge can come before unplugging, and any hands-on work comes after.

## Task 2 (level 2)

Example: "The nailers have left nails sticking up since lunch on Tuesday. At 2:20 p.m. Bri reported that the compressor was running nonstop; its exact start time is not stated. There is a hiss at the coupler on hose 2, which a bin truck backed over on Monday afternoon. The regulator gauge reads 90 psi."

Points a good response covers: when it started, what the compressor does, what the nailers do, the hiss and where, the Monday event, the gauge reading.

The opinion or guess is **Bri's 2:40 p.m. message** ("Pump's probably shot... buy a new one"). It is not based on a check, and buying a compressor is a cost and a decision for the supervisor. The facts point somewhere else first.

## Task 3 (level 2)

Order and reasons:

1. **Drain valve open.** Seconds to check, no tools, no risk.
2. **Air leak at hose 2's coupler.** Most likely: there is a hiss there, and the hose was run over on Monday.
3. **Intake filter blocked.** Quick, but needs the page 4 steps and nothing points to it.
4. **Worn pump.** Last: hardest, most expensive, service centre only, and nothing in the facts points to it.

The regulator (90 psi) is inside the nailer's 70 to 120 psi range, so it is not the cause. The table also lists the regulator under a different symptom (compressor reaches 125 psi and stops), which is not what is happening here.

## Task 4 (level 2)

- a. **A.** Reading a gauge does not involve touching moving or pressurized parts.
- b. **A.** A look at the valve position, no tools.
- c. **B.** Replacing a coupler releases air and opens the system. Follow page 4: off, unplugged with the plug kept under your control (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 10.11), tank at 0 psi (pneumatic energy, section 10.1).
- d. **C.** The manual says the pump is service centre only. If the compressor needs this, it is removed from service and tagged so nobody uses it by mistake (section 12.10).
- e. **B.** Unplug and release pressure first, as page 4 says.
- f. **A.** This is the manual's own check for leaks, done with the compressor running and tools not firing. Keep hands away from the leak.

## Task 5 (level 3)

- a. **Yes, it looks fixed.** After the coupler was replaced, the compressor reached 125 psi and stopped, which is normal. It stayed off for 20 minutes with tools connected, so there is no big leak left. The nailers drove ten nails flush at 90 psi.
- b. It is the simplest, fastest check and takes no tools. Checking it first rules out an easy cause before work begins on the hose. It is also a check that changes nothing, so it does not spoil the one-change-at-a-time test.
- c. Points a good answer covers: the fault is fixed, so there is nothing to test by swapping more parts. Changing them would waste parts and would muddy the record. Instead, inspect hose 2 itself for crush damage (using page 4 if it needs replacing) and note the filter for the next scheduled maintenance. Say it respectfully: Bri's idea is about care for the equipment, and you can agree to check the hose.
- d. Replacing the hose 2 coupler restored normal operation in the recorded tests. That supports the coupler leak as the cause of this fault; it does not establish the pump's condition under every possible load. Bri's proposed replacement was not justified by the evidence.

## Task 6 (level 2)

The saw is not on a cord you can unplug and keep under your control, so section 10.11 does not cover it. Before starting:

- follow the employer's written lockout procedure (section 10.4)
- lock out the wall disconnect, the energy isolating device, in the safe position with your own personal lock (sections 10.3 and 10.4)
- make sure the lock is marked or tagged with your name, and never use a combination lock (section 10.4)
- make sure all parts are secured against movement before working (section 10.3)
- verify the energy is locked out before you start, for example by trying the start switch as the procedure says (section 10.6)
- keep control of your key, and remove your own lock when your work is done (section 10.7)

A learner who also mentions section 10.11(a), the exception when the isolating device is under the worker's exclusive and immediate control the whole time, should be told that the employer's written procedure decides, and that a personal lock is the normal way.

## Check yourself

1. So you know which change fixed the fault, and you do not spend money on parts that were not needed. (Level 2)
2. Any three of: electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal (WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation section 10.1). (Level 1)
3. When it is unplugged and you keep the plug under your immediate control the whole time you work on it (section 10.11). (Level 2)
4. It is removed from service and identified, usually with a tag, so nobody returns it to service by mistake (section 12.10), and it goes to the person authorized to repair it. (Level 2)
