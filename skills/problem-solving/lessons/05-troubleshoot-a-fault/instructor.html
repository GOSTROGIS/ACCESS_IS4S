Troubleshoot a fault step by step

Make the equipment safe, describe the fault exactly, check the likely causes from simplest to hardest, and change one thing at a time.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Identify the issue to address; Gather information to help you address the issue; Analyze the issue; Address the issue

Opening (5 min)

Session 5 of 16 in Problem Solving. Length: 90 min.

Welcome learners. Tell this short story: "A pressure washer at a fictional rental yard would not start. The mechanic changed the spark plug, the fuel filter and the pull cord all at once. It started. Two weeks later the same washer quit again. Which part had fixed it the first time? Nobody knew, so he had to start from the beginning, and he had spent money on two parts it probably never needed."

Ask: "What would you have done differently?" Collect answers on the board. Point to "one change at a time" and "find out what it does first" if they come up. Today we learn a troubleshooting method and the safety rules that come with it.

Objective (2 min)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • describe a fault exactly, using facts and leaving out guesses (Identify the issue to address, Gather information to help you address the issue)
  • use a manual's troubleshooting table to rank possible causes from simplest and most likely to hardest (Analyze the issue)
  • decide which checks they may do, which need the energy controlled first, and which belong to an authorized repair person, using WorkSafeBC Parts 3, 10 and 12 (Address the issue)
  • read a one-change-at-a-time test log and decide whether a fault is fixed (Address the issue: monitor and adjust)

Pre-assessment (3 min)

  1. "Your cordless drill stops working. What are the first three things you check?"
  2. "What does 'lockout' mean to you?"

Learning tasks (72 min)

Learning tasks: 8 + 4 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 12 + 8 + 5 = 72 min. Whole session: 5 + 2 + 3 + 72 + 6 + 2 = 90 min.

Safety note for the instructor: learners do not open, repair or test energized equipment in this session. All troubleshooting is done on paper with a fictional compressor. A lock and tag may be shown but not applied to live equipment.

Post-assessment (6 min)

  1. "A corded angle grinder sparks at the switch. Put these in order: tell the supervisor, unplug it, tag it out of service, stop using it."
  2. "What must you do before you start work on a machine that is locked out?"
  3. "Why is 'the pump's shot, just buy a new one' not a good first step?"

Summary (2 min)

During this session we covered:

  • the seven troubleshooting steps
  • energy sources, and when to unplug or lock out
  • using a manual's troubleshooting table
  • one change at a time, with a test log
  • removing unsafe equipment from service and knowing where your role stops
