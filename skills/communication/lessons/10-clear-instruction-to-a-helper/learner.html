Give a clear instruction to a helper

Give a helper one step at a time, show it, check with a say-back instead of "Do you understand?" and watch them do it before you walk away.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Why this matters on the job

Lesson 1 taught you to listen and say an instruction back. Soon you will be on the other side. A second-year apprentice gives instructions to a first-year. A journeyperson directs a helper. When an instruction goes wrong, it is usually not because the helper was careless. It is because the instruction was too long, too vague or never checked. A clear instruction saves material, time and sometimes a finger.

Key ideas

Before you start, know the job yourself. What is the task? What size, how many, where, by when? What could hurt someone? If you are not sure, ask your lead hand first.

Get their attention. Stop the noise if you can. Face them. Use their name. "Noor, I need you for a minute."

Give one step at a time. People remember a short step better than a long list. Long instructions with six details in one breath lose details. Break the job into short steps and check each one.

Use the four-part loop: tell, show, check, watch.

  1. Tell one step in plain words. Say every number with its unit: "fourteen and a half inches."
  2. Show it once, if it is a hands-on task. Let them see your hands.
  3. Check. Do not ask "Do you understand?" Most people say yes to be polite. Ask: "Tell me back what you're going to do." Or: "Show me where you'll cut."
  4. Watch them do the first one. Then say what was right, and fix anything that was not. Then move to the next step.

Say why when it helps. "Keep the cords along the wall, because the drywallers are carrying sheets through here." A reason helps people remember and helps them make good choices when you are not there.

Safety goes first and stands alone. Do not bury a safety point in the middle of a long instruction. Say it as its own step and check it: "Before you cut, the guard has to snap back. Show me."

Check back later. "I'll be back at 10 to see how it's going." Then come back.

Adapt to the helper. A first-week helper needs more steps and more checks. An experienced helper may only need the goal. A helper whose first language is not English needs plain words, one idea at a time, and something to look at. Lesson 6 covers this.

Worked example: cutting blocking

Tia (fictional) is a third-year carpentry apprentice at Fernhill Framing (fictional). She needs Noor, a first-week helper, to cut blocking.

What Tia used to say:

"Grab the seven-and-a-quarter saw and the speed square, cut twelve blocks at fourteen and a half from the two-by-six offcuts, stack them by bay 3, and sweep the cut-offs into the bin."

Noor cut twelve blocks at fourteen and a quarter and stacked them by bay 2.

What Tia says now:

Tell: "Noor, first step. We need twelve blocks, fourteen and a half inches long, from these two-by-six offcuts."

Show: Tia marks one with the tape and square, and cuts it while Noor watches.

Check: "Tell me back what you're cutting." Noor: "Twelve blocks, fourteen and a half." Tia: "Right. Show me where you'll mark the next one." Noor points to the 14 1/2 mark.

Watch: Noor cuts the second block. Tia checks it with the tape. "That's right on. Keep going. When you've got twelve, come get me and I'll give you the next step."

Tia gave the stacking and sweeping as separate steps after the cutting was done. The job took two extra minutes at the start and saved a stack of wasted blocks.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Good check or not? Write G for a good check or N for not.

a) _ "Do you understand?" b) "Tell me back the three things you're bringing." c) _ "Got it?" d) "Show me where you'll drill the first hole." e) ____ "Makes sense, right?"

Task 2 (level 2) Break it up. Rewrite this as three or four short steps. Put the safety step on its own.

"Take the bender and the half-inch EMT, bend four ninety-degree stubs at ten inches, and watch your hands in the shoe, then lay them out by the panel in room 106."

Task 3 (level 2) Add the why. Add a short reason to each instruction.

a) "Put the cut-offs in the bin, not on the floor." ____ b) "Close the gate behind the truck." ____ c) "Label every cable at both ends." ______

Task 4 (level 2) Find what is missing. Use the six parts from lesson 1 (what, where, how much, when, who, safety). What is missing from this instruction?

"Go get the pipe for the second floor."

Task 5 (level 2) Say it so it can't be misheard. Rewrite each number so it is clear over noise.

a) "Set it at fifteen." ____ b) "Thirteen of them." ____ c) "Two-sixteenths gap." ______

Task 6 (level 2) Tell, show, check, watch. In pairs, one person teaches a simple task with the four parts: tying a bowline, folding a tarp, measuring a board to 23 5/8". The other person is a new helper. Then switch. Write what the teacher's check question was.

Task 7 (level 3) Plain words for a new helper. Your helper, Luis (fictional), is in his first week. His first language is Spanish. You need him to set up cones around a manhole in a parking lot, with 2 m between cones, all the way around. Write your instruction as steps. Say what you will show him and how you will check.

Task 8 (level 3) What went wrong? Your helper laid 40 paving stones in the wrong pattern. You realize you said "Do it like the other side" while pointing at the wrong side.

a) Whose mistake was it? b) What do you say to the helper? c) What would you do differently next time? Use the four-part loop.

Task 9 (level 3) Your trade. Pick a real task from your trade that has at least four steps. Write your instruction for a first-week helper as tell, show, check and watch for each step. Mark the safety step.

Check yourself

  1. Why is one long instruction a problem, even for a careful helper?
  2. Name the four parts of the loop.
  3. Why is "Do you understand?" a weak check? What do you ask instead?
  4. Where does a safety point go in an instruction?
  5. How does an instruction change for a helper in their first week?
