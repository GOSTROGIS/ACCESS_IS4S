Communication · Lesson 10 · Instructor

Give a clear instruction to a helper

Give a helper one step at a time, show it, check with a say-back instead of "Do you understand?" and watch them do it before you walk away.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Session: Communication lesson 10 of 13.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 10 of the Communication series. The session runs 75 minutes. Ask who used a radio call or a read-back since last session.

Hook: read the long blocking instruction from the worked example once, quickly, to the whole group. Ask each learner to write down everything they remember. Compare. Most will miss the length, the bay number or the sweeping. Ask: "Was that your fault, or mine?"

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • break a job into short steps and say each one in plain words with clear numbers (Speak with clarity)
  • use tell, show, check and watch, and check with a say-back instead of "Do you understand?" (Speak with purpose)
  • adapt an instruction for a first-week helper and for a helper with a different first language (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "When someone gives you a long instruction, what do you do?"
  2. "How do you know a helper understood you?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening, action plan check, long-instruction hook, objective. Write what they remember, compare. Paper 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Answer out loud. None 4 min
2.1 Teach "know the job," "get their attention" and "one step at a time" with the chunks figure. Set Task 1. Mark five checks. Learner page 7 min
2.2 Teach the four-part loop with the figure. Act out the worked example with a learner as Noor: first the old way, then the new way. Play Noor, note the difference. Learner page, tape measure, scrap board 10 min
3.1 Set Tasks 2, 3, 4 and 5. Take one answer for each. Break up, add reasons, find gaps, fix numbers. Learner page 12 min
3.2 Run Task 6 in pairs. Circulate and listen for say-back or show-me checks. Teach a simple task, then switch. Rope, tarps, tape measures, scrap boards 10 min
3.3 Set Tasks 7 and 8 in pairs. One learner plays Luis and speaks only a few words of English. Plan and act out the cone instruction, answer Task 8. Learner page, a few cones or cups 10 min
3.4 Set Task 9. Ask two learners to read theirs. Write an instruction for a real task in their trade. Learner page 7 min
4.1 Post-assessment. Give a three-step instruction to a partner using the loop. None 6 min
4.2 Summary and action plan. Commit to using tell, show, check, watch with one person before next session. None 4 min
Total 75 min

Post-assessment (6 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Give your partner a three-step instruction for something in this room. Use the loop." (Watch for one step at a time and a say-back or show-me check.)
  2. "What do you ask instead of 'Do you understand?'"
  3. "Where does the safety point go?"

Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • knowing the job yourself before you give it
  • one step at a time, with plain words and clear numbers
  • tell, show, check, watch
  • checking with a say-back or show-me, not "Do you understand?"
  • giving the reason, putting safety on its own, and checking back later
  • adapting for a new helper or a different first language

Action plan: use tell, show, check, watch with one person before next session, at work or at home.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. It is the other side of lesson 1 (listening and repeating back).
  • The four-part loop is a teaching pattern for this course. Many trades teach a similar method. It is not a WorkSafeBC procedure.
  • Task 6 uses everyday tasks so nobody needs special tools. If learners know a knot, let them teach it.
  • In Task 7, coach the "Luis" role to say "yes" to everything at first. It shows why a show-me check matters.
