# Give a clear instruction to a helper

## Task 1 (level 1) Good check or not?

a) N b) G c) N d) G e) N. "Got it?", "Do you understand?" and "Makes sense, right?" invite a polite yes. A good check asks the person to say it back or show you.

## Task 2 (level 2) Break it up

Sample:

1. "Bring the bender and the half-inch EMT to the bench."
2. "Safety: keep your hands out of the shoe when you bend. Show me where your hands go."
3. "Bend four ninety-degree stubs, ten inches, one-zero."
4. "Lay them out by the panel in room 106."

Any order that puts the safety step on its own, before the bending, is correct. Check each step before giving the next.

## Task 3 (level 2) Add the why

Sample answers. Any true, short reason is correct.

a) "...so nobody trips on them." b) "...so the dog and the public can't get onto the site." (or "...to keep the site secure.") c) "...so the electrician can find each circuit at the panel."

## Task 4 (level 2) Find what is missing

HOW MUCH (what size and how many lengths), exact WHERE (where is the pipe, and where on the second floor does it go), WHEN (now or later) and WHO (who to give it to). SAFETY is also missing if the pipe is heavy or long. WHAT is only partly given ("pipe" with no type).

## Task 5 (level 2) Say it so it can't be misheard

a) "Set it at fifteen, one-five." (Add the unit: "fifteen millimetres" or "fifteen degrees.") b) "Thirteen, one-three, of them." c) "One-eighth gap. That's two-sixteenths." Simplify the fraction and add the unit, "inch."

## Task 6 (level 2) Tell, show, check, watch

**Open task.** A good check question asks for a say-back or a show-me, such as "Show me where the loop goes" or "Tell me the length you're marking."

## Task 7 (level 3) Plain words for a new helper

Sample for a classroom simulation, away from traffic and any real opening:

1. **Safety, first:** "Stay in this safe practice area." Identify its boundary and check that Luis knows where not to go. On a real site, follow the approved traffic-control and opening-protection plan before setup; this exercise is not that plan.
2. **Tell and show:** "Luis, cones. Around this marked practice manhole." Point to the marking and cone stack.
3. **Show:** Place the first two cones and measure 2 m between them. Say "two metres" and show the measurement; do not substitute an approximate pace.
4. **Check:** "Show me where the next cone goes." Luis places it.
5. **Watch:** Watch him place two more. "Good. All the way around." Point in a circle.

A good answer uses plain words, one idea per step, a show-me check and a separate safety step before the activity. It does not ask only "Do you understand?"

## Task 8 (level 3) What went wrong?

a) Yours. The instruction was vague and pointed at the wrong side.
b) Sample: "That's on me. I pointed at the wrong side. Let's fix it together. Here's the pattern I meant." A good answer owns the mistake and does not blame the helper.
c) Tell the pattern in words, show it by laying the first few stones, check by asking the helper to lay the next two while you watch, then watch the first row before walking away.

## Task 9 (level 3) Your trade

**Open task.** A strong answer has at least four steps, each with tell, show, check and watch, a separate safety step and a "check back later" time. Check for plain words and numbers with units.

## Check yourself

1. People forget or mix up details in a long list, especially with noise or in a second language. They only find out it went wrong after the work is done.
2. Tell, show, check, watch.
3. People often say yes to be polite or to avoid looking slow. Ask them to say it back or show you.
4. On its own, as its own step, and check it.
5. Shorter steps, more showing, more checks, and you watch the first one of each step.
