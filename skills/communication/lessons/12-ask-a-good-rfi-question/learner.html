Talk about a drawing and ask a good RFI question

Spot when two drawings disagree, explain the conflict out loud using sheet and detail numbers, and help write a request for information that gets a clear answer.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Adapt to your audience and contexts; Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose

Why this matters on the job

Drawings are made by people, and people make mistakes. The floor plan says one thing, the schedule says another. If you build what you guess was meant, you may build it twice. If you stop and say "the drawings are wrong," nobody knows what you mean. Trades who can point to the exact sheet, say exactly what does not match and ask one clear question save the job time and money. On most jobs that question becomes a request for information, or RFI.

Key ideas

A request for information (RFI) is a written question asking the designer to clarify the drawings or specifications when something is unclear, wrong or missing. During construction it usually goes from the contractor to the architect or engineer. RFIs are numbered and tracked. An RFI answer, by itself, does not approve extra cost or time. That takes a change order.

Who writes it. As an apprentice or journeyperson you usually do not send RFIs yourself. You find the problem and tell your lead hand or foreman. Your company or the GC's superintendent writes and sends the RFI. Your clear description is what goes in it.

Before you ask, check the set. Many questions are answered somewhere else in the drawings: a detail, a schedule, a general note or the specifications. Look first. Ask your lead hand to look with you. An RFI that is already answered in the documents wastes the designer's time.

Talk like the drawings. Use the sheet numbers, detail numbers, gridlines and tags. Reading lesson 5 covers how a set is organized.

Vague Clear
"The door's wrong." "Door 104. Plan A2.1 shows an 810 opening. The door schedule on A6.1 says 915 wide."
"The drain's in the wrong spot." "Floor drain in room 112. Plumbing plan P2.1 puts it 600 from grid 3. The architectural plan A2.1 shows 900."
"There's no detail." "Section 2 on A3.1 calls up detail 5 on A5.2. Sheet A5.2 only has details 1 to 4."

Say it, then write it. First tell your lead hand, pointing at the sheets. Then help write it down. A good RFI has:

  1. A number and the date.
  2. Who it is to and from.
  3. The drawings and details involved.
  4. One clear question. One question per RFI is easiest to answer.
  5. Your suggested answer, if you have one. It speeds things up, but the designer decides.
  6. The date you need an answer, and why: what work is waiting.

While you wait, do not guess. Do not build the part in question. Work somewhere else and tell your lead hand what is on hold.

Worked example: door 104

At Westridge Clinic (a fictional job), Mika is a third-year carpentry apprentice laying out the corridor walls. Framing the door openings starts on 2026-11-13.

She finds the conflict. The plan shows door 104 with an 810 mm opening. The door schedule says door 104 is 915 mm wide.

She checks the set. No general note, door detail or specification section says which one governs.

She says it to her lead hand, Ty:

"Ty, door 104. Plan A2.1 shows an 810 opening. The door schedule on A6.1 says the door is 915 wide. I checked the general notes and the door details. Nothing says which one wins. Can we get an RFI in? Framing starts on the 13th."

Ty says it back: "So the plan and the schedule don't match on 104, and we need an answer before the 13th. I'll get the super to send it. Frame the other openings and leave 104."

The superintendent writes the RFI using Mika's words:

The architect answers on 2026-11-10: "Schedule governs. Frame for the 915 door. Revised A2.1 to follow." Mika frames 104 on the 13th. Nobody built anything twice.

Practice

Task 1 (level 2) Clear or vague? Rewrite each one so it uses the sheet, tag or gridline.

a) "The window looks too small." (Plan A2.2 shows window W3 at 1,200 wide. The window schedule on A6.2 says W3 is 1,500 wide.)

b) "The duct hits the beam." (Mechanical plan M2.1 runs a 400 mm duct at grid C-4. Structural drawing S2.1 shows a beam at grid C, 300 mm lower than the duct.)

Task 2 (level 2) Read the figure. Use the door 104 figure.

a) What width does the plan show? b) What width does the schedule show? c) What is the difference, in millimetres?

Task 3 (level 2) Check the set first. Which of these are worth an RFI? Write Y for yes or N for no, and say why.

a) _ The plan does not show the ceiling height, but the room finish schedule lists it. b) _ The electrical plan shows a receptacle where the architectural plan shows a door swing. c) ____ You can't find the stair detail, and the stair section calls up detail 7 on A5.1, which has only 6 details.

Task 4 (level 2) One question. This RFI asks too much. Split it into separate, clear questions.

"What's going on with the bathroom? The tub doesn't fit, the vanity isn't shown and where does the fan go?"

Task 5 (level 3) Say it out loud. In pairs, one person is the apprentice, one is the lead hand. The apprentice explains the window W3 conflict from Task 1a out loud, pointing to imaginary sheets. The lead hand says it back. Switch, using the duct and beam from Task 1b.

Task 6 (level 3) Write the RFI. Write a full RFI for the duct and beam in Task 1b. Use all six parts. Duct work in that area starts on 2026-11-20. Your company needs three working days to change the duct order if the size changes.

Task 7 (level 3) Answer-needed date. For Task 6: duct work starts on Friday 2026-11-20. Your company needs three full working days (Monday to Friday) before the start day to change the duct order. Count back. What is the latest day the answer can arrive, by end of day? Show your count.

Task 8 (level 3) While you wait. The RFI for door 104 is sent. Your lead hand is away. Another carpenter says, "Just frame it at 810. That's what the plan says." Write what you say. Then write what you do instead.

Task 9 (level 3) Your suggestion. For the window W3 conflict, write a suggested answer you could add to the RFI. Why is it only a suggestion?

Task 10 (level 3) The answer arrives. The architect answers the W3 RFI: "Window schedule governs. W3 is 1,500. See revised A2.2, revision 2." Write the short message you send your crew, and say what you check on the new sheet before you build.

Check yourself

  1. What is an RFI, and who usually sends it on a building job?
  2. What should you do before you ask for an RFI?
  3. Name the six parts of a good RFI.
  4. Why is one question per RFI better than three?
  5. Does an RFI answer approve extra cost? What does?
