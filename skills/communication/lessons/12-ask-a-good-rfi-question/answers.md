# Talk about a drawing and ask a good RFI question

## Task 1 (level 2) Clear or vague?

a) "Window W3. Plan A2.2 shows it 1,200 wide. The window schedule on A6.2 says 1,500 wide."
b) "At grid C-4, mechanical plan M2.1 runs a 400 mm duct. Structural drawing S2.1 shows a beam at grid C that sits 300 mm lower than the duct. Please confirm the duct and beam elevations and required clearance; the reference surfaces are not specified, so these numbers alone do not prove a clash."

Any answer that names the sheets, the tag or gridline and both numbers is correct.

## Task 2 (level 2) Read the figure

a) 810 mm. b) 915 mm. c) 915 − 810 = **105 mm**.

## Task 3 (level 2) Check the set first

a) N. The answer is already in the room finish schedule.
b) Y. Two disciplines' drawings conflict, and someone has to decide where the receptacle goes.
c) Y, after checking the other detail sheets and the drawing list. If detail 7 is not anywhere in the set, it is missing information.

## Task 4 (level 2) One question

Sample, as three RFIs:

1. "Tub in bathroom 205: plan A2.3 shows a 1,500 space. The plumbing fixture schedule lists a 1,525 tub. Which governs?"
2. "Vanity in bathroom 205: no vanity is shown on A2.3 or the interior elevations. Is a vanity required? If so, what size and where?"
3. "Exhaust fan in bathroom 205: no fan location is shown on M2.1 or the reflected ceiling plan. Where should it go?"

Each question names one item, the sheets checked and what is needed. The tub numbers are examples. Accept any clear split into three questions.

## Task 5 (level 3) Say it out loud

**Open task.** A strong apprentice names the tag, both sheets and both numbers, says what they already checked and when the answer is needed. A strong lead hand says it back correctly.

## Task 6 (level 3) Write the RFI

Sample:

- **RFI No. / Date:** 021 / today's date
- **To / From:** Mechanical engineer, through the architect / Site superintendent
- **Drawings:** M2.1 mechanical plan, S2.1 structural plan, grid C-4
- **Question:** M2.1 runs a 400 mm duct through grid C-4. S2.1 shows a beam at grid C that sits 300 mm below the duct elevation. Please confirm which surfaces these elevations refer to, the available clearance, and the approved duct route.
- **Our suggestion:** Ask the designer to review a revised route or duct shape if clearance is insufficient, and confirm the dimensions and performance requirements. Equal area alone does not establish a suitable replacement duct.
- **Answer needed by:** Monday 2026-11-16, end of day. Duct work starts 2026-11-20 and a size change needs three working days to reorder.

## Task 7 (level 3) Answer-needed date

Count back three working days before Friday 2026-11-20: Thursday 11-19 (1), Wednesday 11-18 (2), Tuesday 11-17 (3). The answer must arrive by end of day **Monday 2026-11-16**.

## Task 8 (level 3) While you wait

Say: "The plan and the schedule don't match on 104. There's an RFI in. If we frame it at 810 and the answer is 915, we tear it out. Let's leave 104 until we hear back."

Do: frame the other openings, mark 104 "on hold, RFI 014" on the wall or in the layout notes, and tell your lead hand when they are back.

## Task 9 (level 3) Your suggestion

Sample: "Suggest W3 at 1,500 as shown on the schedule, if the wall framing and the structure above allow it." It is only a suggestion because the designer is responsible for the design and may know about structure, energy rules or the owner's wishes that you do not.

## Task 10 (level 3) The answer arrives

Sample message: "RFI on W3 answered: schedule governs, W3 is 1,500 wide. Use revised A2.2, revision 2. Old A2.2 is out."

Check on the new sheet: the revision number and date in the title block, the revision cloud around W3, the new width and any other changes inside the cloud. Make sure the crew is using the new sheet, not an old copy.

## Check yourself

1. A written question asking the designer to clarify the drawings or specifications when something is unclear, wrong or missing. On a building job it usually goes from the contractor to the architect or engineer.
2. Check the rest of the set (details, schedules, general notes, specifications) and ask your lead hand to look with you.
3. Number and date, to and from, the drawings involved, one clear question, a suggested answer if you have one, and the date the answer is needed with the reason.
4. One question gets one clear answer. Several questions in one RFI can get a partial answer, and the rest gets lost.
5. No. A change order approves extra cost or time.
