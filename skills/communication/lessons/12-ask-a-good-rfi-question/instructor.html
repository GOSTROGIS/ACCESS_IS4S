Communication · Lesson 12 · Instructor

Talk about a drawing and ask a good RFI question

Spot when two drawings disagree, explain the conflict out loud using sheet and detail numbers, and help write a request for information that gets a clear answer.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Adapt to your audience and contexts; Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose

Session: Communication lesson 12 of 13.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 12 of the Communication series. The session runs 90 minutes. Ask who reported a problem early, using the card, since last session.

Hook: hold up two printed sheets (or show the door 104 figure). "These two sheets disagree. You start framing tomorrow. What do you do?" Take answers: guess, ask, build both ways, wait. Today is about asking in a way that gets a fast, clear answer.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • find a conflict between two drawings and check the rest of the set before asking (Adapt to your audience and contexts)
  • describe the conflict out loud using sheet numbers, tags, gridlines and dimensions (Speak with clarity)
  • help write an RFI with one clear question and a needed-by date worked out from the schedule (Speak with purpose)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "Have you ever found a mistake in a drawing? What happened?"
  2. "Who on a site do you think talks to the architect?"

Learning tasks

3.2 Run Task 5 in pairs, then switch. Explain a conflict out loud, say it back. Learner page 8 min
3.3 Set Tasks 6 and 7. Work the Task 7 count on the board with a calendar. Write a full RFI, count back working days. Learner page, calendar 15 min
3.4 Set Tasks 8, 9 and 10. Take two answers for Task 8. Plan what to do while waiting, suggest an answer, read the reply. Learner page 12 min

4.2 Summary and action plan. Commit to finding one conflict or unclear item in a real or practice set. None 4 min
Total 90 min

Post-assessment item (step 4.1)

"Plan A2.4 shows the corridor 1,800 wide between gridlines B and C. Section 1 on A3.2 shows the same corridor 1,500 wide. Framing starts Monday."

Post-assessment (6 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Tell your partner the conflict in 30 seconds, using the sheets." (Check for both sheet numbers, the gridlines and both widths.)
  2. "Write the one question for the RFI."
  3. "What do you do with that corridor wall until the answer comes?"

Summary (4 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • what an RFI is, who sends it, and that an answer does not approve extra cost by itself
  • checking the rest of the set before asking
  • talking like the drawings: sheets, details, tags, gridlines and numbers
  • the six parts of a good RFI and one question per RFI
  • working out a needed-by date, and not building the part in question while you wait

Action plan: find one conflict or unclear item in a real or practice set and describe it the clear way.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. It links to Reading lesson 5 (floor plans and notes) and to any Reading lessons on drawing sets.
  • The drawings in the figures are fictional and simplified. Door widths of 810 mm and 915 mm are common metric door sizes, used here only for the story.
  • The Ontario Association of Architects notes that RFIs are not formally defined in the standard CCDC contracts, even though they are used on almost every job. Do not tell learners RFIs are a legal requirement. They are standard practice, and each contract sets its own process.
  • Remind learners that 2026-11-11 is Remembrance Day, a statutory holiday in BC. That is why the example answer comes on 11-10. If a learner counts working days across a holiday, discuss it.
