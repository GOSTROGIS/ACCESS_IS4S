Saying no and setting limits at work

Say no clearly without over-explaining, offer an alternative when you want to, and refuse unsafe work the way BC law sets out.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 1) Three rounds

This is a role play. A good response:

  • Round 1 is a clear no with no reason.
  • Round 2 piles up excuses. The learner notices that the asker starts arguing with the excuses or that they themselves feel less sure.
  • Round 3 is one clear sentence, a steady voice, and a short reason only if the learner wants one.

Most learners say round 3 felt best because it was clear and respectful. Some say round 1 felt best for simple requests. Both are fine if the learner explains why. A common observation about round 2: the partner pushed back on each excuse or found a way around it.

Task 2 (level 2) Cut the excuses

Sample answers:

  • "No, I don't lend out my tools."
  • "I'm not able to lend it this weekend. The crib might have one you can sign out."

A good answer is one or two sentences, says no clearly, and does not list excuses.

Task 3 (level 2) No, plus an alternative

Sample answers. Check that each one says no clearly and that at least two offer an alternative.

  1. "I can't cover Sunday. I've got family plans. I could take a shift next Saturday if that helps."
  2. "I'm not able to lend money." An alternative is optional here. A no with no alternative is a complete answer.
  3. "I need my lunch break. I can give the trailer fifteen minutes at the end of the day."

Task 4 (level 2) Refuse unsafe work

a) A clear refusal, for example: "I'm refusing to do that cut with the guard off. I think it's unsafe. Can we find another way, like a smaller grinder or a different tool?" The key words are that you are refusing the work as unsafe. Saying only "that looks a bit risky" may not make it clear that you are refusing.

b) If you still believe it is unsafe after the lead hand checks, the supervisor or employer must investigate again with you and a worker member of the joint health and safety committee, a union representative, or another worker you choose (OHS Regulation section 3.12(4)).

c) Both you and the supervisor or employer must contact WorkSafeBC (section 3.12(5)). The Prevention Information Line is 604.276.3100, or toll-free 1.888.621.7233.

d) No. You must not be disciplined or penalized for following the refusal steps (section 3.13). You may be moved to other work for a while at no loss in pay.

Task 5 (level 1) What stops me?

Answers are personal. A good response names a real thought and offers a realistic alternative. Examples:

  • Thought: "They'll think I'm lazy." Realistic view: "One clear no does not make me lazy. I say yes plenty."
  • Thought: "I'll lose the job." Realistic view: "Turning down one extra shift is unlikely to cost me the job, and a clear answer helps the foreman plan."
  • Thought: "It's rude to say no to someone older." Realistic view: "I can say no respectfully, and I can choose the situations where I do."

Check yourself

  1. A flat no, over-explaining, and an assertive no. The assertive no is clear, short and respectful, with a short reason only if you choose.
  2. You sound unsure, the other person argues with each excuse, and you often end up feeling guilty or saying yes.
  3. You must not do the task, and you must report the unsafe condition to your supervisor or employer right away.
  4. So your supervisor or employer knows you are using the refusal process and that they must investigate, not only take note of a concern. WorkSafeBC's guideline says the worker should clearly say they are refusing the work as unsafe.
  5. Yes. Your employer may give you other work for a while, at no loss in pay.
