Saying no and setting limits at work

Say no clearly without over-explaining, offer an alternative when you want to, and refuse unsafe work the way BC law sets out.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 70 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Session: Communication lesson 4 of 13.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 4 of the Communication series. The session runs 70 minutes. Ask who tried one of their assertiveness goals and how it went.

Hook: "Put up your hand if you have ever said yes to something and regretted it before you finished the sentence." Most hands go up. "Today we practise the other answer."

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • say no clearly and briefly, and offer an alternative when they choose (Speak with clarity, Speak with purpose)
  • choose how to say no for the person and the situation, and name what stops them from saying no (Adapt to your audience and contexts)
  • describe the BC steps for refusing unsafe work and say a clear refusal (Speak with purpose)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "What do you usually say when you want to turn someone down?"
  2. "If your supervisor asked you to do something you thought was dangerous, what would you do?"

Learning tasks

Notes for the instructor:

  • The original ACCESS lesson pointed out that different cultures have different norms about when it is acceptable to say no, and that the coach must be sensitive to this. Keep that approach. Let learners choose their own situations, and do not push anyone to role play a refusal that feels wrong to them.
  • Task 4 is about understanding the process, not legal advice. Send specific questions to the WorkSafeBC Prevention Information Line.

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Give me an assertive no to this: 'Can you drive me to the airport at 4 a.m. Saturday?'"
  2. "Name the three steps for refusing unsafe work in BC."
  3. "What are two things that can make saying no hard, and what can you tell yourself instead?"

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • three ways to say no: a flat no, over-explaining and an assertive no
  • keeping it short, using a steady voice, and offering an alternative when you choose
  • noticing the thoughts that stop us from saying no
  • the duty to refuse unsafe work in BC, the three steps, and protection from discipline

Action plan: say no assertively once before next session, in a situation where you want to, and be ready to tell the group how it went.
