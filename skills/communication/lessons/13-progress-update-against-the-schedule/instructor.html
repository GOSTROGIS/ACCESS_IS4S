Give a progress update against the schedule

Read where a job stands on a bar chart, work out how far ahead or behind it is, and give a short update that says where we are, why, the effect, the fix and what you need.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Session: Communication lesson 13 of 13.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 13 of the Communication series. The session runs 90 minutes. It is the last Communication session. Ask who described a drawing conflict the clear way since last session.

Hook: ask two volunteers. The first answers "How's the job going?" with "Going good." The second answers with "Framing is one day behind because the lumber came late. Here's how we get it back." Ask the group: "Which one could your boss act on?"

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • read a bar chart with plan, actual and forecast bars and a today line (Speak with purpose)
  • work out how far ahead or behind an activity is, in days and percent, and what else moves (Speak with purpose)
  • give a short five-part update with a fix or options and a clear ask (Speak with clarity)
  • change the same update for a foreman and for an owner (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "When your boss asks 'Where are we?', what do you usually say?"
  2. "Have you ever seen a bar chart or Gantt chart on a job? What did it show?"

Post-assessment chart (step 4.1)

Draw on the board: "Roofing, planned days 3 to 6. Today is the end of day 5. Roofing is 50% done. Gutters follow on day 7."

Post-assessment (6 min, step 4.1)

  1. "How far behind is roofing, in days?" (Planned 3 of 4 days = 75%, done 50%, so 25% = 1 day behind.)
  2. "What moves if nothing changes?" (Roofing ends day 7, gutters move to day 8.)
  3. "Give the five-part update to your partner in 30 seconds."

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • reading a bar chart with plan, done and forecast bars and a today line
  • working out ahead or behind in days and percent, and what else moves
  • five parts of an update: where we are, why, effect, the fix, what I need
  • reporting early and plainly, and bringing options
  • the same facts for a foreman and for an owner

This is the last session of the Communication series. Hand back any goal sheets and ask each learner to name one habit from the thirteen sessions they will keep using.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit. The Numeracy scheduling lessons teach how to build bar charts and networks and how float works. Keep this lesson on reading and talking. If learners ask about the critical path, say only that the roof waits on framing, so any framing delay moves the roof.
  • Worker-days assume each worker does the same amount of work per day. Real crews are not that simple: a new worker may need help, and too many people in a small space slows everyone. Say so when you check Task 6 and the worked example.
  • The percent method in this lesson compares days of work. It is a simple, honest way to talk about progress. Larger jobs use other measures, such as quantities installed.
