Give a progress update against the schedule

Read where a job stands on a bar chart, work out how far ahead or behind it is, and give a short update that says where we are, why, the effect, the fix and what you need.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 2) Read the chart

a) Days 3 and 4. Yes, the done bar matches the plan bar. b) The end of day 7. c) Plan: days 9 and 10. Forecast: days 10 and 11. d) Framing.

Task 2 (level 2) Percent planned

2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 = 40%.

Task 3 (level 2) Ahead or behind?

Should be done: 4 of 6 days = 4 ÷ 6 = 66.7%. Done: 3 of 6 days = 3 ÷ 6 = 50%. Behind by 4 − 3 = 1 day, which is 66.7% − 50% = 16.7 percentage points, representing about 16.7% of the total siding work (1 ÷ 6).

Task 4 (level 2) Vague to clear

a) Sample: "Drywall is half a day behind on level 2. We'll catch up by Thursday." b) Sample: "Paving is one day ahead. Base course is done on both lots."

Any answer that names the activity and gives ahead or behind in days is correct. The learner may make up the number for this practice, but must label the added details as fictional. A real update must use checked facts.

Task 5 (level 3) The effect

Roof: days 11 and 12 (2 days later than 9 and 10). Windows: day 13. The job finishes at the end of day 13, 2 days later than the plan of day 11.

Task 6 (level 3) Crew-days

3 days × 2 workers = 6 worker-days. With 3 workers: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 days. Yes, it finishes in 2 days.

Task 7 (level 3) Write the update

Working: planned 4 days, end of day 12 is 3 of 4 days, so 75% should be done. Done is 25%, which is 1 day of work. The gap is 75% − 25% = 50 percentage points, representing 50% of the total work, which is 2 days. Work left: 75% = 3 days. If nothing changes, rough-in ends on day 15, so the day 14 inspection cannot happen.

Sample update:

"Electrical rough-in is two days behind after the panel back-order: 25% done instead of 75%. With the panel now available, the current crew is forecast to finish day 15. That would need a later inspection and drywall start. If a second crew can start day 13 at the same rate, we forecast finishing midday 14. We could request inspection day 15 and drywall day 16; otherwise request days 16 and 17. Those bookings and costs need confirmation. Please decide before day 13 starts so we can arrange the crew."

Check the arithmetic in the first option, under the stated equal-output assumptions: 3 days of work with two crews is 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 days, so days 13 and the first half of 14.

Task 8 (level 3) Same facts, two listeners

Sample: "Electrical work is about two days behind after a part delay. With the part now available, keeping the current crew means finishing that work on day 15. A second crew could finish it around midday day 14 if available and equally productive. We need to confirm the cost, inspection and drywall dates before promising the overall finish or move-in date. Please decide before day 13 starts so we can arrange the crew." A good answer uses plain words, keeps forecasts conditional and does not invent a cost or move-in commitment.

Task 9 (level 3) Ahead of schedule

Yes. Sample: "Base course is done, one day ahead. The paving crew is free tomorrow. Can we bring the top course forward a day, or do you want them on the next lot?" The superintendent might move the next activity earlier, move the crew to another job, or keep the day as a buffer for weather.

Task 10 (level 3) Say it out loud

Open task. Check for all five parts, numbers in days, under 30 seconds, and a correct say-back.

Check yourself

  1. Where the job is now, so you can compare the plan with what is done.
  2. 3 ÷ 4 = 75%.
  3. Where we are, why, effect, the fix, what I need.
  4. Others can plan around it: move trades, order materials, tell the owner. A late surprise leaves fewer choices and costs trust.
  5. The owner hears the effect on time and cost and the decision needed, in plain words. Your foreman gets the trade detail: crews, materials and days.
