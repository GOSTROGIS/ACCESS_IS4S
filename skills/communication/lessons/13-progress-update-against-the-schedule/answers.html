Give a progress update against the schedule

Read where a job stands on a bar chart, work out how far ahead or behind it is, and give a short update that says where we are, why, the effect, the fix and what you need.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 3
  • Time: 90 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 2) Read the chart

a) Days 3 and 4. Yes, the done bar matches the plan bar. b) The end of day 7. c) Plan: days 9 and 10. Forecast: days 10 and 11. d) Framing.

Task 2 (level 2) Percent planned

2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 = 40%.

Task 3 (level 2) Ahead or behind?

Should be done: 4 of 6 days = 4 ÷ 6 = 66.7%. Done: 3 of 6 days = 3 ÷ 6 = 50%. Behind by 4 − 3 = 1 day, which is 66.7% − 50% = 16.7% of the siding (1 ÷ 6).

Task 4 (level 2) Vague to clear

a) Sample: "Drywall is half a day behind on level 2. We'll catch up by Thursday." b) Sample: "Paving is one day ahead. Base course is done on both lots."

Any answer that names the activity and gives ahead or behind in days is correct. The learner may make up the number, but must state one.

Task 5 (level 3) The effect

Roof: days 11 and 12 (2 days later than 9 and 10). Windows: day 13. The job finishes at the end of day 13, 2 days later than the plan of day 11.

Task 6 (level 3) Crew-days

3 days × 2 workers = 6 worker-days. With 3 workers: 6 ÷ 3 = 2 days. Yes, it finishes in 2 days.

Task 7 (level 3) Write the update

Working: planned 4 days, end of day 12 is 3 of 4 days, so 75% should be done. Done is 25%, which is 1 day of work. Behind by 75% − 25% = 50%, which is 2 days. Work left: 75% = 3 days. If nothing changes, rough-in ends on day 15, so the day 14 inspection cannot happen.

Sample update:

"Electrical rough-in at the duplex is two days behind. The panel was back-ordered, so we're 25% done instead of 75%. If nothing changes, we finish day 15, the inspection moves from 14 to 16, and drywall moves from 15 to 17. Two options. One: add a second crew on days 13 and 14, finish midday 14, inspect day 15, drywall day 16, so one day late instead of two. Two: keep the crew, two days late, no extra cost. I need your choice by noon tomorrow to book the second crew."

Check the arithmetic in the second option: 3 days of work with two crews is 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 days, so days 13 and the first half of 14.

Task 8 (level 3) Same facts, two listeners

Sample: "The electrical work is running about two days late because a part was back-ordered. We have two choices. We can bring in extra electricians, which costs more and keeps the delay to about one day. Or we keep the current crew, with no extra cost, and the finish moves about two days. Move-in is not affected yet. I'd like your choice by tomorrow noon." A good answer drops trade detail, talks about time and cost, and still asks for a decision.

Task 9 (level 3) Ahead of schedule

Yes. Sample: "Base course is done, one day ahead. The paving crew is free tomorrow. Can we bring the top course forward a day, or do you want them on the next lot?" The superintendent might move the next activity earlier, move the crew to another job, or keep the day as a buffer for weather.

Task 10 (level 3) Say it out loud

Open task. Check for all five parts, numbers in days, under 30 seconds, and a correct say-back.

Check yourself

  1. Where the job is now, so you can compare the plan with what is done.
  2. 3 ÷ 4 = 75%.
  3. Where we are, why, effect, the fix, what I need.
  4. Others can plan around it: move trades, order materials, tell the owner. A late surprise leaves fewer choices and costs trust.
  5. The owner hears the effect on time and cost and the decision needed, in plain words. Your foreman gets the trade detail: crews, materials and days.
