# Phone calls, voicemail and video calls at work

## Why this matters on the job

You will call in when you are running late, phone a supplier about a missing order, answer a client's questions, and sometimes walk a supervisor through a problem on a video call. On some jobs you will check in by phone because you are working alone. On a call, the other person cannot see you, the drawing or the problem. Your words have to carry the whole message.

## Key ideas

### Before you call

- Know why you are calling, in one sentence.
- Have the details in front of you: job address, order or work order number, names, dates.
- Have a pen, or a notes app open.
- Move somewhere quiet if you can.

### Making a call

1. **Greet and say who you are:** "Hi, this is Jordan Lee from Clearwater Roofing."
2. **Say why you are calling, right away:** "I'm calling about the skylight order for 14 Birch Street."
3. **Give the details, one at a time.**
4. **Say back what you agreed:** "So you'll deliver Thursday between 8 and 10. Is that right?"
5. **Close with the next step:** "I'll be on site to sign for it. Thanks."

### Answering a work phone and taking a message

Answer with the company and your name: "Clearwater Roofing, this is Jordan."

If the person they want is not there, take a message with:

- who called, and from what company
- their number, read back digit by digit
- what it is about
- when to call back

### Calling in late or sick

Follow your employer's rules about who to call and how early. Call before your shift starts. Say who you are, what is happening and when you expect to be in. Keep it short. A call is clearer than a text unless your employer says texting is fine.

### Voicemail that gets returned

- Your name and company.
- Your number, said slowly.
- Why you are calling, and what you need.
- By when.
- Your number again at the end.

Spell your name if it is easy to mishear. Keep it under about 30 seconds.

### Video calls

- Test your camera and microphone before the call.
- Find a quiet spot with light in front of you, not behind you.
- Mute when you are not talking. Unmute before you speak.
- In a group, say your name before you speak if people cannot see who is talking.
- Turn on captions if the app has them and it helps you or someone else.
- On a site walk by video, hold the phone steady, say where you are ("This is the north wall, second floor, near gridline C") and describe what the camera is showing.

### Group chats and texts

A crew group chat is good for quick updates, photos and times. It is not good for anything urgent or safety-related, because people may not see it for hours. For urgent or safety matters, speak in person or call. After an important phone call, a short text can confirm what was said: "Confirming: delivery Thursday 8 to 10 at 14 Birch." Do not post private information about other people.

### Choosing the mode

| Situation | Best choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Urgent, or about safety | In person, or a phone call | Fast and two-way |
| Detailed instruction | In person or a call, then a text or email to confirm | Two-way first, then a record |
| Quick update that is not urgent | Text or crew chat | People can read it when they can |
| Need to see a problem from far away | Video call or photos | The other person can see it |

### Checking in when you work alone

In BC, when a worker is assigned to work alone or in isolation, section 4.21 of the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation requires the employer to have a written procedure for checking the worker's well-being. The procedure must set the time between checks and say what happens if the worker cannot be contacted, including emergency rescue. A designated person makes contact at the set times and records the results. There must also be a check at the end of the shift. The regulation's note says visual or two-way voice contact is the preferred way to check.

That means a check-in call is a real safety call. Answer it, keep it clear and give the information your procedure asks for.

### Worked example

You are an apprentice at Clearwater Roofing (a fictional company). You call the client about access to the roof and get voicemail.

> "Hi, this is Jordan Lee from Clearwater Roofing. My number is 604-555-0137. I'm calling about the roof repair at 14 Birch Street. We need the side gate unlocked on Thursday morning so we can bring the ladder through. Can you call or text me by Wednesday at 4? Again, it's Jordan at 604-555-0137. Thanks."

It takes about 20 seconds. The client knows who called, why, what is needed, by when, and how to reply.
