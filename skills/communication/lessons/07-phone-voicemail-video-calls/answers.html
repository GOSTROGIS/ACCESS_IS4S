Communication · Lesson 07 · Answers

Phone calls, voicemail and video calls at work

Make and answer work calls, leave a voicemail that gets returned, run a video call, and pick the right way to send a message.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to other people's different communication modes and tools

Task 1 (level 1) Open the call

Sample answers. Each opening should say who you are and why you are calling.

  1. "Hi, this is Jordan Lee from Clearwater Roofing. I'm calling to check when the window order for the Maple Street job will arrive."
  2. "Hi Rae, it's Jordan. I'm at the Maple Street site and the gate is locked with no one here."
  3. "Hi, this is Jordan from Clearwater Roofing. I'm calling to let you know our crew will be about an hour late this morning."

Task 2 (level 1) Take a message

Message for Rae
From (name and company) Sunita Grewal, Peak Glass Supply
Phone number 604-555-0148
About Window order for the Maple Street job. Two units back-ordered until the 18th.
Call back by Today, before 4

The number must be read back to the caller, digit by digit. A message with the wrong number or no number is not usable, even if everything else is right.

Task 3 (level 2) Leave a voicemail

Sample answer:

"Hi, this is [name] from Granite Point Electrical. My number is 604-555-0162. I'm calling about 220 Harbour Road. We need to know if the power can be shut off on Monday from 9 to noon. Can you call or text me by Friday at noon? Again, that's [name] at 604-555-0162. Thanks."

A good voicemail has all five parts (name and company, number, why, what is needed, by when) and the number twice, and takes about 30 seconds or less.

Task 4 (level 2) Calling in late

Role play. A good call:

  • says who is calling
  • says what happened and how late they will be ("My car won't start. I'll be about 45 minutes late.")
  • says when they expect to arrive or what they are doing about it ("I've got a ride coming, so I'll be there by 7:45.")
  • is short and calm
  • ends with a check: "Is that okay, or do you want me to go straight to the other site?"

Task 5 (level 2) Pick the mode

  1. In person or a phone call to the supervisor right away. It is a safety hazard and needs fast, two-way contact.
  2. Text or crew chat. Not urgent, and everyone can read it when they can.
  3. Video call or photos. The supervisor needs to see it from far away.
  4. Text or email to confirm. The call already happened, and now there should be a record.
  5. In person. It is a detailed, hands-on instruction. Showing and checking work best face to face.

Accept other choices if the reason is sound, except for item 1, which must be fast and two-way.

Task 6 (level 2) Working alone check-in

A good check-in call gives everything the card asks for: "Lou, it's [name] at the [site] pump station, it's 11 a.m., I'm okay, next stop is [site]." Lou repeats it back.

What Lou needs to write down: the worker's name, the site, the time of the check-in, the status (okay or the problem) and the next stop. Under section 4.21(3) of the BC OHS Regulation, the designated person must record the results of each check.

Check yourself

  1. Who you are, and why you are calling.
  2. Name and company, phone number, why you are calling and what you need, by when, and the phone number again.
  3. People may not see it for hours, and you cannot be sure the right person got it. Safety needs fast, two-way contact.
  4. Any two of: the time interval between checks, what to do if the worker cannot be contacted (including emergency rescue), a designated person who makes contact and records the results, a check at the end of the shift.
  5. Any two of: test the camera and microphone, find a quiet spot, put light in front of you, know how to mute and unmute, turn on captions if they help.
