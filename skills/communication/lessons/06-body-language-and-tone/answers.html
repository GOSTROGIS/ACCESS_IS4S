Communication · Lesson 06 · Answers

Body language, tone and talking across a crew

Notice what people say without words, check what a cue means instead of guessing, and make yourself understood across PPE, noise and different first languages.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-3
  • Time: 70 min
  • Components: Listen with intention; Listen to understand; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 1) What does this face say?

Answers depend on the photos. There is no single right answer. A good response names a feeling for each photo and notices that people in the group did not all agree. The lesson: a face is a clue, so check before you act on what you think it means.

Task 2 (level 1) Change the stress, change the meaning

  1. I: someone else said it, not me.
  2. didn't: I deny saying it.
  3. say: I may have thought it or hinted it, but I did not say it.
  4. you: someone else broke it.
  5. broke: you did something else to it, such as lose it or scratch it.
  6. it: you broke something else.

Accept meanings in the learner's own words if they match.

Task 3 (level 2) Spot the listener

Depends on the role play. Typical answers:

  • Poor listening: looking at a phone, walking away mid-sentence, interrupting, carrying on working, turning away, answering a different question.
  • Good listening: stopping work, facing the speaker, nodding, short "okay" or "yep" responses, letting the speaker finish, repeating the key point back, asking a question.

A strong answer also notes that a brief look away is not proof of poor listening.

Task 4 (level 2) Check, do not assume

Sample questions. A good answer describes what was noticed and asks, without accusing.

  1. "You sighed when I asked. Is after break a bad time? I can ask someone else."
  2. "Before you start, can you walk me through the first two steps?"
  3. "I saw you looking at my work. Is something off? I'd rather fix it now."
  4. "You don't look sold on that. What would you change?"

Task 5 (level 2) Plain words

Sample answers. Preserve the original meaning without inventing a setting or measurement.

  1. "Finish this one before lunch. Then we start the next one." Show: point to the next one. Check: "Which one is next?" The original does not say to wait until after lunch.
  2. "Tighten it, but do not overtighten it. What tightness or torque and tool are required?" Once confirmed, demonstrate the approved method and ask the learner to show it back. The original does not specify hand-tightening or prohibit a wrench.
  3. "That needs a small adjustment. Which way and how far should it move?" Confirm the direction and amount, demonstrate, then check together. The original supplies neither a distance nor the exact fault.

A good answer removes slang, uses one idea at a time, and asks for missing technical details rather than guessing.

Task 6 (level 2) Read the figure

a) Arms crossed. The body is turned away. (Also accept "not facing the speaker.")

b) Any two: cold, tired, sore back, thinking, shy, comfortable that way, culture or habit, worried about something else.

c) Sample: "You've gone quiet. Does the plan work for you?"

Task 7 (level 3) You are the lead hand

Sample plan:

a) Move the crew away from the generator, or have it shut off for two minutes. Stand where everyone can see your face and the drawing.

b) Write the time, grid and quantity on a board or the drawing and point to it. Say each number and its digits.

c) Any gesture the crew agrees on, such as a raised fist for "stop work" or a thumbs-up for "ready." It must be agreed before work starts.

d) Ask each worker to say back their own task, or to point to their area on the drawing. Give one idea at a time.

Check yourself

  1. Any four of: body language or posture, facial expression, eye contact, gestures, tone of voice, how close you stand.
  2. The same cue can mean different things to different people. Only the person can tell you what it means.
  3. People often say yes to be polite or to avoid looking unsure. Ask them to say it back or show you.
  4. Any two of: move closer or both step away from the noise, use an agreed hand gesture, write the number down or point to the drawing, then check with a repeat-back.
