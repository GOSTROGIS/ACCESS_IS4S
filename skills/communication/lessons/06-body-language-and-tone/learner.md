# Body language, tone and talking across a crew

## Why this matters on the job

A lot of meaning travels without words: a sigh, a shrug, a raised voice, a step back. On a job site, a hard hat, safety glasses, a mask or hearing protection can hide half of those signals. Many BC crews also include people who grew up with different languages and different ways of showing respect. Misreading a cue can start an argument. Checking what it means can prevent one.

## Key ideas

**We say things without words.** Body language, facial expression, eye contact, gestures, tone of voice and how close we stand all send messages. Those messages can be positive or negative, and we often send them without noticing.

**Tone can change the meaning of the same words.** "Nice work" can be praise or sarcasm depending on how it is said.

**Show you are listening.** Stop what you are doing when you can. Face the person. Nod or say "okay" or "yep" as they go. Say the key point back. People show attention in different ways, so do not judge someone's listening by where they look. The proof is what they do or say back.

**Read the cue, then check it.** A cue is a clue, not a fact. Describe what you noticed and ask:

- "You went quiet when I said Saturday. Does Saturday not work for you?"
- "You're shaking your head. Did I get a measurement wrong?"

**Respect personal space.** Do not stand too close when you talk to someone. How close is comfortable differs from person to person.

**On site, PPE and noise hide cues.** Move closer, or both step away from the noise. Use gestures your crew has agreed on. Write numbers down or point to them on the drawing. Then check with a repeat-back.

**Across first languages:**

- Use plain words. Skip slang and sayings such as "eyeball it," "ballpark it" or "call it a day."
- Give one idea at a time.
- Show as well as tell: point, hold up the part, mark the drawing.
- Do not ask "Do you understand?" Many people say yes to be polite. Ask them to say it back or show you.
- An accent is not a mistake. The Skills for Success description of speaking clearly includes pronunciation "even with accent." Listen for the meaning.

### Worked example

A lead hand at Tidewater Mechanical (a fictional company) gives an instruction to Arman, who joined the crew last month and whose first language is not English:

> "Just eyeball the hangers, ballpark every four feet or so, and if it looks off, holler and we'll call it a day."

The same instruction in plain words, with showing and checking:

> "Put a hanger every 4 feet. Here, I'll mark the first two." (marks the joist) "If the pipe sags between them, stop and tell me. Can you show me where the third one goes?"

Arman points to the next mark at 4 feet. Now the lead hand knows the instruction landed.

## Check yourself

1. Name four ways people communicate without words.
2. Why is a cue "a clue, not a fact"?
3. Why is "Do you understand?" a weak way to check understanding? What can you do instead?
4. You are wearing hearing protection and a mask beside a running compactor. Name two things you can do to get a message across.
