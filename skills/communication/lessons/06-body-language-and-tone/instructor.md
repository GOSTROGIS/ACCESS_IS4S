# Body language, tone and talking across a crew

**Session:** Communication lesson 6 of 13.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 6 of the Communication series. The session runs 70 minutes. Ask who gave a specific compliment since last session and how it was received.

Hook: say "Great. Just great." twice, once warmly and once flatly with a sigh. Ask: "Same words. What changed?" Explain that today is about the part of the message that is not in the words.

## Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- notice body language, facial expression, gesture and tone, and show they are listening (Listen with intention)
- check what a cue means instead of assuming (Listen to understand)
- adapt how they speak for PPE, noise and crew-mates with different first languages (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

1. "How can you tell when someone is not listening to you?"
2. "What is hard about talking to someone on a job site when you both have PPE on?"

Notes for the instructor:

- Do not mark learners on eye contact, facial expression or how still they sit. Norms about eye contact and expression differ between people and communities, and some learners have disabilities that affect these. The original ACCESS sheet said "eye contact (where culturally appropriate)." Keep that.
- Do not describe what any culture or Nation "does" with body language. Teach learners to check meaning with the person in front of them.
- In Task 5, a learner whose first language is not English may be the best judge of which version is clearer. Invite, but do not require, their view.

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

1. "Say 'I'll do it' in a way that means yes, and in a way that means 'fine, whatever.'"
2. "A co-worker rolls her eyes when you mention the new schedule. What do you say?"
3. "Rewrite 'Eyeball it' in plain words."

## Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- body language, facial expression, eye contact, gesture, tone and distance
- how tone and stress change the meaning of the same words
- showing you are listening, and not judging listening by where someone looks
- reading a cue, then checking what it means
- getting a message across with PPE and noise
- plain words, one idea at a time, showing and checking with crew-mates who have different first languages

Action plan: before next session, notice one cue and check it out loud instead of guessing.
