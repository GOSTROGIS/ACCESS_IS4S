Communication · Lesson 06 · Instructor

Body language, tone and talking across a crew

Notice what people say without words, check what a cue means instead of guessing, and make yourself understood across PPE, noise and different first languages.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Listen with intention; Listen to understand; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 6 of the Communication series. The session runs 60 minutes. Ask who gave a specific compliment since last session and how it was received.

Hook: say "Great. Just great." twice, once warmly and once flatly with a sigh. Ask: "Same words. What changed?" Explain that today is about the part of the message that is not in the words.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • notice body language, facial expression, gesture and tone, and show they are listening (Listen with intention)
  • check what a cue means instead of assuming (Listen to understand)
  • adapt how they speak for PPE, noise and crew-mates with different first languages (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "How can you tell when someone is not listening to you?"
  2. "What is hard about talking to someone on a job site when you both have PPE on?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening, action plan check, tone hook, objective. Report back, respond to the hook. None 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Answer out loud. None 4 min
2.1 Task 1. Post five photos of people showing feelings such as concentration, confusion, frustration, boredom and interest. Use photos you have the right to use, such as stock images your organization licenses or photos staff volunteer. After learners write, compare answers. Walk around, write a feeling for each photo, compare. Five photos, tape 7 min
2.2 Teach "we say things without words" and "show you are listening." Run Task 3: with a helper, act out a short work conversation twice, first with poor listening (looking at a phone, walking away mid-sentence, interrupting) and then with good listening (stopping work, facing the speaker, nodding, repeating back). Flipchart both lists. Discuss: "Does looking away for a moment mean someone is not interested?" List the cues for each version, discuss. Helper (staff or volunteer learner), flipchart 10 min
2.3 Task 2. Model the first line, then let pairs do the rest. Say the sentence six ways, write the meanings. Learner page 6 min
2.4 Teach "read the cue, then check it." Run Task 4 and hear several check questions for each item. Write and share check questions. Learner page 8 min
2.5 Teach the PPE and noise points and the "across first languages" points. Read the worked example. Read both versions of the instruction, say what changed. Learner page 6 min
3.1 Task 5. Pairs read their plain versions to each other and the listener says or shows it back. Rewrite three instructions, test them on a partner. Learner page 6 min
4.1 Post-assessment questions. Answer. None 5 min
4.2 Summary and action plan. Choose one cue to check, out loud, before next session. None 3 min
Total 60 min

Notes for the instructor:

  • Do not mark learners on eye contact, facial expression or how still they sit. Norms about eye contact and expression differ between people and communities, and some learners have disabilities that affect these. The original ACCESS sheet said "eye contact (where culturally appropriate)." Keep that.
  • Do not describe what any culture or Nation "does" with body language. Teach learners to check meaning with the person in front of them.
  • In Task 5, a learner whose first language is not English may be the best judge of which version is clearer. Invite, but do not require, their view.

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Say 'I'll do it' in a way that means yes, and in a way that means 'fine, whatever.'"
  2. "A co-worker rolls her eyes when you mention the new schedule. What do you say?"
  3. "Rewrite 'Eyeball it' in plain words."

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • body language, facial expression, eye contact, gesture, tone and distance
  • how tone and stress change the meaning of the same words
  • showing you are listening, and not judging listening by where someone looks
  • reading a cue, then checking what it means
  • getting a message across with PPE and noise
  • plain words, one idea at a time, showing and checking with crew-mates who have different first languages

Action plan: before next session, notice one cue and check it out loud instead of guessing.
