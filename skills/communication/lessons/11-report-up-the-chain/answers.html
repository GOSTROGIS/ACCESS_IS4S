Report up the chain

Know who to tell, how fast and what to say for an unsafe condition, an injury, a delay or a mistake, and give a short report your supervisor can act on.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 1) Which kind?

a) D b) M c) U d) I e) D

Task 2 (level 1) How fast?

a) As soon as you know. b) Before it gets covered or built on, so right away. c) As soon as possible, and the work should stop if it is not safe. d) As soon as possible, even if it does not need first aid. e) As soon as you know.

Task 3 (level 2) Fill the card

Role-play sample. The protective actions below are proposed choices for this exercise, not facts supplied in the case. In a real report, state only what you actually did. What: "A junction box with live wires hanging out and no cover." Where: "Unit 310." When: "Found at 2:10." What I did: "I stayed back, kept people out of the unit and put tape across the door." What I need: "Someone qualified to make it safe, and a decision on whether we keep working on that floor."

A good answer does not touch the wires and does not leave the hazard unguarded while reporting. It reports in person or by phone because it is urgent.

Task 4 (level 2) Take out the blame

a) Sample: "There is material on the stairs. Can we check whether it blocks the route?" Confirm the exact material and location rather than adding a floor or blaming a trade. b) Sample: "The stringers appear to be cut incorrectly. Can you check the measurement with me against the drawing?" The prompt gives no stair identifier, dimension or measured error; do not invent them.

Task 5 (level 2) The rule

a) Report what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition or act as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer. b) Yes. WorkSafeBC says report all injuries and exposures as soon as possible, even if they do not need first aid, to your supervisor and to a first aid attendant if there is one.

Task 6 (level 2) Choose the mode

Sample: a) call or text. b) in person, so you can show it. c) in person, right away, because it is unsafe. d) in person, to the supervisor and first aid attendant. e) call. Reasons: unsafe and injury reports need to be fast and two-way. A delay that is not urgent can be a text, as long as you know it was read.

Task 7 (level 3) Own the mistake

Say it today, as soon as you find it, not tomorrow at 7:00. Sample: "I framed a window opening 50 mm too narrow. The window installer arrives tomorrow at 7:00. Can you check the opening with me and agree on the repair before then?" Give the actual location when reporting. Do not invent a room, repair duration or promise that a repair can be completed without checking.

Task 8 (level 3) Your supervisor is not there

a) Make it safe if you can do so without danger: keep people away, put up any barrier, cones or tape you have. Do not go into the trench. b) The next person up who can act: another supervisor, the superintendent or the employer. Section 3.10 says a supervisor or the employer. c) Sample: "At 6:30 a.m. I found the trench next to the sidewalk at [confirm exact location] left open overnight with no barrier. I could not reach you by phone. [State protective actions actually taken and who was reached, if anyone.] Please call me urgently." Do not claim barriers were placed or someone was told unless that happened.

Task 9 (level 3) Near miss

a) Yes. A load falling near a worker is an unsafe condition, and it could have caused a serious injury. Employers must investigate incidents like this. b) Sample: "A bundle of rebar slipped from a sling and landed one metre from a worker. Nobody was hurt. The crew suggested continuing. We need the lifting operation made safe and the cause checked before work resumes. [Add the actual location, time and actions taken.]" Do not invent a worker name or claim the crew stopped and isolated the sling unless confirmed. c) A small change in where someone stood would have meant a serious injury. Reporting it lets the cause be fixed before it happens again.

Task 10 (level 3) Report on a call

Open task. A strong call says who is calling, gives what, where, when, what was done and what is needed, answers the questions with facts, and ends with a read-back of the next step.

Check yourself

  1. Unsafe condition or act: as soon as possible. Injury or exposure: as soon as possible. Delay: as soon as you know. Mistake: before it is covered up or built on.
  2. Anyone who sees what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition or act must report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer. The person who receives the report must investigate and fix it without delay.
  3. Report it as soon as possible anyway, to your supervisor and to a first aid attendant if there is one.
  4. What, where, when, what I did, what I need.
  5. It is cheaper and faster to fix, and hiding it costs trust when it is found later.
