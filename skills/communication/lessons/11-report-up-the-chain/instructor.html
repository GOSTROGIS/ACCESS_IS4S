Report up the chain

Know who to tell, how fast and what to say for an unsafe condition, an injury, a delay or a mistake, and give a short report your supervisor can act on.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Session: Communication lesson 11 of 13.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 11 of the Communication series. The session runs 75 minutes. Ask who used tell, show, check, watch since last session.

Hook: "Which is worse to hear at 3:00 p.m.: 'I cut the header short this morning,' or 'I cut the header short this morning and we drywalled over it'?" Take answers. Today is about telling the right person, fast, in a way they can act on.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • sort a problem into unsafe, injury, delay or mistake, and report it at the right speed (Speak with purpose)
  • state the BC rules on reporting unsafe conditions and injuries (Speak with purpose)
  • give a short, blame-free report with what, where, when, what I did and what I need (Speak with clarity)
  • choose who to tell and how, including when the supervisor cannot be reached (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

  1. "Who did you last report a problem to at work? How did it go?"
  2. "What stops people from reporting a mistake?"

Learning tasks

Total 75 min

Post-assessment scenario cards (step 4.1)

  • "You twisted your ankle on a rebar end. It hurts but you can walk."
  • "The fuel delivery for the excavator is tomorrow, not today."
  • "You notched a joist deeper than the drawing allows."
  • "A guardrail post on the scaffold is loose."

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Give your card's report to your partner in 30 seconds." (Check for what, where, when, what I did, what I need.)
  2. "What kind of report was it, and how fast does it go?"
  3. "Who do you tell if your supervisor does not answer?"

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • four kinds of report: unsafe, injury, delay and mistake, and how fast each goes
  • section 3.10 and WorkSafeBC's rule on reporting injuries, and why near misses matter
  • the report-up card: what, where, when, what I did, what I need
  • facts first, no blame, and owning your own mistakes
  • choosing who to tell and how, and what to do when the supervisor is not there

Action plan: report one problem early, using the card, before next session.

Notes for the instructor

  • This is a new lesson that answers the 2026-11-08 skills audit.
  • "Make it safe" means only what a worker can do without putting themselves at risk: keep people away, tape off, tag out if trained. Never tell learners to fix electrical or structural hazards themselves.
  • Employer investigation duties are mentioned only so learners know why their report matters. Do not teach the investigation process.
  • Some learners may have been punished for reporting in the past. Acknowledge it. In BC, workers must not be disciplined for raising safety concerns through the proper steps (see lesson 4).
