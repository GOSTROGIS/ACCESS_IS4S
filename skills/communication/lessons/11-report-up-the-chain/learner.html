Report up the chain

Know who to tell, how fast and what to say for an unsafe condition, an injury, a delay or a mistake, and give a short report your supervisor can act on.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Speak with clarity; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Why this matters on the job

Every crew has bad news sometimes: a damaged part, a late delivery, a cut that went wrong, a guardrail that is gone. Bad news does not get better with time. A supervisor who hears it early can fix it, move the crew or call the client. A supervisor who hears it late, or from someone else, has fewer choices and less trust in you. Some reports are also the law. In BC, unsafe conditions and injuries must be reported as soon as possible.

Key ideas

Four kinds of report. Each one has a different speed.

Kind Example How fast
Unsafe condition or act Missing guardrail, frayed cord, someone working under a load As soon as possible. Stop the work if it is not safe to continue.
Injury or exposure A cut hand, something in your eye, breathing in a chemical As soon as possible, even if it does not need first aid
Delay Late delivery, broken tool, you will be late As soon as you know
Mistake Wrong length cut, hole in the wrong spot, wrong part ordered Before it gets covered up or built on

The rules for safety and injuries.

  • Section 3.10 of BC's Occupational Health and Safety Regulation says anyone who sees what appears to be an unsafe or harmful condition or act must report it as soon as possible to a supervisor or the employer. The person who gets the report must investigate and fix it without delay.
  • WorkSafeBC says workers must report all injuries and exposures to hazardous materials as soon as possible, even if they do not need first aid. Report to your supervisor, and to a first aid attendant if your workplace has one.
  • Employers must investigate certain incidents, including a near miss that could have caused a serious injury. Your report is where that starts.

Who to tell. Your supervisor first: usually your lead hand or foreman. If they cannot be reached and it is urgent, go to the next person up: the foreman or superintendent. Lesson 9 of the Collaboration series shows who is who on a site.

What to say: the report-up card. Keep it short and in this order:

  1. What happened or what you see.
  2. Where: floor, grid, room, piece of equipment.
  3. When it happened or started.
  4. What I did: made it safe, tagged it out, stopped, nothing yet.
  5. What I need: a decision, a person, a part, a call.

Facts first, no blame. Say what you saw, not who you think caused it. "The guardrail on the east side of level 3 is down" is a report. "The framers knocked the guardrail down again" starts an argument.

Own your mistakes. "I cut the header 20 mm short" is hard to say. It is much easier than explaining why you hid it. Lesson 5 covers taking criticism after.

Pick the mode. Unsafe and urgent: in person or a phone call. A delay that is not urgent: a call or text. Lesson 7 covers choosing the mode. If you text, make sure it was read. No answer means no report.

Worked example: three reports in one morning

Ana (fictional) is a second-year pipefitter at Riverbend Mechanical (fictional). Her foreman is Grant.

7:40, a delay. Ana texts Grant:

"Grant, the 4-inch valves for the boiler room aren't in the delivery. Driver says they're coming tomorrow. I'm starting the 2-inch runs in the meantime. Can you call the supplier?"

What, where, when, what she did and what she needs, in four lines.

9:15, unsafe. The scissor lift's platform gate will not latch. Ana does not text. She lowers the lift, puts a "Do not use" tag on it and walks to Grant:

"Scissor lift number 2 in the boiler room. The gate won't latch, as of 9:15. I lowered it and tagged it out. We need a mechanic, and I need another lift or a ladder plan for the hangers."

11:00, a mistake. Ana finds she cut two hangers 25 mm short. They are not installed yet.

"Grant, I cut two hangers 25 mm short for the line in grid C. They're not up. I've marked them as scrap. I need two more lengths of rod. It was my measurement. I'll measure off the drawing, not the old rod, from now on."

Grant was not happy about the hangers. He was glad to know before they went up.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Which kind? Write U (unsafe), I (injury), D (delay) or M (mistake).

a) _ The concrete truck will be 45 minutes late. b) You drilled a hole in the wrong stud bay. c) _ A labourer is cutting with the grinder guard removed. d) You got a splinter of steel in your thumb. e) ____ The rental generator will not start.

Task 2 (level 1) How fast? For each item in Task 1, write how fast you report it.

Task 3 (level 2) Fill the card. Write a report for this situation using all five parts.

You are an electrician's apprentice. At 2:10 you find a junction box in unit 310 with live wires hanging out and no cover. Nobody else is in the unit.

What: __________

Where: __________

When: __________

What I did: __________

What I need: __________

Task 4 (level 2) Take out the blame. Rewrite each report so it states facts only.

a) "The plumbers left their mess all over the stairs again." b) "Somebody who can't read a tape cut the stringers wrong."

Task 5 (level 2) The rule. Answer from the Key ideas.

a) What does section 3.10 say a person must do? b) Do you report an injury that did not need first aid? To whom?

Task 6 (level 2) Choose the mode. For each report in Task 1, choose in person, phone call or text. Give one reason for two of them.

Task 7 (level 3) Own the mistake. You are a second-year carpentry apprentice. You framed a window opening 50 mm too narrow. The window installer arrives tomorrow at 7:00. Write what you say to your lead hand, and when you say it.

Task 8 (level 3) Your supervisor is not there. You are on a road crew. At 6:30 a.m. you see a trench that was left open overnight, with no barrier, next to the sidewalk. Your foreman does not answer his phone.

a) What do you do first? b) Who do you tell next? c) Write the text you leave for your foreman.

Task 9 (level 3) Near miss. A bundle of rebar slipped from a sling and landed one metre from a worker. Nobody was hurt. The crew says, "No harm done, let's keep going."

a) Should this be reported? Why? b) Write the report you give your supervisor. c) Why does a near miss matter if nobody was hurt?

Task 10 (level 3) Report on a call. Role-play with a partner. You are calling your foreman to report a delay: the crane for this afternoon's lift is broken down, and the rental company says tomorrow. Use the card. Your partner asks two questions. Then switch, with a report of your own choice.

Check yourself

  1. Name the four kinds of report and how fast each one goes.
  2. What does section 3.10 require?
  3. What does WorkSafeBC say about reporting an injury that does not need first aid?
  4. Name the five parts of the report-up card.
  5. Why report a mistake before it is covered up?
