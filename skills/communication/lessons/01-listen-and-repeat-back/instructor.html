Listening and repeating back instructions

Catch every detail of a spoken job-site instruction and say it back so you both know you have it right.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Listen with intention; Listen to understand

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 1 of the Communication series. The session runs 60 minutes.

Hook (a fictional story): "A new apprentice was told to cut a stack of blocking at fourteen and a half. Over the saw, he heard forty and a half. He cut the whole stack before anyone checked. One sentence, said back, would have saved a morning of material." Ask: "Has an instruction ever gone sideways on you because of one word or one number?" Take two or three answers. No one has to share.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • get ready to receive a spoken instruction and pick out what, where, how much, when, who and safety (Listen with intention)
  • say an instruction back accurately, ask about missing parts and separate what was said from what they assumed (Listen to understand)

Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.2)

  1. "When your supervisor gives you an instruction, how do they know you understood it?"
  2. "What do you do when you only half hear an instruction?"

Listen for whether learners already use a repeat-back or only say "okay."

Drawing instructions (Task 1)

These come from the ACCESS Communication Series. Read them as written in both rounds.

  1. Starting 2 inches from the left-hand side of the paper, draw a 4-inch horizontal line near the bottom of your paper.
  2. From the left end of the line, draw a 3-inch vertical line up towards the top of the paper.
  3. From the right end of the line, draw a 3-inch vertical line up towards the top of the paper.
  4. Join the tops of the vertical lines.
  5. From the left corner, draw a 3-inch diagonal line upwards.
  6. At the top of that 3-inch line, draw another 3-inch diagonal line down to meet the top right-hand corner.

Step 5 says "the left corner" without saying top or bottom. Leave it as written. It is a good example of a gap a listener should ask about.

Post-assessment instruction (step 4.1)

"Before break, bring two buckets of the grey grout from the trailer to the shower in unit 3B. Don't open them yet. Lena wants to check the lot number first."

Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Say the instruction back to your partner." (Check: two buckets, grey grout, from the trailer, to the shower in 3B, before break, do not open, Lena checks the lot number first.)
  2. "Which of the six parts was not in that instruction?" (Answer: nothing important is missing, though WHO you report to when done is not stated.)
  3. "What will you say if you only catch half of an instruction tomorrow?"

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • getting ready to listen: stop, move out of the noise, have something to write on
  • the six parts of a job instruction: what, where, how much, when, who, safety
  • saying it back in order, with every number, and ending with a question
  • asking about gaps instead of guessing, and separating facts from assumptions
  • saying numbers and units so they are hard to mishear

Action plan: use one repeat-back at work or at home before next session and be ready to say what happened.

Note for the instructor: do not mark learners on eye contact, stillness or accent. The ACCESS summary sheet this lesson rebuilds said "eye contact (where culturally appropriate)." Keep that spirit. The evidence of listening is the accuracy of the repeat-back.
