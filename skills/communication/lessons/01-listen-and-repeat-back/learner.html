Listening and repeating back instructions

Catch every detail of a spoken job-site instruction and say it back so you both know you have it right.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Listen with intention; Listen to understand

Why this matters on the job

On a job site, instructions are often given once, out loud, over noise, by someone who is already walking to the next task. A wrong length, the wrong unit or the wrong material means rework, lost time and sometimes an injury. Saying the instruction back takes about ten seconds. It gives the person who gave it a chance to catch a mistake before you start.

Key ideas

Get ready to listen. Stop what you are doing when it is safe to stop. Move closer, or move both of you out of the noise. Have something to write on when numbers are coming: a notebook, the back of a drawing, a note on your phone.

Listen for the six parts of a job instruction. Most instructions answer some of these:

Part Ask yourself
WHAT What is the task or the material?
WHERE Which floor, unit, gridline, room or pile?
HOW MUCH How many, how long, what size?
WHEN Now, before break, by end of shift?
WHO Who else is involved, or who do I report to?
SAFETY What has to happen first, or what must I not do?

You will not always hear all six. If one you need is missing, ask for it.

Say it back. Keep it short and in the same order. Say every number. End with a question such as "Is that right?" Then wait. The other person confirms or corrects you.

Do not fill gaps with guesses. If you did not hear it, it is not part of the instruction yet. "I think he meant the east side" is a guess. Ask.

Make numbers hard to mishear. Fifteen and fifty, or thirteen and thirty, sound alike over a saw or a generator. Say the number, then the digits: "fifteen, one-five." Say the unit every time: feet, inches, millimetres. This helps on a crew where people speak different first languages, and it helps on a phone or radio.

Attention looks different in different people. Some people look at the speaker, some look at the drawing, some take notes. The proof that you listened is an accurate repeat-back, not where your eyes were.

Worked example

Dana, the lead hand at Northbank Framing Ltd. (a fictional company), stops you at the gate:

"Grab six two-by-sixes, twelve footers, from the pile by the east gate and take them up to unit 204 on the second floor. Stack them on the plywood by the window, not on the deck edge. Check with Marco first. He's still cutting in 204."

You say it back:

"Six two-by-sixes, twelve feet, from the east gate pile, to 204 on the second floor. Stack them on the plywood by the window, not the deck edge. Check with Marco first because he's cutting in there. Is that right?"

Dana answers: "Yes. Make it eight, not six."

You confirm the change: "Eight. Got it."

The repeat-back caught a change Dana had not mentioned the first time. If you had only said "okay," you would have made a second trip.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Draw what you hear. Your instructor reads drawing instructions twice. The first time, just draw. The second time, you may ask questions and say steps back. After both rounds, answer:

What made the first round hard?

What did you do differently in the second round?

Task 2 (level 1) Sort the instruction. Work in pairs. One partner reads the card below aloud once while the other listens without looking. The listener then fills in the table from memory. Switch roles with the second card.

Card A. "Take the red extension cord and the work light down to the parkade, level P2, by the elevator. Plug it into the panel marked TEMP 3, not the one marked TEMP 1. I need it set up before the concrete crew gets here at ten."

Card B. "After lunch, help Priya move the scaffold planks from bay 4 to bay 6. Twelve planks. Wear your gloves. Some of them have splinters. Tell me when you're done."

WHAT
WHERE
HOW MUCH
WHEN
WHO
SAFETY

Task 3 (level 2) Say it back and catch the gap. Your partner reads one card. Say it back. Then ask one question about the part that is missing. Switch roles.

Card C. "Go get the box of three-inch screws and bring it to the stairs."

Card D. "We need forty of the short brackets for the second floor by end of day."

What question did you ask for Card C? For Card D?

Task 4 (level 2) Fact or assumption. Read Card A again. For each statement, write F if Card A said it, or A if it is an assumption.

  1. The work light goes to level P2. ___
  2. The concrete crew will be late. ___
  3. TEMP 1 is broken. ___
  4. The setup has to be done before ten. ___
  5. The lead hand wants the cord plugged into TEMP 3. ___

Task 5 (level 2) Make it hard to mishear. Rewrite each phrase so it is clear over noise or on a phone.

  1. "Cut it at fifteen."
  1. "Bring the thirteen ones."
  1. "Move it over a bit."

Check yourself

  1. Name the six parts of a job instruction.
  2. Why do you end a repeat-back with a question?
  3. You heard "the north side" but you think the speaker meant the south side. What do you do?
  4. Give one way to say a number so that it is not mixed up with a number that sounds like it.
  5. Your partner looked at the floor the whole time you gave an instruction, then repeated it back perfectly. Were they listening? How do you know?
