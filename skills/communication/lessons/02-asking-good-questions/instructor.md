# Asking good questions on a new crew

**Session:** Communication lesson 2 of 13.

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 2 of the Communication series. The session runs 70 minutes. Ask who tried a repeat-back since last session and what happened.

Hook: "Think about the most useful question you have ever asked. At work, at school, anywhere. What made it useful?" Give learners one minute to think. Take two answers. Point out that a good question usually got them something they needed to act on.

## Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- introduce themselves to a crew and use and remember names (Speak with purpose)
- tell open from closed questions and choose the right one to learn or to confirm (Listen to understand, Speak with purpose)
- keep a work conversation going with follow-up questions, and ask without judgement (Listen to understand)

## Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.2)

1. "What is the first thing you would say to a new crew on your first morning?"
2. "What is the difference between a question that gets a 'yes' and a question that gets an explanation?"

## Learning tasks

| 3.3 | Task 4. Circulate and check each list has both kinds. | Write five first-day questions and mark them. | Learner page | 5 min |

Note: in Task 5, coach the new worker to keep to work topics. The original ACCESS lesson said "do not ask personal questions, especially if you do not know the person well." Keep that rule. Do not require learners to share personal or family details in any round.

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

1. "Turn this into an open question: 'Do you need help?'" (Example: "What can I help with?")
2. "Give one closed question you would use to confirm an instruction."
3. "How would you ask about a co-worker's method without sounding like you think it is wrong?"

## Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- a short introduction, using names and asking again when you forget
- closed questions to confirm and open questions to learn
- follow-up questions that keep a conversation going
- keeping early conversations to work and general topics
- asking without blame, and choosing the right moment to ask

Action plan: introduce yourself to one person you have not met and ask them one open question before next session.
