Communication · Lesson 03 · Instructor

Assertive, passive and aggressive responses

Tell the three ways people respond under pressure apart, speak up assertively on a crew, and set small goals to practise.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 3 of the Communication series. The session runs 75 minutes. Ask who introduced themselves to someone new since last session.

Hook: "Picture a co-worker who leaves a mess in your work area every single day. What would you do on day one? On day ten?" Take a few answers. Point out that many people say nothing on day one and blow up on day ten. Today is about the middle path.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • tell passive, aggressive and assertive responses apart in workplace situations (Speak with purpose)
  • rewrite and say a response assertively, using what happened, why it matters and what they would like (Speak with purpose)
  • choose how to speak up for the person and the situation, and set small, specific goals for practice (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.2)

  1. "What does the word 'assertive' mean to you?"
  2. "Think of a time someone spoke up to you in a way that worked. What did they do?"

Learning tasks

Step Instructor activities Learner activities Resources Time
1.1 Opening, check last session's action plan, hook, objective. Report back, answer the hook. None 5 min
1.2 Pre-assessment questions. Answer out loud. None 5 min
2.1 Warm-up line. Learners stand in a line. Read six statements from Task 4. Learners step forward if the statement is usually true for them, back if it is rarely true, or stay put. They return to the line after each one. Anyone may stay put for every item. Ask: "What did you notice? What does this tell you about assertiveness?" Move or stay put, then discuss. Task 4 statements 8 min
2.2 Brainstorm the three styles. Ask: "What does an aggressive person look and sound like? An assertive person? A passive person?" Flipchart three columns, then compare with the table on the learner page. Stress that assertive and aggressive both speak up, and the difference is how. Call out behaviours, compare with the table. Flipchart, markers, learner page 10 min
2.3 Teach the three-part assertive statement. Read the worked example, all three versions, in the voice each one would really use. Identify the three parts in the assertive version. Learner page 7 min
3.1 Task 1. Learners read silently and decide. Then go card by card: a volunteer reads aloud, the group says which style, the reader says if they agree, and the group explains why. Read, decide, discuss. Learner page 10 min
3.2 Task 2. Circulate and check each rewrite has all three parts. Rewrite three responses. Learner page 7 min
3.3 Task 3 in pairs. Ask two volunteers to show their assertive version to the group. Group gives feedback on what worked first. Act one card three ways, give and receive feedback. Learner page 8 min
3.4 Tasks 4 and 5. Say clearly that Task 4 is not a test and there is no score. Collect goal sheets if learners agree, to hand back at the end of the series. Rate the statements privately, write three goals. Learner page 8 min
4.1 Post-assessment questions. Answer. None 4 min
4.2 Summary and action plan. Choose one of their three goals to try before next session. None 3 min
Total 75 min

Notes for the instructor:

  • The self-check in Task 4 is a prompt for reflection, not a valid psychological test. Do not total, compare or rank responses. This follows the note in the original ACCESS lesson.
  • Norms about speaking up differ between families, communities and workplaces. Do not tell learners what their culture expects and do not ask anyone to explain their community's norms. Let learners decide when assertive speaking fits for them.
  • The original series included a group discussion about stigma and labels. It is not part of this lesson, because it asks learners to disclose personal circumstances.

Post-assessment (4 min, step 4.1)

  1. "Your lead hand says, in front of the crew, 'Did you even read the drawing?' Give a passive, an aggressive and an assertive reply."
  2. "Which part of the assertive statement do people most often leave out? Why does it matter?"
  3. "Read one of your goals. Is it specific enough that you will know when you have done it?"

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • the passive, aggressive and assertive styles and what each one costs
  • how assertive and aggressive both speak up, but differ in how
  • the three-part assertive statement: what happened, why it matters, what you would like
  • assertiveness depends on the situation, and people choose what fits them
  • small, specific goals for practice

Action plan: try one of your three goals before next session and be ready to say how it went.
