# Assertive, passive and aggressive responses

## Task 1 (level 1) Name the response

1. P. Says nothing and gives up the space.
2. Ag. Brushes off the feedback with an attack and walks away.
3. As. Accepts the joke but states a clear limit and a clear request.
4. Ag. Sarcasm is an aggressive response even without yelling.
5. P. Says it is fine when it is not.
6. As. Names the facts and makes a polite, direct request.
7. P. Hinting instead of asking.

## Task 2 (level 2) Make it assertive

Sample answers. Check that each rewrite has what happened, why it matters and what you would like, and that it does not attack.

- Card 1: "I just cleaned and set up this area for the next pour. Can you stack your material over by the column instead?"
- Card 2: "You're right, I'll check the drawing and move them. Can you show me where the dimension is so I get it right next time?"
- Card 4: "You've borrowed my tape every day this week and I've had to go find it. Can you pick one up from the crib today? You can use mine this morning."
- Card 5: "Being late three times this week is putting me in a bad spot with my foreman. Can we leave ten minutes earlier, or can you text me by six if you're running late?"
- Card 7: "Kai, can you give me a hand lifting this door? It's too heavy for one."

## Task 3 (level 2) Three ways

This is a role play. A good assertive version:

- uses a steady voice and normal volume
- names the facts without insults or sarcasm
- says why it matters
- asks for one clear action

Good feedback from the partner names a specific thing that worked ("You said what you needed without calling me lazy").

## Task 4 (level 1) Where am I already assertive?

There are no right or wrong ratings and no total. A complete response has every statement rated, one area where the learner is already assertive and one area they want to work on.

## Task 5 (level 2) Set three small goals

Goals vary. A good goal names what the learner will do, with whom and when. Examples:

- "Tomorrow at the toolbox talk, I will ask one question."
- "This week, when Sam borrows my tape, I will ask for it back before lunch."
- "On Friday I will ask my supervisor to check my hours on the timesheet."

A goal such as "be more confident" is not specific enough. Ask the learner: "What will you do, with whom and when?"

## Task 6 (level 2) Place it on the line

1. P 2. Ag 3. As 4. P (hinting)

## Task 7 (level 3) You are the lead hand

a) Sample: "When you grind on this side, the sparks land by the fuel cans. That could start a fire. Can you turn the work around or put up a screen?"

b) Sample: "I respect the 30 years. This is about the fuel cans, not your skill. I need the sparks pointed away from them." A good answer stays calm, does not argue about experience and repeats the request.

c) This is now a safety issue. Stop the work if you can do it safely, and report it to the supervisor right away. A good answer moves from asking to reporting.

## Check yourself

1. Both say what is on the person's mind. Aggressive aims to hurt or put down. Assertive is clear and calm with no intention to hurt.
2. The other person has to guess what you want, and they may not pick up on it. Your need does not get said.
3. What happened, why it matters, what you would like.
4. Example: "On Wednesday, when my ride is late again, I will tell the driver I need a text by six if he is running late." Any goal that says what, with whom and when is correct.
