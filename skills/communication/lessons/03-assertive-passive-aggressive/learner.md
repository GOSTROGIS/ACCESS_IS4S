# Assertive, passive and aggressive responses

## Why this matters on the job

Every crew has moments where someone needs to speak up: a trade stacks material in your work area, a co-worker keeps borrowing your tools, someone jokes at your expense, or you need help with a heavy lift. Saying nothing lets the problem grow. Blowing up damages the relationship and can get you sent home. Speaking up clearly and calmly gets the problem fixed and keeps the crew working together.

## Key ideas

There are three common ways to respond.

| | Passive | Aggressive | Assertive |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it sounds like | Says nothing, hints, over-apologizes | Yells, threatens, name-calls, sarcasm, gossip | Says what is on their mind, calmly and directly |
| Respect for self | Low | High | High |
| Respect for others | High | Low | High |
| What often happens | The problem continues. Frustration builds and may blow up later | The point may get across, but people feel humiliated or pushed around | The problem gets named and there is room to solve it |

**Assertive and aggressive both speak up.** The difference is how. An aggressive response aims to hurt, threaten or put down. An assertive response aims to be clear, with no intention to hurt.

**Hinting is passive.** "I wish I knew someone who could help me carry this door" leaves the other person guessing. "Can you give me a hand with this door?" is clear.

**A simple assertive statement has three parts.**

1. What happened, as facts: "When the cords get left across the doorway..."
2. Why it matters: "...someone could trip carrying sheets."
3. What you would like: "Can we run them along the wall?"

**Use a steady voice and normal volume.** Face the person if you can. Assertive does not mean loud.

**Assertiveness depends on the situation.** Most people are assertive in some situations and not in others. You may find it easy to speak up with friends and hard with a supervisor, or the other way around.

**Norms differ.** Families, communities and workplaces have different ideas about when and how to speak up, especially to an older person or someone in charge. You choose what fits you and the situation. The goal is to be able to speak up when you decide to.

**Practise with small, specific goals.** Start small. Take one small risk at a time. Build from easier situations to harder ones. Make the goal specific. "I want to be more assertive at work" is hard to act on. "On Monday I will ask my lead hand to show me the lift plan before I start" is easy to act on and easy to check.

### Worked example

On a renovation job at Westridge Interiors (a fictional company), a co-worker named Tyler leaves his offcuts and cords in your work area every afternoon.

- **Passive:** You say nothing and clean up after him every day.
- **Aggressive:** "Are you blind? Clean up your garbage or I'll throw it in the bin with your saw."
- **Assertive:** "Tyler, when your offcuts and cords end up in my area, I lose time and someone could trip. Can you clear them before you head out?"

The assertive version names the facts, says why it matters and asks for one clear action.
