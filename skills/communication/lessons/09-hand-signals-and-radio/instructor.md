# Hand signals and radio on a job site

## Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 9 of the Communication series. The session runs 60 minutes. Ask who led or helped lead a talk since last session.

Hook: without speaking, use only gestures to guide one volunteer from the door to a chair across the room. Then ask the group: "What made that work? What would have happened if three of us were waving at once?" Lead into why crane work relies on a qualified signaller and one set of standard signals.

## Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

- state the BC rules on who signals a crane, which signals are used, who can stop a lift, and when a radio must be used (Listen with intention)
- read and name signals from the WorkSafeBC hand signal chart (Adapt to other people's different communication modes and tools)
- give a short radio message and confirm it with a read-back (Speak with clarity)

## Pre-assessment (4 min, step 1.2)

1. "Have you worked near a crane, boom truck or hoist? How did the crew communicate?"
2. "If you saw someone walk under a load, what would you do?"

Notes for the instructor:

- Do not describe signals from memory. Use the printed WorkSafeBC chart, and correct learners against the chart only.
- If a learner works or plans to work as a rigger, signaller or crane operator, point them to formal training. Section 14.34(2) requires an operator to show familiarity with the code of signals before operating, and section 15.2 requires rigging work to be done or directly supervised by qualified workers familiar with the code of signals.
- The radio pattern in this lesson is a teaching pattern, not a WorkSafeBC procedure. Learners should follow the radio rules of their own site.

## Post-assessment (5 min, step 4.1)

1. "Who must the operator stop for?"
2. "Name one situation where a radio must replace hand signals."
3. "Give me a radio call to tell the lead hand that the gate lock is broken, with a read-back."

## Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

- why crane work needs a qualified signaller when the operator cannot see
- the operator must stop for a stop signal from anyone
- the standard hand signals in Figure 15-1 of the Regulation, and WorkSafeBC's TG 07-29 chart
- when a radio or other system must replace hand signals, and the ban on multi-channel radios for directing crane movement
- clear radio habits: call, wait, short message, clear numbers, read-back, and no answer means no message

This is the last session of the Communication series. Hand back goal sheets and ask each learner to name one communication habit from the series they will keep using.
