Hand signals and radio on a job site

Know the BC rules for crane hand signals and radios, read the official signal chart, and give and confirm a short radio message.

  • Learner
  • Instructor
  • Answers
  • Level: 1-2
  • Time: 60 min
  • Components: Listen with intention; Speak with clarity; Adapt to other people's different communication modes and tools

Task 1 (level 1) Know the rules

  1. Any person. The operator must stop on a stop signal from anyone (OHS Regulation section 14.47(2)).
  2. When the operator does not have a clear, unobstructed view of the boom, jib, load line, load hook and load through the whole lift (section 14.47(1)).
  3. The standard hand signals in Figure 15-1 of the Regulation (section 15.20).
  4. When distance, weather or other conditions make hand signals hazardous or impracticable (section 14.48(1)).

Task 2 (level 1) Read the chart

The printed WorkSafeBC chart (TG 07-29) is the answer key. A signal is correct only if it matches the drawing and description on the chart. Count a signal as named correctly only if the partner gave the chart's name for it. A good response also corrects the mixed-up signal using the chart.

Task 3 (level 2) Radio relay

The first person heard: "Pump truck at gate three at ten-fifteen. Keep the north lane clear."

What the last person says will vary. Common changes are a wrong gate number, a wrong time ("ten-fifty"), or a lost second part ("keep the lane clear" with no direction). A good answer to "why" points to a missed read-back, numbers that sound alike, or a long message passed in one breath. Groups that used a read-back at every step usually keep the message intact.

Task 4 (level 2) Write the call

Sample answer:

You: "Dana, this is [your name]."

Dana: "Go ahead."

You: "Scissor lift on level three has a flat tire. Do you want me to tag it out of service?"

Dana: "Copy, flat tire on the level three scissor lift. Yes, tag it out. I'll call the rental company."

You: "Tag it out, you're calling the rental company. Copy."

A good call says who is calling whom, waits for an answer, gives one short message with the level clearly stated, asks the question, and ends with a read-back from both sides.

Task 5 (level 2) What does the rule say?

  1. Section 14.47(1). The operator cannot see the load for part of the lift, so the operator must act only on the directions of a qualified signaller who has a clear view of what the operator cannot see.
  2. Section 14.48(1). Weather is making hand signals hazardous or impracticable, so a two-way radio or another audio or video system acceptable to WorkSafeBC must be used.
  3. Section 14.47(2). The operator must stop. A stop signal from any person must be obeyed.
  4. Section 14.49(2). Multi-channel radios are not permitted for directing crane or hoist movement.

Check yourself

  1. So the operator acts on directions from a qualified person who knows the signals and can see what the operator cannot, instead of guessing or following whoever happens to wave.
  2. Yes. Section 14.47(2) says the operator must stop on receiving a stop signal from any person.
  3. In Figure 15-1 of the BC OHS Regulation, and in WorkSafeBC's toolbox meeting guide "Hand signals for hoist and crane operations" (TG 07-29).
  4. The message did not get through. Try again, or go in person.
