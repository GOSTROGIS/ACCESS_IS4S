# Hand signals and radio on a job site

## Why this matters on the job

When a crane operator cannot see the load, or the site is too loud to hear, the crew talks with hands and radios. One wrong signal or a missed word can drop a load or swing it into someone. BC's Occupational Health and Safety Regulation sets out who may signal a crane, which signals to use and when a radio must be used instead. Even if you never signal a crane, you will work near lifts, and you need to know what you are seeing and when you can call a stop.

This lesson is an introduction. It does not qualify anyone to signal a crane or rig a load.

## Key ideas

### Crane and hoist signals: the BC rules

| Rule | Where it is |
|---|---|
| When the crane or hoist operator does not have a clear, unobstructed view of the boom, jib, load line, load hook and load through the whole lift, the operator must act only on the directions of a **qualified signaller** who has a clear view of what the operator cannot see. | OHS Regulation section 14.47(1) |
| The operator must **stop** when they get a stop signal from **any person**. | Section 14.47(2) |
| If hand signals are used between a signaller and the operator, the **standard signals in Figure 15-1** of the Regulation must be used. | Section 15.20 |
| If distance, weather or other conditions make hand signals hazardous or impracticable, a **two-way radio** or another audio or video system acceptable to WorkSafeBC must be used. | Section 14.48(1) |
| **Multi-channel radios** are not permitted for directing crane or hoist movement. | Section 14.49(2) |
| Rigging and slinging must be done by, or directly supervised by, qualified workers familiar with the rigging and with the code of signals. | Section 15.2 |

The official chart is in Figure 15-1 of the Regulation. WorkSafeBC also publishes it as a toolbox meeting guide, "Hand signals for hoist and crane operations" (TG 07-29), at worksafebc.com.

### What the signals look like

Section 15.20 refers to "Figure 15-1." That figure is in the Regulation, and WorkSafeBC prints the main signals on its toolbox meeting guide TG 07-29. The drawings below show the 12 signals on TG 07-29. Two more signals from the ASME B30.5 standard for mobile cranes are marked with *. Each signaller is drawn from the front, the way the operator sees them. Red marks show movement.

These drawings help you recognize signals. They do not replace the official chart, your site's chart or signaller training.

### Why the rules look like this

- **A qualified signaller with a clear view** means the operator acts on directions from someone trained in the signals who can see what the operator cannot, not on waves from whoever happens to be nearby.
- **Anyone can stop the lift** because whoever sees the danger first may not be the signaller.
- **Standard signals** mean any trained operator can read any trained signaller, on any site.
- **The radio takes over when signals cannot be seen.** A signal no one can see is no signal.

### Talking on a radio

These habits make any radio call clearer. They are a pattern for practice in this class. Your site may have its own radio rules, and those come first.

1. **Wait for a gap.** Do not talk over someone else.
2. **Say who you are calling, then who you are:** "Dana, this is Jordan."
3. **Wait for an answer** before you give the message.
4. **Keep it short.** One message at a time.
5. **Say numbers clearly,** digit by digit if needed: "Gate two. Two."
6. **Ask for, or give, a read-back:** "Copy that, truck at gate two, you're coming down."
7. **No answer means no message.** If you do not hear a reply, the message did not get through. Try again, or go in person.

### Worked example

Jordan, a labourer at Harbourline Builders (a fictional company), sees a concrete truck arrive at the wrong gate. Jordan radios the lead hand on the general site channel. This is not a crane call.

> Jordan: "Dana, this is Jordan."
>
> Dana: "Go ahead, Jordan."
>
> Jordan: "Concrete truck is at gate two, not gate one. Driver's asking where to set up."
>
> Dana: "Copy. Truck at gate two. Tell him to wait. I'm coming down."
>
> Jordan: "Wait at gate two, you're coming down. Copy."

Each person said back the key fact. Both know the message got through.
