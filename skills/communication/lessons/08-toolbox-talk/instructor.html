Giving a short toolbox talk

Plan and deliver a five-minute safety talk to a crew, with a clear opening, a few key points, something to show, a check and a closing action.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 100 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Session: Communication lesson 8 of 13.

Accessible participation

Use captions, an interpreter or a permitted accessible transcript when needed; the no-transcript exercise must not remove access. A learner may deliver a talk seated, with AAC or with a co-presenter. Agree on extra time for interpretation or assistive communication. Assess the message, structure and audience check, not a particular posture or unaided speaking speed.

Opening (5 min, step 1.1)

Welcome learners to session 8 of the Communication series. The session runs 100 minutes. Ask who recorded a voicemail greeting or made a work-style call since last session.

Hook: "Think of the best toolbox talk, safety meeting or class you ever sat through, and the worst. What was the difference?" Take answers and flipchart them in two columns. Keep the list up for the whole session.

Objective (shared during the opening)

By the end of the session, learners will be able to:

  • plan a five-minute toolbox talk with an opening, up to three key points, something to show, a check and a closing action (Speak with purpose)
  • deliver it so a group can hear and follow it, using voice, pauses and posture (Speak with clarity)
  • adapt the talk for the crew in front of them and record the meeting (Adapt to your audience and contexts)

Pre-assessment (5 min, step 1.2)

  1. "Have you ever had to speak in front of a group? What was hard about it?"
  2. "What do you think a toolbox talk is for?"

Notes for the instructor:

  • The original ACCESS plan used a Martin Luther King Jr. clip and a Steve Jobs clip. This lesson keeps the King speech only. Link to it or play an excerpt. Do not reproduce the text. The Martin Luther King, Jr. Research and Education Institute notes that it cannot give permission to reproduce King's writings or statements.
  • In step 2.2, do not supply examples of Indigenous speakers yourself, and do not describe how any Nation speaks or presents. Use only what learners choose to share, credited to the person they name.
  • Learners who are anxious about speaking may give their talk seated, to a smaller group, or with a partner showing the item. The goal is a clear, useful talk, not a performance.
  • Only the factual points from the WorkSafeBC guide should be presented as facts. Learners add fictional local details, such as names, for practice.

Post-assessment (3 min, step 4.1)

  1. "What will you do differently the next time you speak to a group?"
  2. "Where can you find a free toolbox meeting guide on almost any safety topic?"
  3. "What three things should a meeting record show?"

Summary (3 min, step 4.2)

During this session we covered:

  • what a toolbox talk is for, and the section 3.2 monthly meeting and record requirement for small workplaces
  • the five parts: open, up to three points, show, check, close with one action
  • preparing with a WorkSafeBC toolbox meeting guide and adding local details
  • delivery: being heard, pauses, steady posture, looking around the group
  • adapting to the crew, and learning from strong speakers

Action plan: offer to lead, or help lead, one real toolbox talk or the opening of a class session before the series ends.
