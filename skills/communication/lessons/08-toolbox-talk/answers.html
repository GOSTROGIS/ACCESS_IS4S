Giving a short toolbox talk

Plan and deliver a five-minute safety talk to a crew, with a clear opening, a few key points, something to show, a check and a closing action.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2-3
  • Time: 100 min
  • Components: Speak with clarity; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Task 1 (level 2) Watch a strong speaker

Answers depend on the excerpt played. A good response gives at least one specific observation for each line, in the learner's own words, about what they actually saw and heard. Examples of the kind of observation to accept:

  • Voice: "He gets louder and slower on the important lines."
  • Pauses: "He stops after a big line before going on."
  • Repetition: "He starts several sentences with the same words, so the main idea sticks." (The published text of the speech repeats "I have a dream" at the start of several sentences.)
  • Body and face: "He stands still and looks out at the crowd."

Do not accept observations copied from something the learner read about the speech. The task is to practise noticing.

Task 2 (level 1) Stand three ways

This is a speaking activity. Most groups say the third version (steady, hands free, looking around the group) made them want to listen, because the speaker looked sure of the topic. A good answer gives a reason.

Task 3 (level 2) Plan your talk

Plans vary with the guide chosen. A good plan has:

  • one topic tied to today's or this week's work
  • a clear reason it matters here
  • no more than three key points, each taken from the guide
  • something real to show
  • a check question that needs more than "yes" or "no"
  • one closing action people can do today

Example for "The right to refuse unsafe work": see the worked example on the learner page.

Task 4 (level 3) Give your talk

The checklist is the answer key. A talk that meets the level 3 task:

  • opens with the topic and why it matters in the first few sentences
  • has no more than three points
  • shows something
  • can be heard at the back of the group
  • pauses after key points
  • asks a real check question and waits for an answer
  • closes with one action
  • finishes in three minutes or less

Feedback from the group should name something specific ("When you held up the harness, I got it") rather than "good job."

Task 5 (level 2) Fill in the record

A complete record has the date and time, the topic of each talk given in the group, the names of everyone who attended and any issues or suggestions raised, or "none." It does not need to be written as formal minutes.

Task 6 (level 3) Change the audience

Sample openings for a talk on the right to refuse unsafe work:

  • Experienced crew: "You all know you can refuse unsafe work. Quick reminder on how we do it on this site, because the rules were updated in 2022 and a couple of you asked who our worker rep is now."
  • First-week apprentices: "Today's talk is about saying no to unsafe work. You have the right to do this. I will show you the three steps on this sheet." (holds up the guide)

Note on the experienced-crew example: WorkSafeBC states that amendments to the refusal rules took effect on August 22, 2022. Learners do not need to include that date. A good answer changes the level of detail and the words for each audience while keeping the facts the same.

Task 7 (level 3) Fix the talk

Missing or weak: the open does not say why ladders matter today or here. The key points are vague ("lots of rules"). Nothing is shown. The check ("Any questions?") gets a yes or no. There is no closing action.

Sample rewrite: "We're on ladders all day today, hanging the soffit. Two points. One, set the ladder on firm, level ground and tie it off. Two, keep three points of contact when you climb. Here's the tie-off strap we're using. Jess, what do you check before you climb? Before you climb today, check your footing with me."

Task 8 (level 2) Time it

a) 30 + 180 + 45 + 45 + 15 = 315 seconds = 5 minutes 15 seconds. b) Over by 15 seconds. c) Sample: cut each key point to 55 seconds (saves 15 seconds), or cut the talk to two key points. Key points are the longest part, so a small cut there is easiest. Do not cut the check or the close.

Check yourself

  1. Open, two or three key points, show something, check, close with one action.
  2. People remember a few points much better than many, and five minutes does not allow time to explain more.
  3. Hold regular monthly meetings with workers to discuss health and safety, make sure the meetings deal with correcting unsafe conditions and practices, and keep a record of the meetings and what was discussed.
  4. Any two of: stand away from noise where everyone can see and hear, speak to the person farthest away, pause after key points, stand steady with hands free, look around the whole group, show as well as tell.
