Giving and taking feedback and criticism

Give thanks and corrections that are specific and fair, take criticism without getting defensive, and know when criticism crosses into bullying.

  • Learner
  • Instructor🔒 (instructor code)
  • Answers🔒 (instructor code)
  • Level: 2
  • Time: 75 min
  • Components: Listen to understand; Speak with purpose; Adapt to your audience and contexts

Why this matters on the job

An apprentice is corrected many times a day. That is how a trade is learned. A journeyperson has to correct others without starting a fight. A crew that also notices good work and says thanks gets along better and works safer. Employers notice people who can hear "that's wrong, do it again" and simply do it again.

Key ideas

Giving thanks and compliments

Be specific. "Good job" is nice. "Thanks for holding that ladder steady on the last run. I felt safe up there" tells the person exactly what they did right.

Be sincere. Only say what you mean.

Pick the time and place. Some people like praise in front of the crew. Some would rather hear it one-on-one.

How you say it matters as much as what you say. A flat, rushed "thanks" can sound like the opposite.

When you get a compliment, accept it. "Thanks, I appreciate that" is enough. Brushing it off ("It was nothing, anyone could have done it") tells the person their thanks did not land.

Giving a correction

  1. What I saw, as facts: "The cords were run across the walkway again this morning."
  2. Why it matters: "Someone carrying sheets could trip."
  3. What to do: "Run them along the wall and tape the crossing."

Talk about the work, not the person. Do it in private when you can. Keep your voice steady.

Taking criticism: the A B C D techniques

Fear of criticism is one of the biggest things that stops people from speaking up. These four techniques help you answer criticism directly and honestly. They are taught one at a time, but people often combine them in one reply. They feel stiff at first, like any new skill, and get easier with practice.

Technique What it does Example
Acknowledge Accept a fair point about a mistake, without grovelling. "It was careless of you to leave the gang box open overnight." / "Yes, I left it open. I'll check it before I leave from now on."
Boundaries Say how you expect to be treated. (In front of the crew) "You're an idiot for leaving that box open!" / "Can we talk over here for a minute?" (Away from the crew) "I get that you're frustrated. I left it open and that was careless. I'd appreciate it if you told me in private and dropped the name-calling."
Clarify Ask for details so you can fix the real problem. "Your setup is a mess." / "I want to fix it. Which part of the setup is the problem? Can you show me?"
Disagree Calmly push back on a statement that is not true, and stay open to a fair point. "You're totally disorganized. The box was open and it's your fault!" / "I disagree that I'm disorganized. I keep my area tidy and get my work done. I did leave the box open last night, and I'm happy to talk about a close-up checklist."

Skip the excuses. "The saw's dull," "nobody told me," "it's not my fault" can be true, but leading with an excuse makes it sound like you are not listening. Acknowledge first. Explain after, if it helps fix the problem.

Criticism or bullying?

Fair feedback is part of work. Bullying and harassment is not. WorkSafeBC defines workplace bullying and harassment as any inappropriate conduct or comment by a person toward a worker that the person knew or reasonably ought to have known would cause that worker to be humiliated or intimidated. It does not include reasonable action by an employer or supervisor about managing and directing workers, such as managing performance, reasonable discipline or assigning work.

WorkSafeBC gives examples of bullying and harassing behaviour, including yelling, humiliating initiation practices or hazing, spreading malicious rumours and calling someone derogatory names. It also says that expressing differences of opinion and offering constructive feedback about work-related behaviour are not bullying.

Workers must report bullying and harassment they see or experience, following their employer's procedures. Every employer must have procedures for reporting. More information is at worksafebc.com/bullying.

Worked example

At Kettle Creek Concrete (a fictional company), the lead hand, Maria, says to you in the morning: "You left the gang box open overnight. Somebody could have walked off with the tools."

Defensive reply: "It's not my fault, Jesse was the last one out."

Acknowledge and clarify: "You're right, I didn't check it. Do you want me to be the one who locks up from now on, or should we make a close-up list?"

The second reply takes the point, avoids blame, and moves to fixing it.

Practice

Task 1 (level 1) Make it specific. Rewrite each compliment so it names exactly what the person did.

  1. "Nice work today."
  1. "You're a good helper."
  1. "Thanks for everything."

Task 2 (level 1) Pass it on. Your instructor passes a wrapped package around the group. When you receive it, give the next person a sincere, specific compliment about something they have done in this course, then hand it over. The receiver accepts the compliment and takes off one layer of wrapping. Keep going until it is unwrapped.

Task 3 (level 2) Which technique? Write A, B, C or D for each reply.

  1. "Hang on. What exactly went wrong with the layout? Show me the measurement that's off." ___
  2. "I hear you. I'd like you to stop calling me 'kid' in front of the other trades." ___
  3. "You're right, I didn't sweep before the inspector came. I'll do it first thing tomorrow." ___
  4. "I don't agree that I never listen. I followed the first three steps exactly. I missed the fourth one and I'll redo it." ___

Task 4 (level 2) Answer the criticism. For each statement, write a reply that uses at least two techniques. Label them.

  1. "You always take forever on the cleanup."
  1. "Did you even look at the drawing? The opening's in the wrong spot."
  1. "You're useless with the transit." (said in front of the crew)

Task 5 (level 2) Give a correction. Your partner on the job keeps leaving the extension cords across the walkway. Write what you would say, using what I saw, why it matters and what to do.

Task 6 (level 2) Feedback or bullying? Using the WorkSafeBC definition, write F for fair feedback or management, or B for bullying and harassment.

  1. Your supervisor says, "Your cuts on that run were off by a quarter inch. Measure twice before you cut the next set." ___
  2. A co-worker calls you a derogatory name every morning in front of the crew. ___
  3. Your foreman assigns you to site clean-up for the afternoon. ___
  4. The crew makes every new apprentice go through a humiliating "initiation." ___
  5. A journeyperson disagrees with your plan for routing the conduit and explains why. ___
  6. Your lead hand yells at you and threatens you after a mistake. ___

Check yourself

  1. What makes a compliment specific?
  2. What are the three parts of a fair correction?
  3. Name the four A B C D techniques and say what each one does.
  4. Why is it a good idea to acknowledge before you explain?
  5. Is being assigned a task you do not like bullying? Why or why not?
