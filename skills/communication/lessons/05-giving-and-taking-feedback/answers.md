# Giving and taking feedback and criticism

## Task 1 (level 1) Make it specific

Sample answers. A good answer names a real action and, if possible, why it helped.

1. "Nice work getting all the forms stripped and stacked before lunch. That let the next crew start early."
2. "Thanks for having the next sheet ready every time I needed it. We got the wall done in half the time."
3. "Thanks for showing me how to set up the laser level this morning. I can do it on my own now."

## Task 2 (level 1) Pass it on

This is a speaking activity. A good compliment is sincere and names something specific the person did in the course. A good receiver accepts it, for example "Thanks, I appreciate that," instead of brushing it off.

## Task 3 (level 2) Which technique?

1. C. Asks for details.
2. B. Sets a limit on how they are treated.
3. A. Accepts the mistake and says what will change.
4. D. Disagrees with the overstatement and accepts the fair part.

## Task 4 (level 2) Answer the criticism

Sample answers. Check that each reply uses at least two techniques, labels them correctly and stays calm.

1. "I don't agree that I always take forever (D). Which part of the cleanup is slowing things down? (C)"
2. "You're right, I measured from the wrong line (A). Can you show me the dimension on the drawing so I get it right this time? (C)"
3. "Can we talk over here? (B) I'm still learning the transit (A). I'd like you to tell me what I'm doing wrong instead of calling me useless in front of the crew (B)."

## Task 5 (level 2) Give a correction

Sample answer: "The cords were across the walkway again this morning (what I saw). Someone carrying sheets could trip (why it matters). Can you run them along the wall and tape where they cross? (what to do)"

A good answer talks about the work, not the person, has all three parts and does not exaggerate ("you always").

## Task 6 (level 2) Feedback or bullying?

1. F. Constructive feedback about the work.
2. B. Calling someone derogatory names is a WorkSafeBC example of bullying and harassment.
3. F. Assigning work is reasonable management action.
4. B. Humiliating initiation practices or hazing are a WorkSafeBC example.
5. F. Expressing a difference of opinion is not bullying.
6. B. Yelling and threats are verbal aggression and intimidation.

## Check yourself

1. It names exactly what the person did, and ideally why it helped.
2. What I saw, why it matters, what to do.
3. Acknowledge accepts a fair point about a mistake. Boundaries says how you expect to be treated. Clarify asks for details. Disagree calmly pushes back on what is not true.
4. It shows you heard the person. Leading with an excuse sounds like you are not listening, even when the excuse is true.
5. No. WorkSafeBC says reasonable action by an employer or supervisor relating to the management and direction of workers, including assigning work, is not bullying and harassment.
